# The ordinary Vrindavan lane temple

*Researched 2026-09-27. Impact: changes the building TYPE, not its detail.*

## The one sentence

**We are modelling the wrong building type.** The ordinary Vrindavan lane
mandir is not a temple-shaped object at all — it is **a house with a gate**.
The cuboid-plus-shikhara recipe is a Nagara-temple diagram; the thing in the
lane is a *haveli-temple*, an introverted courtyard house with a deity in it,
and the only part of it the lane ever sees is **a gateway with a name-board
on it**.

The load-bearing citation, Sinha & Dhariwal, *Myth and Placemaking in
Vernacular Settlements: Insights from Vrindavan* (ISVS e-journal 11.10, 2024),
summarising Nath 1996 and Jain 2007:

> "…this being a house of a God, has had a garbhagriha facing the East and the
> arcades on the other three sides of the courtyard in a tri-partite
> composition. **While no domes or soaring shikhar towers drew attention to the
> building, the entry gate known as gokhe is emphasized by porches on either
> side of the main door and the jharokhas (balconies) above.** This introverted
> building type, combination of temple and house, has become the norm in
> Vrindavan; most temples built in the historic core in the eighteenth and
> nineteenth centuries, including the new Radharaman Temple built in 1826 CE,
> are based on this model."

Everything below follows from that.

## Frontage

Measured off OSM (Overpass, bbox 27.5650,77.6750–27.5950,77.7100, ODbL),
n=112 buildings within 25 m of a mapped road, road-facing edge:

| | p10 | p25 | median | p75 | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 4.6 m | 8.0 m | 13.4 m | 21.5 m | 30.1 m |
| worship (n=21) | 7.1 m | 9.7 m | 15.2 m | 21.0 m | 32.4 m |

**Upper bounds only** — Indian OSM habitually traces a compound, not a plot.
The useful evidence is the small end: Radha Raman itself is 14.7 x 15.3 m with
a **5.7 m road-facing edge**; Prabodhananda Sarasvati's samadhi is 3.1 x 5.9 m.
And the archetype, an untagged `building=temple`: **9.7 m on the lane, 30.4 m
back**. A lane temple is a SLOT, not a block. Depth ~2.5–3.5x frontage.

Distribution to generate (the split is judgement calibrated to the above):
45% at 5–9 m, 30% at 9–14 m, 15% at 3–5 m, 10% at 14–25 m.

**Flush with its neighbours and party-walled.** No forecourt, no setback — the
court is BEHIND the gate, which is the whole point of an introverted type.
Lane widths: gali 2.0–3.0 m, bazaar street 4.0–6.5 m, Parikrama Marg 8–15 m.

### The plinth is a bench

0.35–0.60 m, 2–3 risers (riser 0.16–0.18, tread 0.30–0.36). IS 1080 practice
puts the minimum at 450 mm. But the functional fact matters more — Sinha &
Dhariwal: "Devotees sit on plinths extending from houses in the ghera along
the street **and at the temple entry (gokhe)** for the temple doors to open in
the mornings and evenings."

So it runs WIDER than the gate, 1–3 m each side, and continues across the
neighbours. Not a pedestal under a building: a continuous street bench the
building happens to sit on. Bury the bottom 0.03–0.08 m — the lane level
rises, and Growse dug 2.44 m of accumulated debris off Govind Dev in 1873. A
plinth sitting exactly on the ground plane reads as a prop.

### The gate

No collapsible scissor gates — none seen on any temple front in a
42-photograph corpus; they are a commercial item. What is actually there:
- **~55% open cusped/multifoil arch**, clear opening 1.5–2.5 m x 2.5–3.5 m,
  timber leaves folded into the reveal by day. 5 lobes for a gateway, 3 for a
  window head; 7 is monumental scale and wrong here.
- **~30% MS grille / weldmesh** in a cusped reveal — also the monkey defence.
- **~15% rolling steel shutter**, rusted, on anything with a shop bay.

## The front elevation (two storeys, 7 m frontage, 7.5 m wall head)

| y (m) | element |
|---|---|
| 8.2–8.6 | parapet top (0.7–1.2 m; 0.13–0.17 x wall height) |
| 7.2–7.5 | upper chhajja, projection 0.45–0.90 m, 0.10–0.16 thick, drip lip |
| 5.0–7.2 | first-floor register — arcade or jharokha zone |
| ~4.9 | floor-line string course, 0.18–0.28 tall, 0.09–0.11 projection |
| 3.8–4.4 | lower chhajja / shop hood over the gate |
| 3.3–3.8 | **NAME BAND** |
| 2.4 | arch springing |
| 0.45 | plinth top / sitting ledge |

Floor-to-floor 3.0–3.4 m.

**Solid:void — modellers punch too many windows.** Ground floor is typically
ONE opening: the gate, sometimes flanked by BLIND cusped niches with grilles
that read as openings and are not. Ground-floor void 12–25%. First floor
2–5 openings, void 25–55%. Whole front 20–30% void.

Openings are **arched on the temple, square on everything else** — that
contrast is most of what makes a temple legible in the street.

**One jharokha per building, centred over the gate**, not one per bay.
Width 1.60 m, clear 1.15, projection 0.80, floor at 4.25 m, 5–6 brackets at
0.30–0.40 m pitch.

**Parapet**: most common is a pierced JALI run — posts 0.22 m square at 1.50 m
centres, panels 0.62 m tall, coping 0.12. Then turned balusters, then a plain
dwarf wall. Kangura merlons belong to gateways and the monumental group only.
**Raise the parapet +0.40 m across the gate bay** with a 0.20 m step each
side; a parapet at one height all round is the flattest possible answer.

### Signage — three boards, always

Half the visual identity of a Vrindavan temple front.

1. **Painted name band over the arch**, 0.35–0.50 m tall, **yellow field, red
   Devanagari**. Sometimes incised into the plaster instead of painted.
2. **Municipal board**, ~0.6 x 0.9 m, **blue field, white and red text**,
   bilingual, on the jamb — Nagar Nigam Mathura-Vrindavan.
3. **A small red or yellow tin notice**, 0.25 x 0.35 m — no shoes, no phones.

Plus **pasted paper posters up the lower 1.5 m of both jambs**, peeling.

Names follow the DONOR REGION: "X-wali Kunj", "X Mandir", "X Dharamshala" —
Ratlam Kunj, Sailana Kunj, Datia Nohra, Gwalior Mandir, Manipur Kunj. Not
"Shri X Temple".

## The roof

**60–70% have no tower at all.** The type was defined by not having one.
15–25% have a bangla roof or small chhatri **over the gate bay, not the
sanctum**, 1.5–2.5 m — an ornament on the parapet line. 10–20% have a genuine
shikhara, and it is stubby and modern, 2.5–4.0 m on a 6–8 m building, **over
the sanctum at the BACK of the plot**, so it is usually invisible from the
lane. (Direction well-supported; percentages are an estimate — nobody has
published a roof-type census.)

What is actually up there, all photographed:
- **Water tank on 80%+ of roofs** — stainless or black/white plastic, 500–2000
  L, on a mild-steel stand 0.6–1.2 m, with a vertical inlet pipe. The most
  recognisable item in an Indian skyline.
- **Monkey-proof steel mesh cage over the terrace** — weldmesh on light
  angle-iron, 1.8–2.5 m tall. Vrindavan Today: "Houses are grilled with metal
  so that monkeys can't enter." **Specific to this town, and it will do more
  for recognisability than a shikhara will.**
- **Dhvaja** — thin steel pole 2.5–4 m, triangular white or saffron flag, on
  the gate pavilion rather than the sanctum.
- **A mumty** — the stair head room, 2 x 2.5 m, unrendered brick, one door.
- Laundry on nylon line, TV dishes, floodlights aimed at the facade.
- **The wires.** A thick black bundle crossing every lane diagonally at 5–7 m,
  service drops sagging to each building, a tangle at every pole. In the
  photographs these are genuinely the most prominent object in the frame.
  **Lanes with no overhead wires will look wrong no matter what the buildings
  do.**

## Colour — measured

42 photographs, band 15–50% of frame height, sky and foliage masked,
n = 264,233 px.

**Built surfaces in a Vrindavan lane:**
- Saturation: p10 5%, p25 10%, **median 21%**, p75 39%, p90 54%
- Value: p10 16%, p25 27%, **median 44%**, p75 64%, p90 78%

A low-saturation, mid-dark town. Above S 50% or V 80% in any quantity is
wrong.

| family | share | median | sunlit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **achromatic** (S<12%) — bare cement, grey, whitewash | **30%** | `#908989` | — |
| terracotta / brown-ochre (H 14–28) | 17.8% | `#6e5648` | `#bc947d` |
| buff / cream / golden-ochre (H 28–42) | 9.7% | `#70614c` | `#cfaf8f` |
| red / maroon / oxide (H 0–14) | 8.7% | `#6a4741` | `#b8837d` |
| pale yellow, chalked limewash (H 42–62) | 7.5% | `#8f8653` | `#cac18d` |
| pink / salmon (H 330–360) | 7.2% | `#79434d` | `#c07181` |
| blue + cyan-grey (paint, shutters, boards) | 12.2% | `#2d3743` | `#495565` |

Anchors: Radha Raman's sunlit cream dome `#f5dbc0` is the brightest built
surface found anywhere. Exposed lakhori brick `#836857`. **Algae `#20261f` —
much darker than instinct, not a bright green.** Bare cement in a gali reads
COOL grey-blue `#99a6b1`, not warm.

**Material shares (reasoned, not a survey):** plastered painted brick ~65%,
bare cement plaster ~15%, exposed brick ~8%, ceramic tile on the entrance bay
only ~7%, marble ~4%, **red or buff sandstone ~1%.** Red sandstone belongs to
the ASI sixteenth-century group and is wrong for 97% of these.

### The one authoritative colour regulation

The Mathura-Vrindavan Development Authority requires every building on both
sides of the **Parikrama Marg** to paint its **front elevation** "light yellow
colour (postcard colour)" and install signage "in heritage colour". **No
published hex exists for either — do not invent one and present it as
official.** (The researcher's own reading is ~`#F0E0AC`–`#EFD9A0` fresh,
weathering to `#cac18d`, and flags it as interpretation.)

The consequence for modelling is the real prize: **the front elevation is a
different colour from the other three faces.** True by this regulation and by
ordinary practice. Never paint a Vrindavan building one colour on all sides.

### Weathering — five observed rules

1. **Vertical black streaks from every horizontal edge** — chhajja drip, sill,
   string course, balcony slab. 0.10–0.25 m wide, 0.8–2.2 m long, 2–5 per 6 m.
   Never in the middle of a plain wall.
2. **A damp/algae band at the bottom 0.6–1.2 m**, dark green-black
   `#20261f`–`#49573f`, HARD-EDGED at the top. 2.6 m on riverside buildings —
   the Yamuna silt line.
3. **Paint fails as flakes revealing a stratigraphy** — pale yellow limewash
   over blue-grey over grey plaster over lakhori brick. Irregular patches
   0.2–0.8 m across on 25–40% of painted area, each a DIFFERENT HUE from its
   surroundings, not a darkening of it.
4. **The red-oxide skirting.** Almost every painted building carries a band of
   red/maroon oxide 0.30–0.60 m tall over the plinth and lowest wall — the
   cheap way to hide the damp band. "One of the most reliable small features
   in the whole town and it costs one box."
5. **Painted decoration on small shrines** — black-and-white chevron banding
   on pilasters, white floral rosettes on a dark ground.

### What gets repainted, and what never does

Repainted annually before Janmashtami / Holi / Diwali: the entrance bay, arch
face and jambs; the name band; the red-oxide skirting; the joinery; the
chhajja soffit. Never touched: side and rear walls, the inner face of the
parapet, the roof, the mumty, the tank stand, anything above the first-floor
chhajja on a side elevation.

**Practical rule: give the entrance bay (gate width + 1 m each side, up to the
first chhajja) a fresh clean saturated treatment and everything else 5–20
years of neglect.** The value step between those two zones is most of what
makes a Vrindavan facade look real.

## What a modeller always gets wrong

1. **Putting a shikhara on it.** 60–70% have no tower. Flat roof, parapet,
   water tank; the emphasis goes on the gate.
2. **Making it free-standing.** It is party-walled and **its neighbours are
   TALLER** — 3, 4, 5 storeys of housing against a 2-storey temple. Model it
   as a notch in a continuous wall of buildings.
3. **Setting it back behind a forecourt.** The court is behind the gate.
4. **Punching windows across the ground floor.** There is one opening.
5. **One flat colour.**
6. **Red sandstone** — wrong material for 97% of them.
7. **Fire-engine red and pure white.** Nothing outdoors in Braj is achromatic
   and nothing exceeds S 60% except signboards and cloth.
8. **A uniform grime multiply.** Two directional gradients instead: bottom-up
   damp, top-down streaks starting only at horizontal edges.
9. **Clean skies and empty roofs.**
10. **No signage.**
11. **Generic names** instead of the donor-region pattern.
12. **Symmetrical, aligned, orthogonal.** Vary frontage angle to the lane by
    ±10°, mismatch floor lines across party walls by 0.3–0.8 m, and let each
    parapet differ in height from its neighbour's.
13. **Collapsible scissor gates.**
14. **Ground darker than the building.** Measured plaza dust at Govind Dev is
    `#c9a98e` — LIGHTER than the building. Dark ground makes the temple float.

## Explicitly NOT confident / do not build

- The frontage distribution split, the shikhara percentages, and the material
  shares are **judgement calibrated to measurement, not surveys.**
- **All colour hexes are patch medians from uncontrolled photographs.** Hues
  are reliable; absolute values are indicative. Several samples were
  contaminated by green bounce from trees and by saturated clothing.
- Lane width 2.0–2.5 m is ONE photogrammetric measurement of ONE gali, ±20%.
- Loudspeaker horns: plausible from general Indian practice, **not confirmed**
  on a Vrindavan lane temple in this corpus.
- **Do not invent a hex for the MVDA "postcard colour" or "heritage colour"** —
  the notice names them in words only.
- **Do not encode an MVDA height limit**; reporting says the restriction
  "remained only on paper" and gives no number.
- **Do not fabricate plan geometry for any named small temple.** The record
  for the eleven Madhya Pradesh-owned temples gives no measurements, no room
  counts, no storey heights, no areas. Generate anonymous ones on the rules
  above and name them on the donor-region pattern.
- **Do not use the "5,000 temples" figure as data** — no source that gives it
  claims it is a count.
- **Do not bake photograph pixels into textures.** The Commons corpus was used
  for measurement and description only; the numbers are facts about the world,
  the images are not ours to trace.
