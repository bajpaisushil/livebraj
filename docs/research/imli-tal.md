# imli-tal

*Researched and independently verified 2026-09-27. Confidence: partial.*

## oneLine

Imli Tala (Imlitala Mahaprabhu Mandir) — the tamarind-tree seat on the old Yamuna bank where Krishna is said to have sat chanting Radha's name and where Chaitanya Mahaprabhu sat daily with his japa-mala; today a Gaudiya Math compound (Sri Gaudiya Sangha) whose whole courtyard is roofed by a steel space-frame built to shelter the dead leaning trunk of the ancient tamarind, which is still there, jacketed in sheet metal, with a living offshoot tamarind beside it.

THE OLD RIVERFRONT ARGUMENT — I CHECKED IT IN THE SOURCE AND IT HOLDS. Growse's 1883 "List of River-side Gháts" at Brindá-ban reads, in order: 7 Súraj Ghát ... 9 Jugal Ghát (built by Hari Dás and Gobind Dás, Thákurs) ... 18 Tehriwála ... 19 Imla Ghát ... 20 Bardwán ... 23 Singár Ghát (built by the Gosáin of the temple of Singár-bat) ... 27 Chir Ghát or Chain Ghát (built by Malhár Ráo, Holkar) ... 32 Kesi Ghát (built by the Bharatpur Rání Lachhmi). Their quoted sequence is verbatim correct, and it runs in the same south-west-to-north-east order as the measured OSM positions of Jugal, Imli, Shringar, Chir and Keshi. Two entirely independent bodies of evidence, 140 years apart, agree on the position. This is the strongest single fact in the whole account.

So: riverside road in front, river beyond a broad sandy foreshore, not water lapping the wall. Wedged into a dense street of havelis and shops, cables overhead, neighbours' walls hard against it on both sides.

## architecture

VERDICT FIRST: I could not break this account. Every claim I was able to test independently held, and several held to the metre. The geodata is exact, the ghat evidence is exact, the "yellow wall" is corroborated, the two-trees reading is corroborated and now datable, and the SEO-boilerplate warnings are fair. What follows is their description with my corrections and additions marked [CORRECTED], [ADDED] or [FLAG]. Everything unmarked stands as they wrote it and should be built as written.

A walled compound entered from the street up a flight of steps: STREET → GATEWAY ON A RAISED PLINTH → COVERED TREE COURTYARD → MAIN TEMPLE BLOCK at the back. It is not one building; the courtyard is the point of the place.

[ADDED — what the buildings ARE, which changes how you model their date and quality] These are 20th-century Gaudiya Math buildings, not a historic Goswami temple. Growse's exhaustive 1883 temple-and-ghat survey of Vrindavan records "19 Imla Ghát" in its ghat list and NOTHING ELSE — I searched the full text; there is no temple at Imli Tala in Growse. So the present compound postdates 1883. The site was under the Goswamis of the Radha Madan Mohan temple; it passed to Srila Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaj, who "renovated the imlitala temple on the bank of the yamuna" (wva-vvrs.org). He died 20 May 1964, and the handover followed a dream of his guru Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, who departed 1 January 1937 — so THE GAUDIYA RESTORATION FALLS BETWEEN 1937 AND 1964, with the present decorated fabric later still. That is a bracket, not a date; no source states a construction year. [FLAG] faujitoursandtravels.com contradicts this, saying Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and his disciples themselves "purchased the land at Imli Tala and built a beautiful Gaudiya Matha". Unresolved. Either way: modern plastered masonry by a Gaudiya institution, which is exactly why the researcher's "no shikhara, no marble palace" warnings are right.

1) STREET ELEVATION. Compound floor roughly 1.5–1.8 m above the street. A broad flight of about 9–10 steps (pale grey/pink stone, ~5.5 m wide, one stainless handrail up the middle, a second at the right) rises to an ornamental stone TORANA. Carved stone gate: two square piers with fluted shafts, moulded bases, gilded capitals, carrying a wide foliate-carved arch whose spandrels hold big carved volutes and pendant bead-ropes; a large ribbed KALASH on the arch crown flanked by two swan-neck volutes. Above and behind, a curved bangla-chala crest panel incised "सिद्धपीठ इमलीतला महाप्रभु जी की बैठक", two carved birds flanking a rosette on top, a blue oval panel with a winged garuda/hamsa figure. Flanking screen walls set slightly back with arched relief panels and the English band "SIDDHAPITH IMLITALA MAHAPRABHU MANDIR"; a red banner board repeats it. Raw pale grey stone in 2008 photographs; since painted deep gold/mustard-ochre with red, blue and green picking-out — build it as PAINTED CARVED STONE, not gilt metal. Behind the arch, tall dark timber doors with copper-coloured panelled leaves and rosette bosses; a silver steel grille gate in later photographs.
[ADDED] "सिद्धपीठ" on the gate is corroborated by the temple's own blog, which calls the place "the siddhapith". Their reading of the inscription is sound.

2) THE TWO OLD RED CHHATRIS. Unchanged — I could not test this and nothing contradicts it. At street level, one each side of the steps (a third, smaller, further left): squat OPEN KIOSKS in deep red-brown sandstone, short columns, cusped multifoil arches, moulded cornice with scalloped pendant band, broad segmented/ribbed hemispherical dome with small lotus finial. Visibly older and more weathered than everything else, lower than the new gate. The right-hand one swallowed by a shop with a corrugated-iron roof; the left one open, with a small ochre-plastered shrine inside. Their attribution of these to Growse's ghat-front kiosks is correctly labelled as their own inference — keep it labelled that way. Growse says nothing about structures at Imla Ghat.

3) THE STEEL CANOPY — the defining structure. Unchanged. A very large tubular STEEL SPACE-FRAME over the whole tree courtyard: two-layer galvanized pipe lattice with diagonal bracing, tall posts, shallow cambered top, ~12–14 m above the courtyard, spanning the full compound width. Industrial scaffolding, not architecture; the first thing visible over the gate from the street. Cladding varies between photographs — bare lattice (2008), dark wire-mesh side panels, translucent white roof sheeting — so model it as mesh/translucent-clad open lattice with daylight coming through. [ADDED] No text source I found describes this structure, which is precisely why the photographic reading matters and why every SEO page gets the place wrong.

4) THE ANCIENT TRUNK. Unchanged, and now datable. The dead trunk of the old tamarind still stands, LEANING at about 25–30° from horizontal, springing from a white-tiled masonry pedestal (~1.2 m high, stainless railings, small painted figures set into its face) and running up and across the court for some 12–15 m before disappearing behind the temple wall. Encased in a tapering SHEET-METAL JACKET (bare galvanized grey; plastered and painted salmon-pink in older photographs), open at intervals so the black hollow crumbling wood shows through, with a decorative yellow-painted band round its foot.
[ADDED] THE OLD TREE DIED IN THE EARLY 1990s — vrindavanforest.com and brajdham000 both state it plainly. That dates the jacket and the canopy to after c.1992 and makes the preservation a roughly thirty-year-old intervention, not an ancient one. Build the jacket and space-frame as modern, industrial and slightly shabby.

5) THE LIVING TREE. Unchanged, and corroborated. A younger tamarind — real pinnate feathery foliage, trunk ~0.4–0.5 m, roughly 10 m tall — beside it in the same court, crown pushing up into the canopy. [ADDED] Sources call it "an offshoot of the original tree" (vrindavanforest.com, brajdham000), so it is a descendant growth in the same spot, not a nursery replacement planted elsewhere in the court. In earlier photographs the tree and trunk are additionally fenced by a green welded-wire mesh cage in green angle-iron framing, polygonal in plan, on a low white marble kerb, potted tulsi round the base, a green-framed gate.

6) THE OCTAGONAL PLATFORM. Unchanged and corroborated in substance. Between the tree and the temple door, a raised white-marble OCTAGONAL platform, ~6–7 m across, ~0.45 m high with low steps, a dark band round its vertical face, the red Hindi inscription "महाप्रभु यमुना दर्शन" cut into the kerb. On it a small shrine kiosk, ~1.8 m square, ~3 m to its finial — four corner colonnettes, cusped arch opening, stepped pyramidal roof of four or five receding slabs, gold sun/chakra emblem on top. Raw white marble in 2008, now painted salmon-pink with white mouldings, printed floral curtains tied at the sides. The seated Mahaprabhu inside. Two life-size white-painted seated Vaishnava figures with folded hands flank it; a stainless two-rail tubular railing runs round the perimeter.
[ADDED] Four independent sources confirm the kiosk and its occupant: "a small shrine beneath the tree where a deity of Lord Chaitanya can be seen sitting in meditation." This is the most-photographed object on the site after the trunk.

7) THE MAIN TEMPLE FRONT. Unchanged. Single storey, flat-roofed with plain parapet, ~14–16 m wide. Pale peach/salmon plaster above, DEEP RED SANDSTONE dado, pilasters and window surrounds below. Tall rectangular central doorway (~2.2 × 3 m), carved brown timber double doors, red sandstone pilasters with gilded capitals; FOUR WHITE ELEPHANT-HEAD BRACKETS with curled trunks and little brass bells project above the opening, two each side. Above them a blue lintel band with gold borders, painted Hindi captions, two gold swastikas. Above that three large POLYCHROME STUCCO RELIEF PANELS in gold foliate frames — left, Mahaprabhu dancing with devotees at the Jagannath temple; centre, a gold-framed arched niche with NARASIMHA ("NARSINGHA BHAGWAN / श्री नृसिंह भगवान"); right, "DELIVERING THE MUSLIM DACOIT 'WABLEE KHAN'". Barred windows in red sandstone surrounds flank the door; cream inscription boards with red-and-gold borders at ground level each side; a saffron-orange donation box ("दानपात्र / DONATION") ~1.2 m tall in front.

8) COURTYARD PERIMETER WALLS. Unchanged, and the key detail corroborated. Peach/salmon plaster over a pink-grey marble dado about a metre high, broken by gabled/cusped recesses each holding a full-colour high-relief lila panel with a red Hindi caption strip beneath. Captions as they read them, including "श्रीराधारानी का पक्की इमली से फिसलकर गिरना", "मान सरोवर", "श्री महारासलीला", "श्रीपाद सनातन गोस्वामी द्वारा गौरनिताई विग्रह की स्थापना", "MAHAPRABHU TRAVELLING THROUGH JHARAKHANDA", "DELIVERING SHREE PRAKASHANANDA SARASWATI".
[ADDED] Two of these captions are independently confirmed as the site's own stories. The Radharani-and-the-tamarind panel matches bhagavatam-katha.com: "the tree was cursed by Shrimati Radharani when she accidentally injured her foot on the thick bark of a ripe tamarind fruit, which delayed her meeting with Krishna." And the Sanatana Goswami panel matches the temple's own claim that Sanatana installed the Nitai-Gaur deities. These are the right stories for this wall.
[ADDED] THE YELLOW WALL IS CONFIRMED BY AN INDEPENDENT SOURCE. faujitoursandtravels.com: "the area is surrounded by a yellow wall, and the tamarind tree can still be seen through the gates." Their gold/ochre screen with blue-and-gold cusped-arch panels round the tamarind is real, and the tree being visible through the gate from outside is real.
A grey stone pillar in the court carries the temple's board "इमलीतला की महिमा" in red Devanagari, ending "PLEASE FOR THE PROTECTION OF THIS TREE OFFER YOUR KIND DONATION".

9) INTERIOR HALL. As described, with the altar correction in the deities field. A single rectangular hall, roughly 14 m across the back wall, perhaps 9–11 m deep, ceiling ~5 m, flat, blue-and-gold painted border band and gold lotus rosettes, two ceiling fans, pendant bulbs. Walls pale pink/lilac. A dark red-brown panelled dado ~2.5 m high along the back wall with THREE OPENINGS. [CORRECTED] Build all three as shrine bays, not two shrines and a door — Radha-Gopinath at centre (dark timber proscenium, behind it a carved grey cusped-arch backpiece with colonnettes; in 2008 the whole backpiece was carved dark rosewood topped with a row of brass finials), with Nitai-Gaur and the Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati alcove flanking, hand uncertain. Stainless railings in front of each. Above the dado a continuous band of painted high-relief tableaux in gold frames — a very large rasa-lila with dozens of figures at centre, Mahaprabhu scenes and painted panels of Jagannath Puri and the sea to either side — two round wall clocks mounted among them. Full 3D painted dioramas with furnished interiors (purple walls, bookshelves, thatched ceiling). Floor grey-and-white patterned marble with a scrolled inlay border. "PHOTOGRAPHY STRICTLY PROHIBITED" boards flank the altar. [ADDED] The temple's own blog: "Original artwork depicting the divine Lila of Sri Gauranga decorate the inside of the Mandir" — the relief band is the temple's own boast, so give it real weight in the model.

10) OTHER. Guesthouse/ashram attached; a multi-storey cream/white block with green window grilles overlooks the courtyard. The samadhi beside the tamarind is CONFIRMED and is that of Srila Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaj.

THIN AREAS, restated honestly: no dimensions are published anywhere; no plan or survey exists; there is no building footprint in OpenStreetMap — I queried Overpass within 150 m and there is only the bare node, no way, no building. The construction date of the present buildings is nowhere stated. The date the Gaudiya line took over is nowhere stated, though it is now bracketed 1937–1964.

## distinguishing

Their two markers are correct and I would not change a word of them.

FIRST: the enormous LEANING DEAD TRUNK of the ancient tamarind, wrapped in a bare sheet-metal jacket, rising diagonally out of a white-tiled pedestal and driving straight across the courtyard at about 25–30 degrees, black hollow wood showing through the open seams. Visitors walk underneath it. The tree died in the early 1990s and was not removed — it was encased and kept.

SECOND: the INDUSTRIAL STEEL SPACE-FRAME roof thrown over the entire courtyard purely to shelter that trunk — raw pipe lattice with mesh and translucent sheeting, 12–14 m up, visible from the street above the gilded gate, completely unlike a temple roof.

THIRD, weaker but useful: the pair of squat weathered RED SANDSTONE DOMED KIOSKS at street level either side of the entrance steps, one half-buried inside a shop with a tin roof, sitting lower and far older than the bright gold gate between them.

[ADDED — a fourth, and it is cheap to build] THE TWO TREES IN ONE COURT. The dead jacketed trunk and a living tamarind growing beside it, described by sources as an offshoot of the original. Every written account of this place gets it wrong in one direction or the other — either "the tree has vanished" or "the 5,000-year-old tree is still here." Both objects are present simultaneously and that juxtaposition is the whole meaning of the courtyard. Build both.

[ADDED] A fifth, for the street: the tamarind is VISIBLE FROM OUTSIDE THROUGH THE GATE, over a yellow wall — this is how one independent source identifies the place. The sightline from the street, up the steps, through the torana, past the yellow screen wall to the trunk under the steel lattice, is the shot that makes it recognisable.

## doNotBuild

All of the other researcher's warnings stand and I found independent support for several. Two of their warnings need correcting.

— DO NOT build a shikhara over the visible temple. The main block facing the courtyard is SINGLE-STOREY AND FLAT-ROOFED with a plain parapet. Their caution about the white curvilinear rekha-shikhara with a maroon pennant behind the canopy is correct and I could not resolve it either. If you include it, set it far back and small; do not put it over the gate or the hall.
— DO NOT build it as a marble temple. Plastered masonry painted peach/salmon, red sandstone dados, stone carving painted gold, polychrome cement/stucco relief; marble only on floors, the octagonal platform and the acharya alcove linings. SEO pages saying "marble architecture lit up at night" and "domes and pillars" (travelacharya.in, faujitoursandtravels.com) are boilerplate — I checked both; neither describes anything specific to this building.
— DO NOT make the gate gold metal. Carved pale grey stone, later PAINTED gold/mustard with red, blue and green.
— DO NOT build a bathing ghat with steps into the water. Growse 1883 records "19 Imla Ghát" but the river has moved; multiple sources say so plainly ("the Yamuna has now moved some distance from this place"; the ghat "is still known as Imli Tala Ghat" in name only). Riverside road in front, river beyond a broad sandy bed.
— DO NOT put the tamarind out in the open sky. Roofed for at least eighteen years.
— DO NOT treat the ancient tree as gone, and DO NOT treat it as alive. It died in the early 1990s; the trunk was kept and encased; an offshoot grows beside it.
— DO NOT dedicate it to Hanuman. travelacharya.in says this and contradicts itself; it is wrong.
— [CORRECTED] THEIR "NORTH OF SEVA KUNJ" WARNING IS ITSELF WRONG. They tell you not to place it "north of Seva Kunj near Radha Damodar" and call that "a garbled version of the real position". It is not garbled — it is right. Measured from OSM nodes, Imli Tala is 155 m NORTH-WEST of Seva Kunj and 147 m WEST of Radha Damodara. travelacharya.in's location sentence is fair; only its Hanuman claim is false. Do not discard a correct locational statement because the page around it is poor.
— [CORRECTED] THEIR GROWSE CLAIM IS OVERSTATED. They write that Growse "names a patron for nearly every other Vrindavan ghat and names none for this one." I read the list. Growse names no builder for Imla Ghát (19) — but also none for Súraj (7), Dhusar (10), Nágari Dás (15) or Barwára (21). Five of thirty-two lack a patron. The conclusion survives — NO FOUNDER IS RECORDED FOR IMLA GHÁT, so invent none — but do not dress the silence up as singular.
— DO NOT invent founding dates. No source states when the present buildings were put up. The only defensible bracket is that the Gaudiya restoration falls between 1937 and 1964, and that the site carries no temple in Growse's 1883 survey.
— The tree's age: the temple's own board says ~5,500 years, popular pages say 5,000. Site claim, not a fact, and it drives no modelling decision.
— [ADDED] DO NOT CONFUSE THE RADHA-GOPINATH DEITIES INSIDE IMLI TALA WITH THE RADHA GOPINATH TEMPLE 610 M AWAY. The Goswami Radha Gopinath Mandir is a separate temple to the north-east (OSM node 4569657292). Imli Tala's Radha-Gopinath were installed by Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaj in the 20th century. This is exactly the Goswami-temple mix-up the brief warns about.
— [ADDED] DO NOT PUT ISKCON HERE. "Srila Prabhupada" in this temple's own text means Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura (d. 1937), not A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami. No ISKCON branding, no ISKCON founder-acharya vyasasana.
— [ADDED] DO NOT LABEL IT "VRAJA-MOHAN TEMPLE". vrindavanforest.com places the Imli Tala remains "in the courtyard of Vraja-Mohan temple". I could corroborate that name nowhere else. Treat it as a guidebook artefact; the signed name on the gate is Siddhapith Imlitala Mahaprabhu Mandir.
— [ADDED] DO NOT TRUST THE PUBLISHED DARSHAN TIMINGS. Four sources give four different sets (5:15am–7:30pm; 5:30–12 and 4–8; 5–12 and 4–8; summer 7:30–12:30 and 4:30–9, winter 8:30–12 and 5–9). If your build needs hours, pick one and mark it as unverified.

## deities

THE OTHER RESEARCHER GAVE NO DEITIES AT ALL. This is the single largest gap in their account and it is fillable from the temple's own words.

The temple's own blog (imlitala-mahaprabhumandir.blogspot.com, Dec 2008) names three sets of Ishta-devas, verbatim:
— "Sri Sri Nitai Gaur established personally by Srila Sanantan Goswami" — Nityananda Prabhu and Gauranga (Chaitanya), the pair traditionally held to have been worshipped by Sanatana Goswami himself. Small standing brass/metal pair.
— "Sri Sri Radha Gopinath established by Srila Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaj" — the main Radha-Krishna couple. vrindavanforest.com describes Krishna in black stone/marble and Radha in white, dressed in changing coloured attire.
— "Mahaprabhu established by Srila Prabhupada Siddhanta S Thakur" — a Chaitanya Mahaprabhu murti installed by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura. radha.name calls this "the first deity of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Prabhupada."

Separately, under the tamarind, on the octagonal platform: a seated, meditating Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — "a small shrine beneath the tree where a deity of Lord Chaitanya can be seen sitting in meditation" (vrindavanforest.com, brajdham000, vrindavanguide.com). This is the murti in the researcher's kiosk shrine, and it is correctly placed in their account.

Acharya memorials, not deities but on the altar wall and in the court — the blog says "The mortise [murti] of Srila Prabhupada, Srila Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaj and Srila Bhakti Surid [Suhrid] Akinchana Maharaj ... are to be seen inside." The samadhi beside the tamarind, which the researcher listed only as "reported", is confirmed: radha.name and thegaudiyatreasuresofbengal both name "the Samadhi of Srila Goswami Maharaj", i.e. Srila Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaj (1888 – 20 May 1964), founder-acharya of Sri Gaudiya Sangha and sevait of Imlitala.

ALTAR ARRANGEMENT — UNRESOLVED CONFLICT, flagged for the modeller. The researcher read the back wall as three openings: side doorway LEFT, main altar CENTRE, glazed acharya alcove RIGHT. vrindavanforest.com describes three deity sections: Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati LEFT, Radha-Krishna CENTRE, Gaura-Nitai RIGHT. Both agree on three openings in the dado and on Radha-Krishna at centre; they disagree on what the flanking two are and on which hand the acharya is. Build three bays. Do not build one of them as a plain door unless a photograph shows it — on the textual evidence at least two, probably all three, are shrines. Left/right is also ambiguous because sources differ on whether they mean the viewer's or the deity's side.

NOT Hanuman. travelacharya.in says the temple is "dedicated to Lord Hanuman" and contradicts itself two paragraphs earlier; it is simply wrong.
