# rangaji — exterior survey

*Researched 2026-09-27 by one agent and independently checked by a second whose brief was to refute it. Labels are the researcher's own: MEASURED / SOURCED / DERIVED / ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN.*

*Survey and checker are paired by the workflow journal's launch key, not by completion order — see BACKLOG 2026-09-30 for why that matters.*

## What it is

It is a walled Sri Vaishnava temple-city 236 m long by 135 m wide - the longest temple compound in Vrindavan - five concentric rectangular prakaras of buff sandstone with white stucco Dravidian gopurams rising out of them, entered from the town through a 28 m North Indian gatehouse, with a stepped tank and a four-square garden filling its entire eastern third.

## The silhouette

Everything survives - this is a fully intact, actively used 1851 temple, not a ruin, and nothing here is reconstruction guesswork about a lost building.

WHAT CROWNS IT: nothing crowns the compound as a whole. The roofline is deliberately flat and horizontal - long low cloister ranges and plain parapeted walls - punctured by free-standing white towers. The tallest object is the WESTERN RAJAGOPURAM, seven talas of ivory-white stucco sculpture on a plain battered sandstone base, topped not by a dome or a spire but by an OBLONG BARREL-VAULTED SALA (a ribbed, wagon-roofed oblong cap with flared horn-like ends) carrying SEVEN gold pot-kalashas in a row along its ridge, plus a flagpole with a saffron flag and a lightning conductor. The eastern gopuram is the same grammar at five talas.

Counted from photographs and imagery, the silhouette from any raised viewpoint is:
1. The west gatehouse: a three-storey North Indian pavilion on the OUTER wall, crowned by a curved-eave (bangaldar) roof with a shallow ribbed dome and four corner finials. 28.35 m (SOURCED). This, not a gopuram, is the outward face of the temple.
2. Behind and above it, ~28 m further east, the seven-tala gopuram.
3. Deeper in, further white towers on the inner prakaras. The ESRI image shows at least six or seven distinct white sunlit tower-tops rising out of the courts, two of them squarely on the east-west axis east of the first gopuram, and pairs symmetrically north and south of it.
4. Much lower, near the centre: the SANCTUM VIMANA - a small two-to-three tala white stucco shrine crowned by a horseshoe-arched apsidal gable (a nasika/sukanasa form) with a kirtimukha crest, which only just clears the plain red-brown parapet in front of it. It is emphatically NOT a tall tower.
5. In the eastern third, no towers at all: the open water of the tank, its two little chhatri kiosks, and the tree canopies of the garden.
6. One further element: a square sandstone shrine in an outer court capped by a WHITE RIBBED DOME with a large gold kalasha and white-and-gold figure sculpture (including a big Hanuman) clustered around its base - a north-Indian dome sitting inside a Dravidian compound.

The overall read from the town is therefore: a long, low, blank sandstone wall; one richly carved Mughal-Rajput gatehouse; and behind it a cluster of white, sculpture-encrusted, stepped pyramidal towers, all of them crowned by oblong wagon-roofs, none of them by a dome or a curvilinear spire.

## The elevation

Two completely different elevations exist and you need both.

A. THE WEST FRONT AS SEEN FROM THE TOWN (the public face; North Indian, 28.35 m, SOURCED height):
Reading bottom to top from photographs (File:Shri Rangji Mandir Vrindavan Rajput style Western Gate.jpg, File:Gate of Shri Rangji Mandir Vrindavan.jpg, File:Sri Rangji Mandir Vrindavan Western Gate Arch.jpg):
- Plinth and lower wall: plain buff sandstone ashlar with a carved skirting band; a low moulded base course.
- Storey 1 (approx. 0-9 m): dominated by one huge cusped (multifoil, ~11-13 foils) arch on the axis, ~5.5 m wide, filled with a studded two-leaf timber door. Flanking it on each side, two shallow cusped blind niches with carved floral tympana, plus one small doorway. Opening:solid on this storey ~ 30:70.
- First chhajja: a deep eave (projection ~1.2-1.5 m) carried on a continuous row of carved S-curved (serpentine) brackets, roughly 40-45 brackets across the front; above it a jali balustrade of turned balusters and pierced screen panels ~0.9 m high.
- Storey 2 (approx. 10-17 m): an open colonnaded gallery. Counted from the photograph: a central group of 5 cusped arches over the axis, and a group of 3 arches at each side, separated by solid carved piers - so 11 arched openings across the front. Opening:solid ~ 55:45. This is Growse's "reticulated tracery": jali screens fill the balustrades and some heads.
- Second chhajja and balustrade, same treatment.
- Storey 3 / crowning pavilion (approx. 18-24 m): a recessed pavilion of 5 arched openings with pierced jali infill, set back behind a railed terrace.
- Crown (24-28.35 m): a curved-eave "bangla" roof (concave cornice sweeping up at the ends) carrying a shallow ribbed dome, with four slender corner finials and one taller central finial. Top-heavy visual weight is deliberately avoided - the whole thing tapers.
Colour: one material throughout, warm buff-cream carved sandstone (#BD9A6C lit, #6D5031 shaded). Modern intrusions visible in 2026: razor wire on every parapet, conduit, a CCTV box.

B. THE GOPURAM ELEVATION (Dravidian; the inner faces; 7 talas west, 5 east):
- Base (lower ~30-33% of the height): plain, sharply battered ashlar sandstone, four or five very large flush panels per side, a heavy moulded cornice with small lion/yali corbels at the top. One rectangular gateway, roughly 3.5-4 m wide and 5-6 m high, with timber doors; carved door jambs; painted stone elephants flank the steps at the outer gopuram. Opening:solid on the base ~ 12:88.
- Superstructure: 7 receding talas (5 on the east gopuram). Each tala has, on the axis, ONE small rectangular window with a timber grille (7 windows stacked up the centre line, one per tala). Around each window, paired large standing figures; either side, a continuous frieze of miniature shrines - alternating square kutas and oblong salas - plus seated figures, makara-torana motifs and, at the tala corners, large projecting figures that lean outward. Opening:solid on the tower ~ 4:96; it reads as almost solid sculpture.
- Batter: the crest measures about 40% of the base width (MEASURED off the frontal photograph: tower base spans 0.52 of the frame width, crest 0.21), so the profile is noticeably steeper than a Tamil gopuram of the same tala count.
- Crown: an oblong barrel-vaulted sala with a ribbed roof and flared, horn-like end scrolls, carrying SEVEN gold pot-kalashas in a row on the ridge, plus a flagpole with a saffron flag and a lightning conductor.
- Small inner gates (e.g. the Vaikunth Dwar): the same grammar at 3 talas with FIVE gold kalashas, over a doorway whose surround is brilliantly painted rather than plain.

C. THE CLOISTER ELEVATION (repeats around every prakara, and is most of what a visitor actually sees):
- Continuous single-storey colonnade, flat roofed. Square sandstone piers with a moulded capital, figural relief panels on the lower shaft (standing devis, Krishna-lila scenes) and rearing yali/vyala brackets at the head. Roughly 3.5-4 m tall at ~3 m centres, standing on a plinth ~0.9 m above the court.
- Behind the colonnade, a plain ashlar wall with occasional doorways and, in places, long painted Devanagari inscription bands in red on the wall face.
- Above, a simple moulded cornice with a low sculpted frieze; on the inner mandapas this cornice is crowned by the brightly painted parapet of miniature shrines and figures.
- Opening:solid along a cloister run ~ 60:40 (it is mostly void between piers).

## Material and colour

STRUCTURE (SOURCED/MEASURED): load-bearing local sandstone ashlar, buff to pink (Bharatpur/Rupbas family - INFERRED from colour and region, no source names the quarry), with lime-plaster/stucco figure sculpture on the tower superstructures and vimanas. Timber for the doors and for the rath. Copper gilt for the dhvaja stambha (SOURCED, Growse). Gilded metal for the kalashas.

MEASURED HEX + HSV, sampled from CC BY-SA 4.0 photographs by Aliva Sahoo taken 19 April 2026 (medians of sunlit patches; overcast-to-hazy light, so saturation is slightly suppressed and a warm cast is possible - treat S as +/-5):
- Gopuram stucco, full sun: #E8E0D1 (H39 S10 V91). Mid-tone/ambient: #D7CAAC (H42 S20 V84). Shaded recesses: #AB9D7D (H42 S27 V67).
- Gopuram stucco where mould-streaked (very common, especially the lower talas and cornice soffits): #C4C6C5 (H150 S1 V78) - i.e. it de-saturates to neutral grey, with near-black streaks below every projecting cornice.
- Gopuram stone base / prakara wall, sunlit: #B69358 (H38 S52 V71), #B89561 (H36 S47 V72).
- West gatehouse sandstone, sunlit: #BD9A6C (H34 S43 V74); its crowning dome #CCB29E (H26 S23 V80); bracket/chhajja soffits in shade #6D5031 (H31 S55 V43).
- Cloister piers: lit #B69378 (H26 S34 V71); ashlar wall behind #82664C (H29 S42 V51).
- Court paving flags: #D3C5B4 (H33 S15 V83); in the satellite the big courts read distinctly pink-terracotta, so use a warm pink-buff flag, not grey.
- Tank revetment: upper plaster #B79A88; a pale PINK string course #B4A28C; a deep maroon-red band at the step zone #775443 (H20 S44 V47).
- Tank water: opaque green #8C9982 (H94 S15 V60) - algal, not blue.
- Building range immediately north of the tank, painted PERIWINKLE BLUE with a crenellated merlon parapet: lit #8999BA (H220 S26 V73), shaded #798BAD (H219 S30 V68). MEASURED; but whether this range is the temple's own or a neighbouring town building is UNCERTAIN.
- Polychrome on the inner mandapa roof-parapets: saturated ultramarine/cobalt blue, vermilion red, emerald green, chrome yellow and white on the miniature shrines and figures. Sampled values are unreliable (backlit), so treat as INFERRED: blue ~#2B3E9B, red ~#B8332B, green ~#1F7A4C, yellow ~#E0A81E.
- Painted gate surrounds (e.g. Vaikunth Dwar): cream-pink ground with gold scrollwork jambs on a dark maroon field, framed deity panels on blue and magenta grounds, and steps painted with yellow/red/green floral bands.
- Kalashas and the Vaishnava emblem: gilt/brass gold.
- Stone elephants flanking the outer gopuram steps: painted yellow caparison, black body, white tusks.

HISTORIC COLOUR CHANGE (SOURCED, photographic): in the Government of India Photo Division photograph of August 1949 the gopuram is dark, weathered and evidently unwhitewashed, its sculpture reading almost black against the sky. The ivory limewash seen today is later maintenance. For a present-day 1:1 reconstruction, ivory is correct; do not use it for a 19th-century scene.

## The setting

ORIENTATION (MEASURED): the long axis runs true east-west (OSM long sides bear 89.3 deg). The principal entrance is at the WEST END (SOURCED, Growse: "The principal or western entrance"), facing the old town; the smaller five-storey gopuram and the outer east gate face east.

STREET (MEASURED, OSM ways with geometry): a single north-south street runs past the west front 60-140 m from the west wall; OSM names its southern half "Mathura Road" and its northern half "Gopinath Bazar", the name changing almost exactly at the temple's latitude. Everything else within 300 m is unnamed residential lanes (25 residential + 15 service ways in OSM within 250 m) - a dense warren, not a grid.

HOW CLOSE THE TOWN COMES (MEASURED from ESRI z19 imagery): town buildings abut the NORTH, SOUTH and EAST walls directly - continuous two- and three-storey flat-roofed houses with parapets and roof terraces, with no setback at all on the north and south sides; on the south a lane runs immediately outside the wall. The east side is equally built up right to the tank and garden walls. ONLY the WEST has open space: a broad irregular forecourt between the gatehouse and the street, with trees, parked cars and buses, stalls, and a curving access road that sweeps around the north-west corner. A photograph of the west gate (File:Gate of Shri Rangji Mandir Vrindavan.jpg) shows a wide, almost empty sandy-paved lane in front of it with plain modern sandstone-block buildings alongside.

APPROACH: the pilgrim comes off the north-south street, crosses the west forecourt, and passes under the cusped arch of the North Indian gatehouse; the white gopuram is already visible framed inside that arch. From there the walk is due east along the axis, through gopuram after gopuram, into progressively smaller courts.

VISIBILITY FROM A DISTANCE: the gopurams are the tallest things in this quarter of Vrindavan and are visible over the roofs - the 1949 Photo Division panorama of Vrindavan includes the Rangaji gopuram rising above the town. But there is no long open view: the compound is embedded in the town and you only see the towers, never the walls, until you are in the immediate lanes. MEASURED from imagery, ordinary town roofs around it are 2-3 storeys, so the towers stand roughly 20-25 m clear of the surrounding roofscape.

INSIDE THE WALLS, EAST END: the tank court (NE) and the four-square garden (SE) are separated by a ~20 m processional avenue on the axis. The garden is the only substantial greenery inside; the courts elsewhere are bare paved flags, and the imagery shows large crowds strung along the axis in queues.

THE OTHER GARDEN, 631 m SOUTH: the Brahmotsav garden, mapped as OSM way 99427181 "Rangaji Bhag Garden", 128 x 276 m (3.54 ha), centre 27.57736 N 77.70351 E - 563 m south and 73 m east of the temple centre. Growse (p.261) records the rath being drawn "along the road, a distance of 690 yards" = 631 m, which matches a route west out of the gate and then south along Mathura Road. Growse's footnote adds that this garden "was purchased from the temple of Gopinath, and is still liable to an annual charge of Rs. 18." Satellite shows it today as an orchard/nursery-like walled block of trees and beds.

## What NOT to build

- DO NOT put a gopuram on the outside. The single most likely error. Growse p.260: 'The principal or western entrance of the OUTER court is surmounted by a pavilion, ninety-three feet high, constructed in the Mathura style after the design of a native artist. In its graceful outlines and the elegance of its reticulated tracery, it presents a striking contrast to the heavy and misshapen masses of the Madras Gopura, which rises immediately in front of it.' The gopuras belong to the inner temple-court, not the outer wall. From the street on the west you see a three-storey NORTH INDIAN gatehouse - cusped multifoil arches, deep chhajja eaves on serpentine brackets, jharokha balconies, jali railings, crowned by a curved-eave (bangaldar) pavilion of five arches with a shallow ribbed dome and four corner finials - and the white Dravidian gopuram appears FRAMED INSIDE ITS ARCH, 28 m further in. A CC BY-SA photograph (File:Sri Rangji Mandir Vrindavan Western Gate Arch.jpg) shows exactly that view.
- DO NOT build it as one tower over one hall. It is five concentric rectangular walled enclosures with cloistered ranges, nested around a small sanctum - confirmed on satellite as four clear rings inside the outer wall. The silhouette is horizontal walls and courts with isolated white towers rising out of them, not a single massing.
- DO NOT make the vimana over the sanctum tall or domed. Photographic evidence (File:Shri Rangji Temple Vimana - Dravidian architecture in Vrindavan.jpg) shows a small 2-3 tala white stucco vimana crowned by a horseshoe-arched apsidal gable with a kirtimukha finial, barely clearing the plain red-brown sandstone parapet in front of it. It is dwarfed by the gopurams. No onion dome, no amalaka, no shikhara, no gold finial on it.
- DO NOT paint the gopurams in bright modern South Indian polychrome. MEASURED from April 2026 photographs: the gopuram stucco is warm ivory limewash, #E8E0D1 sunlit, drifting to neutral grey #C4C6C5 where black mould streaks it. The only colour on the tower is one small painted Vaishnava emblem (chakra/namam/shankha) over the third tala and seven GOLD pot-kalashas on the crest. Polychrome DOES belong on the low mandapa roof-parapets in the inner courts, where miniature shrines and figures are painted in saturated blue, red, green and yellow, and on the painted door surrounds - but not on the towers.
- DO NOT build it out of granite, dark schist or white marble. MEASURED: it is local buff-to-pink sandstone (Bharatpur/Rupbas family), #B69358 to #BD9A6C in sun, #6D5031 in shade, laid as large plain ashlar panels, with white lime stucco only on the tower superstructures. Wrong stone makes it read as a transplanted Tamil temple, which is precisely what it is not.
- DO NOT omit the tank and the garden, or shrink them. Growse p.260: the outer walls 'enclose a fine tank and garden in addition to the actual temple-court'. They occupy the whole EASTERN THIRD of the compound: a square stepped tank ~45 x 48 m in the NE quadrant and a four-square char-bagh ~45 x 48 m in the SE quadrant, separated by a ~20 m processional avenue on the main axis. Both are clearly visible on satellite. If you build only the temple-court you have built two-thirds of the monument.
- DO NOT confuse the garden INSIDE the walls with the Brahmotsav garden OUTSIDE. They are two different places. Growse's footnote (p.259n): 'The Seth's Garden, where stands the Brahmotsava Pavilion, was purchased from the temple of Gopinath, and is still liable to an annual charge of Rs. 18.' That one is 631 m away by road (Growse: 690 yards), is mapped in OSM as way 99427181 'Rangaji Bhag Garden', and measures 128 x 276 m. A modeller merging them will get both wrong.
- DO NOT put the flagstaff at the entrance or in front of the gopuram. Growse: 'In front of the god is erected a pillar, or dhvaja stambha'. It stands in the INNER court facing the sanctum mandapa. And do not model it as a carved stone column: it is a slender jointed metal-clad mast with visible ring collars and guy wires, on a carved lotus plinth inside a railing.
- DO NOT make the compound square, or short. It is 1.75:1, 236 m by 135 m. Length along the east-west axis is its defining quality - it is the longest walled temple compound in Vrindavan and reads as a wall running most of a town block.
- DO NOT give the gopurams a smooth pyramidal silhouette. The profile is stepped and bristling: every tala carries a cornice of miniature shrines (kutas and salas), free-standing figures lean out from every level, and the crest is an OBLONG BARREL-VAULTED SALA with a ribbed roof and flared horn-like ends, carrying SEVEN gold pot-kalashas in a row along the ridge (counted in File:Rajagopuram of Sri Rangji Mandir, Vrindavan.jpg). Small inner gates, e.g. the Vaikunth Dwar, are 3-tala with FIVE kalashas.
- DO NOT start the sculpture at ground level. MEASURED from photographs: the lower ~1/3 of each gopuram is a plain, sharply battered ashlar sandstone base with a heavy cornice and almost no ornament, carrying a big timber-leaved gateway. The white sculptured stucco begins abruptly above that cornice. The contrast between bare stone base and encrusted white tower is the building's strongest visual signature.
- DO NOT set it in open ground, on a podium, or behind a plaza. The town presses hard against the north, south and east walls - two- and three-storey houses directly abut the enclosure, one range beside the tank painted periwinkle blue (#8999BA). Only the WEST has an open forecourt, a sandy/paved open area with parked vehicles, trees and stalls, between the gatehouse and the north-south street.
- DO NOT park the chariot in the street. The 50-ft rath lives in a shed beside the west entrance and is drawn out once a year, in Chait, for the ten-day Brahmotsav.
- DO NOT make the east gate the principal one. Growse is explicit that the west is 'the principal or western entrance'; the trust confirms the eastern gopuram is the smaller, 5-storey one.
- DO NOT claim it reproduces Srirangam. Aggregator sites all say 'modelled on the Ranganathaswamy temple at Srirangam'. Growse, the closest thing to an eyewitness source, says only: 'It is built in the Madras style, in accordance with plans supplied by their guru, the great Sanskrit scholar, Swami Rangacharya, a native of that part of India.' No source I found documents a measured copy of any Srirangam element.
- DO NOT invent a name for the designer of the west gatehouse. Growse says only 'a native artist' and does not name him. Do not attribute it to Rangacharya either - Growse credits Rangacharya with the Dravidian plans, and the north Indian gatehouse explicitly to someone else.
- DO NOT describe the founders as 'from Madras' (several sites do). Growse p.260: 'founded by Seths Gobind Das and Radha Krishan, brothers of the famous millionaire Lakhmi Chand' - the Mathura Seth family, converts from Jainism to Sri Vaishnavism (Growse p.14). Works 1845-1851, cost 45 lakh rupees.
- DO NOT pull the footprint from OSM buildings: it is not there. OSM has no building polygon for this temple at all - only an approximate 6-node amenity outline (way 99418417) and the detached garden (way 99427181). Use Growse's measured 773 x 440 ft with the satellite check.

## Uncertain — flag, do not assert

- GOPURAM HEIGHTS ARE UNKNOWN. Not one source I found - trust, tourism board, Growse, Hindi press - gives a height for any gopuram. Growse measures the 93 ft gatehouse and the 60 ft flagstaff but pointedly does not measure the gopuras. My 35 m (west) and 26 m (east) are ESTIMATES from tala arithmetic. A satellite shadow of ~18 m on the court would imply 38-49 m at the sun elevation the same image suggests, but that shadow merges into the adjacent wall's shadow and the image date is unknown, so I do not trust it. This is the weakest number in the brief.
- HOW MANY GOPURAMS. The temple trust and UP Tourism describe two (west 7-storey, east 5-storey). Vrindavan Today says eight. The satellite shows at least six or seven distinct white tower-tops inside the walls, and photographs show a 3-tala gated tower (the Vaikunth Dwar) that is a gopuram by any definition. Most likely: two principal axial gopurams plus several smaller ones on the inner prakaras. I could not resolve the exact count or positions, partly because off-nadir lean displaces the tower tops in the imagery by an unknown amount.
- DHVAJA STAMBHA HEIGHT: Growse says 60 ft above ground plus 24 ft buried; the trust and most Hindi/travel sources say 50 ft. Both cannot be right. Growse was a district officer writing on the spot within thirty years of construction, so I would follow him, but flag it.
- WHETHER GROWSE'S 93 FT PAVILION IS THE ONE STANDING TODAY. The surviving west gatehouse matches his description precisely (Mathura-style, reticulated tracery, graceful outline, gopuram rising immediately beyond it), but I found no dated photograph between 1883 and the 2020s to prove continuity, and no record of rebuilding either. Treat 28.35 m as SOURCED for the design, not verified for the present fabric.
- 'MATHURA STYLE' vs 'JAIPUR STYLE'. Growse calls the west pavilion Mathura style by a native artist; the temple trust calls both stone gates 'carved in Jaipur style'. The visual evidence (cusped arches, serpentine chhajja brackets, jharokhas, bangaldar roof) is generic North Indian/Braj-Rajput and does not settle it. Do not present either label as fact.
- GROWSE'S PHRASE 'the Madras Gopura, which rises immediately in front of it'. I read 'in front of' as the gopuram standing just beyond the gatehouse on the axis and appearing framed through its arch - which is exactly what a 2026 photograph shows. But Growse's viewpoint is not stated and the sentence could conceivably mean the gopuram stood west of the gatehouse. The satellite puts the gopuram east of it, inside, which supports my reading.
- THE TANK'S NAME. Wikimedia captions it 'Gajraj Kund'; the trust calls the water body 'yamuna swarupini pushkarini'; tourism sites just say 'Pushkarni'. These may be the same tank under different names or may distinguish the tank from a separate vessel. Unresolved.
- THE BLUE BUILDING BY THE TANK. The periwinkle-blue crenellated range behind the tank in the photograph may be the temple's own outer accommodation range or an adjoining town house. The satellite cannot resolve wall colour. Do not commit to blue on the temple's own fabric.
- THE TURQUOISE-TILED POOL, ~20 x 10 m, on the axis avenue between tank and garden, is clearly visible in the imagery but I found no source describing it and no photograph of it. Function UNKNOWN; it may be modern.
- SRIRANGAM AS MODEL. Universally repeated online, nowhere sourced. Growse attributes the plans to Swami Rangacharya without mentioning Srirangam. The dedication (Ranganatha, and Goda/Andal) and the concentric-prakara scheme obviously come from the Tamil Sri Vaishnava tradition, but 'modelled on Srirangam' is a travel-site claim, not a documented one.
- EXACT PRAKARA DIMENSIONS. My four inner ring measurements (136x104, 100x72, 68x44, 52x26 m) are read off a 20 m grid overlaid on satellite imagery to about +/-3 m, and the ring edges are partly obscured by roofs and awnings. The outer wall figure, by contrast, is corroborated three ways and is solid.
- STONE PROVENANCE. Buff-pink sandstone is obvious from the photographs; attributing it to the Bharatpur/Rupbas quarries is my inference from region and colour, not a sourced fact.
- PROTECTION STATUS. I did not establish whether the temple is on any ASI or state protected-monument list. As an 1851 living temple it very probably is not, but I am not asserting that.

## Independent check

Confidence: HIGH on everything re-derived from primary files, MEDIUM on the colour and satellite material. I did not take the survey's readings on trust: I downloaded Growse's 1883 full text from the Internet Archive and read pp.14, 132, 254, 260-261 and 266 directly (every quotation checks out verbatim); I pulled way/99418417 and way/99427181 from the OSM API and recomputed sides, bearings, areas and centroids myself (which is how I found the 12 m centroid error); I queried Overpass for named roads; I fetched the trust's, UP Tourism's, TourMyIndia's and Vrindavan Today's pages; and I downloaded the eleven cited Commons photographs, cropped them and counted the kalashas (7 on the rajagopuram, 5 on the Vaikunth Dwar), the talas (7), the gatehouse arches (3+5+3=11, and 5 in the crown), and re-sampled the colours. The "what crowns it" question - the flagged risk - I consider settled in the survey's favour by my own crop of the crest, with one exception: the survey's "shallow ribbed dome" on the west gatehouse is a misreading of a single bangaldar vault. Where I am LESS confident: I could not verify any ESRI satellite measurement (the five prakara rings, the 31 m westward offset of the nest, the tank/garden quadrant split, the west gatehouse footprint, the tower-top count), so I have defaulted those to corrections or contamination notes as instructed rather than endorsing them; and the colour work is limited by the fact that these are uncalibrated phone JPEGs in two different lighting conditions, so my re-samples establish the hue family but cannot adjudicate exact hexes. The Pushkar contamination finding I regard as high-confidence and the single most actionable item here.

### Contamination risks

- RANGJI TEMPLE, PUSHKAR (Ajmer district, Rajasthan) IS THE MOST DANGEROUS CONFUSION AND THE SURVEY NEVER MENTIONS IT. It is also called Rangji / Rangnath, also dedicated to Ranganatha, also the FIRST temple of the Ramanuja Sampradaya in its region, also 19th century, also founded by a SETH (Seth Puran Mal Ganeriwal, 1823 or 1844 depending on source), also a Dravidian-plus-Rajput/Mughal hybrid, and also has a SEVEN-TIERED GOPURAM. Crucially, Pushkar's gopuram carries a widely published height of 125 FEET (38.1 m) - and Vrindavan's has none at all. That number sits squarely inside the 38-49 m shadow-derived range the survey computed and then rightly distrusted, which makes it exactly the figure most likely to leak in and 'solve' the survey's self-declared weakest number. There is also a separate 'Old Rangji Temple' in Pushkar, so travel content about 'Rangji Temple' is routinely ambiguous about which town it means. Rule: any height that ever surfaces for 'the Rangji gopuram' must have its town verified before use.
- TRUST BOARD 1873 vs GROWSE'S 1868 COMMITTEE - A CONFLICT THE SURVEY REPEATS WITHOUT FLAGGING. The trust says the Trust Board was formed in 1873. Growse p.261 says 'In 1868, the whole estate was transferred by the Swami - the deed of transfer bearing a stamp of Rs. 2,000 - to a committee of management, who on his death were bound to appoint a successor.' Growse was writing within a few years of the event and names the stamp duty; the trust's 1873 may be a later re-constitution. Do not present either as settled.
- THE GURU'S NAME DIVERGES BETWEEN SOURCES AND RISKS BECOMING TWO PEOPLE. Growse: 'Swami Rangacharya' (with a footnote listing his Sanskrit works and giving his death as 26 March 1874). The trust and several travel sites: 'Sri Rangadeshik Swamiji' / 'Sri Rangadesik Swamiji' - and some of those recast him as the INITIATOR of the building rather than the supplier of plans ('construction was initiated in 1845 by Sri Rangadesik Swamiji'). Do not let the variant spawn a second figure, and do not let it shift founder credit away from the Seths.
- 'OVER 15 ACRES' IS IN CIRCULATION FOR THIS COMPOUND AND IS ROUGHLY DOUBLE. 773 x 440 ft is 7.8 acres / 3.16 ha. Several aggregators state 'over 15 acres' alongside a corrupted 'outer wall measuring 770 feet by 440 feet'. The corruption of Growse's own number in the same sentence as the area error is a warning that the whole aggregator chain around this temple is unreliable, including for the 'eight gopurams' and 'six storied' claims.
- 'EIGHT GOPURAMS' AND 'SIX STORIED' BOTH CIRCULATE WIDELY. Vrindavan Today: 'eight stately yet delicate south-Indian style gopurams'. Multiple aggregators: 'eight gopuras built in Dravidian style... one of which is six storied and totally covered with beautiful carvings'. My photographic count settles SEVEN talas on the rajagopuram, so 'six storied' is refuted; the count of eight remains unresolved and must not be built as eight. The trust and UP Tourism describe only two principal gopurams.
- GROWSE'S WORD 'GOPURA' AND THE 1949 CAPTION'S WORD 'DOME' ARE BOTH LOOSE - AND THIS PROJECT HAS BEEN BURNED BY EXACTLY THIS. The Government of India Photo Division titles a photograph of an unambiguously sala-crowned Dravidian tower 'The dome of the temple of Ranga at Brindaban, Mathura', and adds 'Build recently' about an 1851 building. A period caption saying 'dome' is not evidence of a dome - the same trap as Growse's 'the central dome is perfect' at Govind Dev, which put an onion dome on the outside of a building that has an internal vault.
- File:Gate of Shri Rangji Mandir Vrindavan.jpg IS PROBABLY NOT THE 93-ft WEST GATEHOUSE. The survey uses it as its evidence for the west forecourt ('a wide, almost empty sandy-paved lane in front of it with plain modern sandstone-block buildings alongside'). But its arch is a tall pointed ogee, not the broad multifoil cusped arch shown in both files explicitly captioned 'Western Gate', and the structure reads as about two storeys against the west gatehouse's three-plus-crown. It is most likely a secondary gate. Do not derive the character of the west forecourt from it - and note it conflicts with the survey's satellite-derived forecourt of 'trees, parked cars and buses, stalls, and a curving access road'.
- THE CHAR-BAGH IS A SATELLITE READING DRESSED IN MUGHAL VOCABULARY. 'Formal four-square char-bagh, four beds each ~18 x 18 m, cross axial paths ~3 m wide, small square platform/pavilion at the crossing' has no textual source whatever - Growse says only 'a fine tank and garden'. Given that the same 20 m-grid technique produced the 12 m centroid error, treat the garden's internal geometry as invented until a photograph or plan turns up. The same applies to the four inner prakara rings (136x104, 100x72, 68x44, 52x26 m), which the survey itself concedes are read off a grid to +/-3 m through roofs and awnings.
- THE SHEESH MAHAL IS ABSENT FROM THE SURVEY. File:Sri Rangji Mandir Courtyard with Dhwaja Stambha carries a sign beside the flagstaff enclosure reading 'शीश महल / SHEESH MAHAL - मात्र 5/- रुपये / ONLY 5/-RUPEES - DO WATCH IT', alongside 'NO PHOTOGRAPHY'. A ticketed mirror-hall inside the inner court is a real interior the brief does not account for at all.
- 'THE ONLY LARGE ESTABLISHMENT IN ALL UPPER INDIA THAT IS OWNED BY THE FOLLOWERS OF RAMANUJA' IS AN 1883 STATEMENT, NOT A PRESENT-DAY FACT. The survey quotes it from Growse p.14 in its source list without a date qualifier. Pushkar's Rangji temple alone (also Ramanuja Sampradaya) shows how the landscape differs once you leave Growse's 'Upper India' framing.
