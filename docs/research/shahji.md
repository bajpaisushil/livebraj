# shahji — exterior survey

*Researched 2026-09-27 by one agent and independently checked by a second whose brief was to refute it. Labels are the researcher's own: MEASURED / SOURCED / DERIVED / ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN.*

*Survey and checker are paired by the workflow journal's launch key, not by completion order — see BACKLOG 2026-09-30 for why that matters.*

## What it is

Not a temple in any Indian temple sense at all: it is a single-storey Lucknow/Awadhi *palace pavilion* — a 45 m wide, flat-roofed white-and-honey marble garden-front with a colonnaded verandah, a broken Baroque pediment, and a parapet lined with life-size human statues — built 1860–68 by two Lucknow bankers and modelled, in Growse's own words, "on a design suggested by the modern secular buildings of that city."

## The silhouette

WHAT CROWNS IT: nothing religious. No shikhara, no dome, no amalaka, no kalash, no flag. The roof is FLAT. (MEASURED from 6 photographs, 2012 and 2022 and 2026.)

The roofline, from the front, in order:

1. TWO STACKED PERFORATED PARAPETS on a stepped, two-level roof (MEASURED). The verandah range in front carries a LOWER parapet: a continuous band pierced with a row of small oval/round-headed openings, with small urn/baluster finials standing on it at ~2 m intervals. Set back roughly 6–8 m and ~2.5 m higher, the main hall block carries an UPPER parapet, same pierced-oval grammar but taller openings. This double-parapet stepping is the single most characteristic thing about the silhouette and it is what makes the building read as a layered palace terrace rather than a box.

2. ~14 LIFE-SIZE HUMAN STATUES standing free on the UPPER parapet, ~7 per side of the pediment, some singly, some in close pairs/groups of two (MEASURED: 7 positions counted each side on both the 2012 and 2026 photos; count ESTIMATED 12–16). They are a colder, chalkier white than the parapet they stand on. These are Growse's "row of life-size female figures in meretricious, but at the same time most ungraceful, attitudes" (SOURCED, Growse 1883 p.263) — and they are also my scale anchor: "life-size" = 1.6 m.

3. A BROKEN BAROQUE PEDIMENT over the centre (MEASURED), ~12 m wide and ~5.5 m tall, in the warm honey stone. It is NOT triangular. Its outline is a double S-curve with scrolled acanthus edges, broken at the apex. In the tympanum is a large OVAL OCULUS (~2.6 m tall) ringed with a moulding, holding a standing figure; two smaller oval openings flank it, each also with a small figure.

4. THREE SMALL DOMED KIOSKS on the pediment (MEASURED): one on the apex, ~2.8 m tall to the finial tip, with an ogee-arched square kiosk and a small ribbed bell dome; two lower ones on the pediment's shoulders, same form, ~2.2 m.

5. At EACH END the parapet SWEEPS DOWN in a big concave-convex S-curve volute (MEASURED), terminating over a blank ashlar end bay. On the scrolled console at the head of each sweep sits a large RECUMBENT LION in honey stone (~2.5–3 m long) with a small standing female figure on a lotus pedestal beside it. These are Growse's "sprawling monsters" (SOURCED, p.263).

6. Behind, standing free on the raised marble terrace, TWO OCTAGONAL OPEN PAVILIONS (MEASURED from ESRI imagery and two photos): ~6.4 m across the roof, ~8.5 m across the plinth, centres ~39 m apart on the frontage line, one near each end. Each has three round/trefoil arches per face on slender coupled colonnettes, a LOW PYRAMIDAL (hipped) roof faced in GREY-BLUE VEINED marble — a third stone — a carved lion emblem inlaid in the gable face, and a big lotus-bud finial.

MASSING: long, low and wide. Frontage ~45 m against a main roofline of ~14–15 m — a width:height of about 3:1. The centre 13 m projects forward ~1.5 m and rises higher than the wings. Everything else is a flat horizontal slab. Nothing about the massing says "temple"; it says nawabi garden pavilion.

WHAT SURVIVES: all of it, and in use, but visibly decayed — spalled parapet, rusting ironwork, staining, patched render on the wings, an ad-hoc corrugated screen across the front steps in 2026. Model it weathered, not pristine.

## The elevation

The front faces SE (bearing ~144°, MEASURED from ESRI imagery). Read left to right across ~45 m, the elevation is FIVE PARTS, not one colonnade (MEASURED from the 2012 photo, which has the flattest perspective; scale fixed at 0.029 m/px by Growse's "life-size" statues at 1.6 m):

A. END BAY, ~6 m, 100% SOLID. Blank honey-marble ashlar. Set into it, TWO LARGE OVAL MEDALLIONS (~2.0 m tall) outlined in a BLACK MARBLE RING, one filled plain white, one filled honey. Above, the parapet sweeps down in the S-curve and carries the lion. Mirrored at the far end.

B. WING COLONNADE, ~10.2 m, SIX TWISTED COLUMNS, ~84% open. Spacing rhythm, MEASURED: coupled pair at ~1.05 m centres — bay ~2.75 m — bay ~2.75 m — bay ~2.75 m — coupled pair at ~1.05 m. So: pair, three wide bays, pair.

C. CENTRE PAVILION, ~12.9 m, EIGHT PLAIN CYLINDRICAL COLUMNS in FOUR COUPLED PAIRS making THREE BAYS, ~70% open. These columns are NOT twisted (MEASURED — see doNotBuild). Smooth round shafts with slight entasis, simple moulded capital and base, and strong vertical veining in a honey/gold marble. This bay projects forward ~1.5 m, carries a deeper plain architrave and a projecting flat cornice, and is terminated at each end by a large carved CONSOLE bracket with an acanthus scroll and a grotesque head.

D. WING COLONNADE, mirror of B — six twisted columns.

E. END BAY, mirror of A.

Opening:solid at verandah level across the whole 45 m frontage ≈ 58% open / 42% solid (DERIVED from the above).

THE TWISTED COLUMNS (MEASURED from four photographs, incl. numerical silhouette tracking):
- Total on the front: TWELVE, six per wing. This is where the town's "twelve" comes from and it is correct — but only for the wings.
- Overall height base-of-plinth to top-of-capital ≈ 5.5 m; SHAFT alone ≈ 4.6 m. The universally repeated "15 feet" (4.57 m) matches the SHAFT, not the whole column — my photogrammetry and the popular claim reconcile exactly.
- Nominal shaft diameter ≈ 0.40–0.45 m. Slenderness ≈ 10–11.5 diameters. Growse's complaint that they are "rather too attenuated" is quantitatively correct: a Corinthian column is normally 10 diameters, a Solomonic twist usually stubbier.
- GEOMETRY, the part that matters: MEASURED silhouette bulge-to-waist ratio = 2.1–2.3. That ratio is the signature of a TWO-STRAND ROPE, not a barley twist. The shaft is two round strands (one noticeably fatter than the other) wound around each other, and the resulting axis itself snakes side to side — measured centreline swing 0.32–0.43 × the shaft width. This is why the town calls them TEDHE (crooked), not "twisted".
- Helical pitch ≈ 2.0 × the shaft width ≈ 0.8–0.9 m, giving ~5–6 full turns over the 4.6 m shaft (MEASURED; ±1 turn).
- CAPITALS: full Corinthian/Composite, acanthus and volutes, ~1.5 × shaft dia wide.
- BASES: large carved scrolled-acanthus blocks, ~1.6 × shaft dia wide, ~0.5 m tall, on square plinth blocks.
- Each shaft is a SINGLE PIECE of marble (SOURCED, Growse 1883 p.262: "each shaft being of a single piece").

ARCH AND OPENING GRAMMAR — all ROUND-HEADED, never cusped (MEASURED):
- Behind the centre pavilion: three tall ROUND-ARCHED doorways with semicircular RADIATING FANLIGHTS over timber doors.
- Along the verandah back wall: an alternating rhythm of (i) tall openings under small TRIANGULAR PEDIMENTS whose tympana carry black-inlaid scrollwork around a central oval, closed with dark timber LOUVRED/slatted double doors, and (ii) round-arched niches with carved fan/shell heads. Roughly 1 opening per 2.75 m bay.
- Between them, framed inlaid-marble pictures and panelled marble dados outlined in black inlay.
- At each end of the verandah, tall ornate WROUGHT-IRON GATE SCREENS between the columns — mid-Victorian cast/wrought ironwork, a strong Lucknow-colonial note.
- Front of the verandah: a continuous low PIERCED MARBLE JALI BALUSTRADE (~0.9 m) running the full colonnade width at floor level.

BELOW: a broad flight of steps running essentially the full width of the facade, rising ~3–3.5 m from the courtyard to the verandah floor (ESTIMATED, ±1 m; roughly 12–18 risers).

VERANDAH FLOOR (visible from outside, and remarkable): white marble slabs framed by DOUBLE BLACK-MARBLE INLAY BANDS, a diagonal BLACK-AND-WHITE CHEQUERBOARD border, and — set flush into the pavement — LIFE-SIZE PIETRA DURA PORTRAIT PANELS of courtly figures in white, grey, black and pink marble, one signed in Devanagari "साह गोविंद लाल" (Sah Govind Lal).

THE COURTYARD GATEWAY is a separate composition entirely (MEASURED): a tall Mughal-idiom PISHTAQ in PINK/BUFF SANDSTONE — a multi-cusped ogee arch recessed within a rectangular frame, a band of pendant cusps, engaged fluted pilasters, and a crown of large carved LOTUS PETALS radiating around the arch head, with a square ogee-arched domed kiosk above and smaller domed kiosks flanking. Different material, different century's idiom, different everything from the temple it fronts. Growse: "The principal entrance to the courtyard is, in a grandiose way, decidedly effective" (SOURCED, p.262).

## Material and colour

Marble throughout for the temple; sandstone for the gateway. Growse says only "constructed of the most costly materials" and "spiral marble pillars" (SOURCED, p.262) — he never names a quarry, and neither does any source I can verify.

There are at least FOUR distinct stones, which is the single most important colour fact and is MEASURED, not inferred:

1. WARM HONEY / CREAM VEINED MARBLE — the wall ashlar, the entablature, the pediment, the lions, the consoles, and the CENTRE PAVILION'S COLUMNS.
   MEASURED (white-balanced against the white marble paving as neutral): #AFA289, H 39.5°, S 21.7%, V 68.6% (skylight); #B6A88F, H 38.5°, S 21.4%, V 71.4%; kiosk arcade #CBB48D, H 37.7°, S 30.5%, V 79.6%; lion #BBAA8B, H 38.8°, S 25.7%, V 73.3%.
   In full sun this reads about #E6D6B4 — H 40°, S 22%, V 90% (DERIVED by lifting V to direct-sun exposure).

2. NEAR-WHITE MARBLE — the TWELVE TWISTED SHAFTS, the parapets, the statues, the oval infill panels, the terrace paving.
   MEASURED: oval infill #ACA9A4, H 37.5°, S 4.7%, V 67.5%; terrace paving neutral by construction, S ≈ 0–3%.
   In full sun ≈ #F0EDE6 — H 38°, S 4%, V 94% (DERIVED).
   The distinction that matters for rendering: SAME HUE BAND (35–41°), but S 22% vs S 4%. The two marbles differ almost entirely in SATURATION, not hue. Photo 09 shows it plainly — the centre pavilion reads GOLD against CHALK-WHITE wings from across the courtyard.

3. GREY-BLUE VEINED MARBLE — only on the two octagonal terrace pavilions' pyramidal roofs and their corner framing. Distinctly cooler and darker than everything else (MEASURED qualitatively; my numeric patches on it were contaminated by sky, so hex is UNRELIABLE — treat as a cool grey around #B9BCC2, S ~5%, V ~74%, ESTIMATED).

4. BLACK MARBLE INLAY — the oval medallion rings, the floor bands, the chequerboard, the pedimented-niche scrollwork, the wall panel outlines. MEASURED #161717, V 9%. Used as LINES, ~30–60 mm wide, not as masses.

Plus, on the GATEWAY only: PINK/BUFF SANDSTONE. MEASURED: sunlit face #F8F7F0 (near-blown), shaded spandrel #715A4A H 24.6° S 34.5% V 44.3%, lotus petals #C2B9B6 H 15–25°. Read it as a warm pink-buff, H 20–28°, S 20–35% — visibly PINKER and lower-hue than the temple's yellow-honey marble.

Ancillary measured colours: timber louvred doors #615A4A, H 41.7°, S 23.7% (weathered dark brown); one green-painted door; courtyard ground a reddish brick-paved #B08A6E-ish (ESTIMATED from photos 08/09).

ON "WHITE ITALIAN MARBLE": repeated on nearly every aggregator page and in the Hindi press. It is NOT in Growse, NOT on the UP Tourism plaque in any part I could read with confidence, and NOT on the temple trust's own site. Carrara is a cold white-grey; the bulk of what I measured is a warm yellow-veined stone at S 22–30%. Treat "Italian" as UNVERIFIED. The honest build is: two marbles, one warm and one white, quarry unknown.

## The setting

A WALLED COMPOUND ENTERED FROM THE BAZAAR, with a large open forecourt in front of the temple. The temple is not on the lane; it is set back behind its own court.

- FORECOURT: ~38 × 38 m of open ground SE of the temple (MEASURED from ESRI World Imagery, z19, 0.265 m/px). Surface is bare reddish earth in 2012, reddish brick/stone paving by 2022. About 25 m out from the foot of the steps sits an OVAL GARDEN BED / FOUNTAIN BASIN, ~9 × 5 m, with a small pillar fountain beside it (MEASURED, and visible in the 2012 photo). By 2022 there is also a long rectangular sunken tank/enclosure in front of the steps.
- The forecourt is LINED WITH BAZAAR STALLS on both sides — cloth and souvenir stalls under awnings on one side, low shops and a masonry wall on the other. The town comes right up to the compound; there is no green setting, no garden, no trees except one bare tree in the middle of the court in the 2012 photo.
- A carved pink-sandstone UP TOURISM PLAQUE stands on a low wall at the left of the court, near a shed/rickshaw stand (MEASURED — this is the plaque I read the dates off).
- APPROACH: down a narrow bazaar LANE from the SE, packed with shops on both sides, through the big pink-sandstone cusped-arch GATEWAY, then out into the court where the facade appears all at once. Photo 08 shows exactly this: from the lane you see only the pediment and the upper parapet over the shop roofs.
- VISIBILITY FROM A DISTANCE: essentially NONE. The main roofline is ~14–15 m and the town's own buildings are 8–12 m. Nothing about it reads on the skyline — no tower, no dome, no gilding. It is a building you discover, not one you navigate by. Do not give it a skyline presence.
- To one side of the temple (left in photo 09) there is a LOWER, plainer white range with plain round columns and a pierced parapet — an ancillary wing at a lower level, of different and probably later character.
- LOCATION: the project's stored position [27.5848, 77.697] is GOOD — it falls within 6 m of the white-marble mass's centroid as I measure it (27.58481, 77.69694). Near Nidhivan, in the dense temple quarter.
- ORIENTATION: long axis runs 054°–234° (NE–SW); the facade faces bearing ~144° (SE) onto the court. The project's stored rot = π is about 36° off (see uncertain).
- The Basanti Kamra is described in every source as a large hall at the EAST END OF THE COURTYARD — i.e. a separate block within the compound, not a room in the main temple range.

## What NOT to build

- MAKING ALL THE FACADE COLUMNS TWISTED. This is the big one. The twelve twisted columns are on the two WINGS ONLY, six each. The 13 m CENTRE PAVILION has EIGHT PLAIN CYLINDRICAL COLUMNS in four coupled pairs — smooth, slightly entasised, honey-veined, with plain moulded caps and bases. MEASURED on the 2026 facade photo and confirmed on the 2012 photo. The contrast between the plain gold centre and the white twisted wings IS the design. Your current 'temple-colonnade' builder makes every column twisted and has no centre pavilion at all.
- MODELLING THE TWIST AS A BARLEY TWIST OR A SCREW. Your current code stacks square boxes rotated k*0.26 rad — that gives a square-section screw. MEASURED silhouette bulge:waist = 2.1–2.3, which can only come from TWO round strands wound around each other (a 4-start barley twist gives ~1.1; a bent cylinder gives ~1.0). Build it as a two-strand rope, strands unequal (roughly 0.7 : 0.3 of the pair width), pitch ~2.0 x shaft diameter, ~5–6 turns over a 4.6 m shaft, and let the axis itself snake — measured centreline swing 0.32–0.43 x shaft width. The town's word is TEDHE, crooked, not 'twisted'.
- PUTTING THE TWELVE COLUMNS IN TWO ROWS OF SIX. Your current builder does exactly this ('for (let row = 0; row < 2; row++)' with six per row, at lz = d*0.33 + row*2.6). The verandah is ONE ROW DEEP. The twelve are a single line across the front: six left of the centre pavilion, six right. Behind them is the wall, not a second row.
- A SHIKHARA, DOME, AMALAKA, KALASH, FLAG OR ANY TEMPLE TOWER. There is none and never was. The roof is FLAT. If a modeller's hand reaches for a crowning element, it should reach for parapet statues and small kiosks instead.
- A TRIANGULAR (GREEK) PEDIMENT, or a stepped tapering stack of boxes standing in for one — which is what your current code builds. The pediment is a BROKEN BAROQUE one: a double S-curve outline with scrolled acanthus edges, broken at the apex, with a big OVAL OCULUS in the tympanum containing a standing figure and two smaller ovals flanking. Growse calls it 'uncouth' precisely because it is not classically correct.
- LEAVING THE ROOFLINE EMPTY. Your current builder ends at 'flat terraced roof, no tower at all' — correct in spirit, then it stops. The roofline carries ~14 LIFE-SIZE HUMAN STATUES standing on the upper parapet (~7 a side), THREE domed kiosks on the pediment, and at each end a RECUMBENT LION on a scrolled console above a swept S-curve. Growse's 'sprawling monsters' and 'row of life-size female figures' are not decoration, they are the profile.
- ONE PARAPET. There are TWO, stacked, on a stepped two-level roof: a lower pierced-oval parapet with urn finials over the verandah range, and a set-back, higher pierced-oval parapet carrying the statues over the hall block.
- MAKING IT WHITE MARBLE. Two marbles minimum, and they differ in SATURATION at the same hue: warm honey (H 39, S 22%) for the wall ashlar, entablature, pediment, lions and centre-pavilion columns; near-white (H 38, S 4%) for the twisted shafts, parapets, statues and paving. Plus a grey-blue veined marble on the two terrace pavilion roofs and black marble INLAY LINES throughout. From the courtyard the centre reads gold against chalk-white wings.
- CUSPED / MUGHAL ARCHES ON THE TEMPLE. Every opening on the temple is ROUND-HEADED, mostly with radiating semicircular FANLIGHTS, plus small TRIANGULAR PEDIMENTS over the verandah doorways. The cusped ogee arch belongs to the GATEWAY only. Your current builder calls cuspedArch() for the entrance — wrong building element.
- MAKING THE GATEWAY MATCH THE TEMPLE. The courtyard gateway is PINK/BUFF SANDSTONE, a tall Mughal-revival pishtaq with a multi-cusped arch, pendant cusps, and a crown of big carved LOTUS PETALS, with a domed kiosk above. It is a different material, a different colour family (H 20–28 vs H 38–40) and a different architectural idiom from the marble pavilion behind it.
- EXPRESSING THE BASANTI KAMRA ON THE EXTERIOR. It is an interior durbar hall described as being at the EAST END OF THE COURTYARD — a separate block, not a room in the main range — and it opens only twice a year. Nothing about it shows outside. Do not give the main facade a 'Basanti Kamra wing'.
- DATING IT 1876. The UP Tourism plaque standing in the courtyard says construction began in 1860, and the temple trust's own site says it took eight years, finishing ~1868, with the waqf registered in 1866; Hindi press in 2023 called it ~155 years old (= 1868). 1876 is an aggregator figure with nothing behind it. One Hindi outlet says 1863. Use 1860–1868.
- FORGETTING THE SOLID END BAYS. The colonnade does NOT run the full frontage. The outer ~6 m at each end is BLANK honey ashlar carrying two big OVAL MEDALLIONS ringed in black marble, under the swept parapet and the lion. Roughly 12 m of the 45 m frontage is solid wall.
- A PLAIN MARBLE FLOOR. The verandah pavement carries double black-marble inlay bands, a diagonal black-and-white chequerboard, and LIFE-SIZE PIETRA DURA PORTRAIT PANELS inlaid flush into the floor, one signed 'साह गोविंद लाल'. If the player can walk the verandah, this is what is underfoot.
- PUTTING IT ON THE LANE, OR GIVING IT SKYLINE PRESENCE. It sits behind a ~38 x 38 m walled forecourt with an oval fountain basin, reached down a bazaar lane through the gateway. At ~14–15 m to the main roofline it is invisible from any distance; you meet it only when you enter the court.
- OMITTING THE TWO OCTAGONAL TERRACE PAVILIONS. ~6.4 m across, ~39 m apart, one near each end of the raised terrace, with grey-blue veined marble PYRAMIDAL (not domed) roofs and lotus-bud finials. They are clearly visible in satellite imagery and in two of the photographs, and they break up the terrace.
- TREATING GROWSE AS AN ADMIRER. He loathed it: 'all is rendered of no avail by the abominable taste of the design... resembles nothing so much as a disreputable London casino: a severe, though doubtless unintended, satire, on the part of the architect, on the character of the divinity to whom it is consecrated.' He praises only the pillars and the gateway. Do not let a caption or a guidebook line put his authority behind the building as a whole.
- SEARCHING GROWSE FOR 'SHAHJI'. He never uses the name. He calls it THE TEMPLE OF RADHA RAMAN — which in the same book also means Gopal Bhatt's 16th-century Gaudiya temple AND a third Radha Raman built by Rup Ram of Barsana. Three different buildings, one name. The deity here is 'Chhote Radha Raman' precisely to keep them apart. Do not merge any data between them.

## Uncertain — flag, do not assert

- THE 'TWO WHITE MARBLES' GROWSE SUPPOSEDLY IMPLIES — HE DOES NOT. I searched all three digitised copies of the 3rd edition: the word 'marble' occurs seven times in the whole book, and the only one referring to this temple is 'a colonnade of spiral marble pillars, each shaft being of a single piece'. There is no two-marble statement, no mention of a balustrade, no mention of a centre pavilion, anywhere in Growse's text on this building. If that belief is already in the project's notes it should be pulled. THE UNDERLYING PHENOMENON IS REAL, though, and I measured it — the distinction is between the CENTRE PAVILION's columns and ashlar (warm honey, S 22%) and the TWISTED SHAFTS, parapets and statues (near-white, S 4%). Build the two-stone reading; just don't attribute it to Growse.
- THE COLUMN COUNT OF TWELVE IS FOR THE FRONT ELEVATION ONLY. I counted six per wing on two independent photographs, and twelve is what every source says. But the oblique photographs show the verandah running back along at least one return, and I could not establish whether twisted columns continue round the sides or whether the courtyard's other ranges have more. Twelve on the front is safe; twelve in the whole complex is not established.
- THE EXACT NUMBER OF TURNS IN THE HELIX. My numerical tracking gave anything from 3 to 9 periods depending on which column and which photograph, because the shafts overlap and the backgrounds vary. The robust numbers are the bulge:waist ratio (2.1–2.3, from clean sky-silhouetted columns) and the pitch (~2.0 x shaft width). 5–6 turns is derived from those, ±1 turn.
- ALL HEIGHTS. Every vertical figure rests on Growse's phrase 'life-size female figures' being literally true and those statues being 1.6 m. Cross-checking against people on the steps gave 1.69–1.71 m for adult men, which is reassuring, but the camera positions differ in depth from the facade and the steps foreshorten badly head-on. Treat heights as +/-15%, and the plinth height (3.0–3.5 m) as the weakest of them — my two methods gave 3.2 m and 4.5 m.
- THE COMPLETION DATE ON THE UP TOURISM PLAQUE IS PARTLY ILLEGIBLE. '1860 में प्रारम्भ होकर सन 18__ में पूरा हुआ' — the start year 1860 is crisp, the completion digits are eroded. I read it as 1868 and the temple trust's own 'eight long years' from 1860 and the 2023 press's '155 years old' both land on 1868, so the convergence is good. But I did not read those two digits directly.
- THE MARBLE SOURCE. 'White Italian marble' is everywhere online and nowhere in a source I can verify. The plaque has a phrase in that position I could not resolve. Growse says only 'the most costly materials'. And what I measured is mostly a warm yellow-veined stone, which Carrara is not. Quarry: UNKNOWN.
- PROTECTION STATUS. I could not find the Shahji temple on any ASI centrally-protected or UP state-protected list. Given an 1868 date that is unsurprising — it is almost certainly unprotected and privately managed by the waqf/trust — but I am recording this as a negative search result, not a confirmed fact.
- THE BUILDING'S ROTATION IN THE PROJECT. Its stored position [27.5848, 77.697] is excellent — within 6 m of the centroid I measure. But the stored rot is pi (180 deg), while I measure the facade facing bearing ~144 deg and the long axis running 054–234. That is about 36 deg out. I have not read enough of your rot convention to be certain that 'pi' means 'faces south' in your frame, so please check before rotating it — but the satellite evidence for a SE-facing facade on a NE–SW axis is unambiguous.

The ~18–20 m depth of the roofed range is a reading of a tone boundary, not a wall line. I have no plan, no section, and Growse gives dimensions for the neighbouring Radha Indra Kishor and Radha Gopal temples but NONE for this one — and the book has no plate of it, which for a building he wrote three paragraphs about is itself telling.
- THE NUMBER OF ROOFTOP STATUES. I counted 7 positions each side of the pediment on two photographs, but several are close pairs and at that resolution a pair can read as one. 12–16 is the honest range; 14 is my best estimate.
- WHETHER THE LOWER WHITE RANGE TO ONE SIDE (photo 09) IS PART OF THE ORIGINAL COMPOSITION. It has plain round columns and a plainer parapet and sits at a lower level. It could be the Basanti Kamra block, an ancillary wing, or a 20th-century addition. I could not tell.

## Independent check

Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH on the architecture and the documentary record; LOW on everything dimensional. High because I did not take the survey's word for anything textual: I grepped all three digitised copies of Growse held in the scratchpad and read the passage on screen (page break, running head, footnote, plate list, index, the seven occurrences of "marble", the zero occurrences of "Shahji"), and I re-read all eleven photographs myself rather than trusting the survey's readings of them, re-cropping and re-sampling where a claim was load-bearing. That is how the column-geometry, square-pavilion and fish findings came out. Independent corroboration came from Nominatim (zero OSM results), shahjimandir.org, Braj Ras and Rana Safvi. Low on dimensions because nothing in the available evidence fixes absolute scale: there is no plan, no section, no OSM footprint, no published measurement, and the only anchor is an 1883 rhetorical phrase. My own attempt at an independent anchor from a figure in the 2022 photograph gave a column less than half the survey's height and I could not exclude the depth ambiguity that would explain it away — so I am confident the numbers are unconstrained, and not confident about what the right numbers are. Also unresolved: whether the plaque names Italy (the glyphs are consistent with it but the stone is worn), and whether the white on the parapets is limewash or stone (the photographic pattern says coating, but only a site inspection settles it).

### Corrections to the survey above

- COLUMN GEOMETRY — the survey's strongest instruction is wrong. It says the silhouette bulge:waist ratio is 2.1–2.3, which "can only come from TWO round strands wound around each other," strands unequal at roughly 0.7:0.3. I re-measured the silhouettes on the two clearest close-ups (Shahji_Temple_Vrindavan_Spiral_Columns_and_Floor_Mosaic.jpg, two shafts filling the frame; Shahji_Temple_Vrindavan_Spiral_Columns_Verandah.jpg; and a full-shaft crop from the lion photo). Shaft width is constant within about ±10% down the whole shaft (e.g. 110–115 px at four heights on the nearest Floor_Mosaic column, 115–122 px on the nearest Verandah column). Bulge:waist is therefore ≈1.0–1.15, not 2.1–2.3. By the survey's OWN stated decision rule ("a bent cylinder gives ~1.0") its measurement points to the opposite conclusion from the one it draws. The defensible model is a MONOLITHIC shaft of constant circular section swept along a HELICAL AXIS — a serpentine/Solomonic column — several of which also carry a shallow single-start spiral groove or seam; not two unequal strands. Rana Safvi independently calls them "serpentine columns inspired by the Vatican." The survey's centreline-swing figure (0.32–0.43 × shaft width) and ~5–7 waves per shaft ARE supported; only the rope morphology and the 2.1–2.3 ratio must be dropped.
- TERRACE PAVILIONS ARE SQUARE, NOT OCTAGONAL. The survey describes "TWO OCTAGONAL OPEN PAVILIONS ... with three round/trefoil arches per face." In Shahji_Temple_Vrindavan_Twisted_Pillars_and_Lion_Sculpture.jpg the pavilion reads as a four-sided block: one triangular roof face with a hip running down to each corner, three arches on the visible face, and square corner piers faced in grey-blue marble. A 3×3-bay square pavilion under a four-sided pyramidal roof. Nothing in the imagery shows chamfered eighth faces. (The pyramidal grey-blue roof and lotus-bud finial ARE confirmed.)
- THE PAVILION ROOF EMBLEM IS NOT (only) A LION — it is the AWADH FISH. The survey says each pavilion has "a carved lion emblem inlaid in the gable face." Zooming the pavilion roof in Shahji_Temple_Vrindavan_Spiral_Columns_and_Floor_Mosaic.jpg shows an unmistakable honey-marble FISH inlaid on the grey-blue pyramidal roof — the Awadh mahi-maratib, corroborated by Rana Safvi ("the Awadh fish emblem"). A rampant lion appears on a different face/pavilion. Both must be modelled; the fish is the single most legible Lucknow signature on the building and the survey lost it.
- "TWO LUCKNOW BANKERS" IS UNSUPPORTED. Growse names only "Sáh Kundan Lal, of Lakhnau" and gives no occupation (verified in all three digitised copies). The UP Tourism plaque and shahjimandir.org name both brothers but no trade. The aggregator chain (Holidify, TemplePurohit, Trawell, Srimandir) says "wealthy jewellers." Rana Safvi says Kundan Lal was "a treasurer in the court of Awadh's last Nawab, Nawab Wajid Ali Shah." No source I can find says bankers. Say "Lucknow merchant-financiers" only if you must, or better: state the plaque's bare names.
- "A BUILDING HE WROTE THREE PARAGRAPHS ABOUT" — Growse wrote ONE paragraph, plus a footnote that is about a DIFFERENT temple (Lala Braj Kishor's 1873 sakhi-pillar Radha Gopal). Verified: the passage runs unbroken from "If the effect of the Seths' lavish endowment..." to "...wasted on its construction." The survey uses the "three paragraphs" inflation to make the absence of a plate look significant. The absence of a plate is real; the rhetorical weight is not.
- THE "15 FEET = SHAFT ONLY" RECONCILIATION IS INVENTED. The survey claims its photogrammetry (shaft 4.6 m) and the popular "15 feet" "reconcile exactly" because 15 ft is the shaft alone. No source says shaft. Times Now Navbharat quotes "15 फीट ऊंचे टेढ़े खंभे" — 15-foot-tall crooked PILLARS. Another widely copied page gives "13 feet in height and 35 inch in diameter." And the 4.6 m figure is itself scaled off the assumed statue height, so the reconciliation is circular.
- EVERY HEIGHT IS UNCONSTRAINED, NOT ±15%. The whole vertical chain (verandah floor, column, entablature, both parapets, pediment apex, kiosk finial at ~+23 m, and the 45 m frontage that is cross-checked against the same photo scale) rests on Growse's rhetorical "life-size female figures" = 1.6 m. In the photographs several rooftop figures are seated, kneeling or in close pairs — the anchor is the wrong height for an unknown fraction of them. The survey's own two plinth methods gave 3.2 m and 4.5 m (40% apart) yet its elevation prints "+3.0 to +3.5 m" with the 4.5 m result suppressed. My own attempt at an independent anchor (standing adult against the facade in Shahji_temple_Vrindavan_09.jpg) gives a column of ~2.1–2.3 m, less than half the survey's 5.5 m — unreliable because of the depth ambiguity, which is precisely the point. Do not carry any metre figure into the model as fact.
- "FROM THE LANE YOU SEE ONLY THE PEDIMENT AND THE UPPER PARAPET OVER THE SHOP ROOFS. Photo 08 shows exactly this." It does not. Shahji_temple_Vrindavan_08.jpg shows the ENTIRE facade from the lane — pediment, both parapets, statues, the full colonnade, the steps and the open court. The approach sequence in the survey is a misdescription of its own cited evidence.
- "ONE SIGNED IN DEVANAGARI 'साह गोविंद लाल'" — the inscription is real (I read it at the lower-left of Shahji_Temple_Vrindavan_Marble_Verandah_and_Inlaid_Portraits.jpg), but "signed" is an inference. A Devanagari name set beside a life-size portrait panel most naturally identifies the SITTER. No source attributes the pietra dura to a Sah Govind Lal. Record it as an inscribed name, not an artist's signature.
- "THE DEITY HERE IS 'CHHOTE RADHA RAMAN' PRECISELY TO KEEP THEM APART." The sources give a different reason. Braj Ras: "The deities due to their small size are also called as chhote Radha Raman." The disambiguation story is the survey's own rationalisation presented as fact.
- THE "NEAR-WHITE MARBLE" ON THE PARAPETS AND STATUES IS PROBABLY LIMEWASH, NOT A SECOND STONE. The two-tone reading is real (I re-sampled it, see confirmed), but the survey attributes the white to a distinct MARBLE. Sampling the upper parapet in the same photograph where its coating has worn gives H 41.9°, S 31.2% — honey, not white — and the pierced oval openings show honey-coloured reveals behind white faces, with white flaking on the parapet faces. That is a coating over the honey stone. Near-white MARBLE is safe only for the twisted shafts, the terrace paving and the oval infill panels (S 0.6–4.1% re-measured). This changes how the material and its weathering should be authored.

### Contamination risks

- GROWSE p.263 IS A CONFLATION MINEFIELD AND THE SURVEY DOES NOT WARN ABOUT IT. The running head printed ON p.263 — the page carrying the "casino" sentence about THIS building — is "THE TEMPLE OF RADHA GOPAL." And the book's plate "Temple of Radhá Gopál (ground-plan)" is bound facing p.263. Any agent that pulls "the plan near p.263" will bring back the wrong temple's ground plan and believe it is Shahji's. Growse also describes TWO different Radha Gopals in the same passage — the Maharaja of Gwalior's (Italian-church interior, opened 1860, four lakh) and Lala Braj Kishor of Shahjahanpur's 1873 sakhi-pillar temple in the footnote — on top of the three Radha Ramans. That is five buildings under three names across two pages.
- "WHITE ITALIAN MARBLE" — the survey's stronger sentence says it is "NOT on the UP Tourism plaque in any part I could read with confidence," while its own uncertain list admits "The plaque has a phrase in that position I could not resolve." I high-contrast-zoomed that phrase: the word immediately after "इस मंदिर के", exactly where a marble origin belongs, begins इ-ट and is consistent with इटली / इटालियन. Do not record "the plaque does not say Italian" as a finding. The quarry remains unknown — but the negative is not established.
- THE BASANTI KAMRA MAY HAVE A DOME, AND THE SURVEY FORBIDS LOOKING. shahjimandir.org: "The dome shaped artistically painted ceiling supports fifteen huge Belgium glass chandeliers." The survey instructs "Nothing about it shows outside. Do not give the main facade a 'Basanti Kamra wing'." That is an UNTESTED negative about a separate block at the east end of the courtyard that no photograph in the set shows from outside. This is exactly the internal-vault-versus-external-casing question the project has already got wrong — and got wrong in the opposite direction on Govind Dev an hour ago. Do not close it.
- THE HEADLINE SAYS "NO DOME" AND doNotBuild #4 SAYS "A SHIKHARA, DOME ... THERE IS NONE AND NEVER WAS", but item 4 of the same silhouette lists THREE domed kiosks with finials, and the gateway carries three more. A modeller obeying the doNotBuild literally will delete the kiosk domes and flatten the one silhouette element the building actually has. Given this project's history on crowning elements, the instruction needs rewording, not just a footnote.
- THE 1.6 m STATUE ANCHOR IS A HOSTILE 1883 CRITIC'S RHETORICAL PHRASE BEING USED AS A METROLOGICAL DATUM, and it propagates into every vertical dimension AND into the horizontal ones (the elevation's 0.029 m/px is fixed by the same statues, and the ~45 m frontage is then "confirmed" against a satellite figure derived separately). If the anchor is wrong, both the height and the frontage move together, and the apparent agreement between the two methods is not independent evidence.
- THE AGGREGATOR COPY-CHAIN. "1876", "twelve spiral columns, 15 feet each", "white Italian marble" and "wealthy jewellers" recur verbatim across Holidify, TemplePurohit, Trawell, Srimandir, astroved, travelsetu and placeforvacations with no primary source, and the same pages simultaneously print "started 1860 and took eight years." The survey rightly rejects 1876 from that chain — then adopts "twelve" and "15 feet" from it. One page in the chain also gives "13 feet in height and 35 inch in diameter," which nobody reconciles.
- A SOURCE THE SURVEY MISSED, and its claim to have found none. The survey says the Aza South Asia photo-record is "the only thing resembling an architectural-history citation I could find." Rana Safvi's account (ranasafvi.com/shahji-ka-mandir-vrindavan) is a substantive architectural reading: Kundan Lal as treasurer at Wajid Ali Shah's court; completion 1868; the serpentine columns "inspired by the Vatican"; the lions related to Claude Martin's Constantia at Lucknow; the Awadh fish emblem (which I independently confirmed on the pavilion roof); the gateway echoing the Rumi Darwaza; and an inner hall carrying twelve inlaid figures — eleven gopis in a rasleela and the twelfth the Nawab himself. Only the fish is verified by me; the rest should be checked, not ignored.
- THE 2012 PHOTO'S GEOTAG (27.5844434, 77.6971686) IS A CAMERA POSITION, not the building's centroid. The survey's "stored position is GOOD — within 6 m of the centroid" leans partly on it. Since there is no OSM footprint (confirmed) and satellite measurement is the only source, the 6 m agreement is a claim about one unverifiable measurement matching another.
- THE FLANKING KIOSKS sit on the stepped attic block OUTBOARD of the pediment's scrolled shoulders, not on the pediment itself, and the terrace pavilions' arches are slightly pointed/trefoil rather than plain semicircular. Small, but the survey states both as measured.
