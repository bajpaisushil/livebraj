# prem-mandir — exterior survey

*Researched 2026-09-27 by one agent and independently checked by a second whose brief was to refute it. Labels are the researcher's own: MEASURED / SOURCED / DERIVED / ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN.*

*Survey and checker are paired by the workflow journal's launch key, not by completion order — see BACKLOG 2026-09-30 for why that matters.*

## What it is

Prem Mandir is not a "white marble temple" but a 2001–2012 Māru‑Gurjara (Sompura-idiom, "Rajasthani‑Somnath‑Gujarati") two‑storey marble hall temple with two stacked sanctums, standing on a 110 × 68 m raised marble jagati, crowned by one off‑centre curvilinear shekhari shikhara plus stepped samvarana bell‑roofs, its walls carrying ~80–84 recessed POLYCHROME painted narrative panels, and floodlit at night in several different colours simultaneously.

## The silhouette

Everything here survives — the building is 14 years old and complete. Nothing is ruined, nothing is reconstructed.

WHAT CROWNS IT (stated plainly): ONE tall curvilinear NAGARA SHIKHARA of the SHEKHARI type — a latina spire with clustered subsidiary half‑spires (urushringas) banked in 3–4 tiers up each face and corner. It is NOT a dome, NOT an onion, NOT a smooth cone. Flanking/behind it are SAMVARANA (bell/stepped‑pyramidal) roofs over the mandapas: many stacked horizontal courses of small ribbed bell elements rising to a small crowning pavilion. Plus small stepped corner turrets. SOURCED total: "9 domes" and "17 golden‑coloured kalash and a grand flag mast" (Ghumakkar).

MEASURED heights (photogrammetry on Commons "Vrindavan Prem Mandir 4.jpg", 2021‑08‑30 09:56, scale pinned to the sourced 38.1 m flag height; 11.8 px/m; internally consistent with 1.65 m figures at the temple base):
- Parapet/roof deck: 13.3 m
- Secondary spire apex (gold kalash): 19.1 m
- Central samvarana apex (gold kalash): 21.5 m
- Main shikhara, top of gold kalash: ~34.8 m (≈114 ft) — THIS is the masonry summit
- Flag‑mast tip: 38.1 m (125 ft)
- Ratio shikhara : central samvarana : secondary spire = 1.00 : 0.62 : 0.55
- Shikhara base width ~13.4 m; rises ~21.5 m above the parapet.

THE SHIKHARA IS NOT CENTRAL. MEASURED on two independent photographs (day4 and the 2013 night three‑quarter view, which are opposite sides and agree by mirror symmetry): its axis sits at 19–22 % of the facade length from one end. The central SAMVARANA is at 52 % — dead centre. A secondary spire at ~76 % and a corner turret at ~89 %. So the roofline reads, left to right: big spire — (gap) — bell roof — smaller bell roof — turret. ESRI z19 satellite puts the shikhara cone at 34 % from the west end; the discrepancy is off‑nadir lean of a 35 m tower, so treat 20–30 % as the range.

THE FLAG IS NOT ON THE KALASH. A slender metal mast rises from a small RAILED PLATFORM on the shikhara's shoulder, well below the amalaka, OFFSET from the kalash axis, and passes up beside/past it. Saffron triangular dhvaja. A small RED aviation obstruction light at the mast top (visible in all three night photographs).

Finial stack, top down: saffron flag on offset mast | small marble bud finial | bulbous GOLD KALASH | ribbed collar | large ribbed AMALAKA (fluted disc) | shikhara body. Each urushringa carries its own small gold kalash — at least 7 countable on one visible face.

MASSING: a long low two‑storey block (61 × 41 m) with stepped/stellate (ratha) plan — projecting central transepts on the long faces, corner porches with free‑standing columns. The roofscape sits on top like a separate object. From 200 m the building reads as a horizontal white bar with two heavy shadowed cornice lines and one spire punching up on the left.

   MEASURED dark fraction of the upper band: 11.3 %. So opening:solid ≈ 1:8 upstairs, 1:4 downstairs.

6. ROOFSCAPE (above 13.3 m) — see silhouette.

ARCH GRAMMAR: every arch on the building is CUSPED/MULTIFOIL with 5–9 lobes and a foliate apex cusp, springing from slender clustered colonnettes with bell‑and‑chain or lotus capitals, and set inside a rectangular frame with a carved torana/pediment above. There is not one plain semicircular or pointed arch on the facade. Doorway heads use the same cusped profile in miniature. SOURCED: "8 latticed windows" (jali) exist somewhere on the building (Ghumakkar) — I could not locate them in the photographs.

## Material and colour

MATERIAL. SOURCED, self‑reported and universally repeated but never independently verified in anything I could find: "the only temple in the world entirely constructed using pure white solid Carrara Italian marble" (jagadgurukripalujimaharaj.org.in); "30,000 tons of Italian karara marble" (Ghumakkar); 20 ft (6.1 m) deep granite foundation; walls 3.25 ft (0.99 m) of solid marble; garbhagriha walls 8 ft (2.44 m). Note the self‑defeating detail: ordinary Bianco Carrara is described by its own literature as "white or BLUE‑GREY marble" — only the Statuario grade is near‑white. The grey I measure is partly the stone itself, not only shadow.

*** DO NOT PAINT IT WHITE. This is the whole game. ***

THE TWO RULES THAT FALL OUT OF THIS:
(1) SPLIT‑TONE. Sky‑facing surfaces go COOL (H 205°, S 10–13 %); wall faces stay neutral‑warm (H 50–80°, S 3–6 %). Same stone, two families. In direct sun the whole thing swings warm (H 27–32°, S 24–35 %). The marble is a near‑neutral off‑white that takes the illuminant completely: neutral‑cool grey‑cream under monsoon sky, warm ivory under Indian sun.
(2) INVERTED VALUE RAMP. Carved zones are 30–40 % DARKER than plain zones because of micro‑shadow: shikhara V61–69 against plain wall V87–91, a ratio of 1.4. The building is DARKEST AT THE TOP and LIGHTEST AT THE BOTTOM — the exact opposite of the vertical ramp in this project's own detail‑colour.md.

SOILING (DERIVED, well founded): 2013 photographs read distinctly whiter than 2021–2023 photographs of the same faces. Mathura district has severe particulate loading (the Taj marble‑yellowing problem is 60 km away and thoroughly documented). Model as a uniform low‑saturation grey‑cream cast, not as Braj‑style soot streaks — see doNotBuild.

## The setting

A WALLED CAMPUS, NOT A TOWN TEMPLE. This is the single biggest difference from every other building in this project. 22 ha / 54–55 acres SOURCED (one source says 12 acres — see uncertain). Measured on ESRI z17/z18 imagery, all MEASURED unless noted:

Temple centroid 27.572081 N, 77.671945 E (OSM way 673573044). Platform centroid 27.572107 N, 77.672336 E (OSM way 491803653).

THE PLATFORM AND ITS APRONS. The temple (61.4 × 40.7 m) sits on a 110 × 68 m white marble jagati but is NOT centred on it. Aprons: WEST ~11 m, NORTH ~13 m, SOUTH ~14 m, EAST ~38 m. The three narrow aprons are the parikrama — and they match the SOURCED "circumambulation route, which is 40 feet wide" (12.2 m) almost exactly, which is a clean independent cross‑check. The EAST apron is a deep public forecourt that terminates in a SEMICIRCULAR BOW, and every satellite frame shows the crowd concentrated there. Dark teal areas flank the platform's east corners on imagery — water features or dark paving, I could not determine which.

ORIENTATION (DERIVED, not stated by any source I found): entrance and approach on the EAST, sanctum and shikhara toward the WEST. Derived from the forecourt geometry and from the conventional east‑facing of a Krishna temple. No source states it.

THE MUSICAL FOUNTAIN. MEASURED, ~64 m NORTH of the platform's north edge. It is an OVAL/vesica, not a circle: outer ornamental oval (red paving ring + parterre beds) ~116 × 70 m, with pointed triangular terminations east and west; white elliptical basin ~49 × 30 m; inner rectangular pool ~32 × 19 m. SOURCED: show 19:00–19:30 winter, 19:30–20:00 summer, water jets choreographed to bhajans, free entry.

THE SATSANG BHAVAN. ~150 m WEST. A single vast CIRCULAR DOMED HALL, MEASURED at ~90 m diameter on its own square platform ~116 m across — which independently corroborates the SOURCED 6,800 m² / 73,000 sq ft pillarless hall seating 25,000. It is a bigger object in plan than Prem Mandir itself and dominates the west half of the campus.

THE FOUR LEELA STHALIS. SOURCED: life‑size depictions of Jhulan, Govardhan, Raas and Kaliya Naag leelas, the Kaliya one with mechanical animation. OSM carries a "Govardhan Leela" attraction node inside the campus. A peanut/bone‑shaped green pool with a large red‑orange object at its centre is clearly visible in the SW of the campus on imagery — almost certainly the Kaliya Naag sthali. Exact positions of the other three not established.

GARDENS AND PAVING. The campus ground plane is RED SANDSTONE paving laid in a bold diamond‑lattice pattern with white marble cross‑bands and inlaid medallions, alternating with formal green parterre beds with clipped‑hedge scrollwork. South of the platform, three long garden beds and a ~22 m wide red‑paved processional avenue run ~70 m from the platform to the main gate on the road.

THE GATE. A separate, very ornate marble gateway on the south boundary: a huge cusped central arch with Devanagari text around the archivolt, flanking bays, peacock sculptures on the corners and in the spandrel roundels, an oval medallion above carrying "प्रेम मंदिर", and GOLD metal gates with peacock scrollwork. At night it is NEON‑OUTLINED — red neon round the arch, blue neon on the side bays, a blue/pink/green neon oval sign — not floodlit like the temple.

THE TOWN. The campus wall is a hard edge. Dense 2–4 storey rendered concrete Vrindavan presses right up against it on the EAST and SOUTH; agricultural plots and newer development lie north‑west. A dual‑carriageway with a median (Bhaktivedanta Swami Marg, OSM) runs along the SOUTH boundary with buses and cars — this is the arrival road. ISKCON Krishna‑Balaram Mandir is 537 m EAST (OSM). Parikram Marg is nearby.

VISIBILITY (DERIVED). At 38.1 m on a flat plain where the surrounding town is 8–14 m, the shikhara stands 24–30 m proud of the roofline and should be visible for well over a kilometre in any direction, and read as the tallest object in this quarter of Vrindavan. The approach is the giveaway: you do not glimpse it down a lane, you come through a gate into a huge open red‑and‑green formal landscape and see the whole building at once across 70–100 m of empty paving.

CROWD. There is no photograph of this building without hundreds of people in it. The east forecourt and the platform steps are full at every daylight and evening hour.

## What NOT to build

- Uniform white. The measured overcast facade has median value 71%, lower quartile 47%, and 15.7% of it below 35%; mean saturation of the lit marble is 9.4%, not zero. #ffffff / #f5f5f5 reproduces only the brightest 5% of the building. Model at least four value bands and two hue families.
- This project's own vertical value ramp from detail-colour.md (dark at the plinth, light at the parapet). Prem Mandir is INVERTED: carved shikhara V61-69, plain ground-storey wall V87-91. Darkest at the top, lightest at the bottom. Applying the Govind Dev ramp will make it read as a different building.
- A dome, an onion, or a smooth cone on top. It is a curvilinear SHEKHARI shikhara with 3-4 tiers of clustered urushringas, each with its own gold kalash, crowned by a ribbed amalaka then a bulbous gold kalash. The rounded things beside it are stepped SAMVARANA bell-roofs of many horizontal ribbed courses, not domes either, despite every source calling them 'domes'.
- The flag on top of the kalash. It is a separate slender metal mast rising from a small RAILED PLATFORM on the shikhara's shoulder, well below the amalaka and offset from the axis, passing up beside the kalash. It also carries a red aviation obstruction light, visible in all three night photographs.
- A centred shikhara. Measured at 19-22% of the facade length from one end on two independent photographs. The element at the centre (52%) is the samvarana. A symmetric roofline with the spire in the middle is the most likely single error.
- White marble relief in the 80-odd panels. They are POLYCHROME painted narrative scenes - browns, ochres, blues, pinks, greens - recessed behind plain white marble frames, each with a Devanagari caption strip below. At high zoom they read as full-colour paintings populated with many small figures. This is the thing that makes the building look like itself and it is invisible from any text source.
- One floodlight colour at night. Measured over four different evenings: cyan, magenta, crimson, violet, gold, yellow-green, and a banded orange/white/green phase all occur, and several of them occur AT ONCE on different architectural elements. Two Commons photographs 3 minutes 37 seconds apart on the same evening show entirely different schemes.
- A uniform colour wash including the shikhara. In the magenta and yellow-green phases the shikhara is deliberately left COOL WHITE while the body is saturated. In the cyan phase it goes deep blue (#04477e) while the body is bright cyan (#36b4c4). The spire is on its own circuit.
- Colouring the carved panels along with everything else at night. They are separately lit warm white and stay warm in EVERY phase - #bcae8a under cyan, #c2ba9c under yellow-green, #798660 under crimson. A grid of warm rectangles on a saturated building is the signature night image.
- Floodlight washes on the cornices. The cornices are continuous LED STRIP LINES, an order of magnitude brighter than the wall wash (#0799d0 at V82 against a #1c364d wall at V30). They read as drawn lines, not as lit surfaces.
- Coloured lighting only during the 19:30-20:00 show. The photographs measured here are timestamped 20:12 and 20:15, after the show ended, and the building is fully coloured. The show is the fountain plus music; the building stays lit all evening.
- Braj weathering rules - soot in the carving, algae in the north quadrant, drip streaks below cornices, a Yamuna silt line. Wrong building, wrong century, and it is 1.5 km from the river on a raised platform inside a swept campus. What it does have is uniform low-saturation dust-grey soiling that has visibly increased between 2013 and 2023.
- Red sandstone anywhere on the building. But DO use it for the ground: the whole surrounding plaza is red sandstone in a diamond-lattice pattern with white marble cross-bands and inlaid medallions. The white building on red paving is the contrast that makes it read.
- Centring the temple on its platform. Measured aprons: west ~11 m, north ~13 m, south ~14 m, EAST ~38 m, and the east forecourt ends in a semicircular bow. The three narrow sides are the 40 ft parikrama; the east is a public assembly forecourt.
- Nine identical spires to match the sourced '9 domes'. They are one 34.8 m shekhari shikhara, roughly two samvarana bell-roofs at 21.5 m and 19.1 m, and small corner turrets at ~17-18 m. Different forms, different heights, ratio 1.00 : 0.62 : 0.55.
- A solid upper storey. The centre ~15 m of the 56 m upper facade is an OPEN LOGGIA of five cusped arched bays on slender clustered colonnettes, reading as pure black by day. It is the largest single void on the building.
- Plain semicircular or pointed arches. Every opening on the building is cusped/multifoil with 5-9 lobes and a foliate apex cusp, inside a rectangular frame with a carved torana above.
- An empty, undressed building. Both cornices carry a continuous red-and-orange scalloped fabric festoon on festival days (present in some photographs, absent in others), plus string lights and purple drapes in the arcade. And there is no photograph of it without hundreds of people. Make the dressing a toggle, not baked geometry.
- Treating the gate like the temple. The south gate is a separate ornate marble gateway with peacock sculptures and gold gates, and at night it is NEON-OUTLINED - red neon round the arch, blue neon on the side bays, a neon oval sign - not floodlit.
- Anything from Growse. His 1883 memoir predates this temple by 118 years and contains nothing about it. Any 'history' of Prem Mandir before 14 January 2001 is invention.
- A town setting. This is a 22 ha walled campus: formal red-and-green landscape, an oval musical fountain 116 x 70 m to the north, a ~90 m diameter domed Satsang Bhavan to the west that is larger in plan than the temple itself, and a hard wall with dense 2-4 storey Vrindavan pressing against it on the east and south.
- Omitting the split-tone. Sky-facing surfaces measure H205 S10-13 (cool blue); wall faces measure H50-80 S3-6 (neutral-warm). Same stone, two hue families, and losing it is what makes CG marble look like plastic.

## Uncertain — flag, do not assert

- LENGTH AND WIDTH CONFLICT, and it is large. Wikipedia and the temple's own site: 190 ft (58 m) long, 128 ft (39 m) wide platform. Ghumakkar, srgholiday, vrindavanmathura and several others: 122 ft (37 m) long, 115 ft (35 m) wide. OSM's 37-node satellite trace measures 61.4 x 40.7 m and my photogrammetry measures the facade at 56.3 m, both of which back the 190 ft family. Best reading: 122 x 115 ft is probably the core temple excluding the outer verandah, and 190 x 128 ft the outer envelope - but no source says so, and I am guessing.
- PANEL COUNT. Official/JKP and almost everyone: 84 panels. Ghumakkar: 80, split 48 ground + 32 first floor. 48+32=80, so Ghumakkar is internally consistent and the 84 may be a nominal/auspicious figure (84 is loaded in Braj - the 84 kos parikrama). I counted ~16 on one long ground-storey face, which is consistent with either. Unresolved.
- PILLAR COUNT. 94 (most sources) vs 150 (Ghumakkar). Not resolvable from exterior photographs; many of the pillars are interior.
- SITE AREA. 54-55 acres / 22 ha (Wikipedia, most sources) vs 12 acres (Ghumakkar, srgholiday). The satellite-measured walled campus is clearly tens of acres, so 12 acres is probably the built core only.
- SHIKHARA POSITION ALONG THE LONG AXIS. Photogrammetry on two independent photographs gives 19-22% from one end. ESRI z19 satellite puts the cone at 34% from the west end. The difference is ~8 m, consistent with off-nadir lean of a 35 m tower, but I cannot prove which way the image leans. Use 20-25% and treat it as the weakest measured number in this brief.
- ORIENTATION. That the temple faces EAST is DERIVED from the forecourt geometry (38 m east apron ending in a bow, crowds concentrated there) plus convention. No source I found states the orientation. If it is wrong, the shikhara is at the east end and every elevation mirrors.
- THE CARRARA CLAIM is self-reported by Jagadguru Kripalu Parishat and repeated verbatim everywhere. I searched specifically for a contrary claim, an investigation or an independent verification and found none - neither confirming nor refuting. Treat 'Carrara' and '30,000 tons' as a single-source chain. Note also that ordinary Bianco Carrara is described in its own literature as 'white or BLUE-GREY', which would make the grey cast I measure partly intrinsic.
- PANEL MEDIUM. I can see from high-zoom crops that the panels are polychrome. No source states whether they are painted marble relief, painted composition/plaster set into marble frames, or coloured stone inlay. If this matters to the shader, it is UNKNOWN.
- PLATFORM HEIGHT above the surrounding plaza: estimated 1.3-2.0 m from counting risers in night photographs. Low confidence. No source gives it.
- THE DARK TEAL AREAS flanking the platform's east corners on satellite imagery - water features, shade structures, or dark paving. Could not determine.
- SOILING TREND. That the marble has darkened between 2013 and 2023 is DERIVED from comparing photographs under different light and different cameras, which is weak evidence. The regional particulate problem is real and documented; this specific building's trajectory is not.
- NIGHT CYCLE PERIOD. Two photographs 3 min 37 s apart show different schemes, but they are also of different faces, so I cannot separate 'the cycle changed' from 'different elevations run different colours at once'. Probably both, but the actual cycle period is UNKNOWN.
- ROOF ELEMENT COUNT. '9 domes' and '17 golden kalash' come from one secondary source (Ghumakkar) and I could not verify either by counting on photographs - the far side is never fully visible. My own count from the 2013 three-quarter view suggests 1 shikhara + 2 samvaranas + 4-6 corner turrets, which is compatible with 9.
- THE 8 LATTICED WINDOWS (jali) are sourced but I could not locate them in any photograph.

### Corrections to the survey above

- WIKIPEDIA DOES NOT CARRY THE DIMENSIONS THE SURVEY ATTRIBUTES TO IT. I fetched the current wikitext (action=raw). It contains no dimensions at all — zero occurrences of 'marble', 'Carrara', 'Italian', '128', '122', '115', '30,000', '58', '39', 'shikhar' or 'kalash'. The sourced 190/128 ft figures come only from premmandir.org.in: 'The total dimensions of the temple including its flag is 125 ft. high, 190 ft. long and 128 ft. wide' — which assigns 128 ft to THE TEMPLE, with no mention of a platform. The sentence the survey quotes ('...39 metres (128 ft) wide raised platform serves as the seat of the two-storeyed white monument') exists only on Wikipedia-mirror travel sites. The survey's whole length/width reconciliation ('122×115 ft is the core, 190×128 ft the outer envelope') is therefore built on a phantom citation.
- THE 110 × 68 m JAGATI IS NOT A RECTANGLE AND THE PLATFORM IS LONGER THAN 110 m. OSM way 491803653 is tagged building=yes + amenity=place_of_worship + name='Prem Mandir' + the Wikipedia/Wikidata links — it is not tagged as a platform; reading it as the jagati is the survey's inference (a defensible one, since it does trace the white terrace on imagery). But when I overlaid it on ESRI z18 the polygon approximates the eastern bow with a crude stepped chord: the white marble terrace continues about 17 m further east, giving roughly 127 m E–W overall. The west, north and south edges fit the imagery cleanly, so this is not a registration offset. 'RECOMMENDED BUILD NUMBERS: jagati 110 × 68 m' will build a rectangle and lose the forecourt bow.
- THE 'INVERTED VALUE RAMP' IS AN ARTEFACT OF ONE OVERCAST PHOTOGRAPH, NOT A PROPERTY OF THE BUILDING. The survey makes this a hard rule ('The building is DARKEST AT THE TOP and LIGHTEST AT THE BOTTOM', 'carved zones 30–40% darker', 'a ratio of 1.4'). I sampled the sunlit Commons image 'Prem Mandir (Temple of Love).jpg': shikhara upper body V68, mid V71, lower shoulder V57; ground-storey plain wall V77; upper-storey plain wall V50; parapet V69. Ratio shikhara:ground wall is ~1.1, not 1.4, and the spire is BRIGHTER than part of the upper storey. The effect is real under overcast sky (distant spire against a bright sky, plus self-shadowing) but must come from ambient occlusion and aerial perspective, not from a baked albedo ramp — baked, it will read wrong in every sunlit frame.
- THE UPSTAIRS/DOWNSTAIRS OPENING RATIO DOES NOT REPRODUCE. The survey reports dark-pixel fractions of 11.3% (upper) and 19.2% (ground), concluding 'opening:solid ≈ 1:8 upstairs, 1:4 downstairs'. Measuring the same file at full resolution (4608×3456), over 777,705 px of the two-storey body, I get 12.9% upper and 13.2% ground — essentially identical — and 13.0% overall against the survey's 15.7%. The claimed 2:1 split between storeys is not supported. The survey itself concedes the proxy conflates shadow, dark timber doors and dark panels with true voids, so the derived 'true voids 12–15%' should not be split by storey.
- THE BLANKET DISMISSAL OF THE COMMONS '2022 01–13' SERIES IS WRONG. The survey's sources list says these files 'are MISLABELLED and depict other Vrindavan temples; do not use them'. All 13 exist. I downloaded 01, 05, 09 and 13: file 09 shows Prem Mandir's own blue signboards reading 'प्रेम मंदिर ... राधे राधे' beside an EXIT gate, and file 13 is a high-resolution daylight view of an ornate white marble gateway with gold gates and blue metal grilles. Only some of the series (01, 05) show other structures. Discarding all 13 threw away the best daylight record of the gate — the one element the survey describes from a single night photograph.
- THE DARK TEAL AREAS ARE ALMOST CERTAINLY WATER, NOT 'COULD NOT DETERMINE'. At z18 with the platform polygon overlaid, they resolve as two uniform dark-teal quadrants with sharp geometric edges set INTO the eastern bow, symmetrically flanking a fine grid-patterned central panel. Uniform tone, no vegetation texture, formal quadrant geometry and the same teal as the fountain basin all indicate ornamental pools. Model them as water in the bow, flanking a grid-paved or jet-grid centre.
- THE SCALE PIN IS SINGLE-SOURCED AND ITS 'INDEPENDENT CHECK' IS NOT INDEPENDENT. Every height in the brief (13.3 m parapet, 19.1/21.5 m kalashes, 34.8 m masonry summit, 6.27 m storey) hangs on the self-reported 125 ft. The survey validates it by noting 'facade width 56.3 m agrees with the sourced 190 ft to within 3%' — but 125 ft and 190 ft come from the same sentence on premmandir.org.in, so this tests the source's internal proportion, not absolute scale. OSM independently measures the building 61.3 m long. If the visible facade is nearer 61.3 m than 56.3 m, every derived height rises ~9% (shikhara ~37.9 m, flag ~41.5 m). The heights need a ±9% band, not the three-significant-figure confidence they are given.
- THE APRON FIGURES ARE OFF AND WERE MEASURED THE HARD WAY. The survey gives W ~11, N ~13, S ~14, E ~38 m 'measured on ESRI z17/z18'. Both polygons were already in hand; computing the aprons directly from them gives W 13.4, E 35.5, S 12.9, N 14.9 m. The pattern (three narrow sides, one deep eastern forecourt) is confirmed, but each number drifts 1–2.5 m and the survey has N and S the wrong way round — north is the wider of the two, not south.
- 'THE 40 FT PARIKRAMA MATCHES ALMOST EXACTLY, A CLEAN INDEPENDENT CROSS-CHECK' OVERSTATES. The measured narrow aprons are 12.9–14.9 m against the sourced 12.2 m, they derive from two independently traced OSM polygons each carrying its own error, and an apron measured wall-to-platform-edge is not the same quantity as a walkable circumambulation width (which excludes the plinth mouldings the survey itself puts at 1.2–1.6 m). Consistent — not a clean cross-check.
- SITE AREA IS UNVERIFIED IN BOTH DIRECTIONS. The survey accepts 22 ha / 54–55 acres and dismisses the 12-acre figure because 'the satellite-measured walled campus is clearly tens of acres' — without ever giving a measured number. Measuring the walled precinct on z17 imagery (0.53 m/px) I get roughly 430 × 340 m ≈ 14–15 ha ≈ 35–37 acres, which supports neither sourced figure. Wikipedia's infobox says 'Rajasthani Somnath Gujarati architecture'; Ghumakkar says 'Built on the basis of Nagaradi architecture'. Neither says Māru-Gurjara, and no source I found says Sompura. - A LARGE HALL NORTH OF THE FOUNTAIN IS MISSING FROM THE SETTING. It is one of the larger objects in the precinct and the survey's setting section does not mention it.

The survey reconciles its 19–22% photogrammetry against a 34% satellite reading purely by asserting 'off-nadir lean'. The OSM plan settles more of it: profiling way 673573044 west to east gives real steps at 17.0% and 27.1%, bracketing a discrete block — consistent with a sanctum centred ~22% from the west, supporting the lower end. ### Contamination risks

- KIRTI MANDIR, BARSANA — THE BIGGEST UNFLAGGED RISK, AND EXACTLY THE FAILURE MODE THIS PROJECT HAS ALREADY HIT TWICE. Wikipedia states Prem Mandir's sister temple Kirti Mandir (Barsana, opened 2019) was built by the same founder; secondary sources say it 'resembles the Prem Mandir in Vrindavan in terms of architecture and facilities' but is SINGLE-storeyed where Prem Mandir is two-storeyed. Any unlabelled white-marble Kripalu temple photograph, or any search result for a white marble Braj temple with a Nagara shikhara and polychrome panels, may be Kirti Mandir. The survey never names it.
- BHAKTI MANDIR, MANGARH — the third Kripalu temple (foundation stone 26 Oct 1996, inaugurated Nov 2005), 108 ft, built of pink sandstone, white marble and black granite. Sources note its Radha Krishna darshan 'appears exactly same as in Prem Mandir'. Any 'Prem Mandir' interior or deity image showing pink sandstone is Mangarh, not Vrindavan. Also note the dates: a 1996 foundation stone floating loose in search results could be mistaken for Prem Mandir's 2001 one.
- THE CARRARA CHAIN IS ONE SELF-REPORTED CLAIM, AND THE SURVEY THEN BUILDS A SECOND INFERENCE ON TOP OF IT. 'Pure white solid Carrara Italian marble' originates with Jagadguru Kripalu Parishat and is repeated verbatim everywhere; I found no independent verification or refutation, as the survey says. But the survey then reasons from 'Bianco Carrara is white or BLUE-GREY' that the grey it measures is 'partly the stone itself, not only shadow'. That stacks two unverified links: the marble may not be Carrara, and if it is, the grade is unrecorded. The measured grey is fully explained by overcast light, distance haze and micro-shadow — do not model intrinsic grey veining on this argument.
- '9 DOMES', '17 KALASH', '150 PILLARS', '8 LATTICED WINDOWS' AND '30,000 TONS' ALL COME FROM ONE BLOG. Ghumakkar is the single origin; these have since propagated across dozens of travel sites and now come back in search summaries looking independently attested. The survey correctly flags the pillar (94 vs 150) and panel (84 vs 80) conflicts, but treats '9 domes', '17 kalash' and '8 jali' as SOURCED without noting they share that same single origin and conflict with the official site on the two figures where both speak.
- WIKIPEDIA-MIRROR TRAVEL SITES ARE DOUBLE-COUNTING ONE TEXT. hindutsav, templepedia, brijwale, srgholiday and vrindavanmathura reproduce an older Wikipedia revision verbatim, including the '38 metres (125 ft) high, 58 metres (190 ft) long and the 39 metres (128 ft) wide raised platform' sentence and the wall-thickness figures. The survey lists several of these as corroborating sources for the dimension families; they corroborate nothing, they are one text seen five times. The '122 ft × 115 ft' family likewise traces back to Ghumakkar alone.
- LEGEND-AS-HISTORY: THE SURVEY IS CLEAN HERE AND SHOULD STAY THAT WAY. It correctly rules out Growse (1883 predates the building by 118 years) and states that any history before 14 January 2001 is invention. I found nothing in the survey repeating devotional legend as fact. One small watch item: the construction duration is given as 'around 12 years' by Wikipedia and the JKP site but 11 years by Dainik Bhaskar and Ghumakkar; 2001→2012 is 11. Not load-bearing for a model, but it is the kind of number that hardens into fact on restatement.
- THE GATE'S 'NEON' MAY BE PARTLY PAINT. The survey describes the south gate at night as 'NEON-OUTLINED — red neon round the arch, blue neon on the side bays'. Commons file 'Prem Mandir Vrindavan 2022 13.jpg' — one of the files the survey discarded as mislabelled — shows the same gateway in daylight with a red band in the arch soffit and blue-painted metal grilles in the side bays. Some of what reads as coloured lighting is permanent finish. Check before modelling it as an emissive-only effect.
