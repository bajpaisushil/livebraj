# pagal-baba

*Researched and independently verified 2026-09-27. Confidence: partial.*

## oneLine

The tall white-marble temple on the Mathura road at the south-west edge of Vrindavan — "Leeladham", the Pagal Baba Ashram temple, founded in 1969 by Srimad Leelanand Thakur Ji Maharaj ("Pagal Baba"), a former judge, where you climb eight floors of separate shrines on foot to a gallery looking out over the town.

## location

27.55986 N, 77.67885 E — centroid of OSM building polygon way 672984768, recomputed from its nodes. Independently corroborated: the EXIF geotag on Wikimedia Commons File:Mata Sudevi kund.JPG is 27 deg 33' 35.32" N, 77 deg 40' 44.18" E = 27.55981, 77.67894, which is 10 m from that centroid.

Postal identity: Liladham / Pagal Baba Ashram, Mathura Road (Mathura-Vrindavan Marg), Vrindavan, Mathura district, Uttar Pradesh 281121.

It stands on the west side of Mathura Road at the south-west edge of town where Vrindavan gives out to fields, behind a long walled forecourt. Verified distances and bearings from the building centroid:

- District Combined Hospital, Mathura Road — 0.30 km, 105 deg ESE (OSM way 671701979)
- Shri Laxmi Life Line Hospital — 0.28 km, 74 deg ENE (OSM node 7027409447)
- Vrindavan power substation — 0.36 km, 148 deg SSE (OSM way 116046190)
- Mathura Road, nearest carriageway — 0.15 km, 133 deg SE; named carriageways also at 0.27-0.29 km, 80-84 deg E
- ISKCON Krishna-Balaram Mandir — 1.40 km, 356 deg N [account said 2.52 km NE — WRONG]
- Prem Mandir — about 1.5 km, 336 deg NNW [account said 1.43 km NE — bearing WRONG]
- Vrindavan railway station — 2.38 km, 40 deg NE
- Banke Bihari Mandir — 2.49 km, 27 deg NNE [account said 3.03 km NE]
- Vrindavan Chandrodaya Mandir — 3.72 km, 292 deg WNW [account said 0.94 km NW — BADLY WRONG; Chandrodaya is out on the Chhatikara road]
- Mathura Junction — 8.9 km, 183 deg S

The account's whole landmark table is rotated clockwise: from Pagal Baba, the Vrindavan temples lie NORTH to NNW, with only the railway station and Banke Bihari to the NE/NNE.

The account's warning about the OSM point-of-interest node is CORRECT and I confirmed it: node 6094947292 (amenity=place_of_worship, name:en "Pagal Baba temple") sits at 27.55863, 77.67916, which is 140 m from the building at bearing 167 deg (SSE), and 2 m from power-line tower node 1309625628. It is misplaced. Use the building polygon.

Note for anyone wiring the site up: OSM contains NO mapping of the compound — no wall, no kund, no dharamshala blocks, no lawn. Only the temple rectangle and a few maxar-traced service tracks looping to the north and east at 30-200 m.

## scale

FOOTPRINT — firm, and the account is right. I recomputed OSM way 672984768 from its four nodes: 53.1 m x 34.9 m, 1,857 m2, long axis 125.1/305.1 deg. Hindi BhaktiBharat's 18,000 sq ft (1,672 m2) agrees within tracing error. Build 53 x 35 m.

HEIGHT — I redid the photogrammetry independently and get a slightly LOWER answer than the account [REFINED]. On File:Front view of pagal baba mandir in mathura.jpg (1280 px wide version), a person standing on the entrance terrace measures 27.3 px, giving 0.060 m/px at the front plane. That scale independently returns a 53 m frontage — the same number OSM gives, so the scale is validated rather than assumed. Measured silhouette from the terrace ground line to the central kalash, found by sky-edge detection, is 796 px = 47.8 m uncorrected. Correcting for the central tower standing about 17.5 m behind the front plane, with camera distance 65-80 m derived from the same focal scale, gives 55-61 m to the tip of the kalash. Every published source, English and Hindi, says 221 ft = 67.4 m. That is a temple figure, not a survey, and my measurement runs about 10 percent under it. WHAT TO BUILD: about 59 m to the tip of the central kalash, tolerance +/- 6 m. Split roughly 60/40 — about 36 m of occupied storeys (eight levels, 4.5-5.0 m floor to floor, high shrine halls with galleries) and a crowning arcade-and-dome stage of about 22 m. Central dome about 13 m across. Chakra medallion 4-5 m. The two entrance stair flights rise one storey, about 5 m. The two stair towers rise from first-floor level to roughly 40 m before their spires begin.

COMPOUND — measured off the rooftop photograph, scaled by a parked car and a group of standing people, and SMALLER than the account says [REFINED]. Court between the two side blocks: about 55-65 m wide. Mata Sudevi Kund: about 30 m across the court by about 22 m along it, roughly 3 m deep, three or four tiers of steps — not 40-50 x 20-30. The two arcaded side blocks: two storeys, about 7 m tall, roughly 100 m long, 10 m deep. srimandir gives the whole campus as five acres = 20,234 m2, which at a 90 m overall width implies a walled enclosure about 225 m long — so the court is probably LONGER than the 150 m the account assumes, not shorter.

DISTANCE TO THE ROAD — the account's "150 m, firm from OSM" is mis-assigned [WAS WRONG]. I ray-cast from the building centroid against every primary/secondary/trunk way within 900 m. Bearing 125 deg (SE) hits Mathura Road at exactly 150 m — that is where the figure came from. Bearing 35 deg (the claimed facade normal) hits no main road within 900 m. The nearest Mathura Road carriageway is 118 m from the nearest building corner and 147 m from the centroid, at bearing 133 deg; the named carriageways then run past the site at 267-290 m to the east. So the road wraps the site's south-east and east flank. Do not lay the court out as a 150 m axis ending at the road.

## architecture

Corrected version. I checked every load-bearing claim against OSM, Wikimedia Commons photographs (which I measured), BhaktiBharat (EN and HI), srimandir, trawell and brajrasik. Most of the other researcher's description survives; six things do not. I mark each correction [WAS WRONG] or [REFINED].

PLAN AND FOOTPRINT — firm. I re-downloaded OSM way 672984768 and recomputed it myself from the four node positions: 53.1 m x 34.9 m, area 1,857 m2, long-axis bearing 125.1/305.1 deg, centroid 27.55986 N 77.67885 E. The researcher's 53.2 x 35.0 / 1,861 m2 is right. Hindi BhaktiBharat's 18,000 sq ft = 1,672 m2 is the same building within tracing error (the English page of the same site prints "1800 sq ft", a dropped zero — do not use it). A single symmetrical rectangular block, one central axis. Build 53 x 35 m.

ORIENTATION — UNRESOLVED, and the account overstates it [WAS WRONG]. The 53 m faces look toward 35 deg (NE) or 215 deg (SW); the 35 m faces toward 125/305. Photogrammetry settles that the entrance front is a 53 m face (see scale note), so the facade normal is 35 or 215 — but which one is not determinable from open data, and OSM has nothing of the compound mapped. Treat the compass as inferred. What is NOT true is the claim that the court runs 150 m NE to Mathura Road (see scale).

MASSING — right in spirit, wrong in geometry [REFINED]. It is a stepped, receding, pyramidal mass — correct, and it is not a shikhara temple. But it is not a uniform ziggurat in which every storey steps back the full width. What the photographs show:
- Ground + first storey: the full 53 m, running out as two wings to square corner pavilions at each front corner.
- Above the wings: a narrower central block (roughly 30-35 m), flanked left and right by two TALL STAIR TOWERS (below).
- Above that: three or four further receding stages, each perhaps 18-25 m wide, each with an open walk-round gallery, a deep flat chhajja on brackets, and a low parapet of small square balusters. Roughly 7-8 occupied levels in all; only two of them are full width.

THE TWO FLANKING TOWERS ARE SOLID STAIR SHAFTS [WAS WRONG — the single worst modelling error in the account]. The account says the tall pyramidal spires each sit "on a square open pavilion with its own flat eave slab on columns". The two biggest do not. In File:Sri Krishna temple at Mathura.jpg, magnified, each is a plain blank marble shaft with PAIRED NARROW VERTICAL SLIT WINDOWS at four stacked levels (plus small square openings lower down), rising unbroken from about first-floor level, capped by a projecting flat eave slab and then a tall straight-sided four-sided pyramidal spire in stepped marble courses with a bulbous amalaka-and-kalash finial. They are staircase towers, and their shafts are the dirtiest, greyest surfaces on the building. Build them as closed shafts with slit lights, not as open colonnaded kiosks. The smaller pyramidal spires lower down DO sit on open pillared pavilions; the account's description fits those.

THE CROWN — confirmed, with the ornament toned down [REFINED]. An open arcaded pavilion stage (square piers, cusped openings, balustrade), and on it a cluster of five domes: one large central dome on a small square lantern with an opening in it, and four smaller ones at the corners. Profile is bell/ogee — taller than hemispherical — built in visible horizontal marble courses with only faint vertical ribbing. Not "bulbous beehive with vertical flutes". Each has a fluted amalaka-like collar and a stacked pot-and-multi-disc spike finial. A thin metal flagpole rises from the central dome well above the kalash, carrying a saffron/yellow flag (present in 2013, 2014, 2018; furled in June 2016). Crown stage is roughly the top third of the silhouette. Correct.

ENTRANCE FRONT — confirmed, with two fixes. Two straight flights of steps climb inward from the garden to the first-floor terrace, plain white square-lattice balustrades, meeting at a central platform. Over the doorway a projecting porch of four round columns with bell/lotus capitals carrying a bracketed cornice and a canopy, and above it a LARGE stepped-coursed dome with amalaka and kalash — in the rooftop photograph this is the biggest dome on the whole roof in plan, not "a small ribbed dome" [REFINED]. Set in the wall between the porch columns, a circular medallion window roughly 4-5 m across: moulded marble ring, black radial spoke/chakra pattern, a pale eight-petalled star rosette at the centre and a small red-painted figure beneath it. The Sudarshan-chakra window is real and is the centre of the composition.

THE JHANKI HALL [REFINED]. At ground level under the terrace, a glazed shopfront hall — correct — under a temporary white fabric marquee with a red scalloped valance (2016) or plain white (2013, 2016). But the electric tableaux also occupy a SEPARATE FORWARD PAVILION at the right-hand end of the front, a square pavilion with a stepped pyramidal roof, ribbed dome and kalash, signed in Hindi "विद्युत लीला घर" (electric leela house), with a second board "विद्युत चलित झाँकियाँ" and an arrow directing visitors. Build the pavilion.

OPENINGS [REFINED]. Ground and first storey of the wings: rectangular openings filled with dark square-pierced jali screens, plus dark-framed doors and windows — correct. Upper galleries: NOT rectangular jali. They are rows of CUSPED / MULTIFOIL ARCHED niches and openings between plain piers, dark in shadow, with balustrades in front. Split the two treatments by level.

MATERIAL AND COLOUR — confirmed. RC frame clad in white-grey veined marble slabs in large rectangular courses with the joint grid plainly visible. Off-white to pale grey-cream, weathered, black and green streaking down every cornice, ledge and terrace; the stair-tower shafts are visibly greyer than the spires above them. A dark blue-grey to near-black polished stone dado runs along the base of the front stairs and around the ground storey — clearly visible in 2016 photographs. Roof terraces are irregular crazy-paving grey marble; a strip of black/white/ochre diamond mosaic runs down the axis of the entrance roof (visible in the rooftop photograph). Split air-conditioning units are bolted to the upper walls in the 2018 photograph — a modern detail worth including.

INTERIOR — as described, unverified by me. Plain marble-lined halls, shrines in recessed niches with stepped/cusped surrounds, hanging curtain, gold-yellow cloth backdrop, brass and steel rails, black stone "दान पात्र" donation counter, suspended ceilings, tube light. No carved figurative stonework: tourism sites (srimandir, trawell) say each floor has "idols depicting scenes from Krishna's life and the Ramayana", but that describes the murtis and the electric tableaux, not carved stone relief. No photograph shows figurative carving anywhere outside.

CIRCULATION. Internal stairs, and the two stair towers are now identified on the elevation. No source I found mentions a lift; trawell explicitly describes stairs. Treat "no lift" as probable, not proven. A continuous open verandah runs round each upper storey.

THE COMPOUND — real, but re-measured [REFINED]. Confirmed from the rooftop photograph File:Top view in Pagal BABA temple, mathura.jpg and from ground shots: a paved terrace; a lawn with a circular clipped-hedge ring and, at its centre, a slender white marble fountain of two wide flat parasol/lotus dishes on a turned column with a finial, on a stepped square pedestal in a round basin; a marble balustrade with square-lattice balusters and urn finials; a sunken rectangular stepped tank, three or four tiers down to dark green water; paved forecourt with parked cars. Down both long sides, two long two-storey blocks with continuous arcaded verandahs on both levels, flat roofs with parapets, plastered cream/white, not marble — the dharamshala and offices. The paths are laid in a bold harlequin diamond pattern in red, yellow, turquoise and white cement tile. Columnar evergreens line the lawn, conical topiary along the temple balustrade, plain steel streetlight poles. At least two small detached white shrines with their own spires in the grounds, one with a genuine curvilinear Nagara shikhara, standing near the kund. Boundary walls are white square-lattice parapet over a solid base — the stretch beside the kund is marble-faced, but the stretch in the 2014 photograph reads as painted concrete, not marble.

THE KUND IS NAMED [MISSING FROM THE ACCOUNT]. It is MATA SUDEVI KUND (माता सुदेवी कुण्ड). Two independent photographs seven years apart (2009, 2018) show the blue Hindi signboard beside it, with a rules notice below. Sudevi is one of the ashta-sakhis of Braj, so this is a named Braj tirtha that the ashram was built around, not just an ornamental tank. It is NOT an ASI-protected monument — the Wiki Loves Monuments template on the 2009 photograph records the ASI number as "NA".

THIN SPOTS, HONESTLY. Still thin, and the account is right to say so. No plan, section or dimensioned drawing published anywhere. The depth of the building behind the front plane, the rear elevation and all floor-to-floor heights are unphotographed. Which of the two long faces is the front is not resolvable from open sources. Compound dimensions are photogrammetry from one oblique rooftop photograph and should be treated as sketch.

## distinguishing

Three things, and the third is new.

1. THE STEPPED WHITE MARBLE MOUNTAIN WITH TWO STAIR TOWERS IN IT. Receding storeys, each leaving an open walk-round gallery with a deep eave slab and a low square-baluster parapet, so from outside you see people standing at three or four different heights on one facade. Crowning it, an open arcaded pavilion carrying five coursed bell-profile domes (one large, four at the corners) and a saffron flag on a thin pole. Flanking the central tower, two TALL BLANK MARBLE SHAFTS pierced only by paired narrow slits, each capped by a straight-sided four-sided pyramidal spire — the staircases, and the strongest verticals in the whole composition. Nothing else in Braj has this profile.

2. THE BIG CIRCULAR CHAKRA WINDOW over the front door, roughly 4-5 m across: moulded marble ring, black radial chakra, pale eight-petalled star at the centre, a small red figure beneath — set between two straight flights of steps that climb inward from the garden, under a four-columned porch with a large stepped-coursed dome. It is the first thing a pilgrim recognises.

3. MATA SUDEVI KUND IN THE FORECOURT. The big sunken stepped tank in front of the temple is a named Braj kund with its own blue Hindi signboard, and the ashram is laid out around it: temple, terrace, lawn with a two-tier marble parasol fountain in a circular hedge, balustrade, kund, forecourt, all on paving laid in red, yellow, turquoise and white diamonds, with two long two-storey arcaded dharamshala blocks down the sides. Behind all that, the vertical stack of separate shrines — one dham per floor, climbed on foot, ending at a gallery over Vrindavan.

## doNotBuild

- NOT a Nagara shikhara temple, whatever the guidebooks say. BhaktiBharat, srimandir and trawell all print "Nagara". There is no curvilinear rekha-shikhara and no garbhagriha under one. The crown is five coursed bell-profile domes over an open arcaded pavilion, plus straight-sided pyramidal spires. No Khajuraho/Rangji spire.
- DO NOT put the two big flanking spires on open colonnaded pavilions. They cap solid stair shafts with paired slit windows. This is the single correction most likely to save the model.
- DO NOT build eight identical full-width terraces stepping back like a ziggurat. Only ground and first storey run the full 53 m out to the corner pavilions; above that it is a central tower between the two stair towers.
- NOT Taj-Mahal-like. One published source calls it designed to resemble the Taj's grandeur. Ignore it. No onion dome on a high drum, no minarets, no iwan, no charbagh.
- NOT Prem Mandir. No figurative carved relief, no lace tracery, no coloured night-lighting show. Ornament is plain: slab joints, square jali grilles, plain mouldings, bracketed eave slabs, bell-capital columns, cusped arch heads.
- Not red sandstone, not pink, not painted. White-grey veined marble slab cladding over a concrete frame, visible joints.
- Not gleaming. Every photograph 2009-2018 shows it weathered — grey-cream, black and green streaking down cornices and ledges, algae on the roof terraces, the stair-tower shafts noticeably dirtier than the spires. Build the dirt. It reads brighter at noon in May than under monsoon cloud, but it is never clean.
- No single interior hall, no single altar. A stack of separate shrine floors off open outdoor galleries, reached by stairs. Do not add a lift.
- The awning over the ground-floor glazed hall is temporary fabric — a white marquee with a red scalloped valance, or plain white. Do not build it in marble.
- DO NOT put Chandrodaya Mandir next door. The account says 0.94 km NW; OSM/Nominatim puts it at 27.57257 N 77.64390 E, about 3.7 km WNW on the Chhatikara road. And the whole Vrindavan temple cluster lies NORTH to NNW of here, not north-east: ISKCON Krishna-Balaram 1.40 km at 356 deg, Prem Mandir about 1.5 km at 336 deg, Banke Bihari 2.49 km at 27 deg. The account rotates all of them clockwise.
- DO NOT build a 150 m court running north-east from the front to Mathura Road. That 150 m is the distance to the road along bearing 125 deg (SE), not along the facade axis. A ray along the claimed 35 deg facade normal meets no main road within 900 m.
- Do not place it in the old town. It stands on the Mathura-Vrindavan road among fields and low village building, about 2.5 km SSW of Banke Bihari, invisible from the galis. A landmark of the road approach, not of the ghats.
- Storey count is disputed: nine in BhaktiBharat, srimandir and trawell; ten in a Wikimedia uploader's caption; eleven in one srimandir passage and on Flickr; five in another guide. Only eight shrine levels are actually named. Do not build eleven floors of shrines.
- Do not credit the building to "the followers of Pagal Baba" as its builders. That phrasing comes from one Wikimedia Commons uploader's caption and contradicts every substantive source. Pagal Baba founded it himself in 1969; he took mahasamadhi on 24 July 1980; the deities were installed in 1981, after his death, so the ashram finished it. His samadhi sthal and an akhand jyoti are on the ground floor. Some sites garble his name to "Shri Sheela Nand Ji Maharaj" — the name in the primary-adjacent sources is Srimad Leelanand Thakur.
- The saffron flag flies from a thin pole. Not a finial, not a spire.
- The corner pavilions at the ends of the front wings belong to the temple block. The two or three small detached shrines further out in the grounds — one with a real curvilinear shikhara, near the kund — are separate. Do not merge them.

## deities

Shri Radha-Krishna is the presiding pair, but the point of the building is that each floor is a separate dham. BhaktiBharat (English and Hindi pages) names eight levels:

- Ground floor — Shiv Mandir; Durga Dham; Kali; Bhairav; Srimad Lilanand Thakur Dham (Pagal Baba's samadhi sthal); Akhand Jyoti (eternal flame); the electric jhanki hall
- First floor — Shri Radha-Krishna; Shri Ganesh
- Second floor — Shri Krishna-Balram
- Third floor — Shri Nand-Yashoda
- Fourth floor — Shri Ram (Ram Darbar)
- Fifth floor — Shri Vaman Avatar
- Sixth floor — Shri Narayan
- Seventh floor — Om Dham

Sources say nine storeys but name only these eight. Do not invent shrines for a ninth.
