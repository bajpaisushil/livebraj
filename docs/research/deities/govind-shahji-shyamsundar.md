# Deity forms: Govind Dev, Shahji (Chhote Radha Raman), Radha Shyamsundar

Filed from workflow run wf_7ae47b98-f89, paired by journal key (survey and checker results joined on the `key` of their `started` events, never by completion order).

**Status:** CHECKED by an independent refuter

These are living, worshipped murtis. The survey's own sensitivity notes come first and bind everything below them.

## Sensitivity

- These are living, worshipped murtis. Show them only as a devotee sees them in darshan: dressed, ornamented and on their thrones. Never undressed, and never as bare sculpture.
- No interaction: no touching, picking up, rotating, zooming, collecting or scoring, and no reward for taking darshan (the project's rule against gamified devotion).
- Keep the camera where pilgrims actually stand: at Govind Dev in the nave, looking through the west doorway; at Shahji at the hall door behind the wooden railing; at Radha Shyamsundar in the courtyard before the arches. No views from behind or above an altar, and no walking onto one.
- The face is the heart of darshan. A simple face in the right tone and soft altar light is more respectful than a detailed face that is wrong. Never caricature or distort it.
- Honour closed hours. The Govind Dev doorway is shut with wooden doors outside darshan (Commons 2015). Yogamaya's chamber is 'closed for the entire year except on Navaratri'. Shahji's Basanti Kamra opens only twice a year.
- Photography is 'strictly prohibited' inside Radha Shyamsundar (VrajVrindavan) and reportedly prohibited at Govind Dev (mathuravrindavantemples.com). Do not take Deity images from pilgrims' photographs without the temple's permission.
- At Radha Shyamsundar, Radharani's feet are shown only once a year (Utthana Dvadashi). Keep them covered in the model.
- At Govind Dev, 'His footprint' is worshipped at the chhatri behind the old temple (Vrindavan Today 2021 photo caption). It is an object of worship: never make it walkable floor detail. By contrast, Shahji's founders set their own portraits into the floor so that devotees' bare feet would pass over them and bring them the dust of Braj (trust site); walking over those is correct.
- Shahji's figure of Nawab Wajid Ali Shah in sakhi-vesha records the founders' friendship with him (Amar Ujala 2020). Treat it with the same dignity as the gopi figures.
- Keep each lineage distinct, and do not homogenise them with ISKCON styling. Radha Shyamsundar is a Shyamanandi (Odia Gaudiya) seat with Odia-script donor plaques. Shahji is the Shah family's Gaudiya temple in Lucknow Nawabi dress. Govind Dev belongs to Rupa Goswami's line.
- Do not act out origin legends on or around the altars: the cow's milk at Goma Tila, the heart-manifestation of Shyamsundar, the Bharatpur treasury.

## Do not model

- PROJECT RECORDS CHECKED. (a) 'Govind Dev's original murti is in Jaipur': CONFIRMED (Growse 1883 p.245; Entwistle 1987 pp.409–410). (b) 'Growse documents Krishna as GIRIDHARI with Mahaprabhu and Nityananda in the rebuilt sacrarium': CONFIRMED VERBATIM (3rd ed. p.243, footnote to 'The sacrarium has been utterly razed to the ground'). But it is an 1883 snapshot of a brick sacrarium 'roughly rebuilt ... about the year 1854'. It gives no pose, material or size, and it is not a description of today's altar, whose central Deity current sources call the pratibhu GOVINDADEVA. (c) 'Shahji's Deity is Chhote Radha Raman': CONFIRMED (Braj Ras). But it is a PAIR — Krishna and a Radha murti — about 15–25 cm and gold-toned, formally 'Thakur Radha Raman Lal Ju'. (d) The altars.js side placement at Radha Shyamsundar matches the best-supported reading, but its black-stone skins for Lala-Lali and Radha-Kunjabihari are invented.
- Govind Dev: do not give the central Krishna a raised arm lifting Govardhan (altars.js arm:'raised' plus a grey hill; the code comment says 'Growse documents the form', which he does not). 'Giridhari' in Growse is a name: Entwistle 1987 p.79 says Giridhari 'is often used for flute-playing images'. The 2021 photograph shows a flute at His lips, and the raised arm in the Braj Ras photograph belongs to the flanking golden Chaitanya.
- Govind Dev: do not bring the Jaipur altar to Vrindavan. The original Govindadeva ('much larger than is usual', Entwistle; 'a beautiful black marble Deity', Holy Dham), His original Radha, Kashishvara's ashta-dhatu Gaura-Govinda (at His right in Jaipur) and the Lalita and Vishakha figures added by Jaipur's rulers are all in Jaipur. The Commons files 'Govind dev ji.jpg' (two gold-draped sakhis flanking; silver plinth lettered 'श्री राधागोविंद देवजी') and 'Церемония арати.jpg' show Jaipur.
- Govind Dev: do not use 'black marble' for the Vrindavan pratibhu. Holy Dham says it of the Jaipur Deity, and search summaries conflate the two.
- Govind Dev: do not put a Deity inside the old ASI nave or on the razed sanctum. The living altar is in the early-19th-century temple behind, seen through the west doorway. Popular belief holds that 'worship cannot continue in that temple' because it was desecrated (ISKCON Desire Tree / Holy Dham).
- Govind Dev: do not put Vrinda Devi in the south-west chapel. It 'has remained empty' since her image was taken to Kaman (Entwistle 1987), where it 'remains to this day' (p.184).
- Govind Dev: do not shrink Chaitanya and Nityananda to token side figures because Growse called them 'subordinate'; in the one photograph their crowns reach Govinda's. Do not make them dark: they read golden. The current altars.js also gives them no crown, but in the photograph they wear crowns (dress, changeable).
- Govind Dev: Yogamaya / Patal Devi is a small marble relief of Durga, fully dressed, in a closed underground chamber. Do not make her a free-standing multi-armed goddess, and do not make her visible from the nave.
- Shahji: do not borrow Gopal Bhatt's Radha Raman: no black shalagram, and no crown standing in for Radha. Here Radha is a murti. Growse's 'temple of Radha Raman' (1883 p.262) is Shahji. VRI's Radha Raman entry wrongly gives Radha Raman Shahji's spiral marble columns and 'Seth Kundanlal' with a 1542 date. Do not merge the two.
- Shahji: do not model the pair at about a metre (altars.js h 1.0–1.06, i.e. ~1 m figures). ESTIMATED 15–25 cm, on a ~0.8 m-wide silver throne.
- Shahji: do not seat the Deities in the Basanti Kamra all year. They go there only on Vasant Panchami and Shravan Shukla 13–14. The 2016 Commons photograph shows a festival state.
- Shahji: these are sculpture, not murtis: the twelve life-size figures in the hall (eleven dancing gopis, plus Nawab Wajid Ali Shah in sakhi-vesha 'attending to the sewa', per the trust site), the fourteen pietra-dura gopika panels, the gilt female figures flanking the Basanti Kamra throne and the founders' portraits in the floor. Never put them on the altar or give them worship cues.
- Shahji: do not add a 'Lalit Mohini' deity. Braj Ras's 'Lalit Kishori and Lalit Mohini Sevya Thakur' (repeated in temple-architecture.json) appears to confuse the founder's brother, Lalit Madhuri (Shah Phundan Lal; Entwistle pp.218–219; the trust site's verses are signed 'ललिता माधुरी'), with Swami Lalit Mohini Dev of the Haridasi Tatiya Sthan.
- Radha Shyamsundar: this is NOT ISKCON's Radha-Shyamasundara (Krishna-Balaram Mandir: white-marble Radha, with Lalita and Vishakha). Never attach client/assets/deities/radha-shyamsundar.jpg to this temple. The Commons file 'Sri Sri Radha Shyam Sundar.jpg' (Aliva Sahoo 2026) actually shows ISKCON's Krishna-Balaram altar (white Balarama, black Krishna, cows).
- Radha Shyamsundar: do not add Lalita/Vishakha, Gaura-Nitai or Jagannath; none is documented on this altar. One uncaptioned Braj Ras album photograph shows a Jagannath-like face, but it is unidentified.
- Radha Shyamsundar: do not show Radharani's feet, do not display the Manjughosha anklet (no source says it is shown), and do not stage the heart-manifestation legend.
- All three temples: do not carve dress, garlands, crowns, turbans, woollens, flutes or facial gold patterning into the murti. Model the Deity's form and the shringar as separate, swappable layers. Flutes are removable ornaments (Entwistle p.78).
- All three temples: no freely licensed photograph exists of the Govind Dev or Radha Shyamsundar murtis. Braj Ras, Vrindavan Today and temple-website images are copyright: measure from them, but never ship them or trace them as textures.

## The murtis, one by one

### Sri Govindadeva, the PRATIBHU (representative replica). The ORIGINAL Govindadeva has been in Jaipur since the 18th centu

**temple:** Shri Govind Dev Ji, Vrindavan: the living shrine. It is the early-19th-century temple (1819–20, Nanda Kumar Basu) 'at the back of' the 1590 red-sandstone monument (ASI N-UP-A195), and pilgrims see it from the old nave through the square-headed west doorway.

**name:** Sri Govindadeva, the PRATIBHU (representative replica). The ORIGINAL Govindadeva has been in Jaipur since the 18th century (Growse 1883 p.245: 'the image of the god was transferred to Jaypur'; Entwistle 1987 pp.409–410). So have His original Radha and Kashishvara's Gaura-Govinda. Do not model the original here.

**posture:** Standing and two-armed, with both hands raised toward His own right to hold the flute at His lips. OBSERVED in the Vrindavan Today photograph captioned 'Presiding deity of Shri Govinda Dev in Vrindavan (New Govinda Dev)' (16 Oct 2021; copyright, measurement only). The legs are hidden by the dress, so the crossed-leg tribhanga is ESTIMATED from type, not seen. SOURCED for the type: the pratibhu is described as a replica of the original (Gaudiya Treasures of Bengal 2023). The original is the standing tribhanga flute-player with 'his hands raised beside his right shoulder' (Entwistle 1987 p.78, plate 5). Growse (1883 p.243 fn) calls the image here 'Krishna in his character of Giridhari'. That is a NAME, not a pose: Entwistle p.79 says Giridhari 'is often used for flute-playing images'. No source or photograph shows a raised arm lifting Govardhan.

**height:** UNKNOWN: no text gives a height. ESTIMATED 0.8–1.1 m visible, crown to seat, from the 2021 Jhulan photograph, scaled on its floor tiles (assumed ~30 cm). This holds only if that photograph shows the pratibhu and not the 'small Radha and Krishna' also on this altar. Low confidence.

**material:** UNKNOWN. Only the appearance is known: the face reads near-black in two photographs (Braj Ras, uploaded 2020-03-20; Vrindavan Today 2021) — MEASURED appearance. Holy Dham's 'black marble' describes the ORIGINAL in Jaipur; do not transfer it.

**murtiColour:** UNKNOWN from any freely licensed photograph. Indicative only: near-black ≈#25180F–#2A1F18, MEASURED (uncalibrated, warm lamp light) from a copyrighted Braj Ras photograph (all rights reserved; measurement only). The face shows large white eyes, and in 2021 gold patterning that is probably applied shringar.

**attributes:**
- Flute (vamsi) held at the lips with both hands, toward His own right (OBSERVED 2021). It is a separate ornament: 'the flute is separate from the image and is placed in his hands in the daytime and removed at night' (Entwistle 1987 p.78, general practice). It is not part of the carved form.
- Two arms only (Govinda type: SOURCED for the original; here OBSERVED only as two hands at the flute).
- Headdress CHANGES: a tall gold crown under a large pink-and-gold lotus-shaped crest (Braj Ras, c.2020); a multicoloured turban with a peacock feather (VT 2021).
- Large white eyes and gold patterning on the dark face in both photographs. Whether these are permanent (inlaid or painted) or applied daily is UNKNOWN.
- Garlands, necklaces and sequinned dress: CHANGING.
- NO raised arm and NO Govardhan hill (see doNotModel).

**arrangement:** Centre. SOURCED (ISKCON Desire Tree and Holy Dham, ≤2014; Gaudiya Treasures of Bengal 2023): 'Govindaji in the middle, to His left is Lord Caitanya, and to His right is Lord Nityananda. Below are small Radha and Krishna Deities. Below Them are Lord Jagannath and a Govardhana-shila.' Read in the Deity's own frame, Chaitanya stands at His own LEFT (viewer's right) and Nityananda at His own RIGHT (viewer's left). The Braj Ras photograph is consistent: the figure at the viewer's right raises a pointing index finger. Narayana Maharaja's 'with Sri Radhika to His left' conflicts with Chaitanya's position; see the Radharani entry.

**altarSetting:** Seen from the old red-sandstone nave through the square-headed, sculpture-framed west (choir) doorway. Commons photographs from 2012 and 2026 show pilgrims walking the nave axis toward the lit doorway. SOURCED: the living shrine is the early-19th-century temple 'at the back of the original one' (Entwistle pp.409–410), 'to the north-west of the old sandstone edifice' (VT 2021), which 'looks like a natural extension of it' (Satyaraja Dasa, BTG). OBSERVED c.2020 (Braj Ras): red-sandstone jambs, an overhead canopy of marigold and leaf garlands, a pink-magenta cloth backdrop, and the three figures standing side by side. OBSERVED 2021 (VT, Jhulan): a silver repoussé swing with silver chains and pillars on a silver repoussé plinth, a white-and-gold frilled seat, a green embroidered backdrop, two small gold chhatras (one over each Deity), and a black-and-cream checkerboard marble floor.

**fixedVsChanging:** FIXED (as documented): one standing, dark, two-armed Krishna at the centre of the altar, with Chaitanya and Nityananda standing either side. CHANGING daily and seasonally: dress (green-gold sequins c.2020; green Jhulan outfit 2021), crown or turban, garlands, jewellery, gold facial decoration, the flute. SPECIAL: at Jhulan (Shravan) He is shown in a large silver swing with a Radha at His left (VT 2021). Darshan hours per travel guides are about 4:30 am–12:30 pm and 5:30–9 pm. Outside darshan the west doorway is shut with wooden doors (Commons photograph, 29 Dec 2015).

### Yogamaya Devi, called 'Patal Devi' by Brajwasis

**temple:** Shri Govind Dev Ji, Vrindavan: the underground chamber (Yogapitha) beneath the south-west chapel of the 1590 temple

**posture:** UNKNOWN in detail. SOURCED: 'a small marble relief of Durga ... which is given the name Yogamaya or Patal Devi' (Entwistle 1987 pp.409–410). It is a relief, not a free-standing figure. The arms, weapons and vahana are hidden by dress in the 2022 photograph.

**height:** UNKNOWN. SOURCED: 'very small and delicately beautiful' (Vrindavan Today, 9 Apr 2022); 'small' (Entwistle).

**material:** SOURCED: marble (Entwistle 1987).

**murtiColour:** UNKNOWN from licensed photographs. Indicative: a pale stone face heavily marked with vermilion (sindoor), reading pinkish-brown ≈#915A51–#936459 (copyrighted VT 2022 photograph; measurement only).

**attributes:**

- Jewelled silver crown and a small silver chhatra (OBSERVED 2022; changeable ornament).
- Dressed in maroon/red and gold with marigold and turquoise-blue flower garlands (OBSERVED 2022; CHANGING).
- Arms, weapons, lion: UNKNOWN (covered).

**arrangement:** Alone, underground, not on the main altar. SOURCED tradition (VT 2022): before Govindadeva went to Jaipur He sat 'with Vrinda Devi on the left and Yogamaya on the right'. Vrinda Devi is now at Kaman (Entwistle p.184).

**fixedVsChanging:** FIXED: the small marble relief. CHANGING: dress, crown, garlands. ACCESS: 'Patal Devi temple is closed for the entire year except on Navaratri'; on other days the priest may open it on request (VT 2022).

### Thakur Sri Radha Raman Lal Ju ('Chhote Radha Raman')

**temple:** Shahji Mandir (Lalit Nikunj, the 'Tedhe Khambe wala' temple), near Nidhivan, Vrindavan. Built 1860–68 by Shah Kundan Lal ('Lalit Kishori') and Shah Phundan Lal ('Lalit Madhuri') of Lucknow.

**name:** Thakur Sri Radha Raman Lal Ju ('Chhote Radha Raman'): Krishna. The 1866 waqf names 'Sri Thakur Radha Raman Ji Maharaj' (trust site). 'Chhote' is because 'the deities due to their small size are also called as chhote Radha Raman' (Braj Ras). This is a different Deity from Gopal Bhatt's Radha Raman.

**posture:** Standing upright, both hands raised at His right to hold the flute. OBSERVED in the CC BY-SA 2026 Commons photograph and in Braj Ras close-ups (uploaded 2020). The legs are hidden by the spread poshak, so tribhanga is UNKNOWN.

**height:** ESTIMATED 15–25 cm including the crown; no text gives a figure, only 'small'. Two scalings agree: (a) against the small silver bowls on the altar table (assumed 7–10 cm) and the silver throne (~0.8 m wide) in the 2026 CC BY-SA photograph, about 20 cm; (b) against a priest's head beside the throne in a Braj Ras photograph (throne plus chhatra ≈0.6–0.7 m), about 16 cm.

**material:** UNKNOWN: no text states it. OBSERVED: the face and hands read as polished yellow metal with painted features, consistent with a metal murti. Do not name an alloy.

**murtiColour:** MEASURED from the CC BY-SA 3.0 photograph (Aliva Sahoo, 19 Apr 2026; warm indoor light, uncalibrated): face highlights ≈#C9A97E–#D1B484, mid-tones ≈#9A7B4B. It reads gold/brass, with painted white almond eyes, black pupils, red lips and a red tilak (Braj Ras close-ups). NOT black, NOT blue.

**attributes:**
- Gold flute at the lips, held to His right (OBSERVED; a removable ornament).
- In several photographs a long thin gold rod runs diagonally down His right side: a flute or staff ornament, identity UNKNOWN.
- Tall jewelled gold crown (OBSERVED; CHANGING).
- Long black hair (wig) falling either side of the face (OBSERVED; CHANGING).
- A peacock feather set on the throne above and behind the pair (OBSERVED 2026).
- Painted eyes, red lips, tilak (OBSERVED; permanence UNKNOWN).

**arrangement:** Krishna on the viewer's left; Radha at His own LEFT (viewer's right). OBSERVED in the 2020 and 2026 photographs, and standard (Entwistle p.78).

**altarSetting:** DAILY (OBSERVED 2026 CC BY-SA; 2020 Braj Ras): a silver repoussé singhasan with a high scalloped back worked with two peacocks and scrolls, corner finials and paw feet, and red gold-embroidered bolsters either side. Behind stands a small silver openwork chhatra with hanging drops on a staff. One marigold garland encircles both Deities, and the pink poshak is spread fan-wise over the seat. The throne stands on a two-tier table. The upper cover is multicoloured paisley embroidery with small silver bowls, a brass bowl and incense stands; the lower cover is gold embroidery with a row of marigold-filled bowls. Behind the table is a grey marble seat whose arm-supports are scaled, curling creatures. Around it: white marble columns either side, a dark carved door, glass-shaded wall lamps and a crystal chandelier. Devotees look in through a doorway behind a wooden railing (Braj Ras). The trust gives the hall as 40 × 35 ft under a 40-ft dome with nine chandeliers. BASANTI KAMRA (twice a year; trust site): an all-yellow room with a 'gold plated' singhasan, doors and mirrors 'some as big as ten feet high', fifteen Belgian glass chandeliers and marble fountains. The 2016 CC BY-SA photograph shows a tall gilt frame set with mirror and coloured glass, twin gilt serpents on top, and two gilt standing female figures on brackets either side.

**fixedVsChanging:** FIXED: two small gold-toned figures, Radha at Krishna's left, on the silver throne in the main marble hall for daily darshan. The same throne appears in the 2020 Braj Ras and 2026 Commons photographs. CHANGING: poshak (pink with gold embroidery in 2020 and 2026; yellow 'vasanti' dress on Vasant Panchami per Amar Ujala 2020 and Punjab Kesari), crowns, jewellery, garlands, flute/staff. SEAT CHANGES: (1) the gold-plated singhasan in the Basanti Kamra, only on Vasant Panchami — the anniversary of the installation on Magh 5, 1868 (Braj Ras biography; Amar Ujala 2020) — and on Shravan Shukla 13–14 (Amar Ujala 2020; Bhaskar Digital 2026); (2) a silver hindola in the garden at Jhulan (Amar Ujala caption); (3) a phool bangla in summer (Amar Ujala caption). Hours (Braj Ras): summer mangala 7:30 am and shayan 7:30 pm; winter 8:30 am and 6:30 pm.

### Sri Shyamsundar

**temple:** Shri Radha Shyamsundar Mandir, Mohalla Seva Kunj (near Loi Bazaar), Vrindavan. This is Shyamananda Prabhu's temple, served by the Shyamanandi lineage of Gopiballavpur. It is NOT ISKCON's Radha-Shyamasundara.

**name:** Sri Shyamsundar: the large central Deity, carved for and installed by Baladeva Vidyabhushana on Vasanta Panchami 1719 (temple booklet; Gaudiya Treasures of Bengal 2023)

**height:** UNKNOWN. SOURCED relative: 'tallest' of the three sets (Tirthayatra), made because the originals 'were too small in size' to dress (temple booklet). MEASURED relative (one photograph): His Radharani's crown reaches about His face level, i.e. she is ≈0.8 of His height.

**material:** SOURCED: 'a block of stone from Nilagiri, Odisha' (the temple's own booklet, reproduced by bkdemian.blogspot 2016); 'a large stone block from Nilgiri, near his birthplace village of Athantar in Odisha' (Gaudiya Treasures of Bengal 2023); 'a special nila shila from Odisha' (Tirthayatra). The petrology beyond 'nila shila' is UNKNOWN.

**murtiColour:** UNKNOWN from licensed photographs; photography is 'strictly prohibited' in this temple (VrajVrindavan). Indicative: near-black (≈#080704 in shadow; copyrighted Braj Ras close-up, measurement only), with bright applied gold/yellow patterning.

**attributes:**
- Flute held to His right, right hand raised (OBSERVED once; removable).
- Very large white eyes with black pupils and red rims (OBSERVED; permanence UNKNOWN).
- Gold/yellow patterning on the cheeks and forehead, a gold Vaishnava tilak and a gold nose ornament (OBSERVED; very likely applied shringar).
- Large fan-shaped ear ornaments (OBSERVED).
- Headwear changes: a red knitted winter cap with a crest in the observed photograph.

**arrangement:** Centre. His Radharani at His own LEFT (viewer's right) — OBSERVED. For the side sets see the Lala and Kunjabihari entries. The best-supported reading puts Lala-Lali at the large Deities' own LEFT (viewer's right) and Radha-Kunjabihari at Their own RIGHT (viewer's left).

**altarSetting:** The central section of one altar carrying three sets, in the covered altar hall behind the five-arched front facing the courtyard (Hare Krishna Movement 2025; Vrindavan Forest). OBSERVED: a large circular red backdrop with flower appliqué and a blue side curtain in winter (Braj Ras). The temple's own website button image (captured 2023) shows a decorated frontage of three arched bays — left, centre, right — consistent with three sets, but it is a 150-px image and low confidence. CHANGING (temple booklet): daily dress; winter knitted woollens (Braj Ras photographs); 35 days of Kartika shringar enacting pastimes; dressed as Sita-Rama on Rama Navami; covered head to foot in sandal paste (chandan) on Akshaya Tritiya, 6–10 pm; flower outfits (phool bangla) every evening from Akshaya Tritiya to Guru Purnima. Album titles on the temple's own website also list snake-charmer and female bangle-seller veshas.

## Uncertain

- Govind Dev: the frame of 'to His left is Lord Caitanya, and to His right is Lord Nityananda' is not stated. The Deity-frame reading is supported by one photograph.
- Govind Dev: whether a Radha stands beside the pratibhu every day. For: Narayana Maharaja, and the 2021 Jhulan photograph. Against: the ISKCON Desire Tree / Holy Dham altar list, and the Braj Ras daily photograph. The Jhulan pair may be the 'small Radha and Krishna'.
- Govind Dev: exactly where the living altar sits, in the old choir or in the 1819–20 temple beyond it. Entwistle (1987) says both that 'the original shrine at Vrindaban houses smaller images of Krishna and accompanying figures' and that the temple 'at the back' is 'now the focus'. VT 2022 puts 'the main alter' adjacent to the Yogamaya entrance, with an inner passage to it.
- Govind Dev: what became of the c.1854 brick sacrarium and its 'Giridhari' image is not documented. It is equally undocumented whether Growse's Giridhari is today's pratibhu, the Giridhari/Govardhana-shila, or something else.
- Govind Dev: the pratibhu's date is given as 1748 (Narayana Maharaja, Sri Vraja-mandala Parikrama) and as 'around the time of the reign of ... Mohammad Shah (1726-1748)' (VT 2021).
- Govind Dev: the pratibhu's material and height; the material and arm poses of Chaitanya and Nityananda; the Laddu Gopal, attested by one source only (Satyaraja Dasa).
- Govind Dev: Yogamaya. Tradition says she is 'one of Rupa Goswami's deities', hidden underground from the Mughals (Satyaraja Dasa; VT 2022). Entwistle says 'a small marble relief of Durga has been placed' in the cellar, implying a later placement.
- Govind Dev: Narayana Maharaja says Kashishvara's Gaura-Govinda 'is nearby, in the southern part of the Govindaji Temple'. Holy Dham and VT 2021 place it at Govinda's right in Jaipur. It is not modelled here.
- Govind Dev: the chhatri 'behind the old Govinda Dev temple, where His footprint is worshipped' (VT 2021 caption) is probably Rani Bhim's 1636 chhatri. Growse re-erected it on the sacrarium platform, and Entwistle says it was 'set up against the north wall' (his compass is turned 90° from Growse's). This partly answers govind-dev.md's 'present status unverified', but the caption does not name the chhatri, and the footprint's form is UNKNOWN.
- Shahji: the pair's material. Their heights are ESTIMATED only. The long diagonal gold rod may be a flute or a staff.
- Shahji: the Basanti Kamra schedule. Twice a year per Amar Ujala 2020 (Vasant Panchami for two days, plus Shravan Shukla 13–14) and Bhaskar Digital 2026; 'once a year' per India TV 2026. Punjab Kesari's 'large throne on Shayani Ekadashi in Sawan' has a confused calendar.
- Shahji: the Lucknow origin of the Deity rests on one source (Braj Ras biography): sent to Vrindavan with Lalit Kishori's guru, Radha Govind Goswami, and installed at Lalit Nikunj on Magh 5, 1868. Building dates conflict: 1860–68 (trust site, Braj Ras; Entwistle gives 1868); 1876 (aggregators); 1835 (VRI).
- Radha Shyamsundar: the left/right frame of the side sets. The best-supported reading is given above; confirm it with a permitted photograph or a visit before building. Also unknown: the materials, heights and postures of all six murtis; whether the large Shyamsundar's eyes and facial decoration are permanent; and which Braj Ras album photograph shows which set.
- Radha Shyamsundar: the 'Vrinda Devi small temple' in the courtyard (VrajVrindavan) is of unknown form (tulasi shrine or image). It is not treated as a murti here.
- Radha Shyamsundar: brajdham000 reports that some historians say Shyamananda brought the small Shyamsundar from South India, contrary to the temple's tradition. This does not affect the form; depict neither story.
- General: every hex value taken from a copyrighted photograph is indicative only (uncalibrated warm lamp light). Only the Shahji values come from a freely licensed photograph.
