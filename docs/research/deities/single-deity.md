# Deity forms: Banke Bihari, Radha Raman, Radha Vallabh

Filed from workflow run wf_7ae47b98-f89, paired by journal key (survey and checker results joined on the `key` of their `started` events, never by completion order).

**Status:** UNVERIFIED: its checker did not run (org spend limit, 2026-09-30). Build nothing from a claim here that the survey itself marks ESTIMATED or UNKNOWN.

These are living, worshipped murtis. The survey's own sensitivity notes come first and bind everything below them.

## Sensitivity

- These are sevya, living and served, deities in the hereditary care of Goswami families. Both Radha Vallabh ('photography prohibited inside') and Banke Bihari (no phones or electronic gadgets) forbid photography in the sanctum. A 3D likeness is itself a devotional image. Ask the sevayats before shipping one; the project's own notes say temples are often glad to help.
- Never add a Radha figure. At all three temples her presence is deliberately non-figurative. The Radhavallabh sect holds she is felt, not looked at. At Radha Raman and Banke Bihari the deity is himself Radha and Krishna in one, and she is served as a seat.
- Keep Banke Bihari's jhanki. The curtain exists because his gaze is held to be too overpowering to be borne without a break (bihariji.org; Entwistle p.402). Keep the 2-3 minute cadence (AajTak 2026), and do not offer a free-zoom, staring or photo-mode camera on his face.
- Banke Bihari's feet are shown to the public one morning a year, on Akshaya Tritiya, and his near-unclothed sarvang darshan one evening a year. Never make either a toggle, an unlock or a reward. If shown at all, show them only on the real calendar day.
- Radha Raman's abhisheka and Banke Bihari's sarvang darshan are liturgical states. Do not model the undressed murti or the bathing as spectacle, and do not sculpt anatomy that the dress normally hides.
- Radha Raman's back is for initiated priests only (Case p.76). Place the model hard against its throne-back and stop the camera orbiting behind any of the three altars.
- No interaction with the murtis: no touching, picking up, collision play or physics, and no player entry into the garbhagriha. Among the Radharaman Goswamis, initiation involves touching the deity's feet (Case p.77). The project already rules out gamified devotion.
- At night the doors close and the deity is put to bed out of sight (Case p.91). Never show the deity asleep, or his bed.
- Keep the three traditions apart: Haridasi at Banke Bihari, Radhavallabhi at Radha Vallabh, Gaudiya at Radha Raman. Chaitanya's relics, shaligrams and Gaudiya iconography belong only at Radha Raman.
- Low-poly caution, which the project reached independently: a modelled face can read as a doll. Let the dressed silhouette, crown, throne, umbrellas and applied eyes carry the likeness, and keep each face a smooth, glossy black form.
- Offerings go to the hundi, never into the murti's hands in-game. The only thing that moves is the seva: priests, lamps, the curtain.

## Do not model

- Any FIGURE of Radha at any of the three temples. All three worship her without a figure: at Radha Raman a draped form with a jewelled crown (Case p.84); at Radha Vallabh a golden crown, chhatra and red velvet gaddi (temple portals); and at BANKE BIHARI TOO, her gaddi at his left (Bharatkosh/Hindi Wikipedia; Case p.84; Entwistle p.78; bihariji.org 'along with Priyaji'). CORRECTION to the project: 'Banke Bihari is the united form' is true but incomplete. Add the gaddi, and do not invent its crown.
- Radha on the viewer's LEFT. At all three temples her seat is on the DEITY's left, which is the viewer's right.
- Blue, white, bronze or marble skin. All three murtis are black: Radharaman is black stone born from a shaligram; Bihari ji is 'one single black charming idol' (temple); Radhavallabh Lal is black in every photograph.
- A half-Radha, half-Krishna body (an ardhanarishvara-style split) for Banke Bihari. The union is theological. Any dual character appears only in the dressing: two crown ornaments and garments cut differently on each side (AajTak 2025).
- A flute in Banke Bihari's hands or at his lips on an ordinary day. That happens only on the evening of Sharad Purnima; on other days the flute lies at the throne with a small water container (bihariji.org).
- Banke Bihari's feet or lower legs. They are always covered, except the morning of Akshaya Tritiya.
- A smooth Shrinathji-type mukut on Banke Bihari on an ordinary day. His usual crown is the jagged 'tipara' (bihariji.org).
- Bells or a conch at Banke Bihari (Entwistle p.402). Radha Raman DOES use bells, gongs and conch (Case pp.83-94), so do not generalise the no-bell rule to it.
- Radha Raman at anything but miniature scale. He is 28.3 cm (Case p.73). The sanctum is filled by the silver lion-foot throne, the peacocks, the lamps and the umbrellas, not by the figure.
- A prominent flute as Radha Raman's main attribute. The documented constant is the cowherd's staff in his right hand (Case p.73); the flute is disputed.
- Radha Raman's back, or shaligram or discus marks on it. Only initiated priests see his back (Case p.76). The project's note that the markings are 'shown at his appearance-day abhisheka' is not supported by Case.
- Carved white eyes on Radha Raman. They are white petal shapes with black irises, fixed on daily with beeswax (Case p.89). Model them as applied pieces.
- The citation 'not more than twelve inches (Entwistle p.412)'. That phrase is not in Entwistle; cite Case p.73 for 11⅛ in.
- An invented lower body, tribhanga, material or height for Radhavallabh Lal. None is documented, so keep him inside the dress.
- Radhavallabh Lal without his flute or braid; both appear in every inspected shringar. Do not put the braid on his right, and do not make the braid a separate Radha figure.
- Any murti in the OLD Radha Vallabh temple (Hit Mandir, ASI N-UP-A198). The deity has stood in the new temple next door since his return from Kaman (Entwistle p.402).
- Bihari ji in Nidhivan. His prakatya sthal there holds only his symbolic footprints, bathed on Bihar Panchami (bihariji.org).
- Seasonal states made permanent. Each belongs to its own date or season: the Phool Bangla (Chaitra Shukla 11 to Hariyali Amavasya, evenings, in the jagmohan); the one-day gold-and-silver hindola with its life-size sakhi figures; the Sharad Purnima white dress, mukut and flute; the Akshaya Tritiya sandal paste and sarvang darshan; Radha Vallabh's Diwali-night glass 'Hatthari'; and Banke Bihari's winter velvet-lined 'Hutery' as against its slim-pillared summer silver throne.
- Gaudiya relics on the other two altars. Chaitanya's asana and Gopal Bhatta's shaligrams belong only at Radha Raman, and even there they are mostly out of sight.
- These Commons files as the deity. Category:Banke_Bihari_Temple holds about 403 files by Guptaele; I sampled most of them on contact sheets. What I saw was almost all ISKCON Krishna-Balaram (altars, Prabhupada samadhi, murals), plus a 2011-04-18 set showing a fair Radha-Krishna pair under a wooden canopy, and none showed Bihari ji. File:Banke_Bihari_Vrindavan.jpg (Kridha20) has a signboard reading 'ठाकुर श्री राधास्नेह बिहारी': it is the neighbouring Sneh Bihari temple. File:Radhavallabhlalj.jpg carries a Braj Ras watermark, so its CC BY-SA claim is doubtful. The Shahji 'Chhote Radha Raman' files are not Radha Raman.
- Any photograph from the temple sites, Braj Ras or the news as a texture or tracing source. All are all-rights-reserved: inspect and measure only.
- A statue of Swami Haridas on Banke Bihari's altar. The form of the representation that goes with Bihari ji to the swing is undocumented.
- Animated murtis: no blinking, no flute-playing, no swaying. Motion belongs only to the seva: curtains, lamps, priests, garlands.

## The murtis, one by one

### Thakur Shri Banke Bihari ji Maharaj ('Bihari ji'; worshipped by Swami Haridas as Kunj Bihari). Held to be Shyama-Shyam, 

**temple:** Shri Banke Bihari Mandir, Bihari Pura, Vrindavan

**name:** Thakur Shri Banke Bihari ji Maharaj ('Bihari ji'; worshipped by Swami Haridas as Kunj Bihari). Held to be Shyama-Shyam, Radha and Krishna, united in ONE black image

**posture:** Standing, tribhanga. SOURCED: Entwistle 1987 pp.401-2 says his disciples called the image 'bake (crooked), referring to the tribhangi posture in which he stands'. Only the dressed upper body is ever seen. The lower body and feet are always under the dress (SOURCED: bihariji.org Akshaya Tritiya page; AajTak 19 Dec 2025). Hand and arm positions on ordinary days: UNKNOWN. No source describes them, and he holds no flute except on Sharad Purnima (SOURCED: bihariji.org), so do not raise empty hands into a flute pose on the strength of the posture name. Head tilt and the direction of the bends: UNKNOWN.

**height:** UNKNOWN. No temple, scholarly or Growse source gives a size; Growse 1883 describes only the building (checked by full-text search). ESTIMATED, low confidence: from a copyrighted Braj Ras photo of the summer Phool Bangla in the jagmohan, scaled against a Goswami standing beside the pavilion (perspective not corrected): face from forehead to chin about 12-20 cm; dressed silhouette from crown tip to the lowest visible hem at least about 0.9-1.1 m. That makes him several times larger than Radha Raman (28 cm). An aggregator's '2.5 ft' (prayagsamagam.com) is unsourced. Use these figures only for blocking out the scene, and ask the temple for the real size.

**material:** SOURCED (temple, bihariji.org MandirHistory): 'the couple turned itself into one single black charming idol, the same one that you see in the temple today', granted to Swami Haridas. It is held to be self-manifested, not sculpted. Stone type: UNKNOWN. The web claims of 'black stone', 'black marble' or 'kasauti' are unsourced.

**murtiColour:** UNKNOWN as a hex. I found no freely licensed close photograph of Bihari ji: phones and electronic gadgets are barred inside (bihariji.org Do's & Don'ts), and every 'Banke Bihari' deity file on Commons I checked is of another temple. SOURCED: black ('black charming idol'; 'the dark Lord'). Inspected but NOT licensed (Braj Ras phone photos): the face reads near-black and satin, median about #322f2b-#3c3933 under temple light. Do not ship that value as MEASURED.

**attributes:**
- Usual headdress is the 'tipara' crown, 'with lots of designs and cuts, devoid of a smooth outline' (SOURCED: bihariji.org Sharad Poornima page). Peacock-feather work on it is visible in inspected photos.
- Sharad Purnima only: 'a crown similar to that of Shri Srinathji', smooth outline (SOURCED: bihariji.org). The news calls it a 'mor mukut' (ETV Bharat 2022; Amar Ujala 28 Oct 2023).
- Flute (murali): on ordinary days it is NOT in his hands. 'On all other days the flute is just kept at the throne on the right side along with the small water container.' Only on the evening of Sharad Purnima is it 'in the hands and on the lips' (SOURCED: bihariji.org; Entwistle p.402).
- Eyes: large white eye-shapes with black pupils on the black face (OBSERVED in inspected copyright photos and in the temple site's own banner art). Whether they are applied, as at Radha Raman, or part of the stone: UNKNOWN.
- Kumkum shringar is applied to the face during the dressing (AajTak 19 Dec 2025, secondary).
- Dual dressing: 'two kinds of crown' ornament, a peacock feather on one side and a chudamani (crest jewel) on the other, and garments flowing on one side and flared on the other (AajTak 19 Dec 2025, secondary; which side is which: UNKNOWN). Braj Ras also shows him on occasion in Radharani's full shringar (pink lehenga, braid).
- Jewellery 'like crown and necklaces' is put on every morning at shringar (SOURCED: bihariji.org). Bud and flower garlands are visible in photos.
- Dress (poshak): a jama with frills below the waist, a patuka over the shoulders, and a belt (bihariji.org Deepavali page, quoting a devotional verse). Heavy velvet in winter (SOURCED: bihariji.org).
- Feet: always covered by the clothes, and alta (red lac) is applied to them (AajTak 2025, secondary).
- No bell and no conch is ever sounded before him (SOURCED: Entwistle p.402).

**arrangement:** Bihari ji in the centre. Radha's (Priya ji's) gaddi is on HIS LEFT, the viewer's right (SOURCED: Bharatkosh and Hindi Wikipedia, 'बांके बिहारी जी के बाँयी ओर राधा जी की गद्दी विराजमान है'). The flute and a small water container lie 'at the throne on the right side' (bihariji.org); whose right is not stated: UNKNOWN. A representation of Swami Haridas goes with him to the swing (bihariji.org). Its form and its place on the altar: UNKNOWN.

**altarSetting:** The garbhagriha sits behind a silver-clad front of three cusped arches, with the deity in the central arch (Commons 'Banke-Bihari-Mandir-Vrindavan.jpg', CC BY-SA 4.0; project docs/research/banke-bihari.md). THRONE (SOURCED: bihariji.org Deepavali page): from Holi to Deepavali, 'a simple and light weight throne having slim pillars and frame of silver', with light canopy cloth, frills and hangings. From Deepavali to Holi, the 'Hutery': 'heavy rounded and intricately carved silver pillars' carrying 'an equally heavy and decorated silver frame for roof', 'lined with heavy velvet'. In the garbhagriha he sits on a silver asana (thesootr and mpbreakingnews, Feb 2026). CURTAIN: 'a heavy velvet curtain' that the Goswamis draw in front of him every two to three minutes and pull back after a few moments (AajTak 4 Sep 2026). Entwistle p.402: it is 'repeatedly pulled in front of it and held closed for a few seconds'. Colour: UNKNOWN. JAGMOHAN, 'the elevated hall in front of sanctum sanctorum' (bihariji.org): he is traditionally seated here for the Phool Bangla evenings, Sharad Purnima, Rangbhari Ekadashi to Holi, Akshaya Tritiya and Janmashtami. On 18-19 Feb 2026 the Supreme Court-appointed High-Powered Committee moved him to the jagmohan platform for daily darshan. Goswamis and devotees protested (the garbhagriha was found locked), the committee partly backed down and called an emergency meeting, and the dispute continued (India TV, ETV Bharat, bansalnews 19 Feb 2026; thesootr 20 Feb 2026). Build BOTH positions and treat which one is current as UNKNOWN. The Hariyali Teej hindola stands on a platform outside the jagmohan.

**fixedVsChanging:** FIXED: one black standing image; no flute except on Sharad Purnima; feet hidden except on Akshaya Tritiya; Radha's gaddi at his left; the jhanki curtain. CHANGING (SOURCED from bihariji.org unless noted): dress and jewellery change daily, with three sevas a day (shringar, rajbhog, shayan) and no mangala aarti except at Janmashtami. SUMMER: from Kamada Ekadashi (Chaitra Shukla 11) to Hariyali Amavasya he sits in the evenings in a Phool Bangla built in the jagmohan. The pavilion is woven from strings of flowers on nailed wooden frames in jali patterns. His ornaments that season are cut banyan leaves set with buds of bela, tagar, motia, mogra and kaner, and his dresses are fine black malmal studded with mogra (white) and kaner (red and yellow) buds. ETV Bharat 2023 gives the season as 108 days. AKSHAYA TRITIYA: charan darshan in the morning, the one day his feet are shown. In the evening, sarvang darshan: 'wearing only a dhoti', coated in sandal paste, with a sphere of sandal paste at his feet. HARIYALI TEEJ: a green poshak; 'along with Priyaji and Swami Haridasji' he moves to the gold-and-silver hindola in the courtyard. The swing was made by Banaras artisans 1942-47, weighs about 5 t in about 180 parts, used about 10,000 tola of gold and 1 lakh tola of silver, and carries dancing peacocks and life-size sakhi figures of Lalita, Vishakha, Chitralekha and Rangdevi (pardaphash 2026; The Week/PTI 27 Jul 2025). SHARAD PURNIMA: all-white zari dress, the Shrinathji-type mukut, and the flute in his hands and at his lips. He sits on a silver 'hut' throne in a jagmohan dressed as a forest, and darshan lasts until the moonlight reaches his feet. DEEPAVALI: the throne changes. BIHAR PANCHAMI (Margashirsha Shukla 5), his appearance day: the abhishek is of his symbolic footprints at the prakatya sthal in Nidhivan, not of the image. JANMASHTAMI: midnight abhishek and the only mangala aarti of the year (Amar Ujala 5 Sep 2026).

### Shri Radharaman Lal ju (Radharamana), the deity of Gopal Bhatta Goswami. It manifested on Vaishakh Purnima 1542 (Samvat 

**temple:** Shri Radharaman Mandir, Radharaman Ghera, Vrindavan

**name:** Shri Radharaman Lal ju (Radharamana), the deity of Gopal Bhatta Goswami. It manifested on Vaishakh Purnima 1542 (Samvat 1599 per the temple's own board); the 484th appearance day was 1 May 2026

**posture:** Standing tribhanga. SOURCED from Case 2000, pp.75-76 (the appearance account): 'a small black figure, his head tipped slightly to the left, his torso leaning slightly to the right from a slender waist, his knees gracefully bent and his right foot crossed over his left'. Case p.73: he 'stands in a relaxed pose with his hands raised to hold a flute to his lips — although, in fact, those hands are never seen to hold a flute'. Entwistle p.78 gives the generic flute-player tribhanga: right calf crossed in front of the left shin, ball of the right foot on the ground, hands raised beside the right shoulder. OBSERVED in a copyright Braj Ras 'charan darshan' photo: slender, glossy black legs with one crossed over the other, and gold bell anklets. He faces EAST (Case pp.81-82).

**height:** SOURCED: 'only eleven and one-eighth inches high', about 28.3 cm (Case 2000 p.73). Other sources: '12 angul', about 23 cm (aaradhika.com); 'about ten inches' (tirthayatra.org); 'about twelve inches' (vrindavanforest.com). Entwistle p.412 says only 'the smallest of the main deities of Vrindaban'. CORRECTION: the project cites 'not more than twelve inches' to Entwistle p.412, but a full-text search finds that phrase in neither Entwistle, Case nor Growse. Cite Case and use 28.3 cm.

**material:** SOURCED: he manifested from one of Gopal Bhatta's shaligram shilas, the Damodara shila (Case pp.73-76; Entwistle p.412), and is 'the black stone image of Radharamana' (Case p.72). The tradition's argument that he was not carved: 'if a cutting tool is applied to a salagrama it will simply flake away' (Case p.76).

**murtiColour:** MEASURED from Commons 'Radha Raman Temple, Vrindavan.jpg' (Barry Silver, 2009, CC BY 2.0). The face is only about 25 px across, shot on a compact camera under warm light: the darkest 15% of face pixels have a median of #32231c, the darkest 40% #514038. Read this as near-black. SOURCED: 'his jet-black face' (Case p.89); the lip is sometimes 'made shiny with oil' (Case p.89). Model it as near-black (about #1a1614) with a polished, low-roughness finish.

**attributes:**
- A cowherd's staff (lakuti) in the RIGHT hand: 'Radharamana's right hand always holds a cowherd's staff, by which he is seen to support himself' (SOURCED: Case p.73). OBSERVED as a thin gold staff slanting down toward his right (the viewer's left) in the CC BY 2.0 photo and in copyright Braj Ras photos.
- Flute: the sources CONFLICT. Case (2000) says the hands 'are never seen to hold a flute'. Popular and Hindi sources (Hindi Wikipedia, aaradhika.com, tirthayatra.org) say he holds or plays one, and a thin gold rod near the hands shows in one copyright photo. Model the hands raised in the flute pose, but add no prominent flute until the temple confirms.
- Headdress: 'a turban or crown, always with a peacock feather' (SOURCED: Case pp.87-88).
- Eyes: 'white elongated petal shapes that stand out against his jet-black face', with black irises, 'fixed to his form with softened beeswax' as the last step of dressing (SOURCED: Case p.89). They are applied ornaments; model them as separate shapes, not carved into the stone.
- 'Sometimes two small white teeth can be seen in the center of his smile' (Case p.89; tirthayatra.org).
- Jewellery: necklaces, earrings, bracelets and anklets, belts and girdles (SOURCED: Case p.88). Gold bell anklets are visible in photos.
- Back: it bears the 'gold-flecked imprint' of the shaligram (Case p.76), and discus markings on the back and feet (Entwistle p.412). Only an initiated priest may see his back (Case p.76), so it is never shown.

**arrangement:** Radharaman in the centre. Radha's seat is on HIS LEFT, the viewer's right (SOURCED: Case p.84; tirthayatra.org; ISKCON Desire Tree). On HIS RIGHT, the viewer's left, 'mostly out of sight to those standing in the temple courtyard', is a golden umbrella over a stool holding Gopal Bhatta's remaining shaligrams. Behind that is a small throne holding the stool that Chaitanya gave Gopal Bhatta (SOURCED: Case p.84). All four are offered arati in turn. The staff slopes down toward his right.

**altarSetting:** SOURCED (Case p.83): 'He stands on a small embossed golden platform, which is, in turn, placed on an embossed silver throne with lion feet.' Below the throne are silver figures given by devotees ('cows, swans, pots'), arranged roughly symmetrically, and 'Over Radharamana's head a small golden umbrella is balanced.' OBSERVED in the CC BY 2.0 photo (2009): a scrolled silver throne-back. ESTIMATED, using his 28.3 cm as the scale: the lion-foot embossed silver platform is about 1.0-1.2 m wide; the umbrella over him about 12 cm across, on a rod; a silver peacock of about 45 cm at each end. Also visible: tall silver lamp-stands in front, silver pots and a bowl on the platform, a chequered black-and-white marble floor, and a red patterned cloth lining the back of the sanctum. The sanctum is behind three cusped arches of unpainted sandstone and an embossed silver double-leaved door, on a marble platform about 4½ ft high (Case pp.81-82; already in research/radha-raman.md). Curtains in front of the deity open for each darshan (Case p.83). Bells and gongs ARE used here, unlike Banke Bihari.

**fixedVsChanging:** FIXED: the small black standing form; the staff in the right hand; a peacock feather on the headdress; a small golden umbrella over his head; Radha's seat at his left. CHANGING (SOURCED: Case pp.83-93): a new costume every day, often but not necessarily in the colour of the day. Sunday red, Monday pink, Tuesday coral, Wednesday green, Thursday yellow, Friday white or multicoloured, Saturday dark blue or black. Each outfit has his costume, a dupatta, a shawl and a drape for the back of the throne, plus a sari and shawl for Radha in the same fabric. The eyes are put on fresh each day. The basic costume stays all day while the details change, and flower garlands are added in the evening. At mangala and shayan he wears night clothes; in winter, 'a little flat-topped cap that covers his ears' and a shawl. On the coldest days coals burn in a brazier in front of the throne. He is carried to inner rooms for his bath and breakfast. FESTIVALS: public abhisheka with 108 substances on his appearance day (Vaishakh Purnima) and at Janmashtami (Case p.96; Entwistle p.412). A framed piece of Chaitanya's loincloth is shown only on those and a very few other days (Case p.84). During special sevas, bangalas are built on the platform: strung flowers in summer, dried fruit or paper appliqué in winter (Case p.92). Braj Ras photos show him in yellow at Holi and at other times in green, orange and red.

### Gopal Bhatta Goswami's remaining shaligram shilas, with Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's asana (seat) on a small throne behind the

**temple:** Shri Radharaman Mandir, Vrindavan

**name:** Gopal Bhatta Goswami's remaining shaligram shilas, with Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's asana (seat) on a small throne behind them. Mostly OUT OF SIGHT

**posture:** Stones on a stool; not a human form. The asana is a relic seat, not a figure.

**height:** UNKNOWN. Case does not give their number or size ('the remaining salagramas'). The tradition speaks of twelve shilas, one of which became Radharaman (tirthayatra.org).

**material:** Shaligram shilas: 'river-washed, rounded black stones containing ammonites' from the Kali Gandaki at Damodarakunda (SOURCED: Case pp.73-75). The asana: 'a wooden seat said to have been presented to him by Chaitanya' (Entwistle p.412).

**murtiColour:** UNKNOWN (black, by Case's general description of shaligrams).

**attributes:**
- A stool under a golden umbrella (SOURCED: Case p.84).
- Behind it, a small throne holding Chaitanya's stool (asana) (SOURCED: Case p.84; Entwistle p.412).
- A framed fragment of Chaitanya's loincloth, 'framed in silver', is shown in public only on the appearance day, at Janmashtami and on a very few other days (SOURCED: Case p.84). Entwistle p.412 also records a shawl of Gopal Bhatta kept in the temple.

**arrangement:** Radharaman's RIGHT, the viewer's left. At most, model a covered stool with a small golden umbrella. Do not model individual stones.

**altarSetting:** In the inner sanctum at Radharaman's right, largely hidden from the courtyard.

**fixedVsChanging:** Fixed. The loincloth relic comes out only on those days.

### Shri Radhavallabh Lal ju ('Thakur ji', 'Shri ji'), worshipped as the consort of Radha and held to be the yugal (united) 

**temple:** Shri Radhavallabh Mandir, Vrindavan: the living 'new' temple in Radhavallabh Ghera, next to the old ASI-protected temple (Hit Mandir)

**name:** Shri Radhavallabh Lal ju ('Thakur ji', 'Shri ji'), worshipped as the consort of Radha and held to be the yugal (united) form

**posture:** Standing, with both hands raised to a flute at his lips: OBSERVED in the temple's own shringar photographs (2025-26) and in a Commons photo its uploader released as CC0 (2023). Tribhanga: UNKNOWN. No source found says so; the lower body and feet are always dressed and never documented. One aggregator (prayagsamagam.com) claims 'standing in tribhanga with a flute at his lips'. It is unsourced and unreliable.

**height:** UNKNOWN. I found no figure in either temple portal, Entwistle, Growse or Hindi sources. ESTIMATED, low confidence, from the CC0 photo scaled against a Goswami's hand at the garland frame: face from forehead to chin at least about 10 cm; top of the head to the throne seat roughly 0.5-0.8 m. The crown rises about 1.8 face-heights above the forehead. The hand is nearer the camera, so these are lower bounds.

**material:** UNKNOWN. TRADITION (SOURCED: temple, radhavallabhmandir.in; Hindi Wikipedia citing popular belief): not made by any sculptor. Lord Shiva gave the image 'from His heart' to an ancestor of Atmadeva, who gave it to Hit Harivansh at Charthawal when Hit Harivansh married his daughters.

**murtiColour:** MEASURED from Commons 'Radhavallabh Lal Ju 0023 Vrindavan dham.jpg' (Beenu2024, 11 Dec 2023, declared CC0 by the uploader). Face base #1e161c to #291f20 (V 11-16%), mid-tones #282025, specular highlights #c7c0b9 to #e4dfd8: a glossy, near-black polished surface. The temple's own photos agree, reading warmer under yellow light at about #23180c-#392517 (copyright; inspect only).

**attributes:**
- A flute at the lips, held diagonally with its lower end toward his right (the viewer's left): OBSERVED in every shringar I inspected (temple gallery 2025-26; the CC0 photo). The temple portals say nothing about it.
- A long black braid (veni) falls over his LEFT shoulder and chest to below the waist. It is bound with ornaments or strings of buds and ends in a black tassel with a gold finial. OBSERVED in every inspected shringar. Read as Radha's hair: 'the deity of Radha Vallabh Ju boasts a long braid cascading from their crown', 'a yugal jodi' (The Gaudiya Treasures of Bengal, 2023). Whether it is part of the image or added daily: UNKNOWN.
- A crown (mukut) with a peacock-feather plume over a turban (pag): OBSERVED. The design and colour change with each shringar.
- Face decoration on some days: gold or silver dot patterns on the forehead and cheeks, and a pearl nose-drop (OBSERVED in the CC0 photo). The eyes show as the dark stone in some photos and as white shapes with black pupils in others, so any applied eyes vary.
- In winter: shawls and velvet hand-coverings (OBSERVED in temple photos).

**arrangement:** Radhavallabh Lal in the centre. Shri Radha's golden crown, chhatra and red velvet gaddi are on HIS LEFT (vamang), the viewer's right. SOURCED: shriradhavallabhlal.com, 'Official Divine Portal' ('ठाकुर जी के वामांग (बाईं ओर) ... स्वर्ण मुकुट, छत्र एवं लाल मखमली पावन गद्दी'); shrimathuraji.com ('केवल वामअंग में मुकुट की सेवा होती है'); and OBSERVED in every inspected photo. His braid falls on the same side, beside her seat. The project's crownSideUndocumented can now be resolved to his left.

**altarSetting:** SOURCED: 'no separate idol of Radha — a crown on throne represents her' (radhavallabhmandir.in), so he stands on a throne (singhasan). OBSERVED (temple photos 2025-26, copyright, inspect only): a cusped niche lined with a cloth canopy and backdrop, yellow in several winter photos and painted forest or garden scenes in others. Printed-cloth bolsters (masnad) behind him. In some shringars a silver repoussé throne-back carries a large silver peacock at his left and a silver finial behind his head; silver caskets and a gilt tray sit in front, inside garland frames. Winter photos show a semi-transparent slatted blind with a red cord hanging in front of the sanctum (purpose and season: UNKNOWN). Sanctum front (Commons, Goutam1962 2022, CC BY-SA 4.0): a raised platform with a white pierced-marble balustrade, cusped arches hung with orange and red cloth, heavy carved panelled doors, red curtains and a steel railing. Photography is prohibited inside (radhavallabhmandir.in).

**fixedVsChanging:** FIXED (OBSERVED): black face, flute at the lips, braid on his left, Radha's crown and gaddi at his left. CHANGING (SOURCED from radhavallabhmandir.in unless noted): shringar is one of the three parts of seva, with raga and bhog. Ashtayam seva runs in eight daily phases with seasonal timings. During the Khichri festival (Paush to Magh) he wears 'costumes and other decorations appropriate for different festivals and incidents in the life of Krishna' (Entwistle p.402). Other seasonal states: 40 days of Holi from Basant Panchami; Gulab Dol, dressed in roses. From Akshaya Tritiya, the Chandan Utsav: 'the entire form is coated with Malaiyagiri sandalwood paste', patterned with jasmine and mogra; how long it lasts is UNKNOWN. Summer brings the Phool Bangla and Nauka Vihar. From Hariyali Teej to Raksha Bandhan he rides swings of sandalwood, flowers, fruit and gold-plated work, 'both the Divine Couple' together. Sharad Utsav: a white shringar. Diwali night: 'Hatthari' darshan 'in a glass palace'. Also Radhashtami (9 days), Hitotsav (11 days) and the Vyahlu (wedding) utsav.

### Shri ji (Radha) at Radhavallabh

**temple:** Shri Radhavallabh Mandir, Vrindavan

**name:** Shri ji (Radha) at Radhavallabh: a golden crown, chhatra and red velvet gaddi. NON-FIGURATIVE, not a murti

**posture:** Not a figure. SOURCED: 'no separate idol of Radha — a crown on throne represents her' (radhavallabhmandir.in TempleStory). Darshan is of 'Shri Radhavallabh Lal (and the crown representing Radha Rani)' (radhavallabhmandir.in WhyHowDarshan). The sect's doctrine is that Radha is supreme and felt rather than looked at; a figure would contradict it.

**height:** MEASURED as a proportion from the CC0 photo: her crown is about 0.75x the height of Radhavallabh Lal's crown, and its top sits at about his waist. The dressed seat below the crown is roughly as tall again. Absolute size: UNKNOWN.

**material:** A gold (swarna) crown and a red velvet gaddi (SOURCED: shriradhavallabhlal.com). The chhatra's material: UNKNOWN.

**murtiColour:** N/A: non-figurative. The gaddi is draped in the day's fabric and garlands (OBSERVED).

**attributes:**
- A small crown with a peacock-feather plume and paisley motif (OBSERVED; the design changes).
- A chhatra, an umbrella or canopy (SOURCED: shriradhavallabhlal.com).
- A red velvet gaddi, usually hidden under the day's drape and garlands (SOURCED and OBSERVED).

**arrangement:** Radhavallabh Lal's LEFT, the viewer's right.

**altarSetting:** On the same throne, beside him at his left, below his shoulder line.

**fixedVsChanging:** Always present at his left and dressed to match him every day. She swings with him in Shravan ('Both the Divine Couple are swinging together', radhavallabhmandir.in).

## Uncertain

- BANKE BIHARI, WHERE HE SITS TODAY: in February 2026 the Supreme Court-appointed High-Powered Committee ordered daily darshan from the jagmohan platform instead of the garbhagriha. The Goswamis protested and the committee partly retreated (19-20 Feb 2026 reports), and in May 2026 the Supreme Court added elected Goswami representatives to the committee. Which arrangement is in force in September 2026 is UNKNOWN. Model both positions.
- BANKE BIHARI, THE MURTI: height, stone type, hand positions on ordinary days, and head tilt are all UNKNOWN. My size estimate (face about 12-20 cm, dressed silhouette at least about 0.9-1.1 m) comes from one copyright photo scaled against a person, with no perspective correction.
- BANKE BIHARI, RADHA'S GADDI: does it carry a coronet or her name? Entwistle says 'her name or a coronet' for the three temples collectively. Its look, size and dressing: UNKNOWN.
- BANKE BIHARI, SWAMI HARIDAS: the temple says 'Along with Priyaji and Swami Haridasji, He moves out of the sanctum sanctorum' to the swing. Whether Swami Haridas is a portrait, a seat or an image, and where it sits on the altar: UNKNOWN.
- BANKE BIHARI, FLUTE: it is 'kept at the throne on the right side'; whose right is not stated. On Sharad Purnima, the temple and Amar Ujala 2023 put it at his lips, while ETV Bharat 2022 says 'at the waist'.
- BANKE BIHARI, CURTAIN: colour UNKNOWN. Timing: every 2-3 minutes, closed for moments (AajTak 2026); 'repeatedly... held closed for a few seconds', with his gaze bearable 'for no more than a couple of minutes' (Entwistle); 'every 15-30 seconds' (vrajvrindavan.com, low reliability).
- BANKE BIHARI, DUAL DRESSING: the two crown ornaments (peacock feather on one side, chudamani on the other) and the asymmetric garments come from one secondary source (AajTak, 19 Dec 2025). Which side is which: UNKNOWN.
- BANKE BIHARI, HISTORY: the image is the same one today (temple). It was worshipped at Nidhivan as Kunj Bihari, moved to its present site 'in the latter part of the eighteenth century' (Entwistle p.402), and into the present building in 1862-64. Oral tradition says it stayed hidden in Vrindavan under Aurangzeb, 'once' concealed in the Yamuna (Entwistle p.185). Popular claims that it went to Bharatpur were not substantiated in any source I found.
- The project's claim that Banke Bihari's 'outfit is not simply repeated identically' was not verified in this pass.
- RADHA RAMAN, FLUTE OR STAFF: Case (2000) says the hands are never seen with a flute and the right hand always holds a staff. Hindi Wikipedia, aaradhika.com and tirthayatra.org say he holds a flute. One copyright photo shows a thin gold rod near the hands as well as the staff. Needs confirmation from the temple.
- RADHA RAMAN, HEIGHT: 11⅛ in (Case) against '12 angul' (about 23 cm, aaradhika.com), 'about ten inches' (tirthayatra.org) and 'about twelve inches' (vrindavanforest.com).
- RADHA RAMAN, RADHA'S SEAT: Case describes a draped form topped by a jewelled crown. Two popular sources describe a Gomati chakra worshipped on a small throne at his left, and one calls the crown silver. Possibly all of these are one object.
- RADHA RAMAN, DID HE EVER LEAVE: Case p.80 ('never had to be removed') and Entwistle p.185 ('seems to have remained in Braj') say no. A 2011 comment on dandavats.com by Deena Bandhu Dasa reports that Padmanabha Goswami told him the deity 'did go to Rajasthan'. Either way it is the same murti; this does not affect the model.
- RADHA RAMAN, AN EXTRA FIGURE: a copyright Braj Ras Holi photo shows a small crowned figure with a metallic, golden-brown face, dressed in the same yellow, at the far left of the throne (his right). Identity, and whether it is always there: UNKNOWN. Do not model it without confirmation.
- RADHA RAMAN, BACK: vrindavanforest.com says 'one can clearly see half of the shalagramashila... still attached to deities back'. Case says only initiated priests may see the back. Treat the back as never shown.
- RADHA RAMAN, OTHER PROJECT CLAIMS: the silver elephant at Dussehra and the silver jhula at Hariyali Teej were not verified in this pass. The body comparison varies by source: the face of Govind Dev and the feet of Madan Mohan, with the chest (dandavats) or waist (aaradhika) of Gopinath. That is theology, not a modelling instruction.
- RADHA VALLABH, THE MURTI: posture, tribhanga, material and height are undocumented. Whether the braid is part of the image or dressed on daily, and whether the white eyes are applied, are UNKNOWN. The purpose and season of the slatted blind in winter photos, and how long the Chandan Utsav coating lasts, are also UNKNOWN.
- RADHA VALLABH, HISTORY DATES: installed in Vrindavan in Samvat 1591, i.e. 1534 CE (shrimathuraji.com; shriradhavallabhlal.com). Removed under Aurangzeb, when oral tradition says seven devotees were killed; at Kaman 1682-1785 (Entwistle p.184), or 'brought back from Kaman in 1784' (Entwistle p.402), or '123 years' at Kaman (Hindi Wikipedia). The image in the new temple is the original, per the sect and Entwistle.
- RADHA VALLABH, TWO 'OFFICIAL' SITES: radhavallabhmandir.in and shriradhavallabhlal.com both claim to be official. They agree on the facts used here.
- RADHA VALLABH, THE OLD TEMPLE: whether any image is kept in the room on the cella plinth at the west end of Hit Mandir is UNKNOWN. A closed shrine door is photographed there, and Growse calls the room a kitchen.
- COMMONS LICENCES: the CC0 claim on Beenu2024's photo and the identity of Kridha20's 'Radha Vallabhlal.jpg' need checking before either is relied on.
- COLOUR SAMPLES: all measured hexes come from JPEGs taken under warm temple light, and the Radha Raman face was about 25 px. Treat each value as 'near-black, glossy', not as a calibrated albedo.
