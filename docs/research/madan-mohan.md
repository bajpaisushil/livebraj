# madan-mohan — exterior survey

*Researched 2026-09-28 by one agent and independently checked by a second whose brief was to refute it. Labels are the researcher's own: MEASURED / SOURCED / DERIVED / ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN.*

## What it is

It is a ruined 16th-century red-sandstone hall-and-sanctum temple standing on a huge artificial mound, and its whole visual identity is an asymmetric pair of octagonal curvilinear towers of deliberately unequal character — a plain, blunt, uncrowned one over the sanctum and a taller, wholly panel-encrusted one with a great fluted lobed crown over a small side chapel — rising above a roofless, part-brick-patched nave and a square pyramid-roofed gateway.

## The silhouette

WHAT SURVIVES (SOURCED Growse 1883 pp.251–252, CONFIRMED by photographs 1789–2026):

Three masses stand on the mound today, reading left-to-right in most approach views:

1. SANCTUM TOWER ("sacrarium") — SURVIVES, complete. Growse, p.251: "That surmounting the sacra[r]ium is a plain octagon of curvilinear outline tapering towards the summit." In every photograph (Daniell aquatint 1789; Commons MM photos 2006, 2013, 2023, 2026) it is a smooth egg/bullet-shaped octagonal mass of plain coursed ashlar, its only relief a flat pilaster-rib on each of the eight angles, plus one small high opening. **It is crowned by NOTHING.** It ends in a blunt, slightly flattened rounded cap — no amalaka, no kalasha, no finial, no metal, no flag. Its blunt top is visible from outside from every direction.

2. CHAPEL TOWER — SURVIVES, complete, and is the tallest thing on the hill. Growse p.251: "Attached to its south side is a tower-crowned chapel of similar character, but much more highly enriched, the whole of its exterior surface being covered with sculptured panels; its proportions are also much more elegant." Same octagonal curvilinear profile but every face is a grid of sunk square panels, the eight angles carry engaged bead-string colonnettes, and horizontal bands of pendant bud-corbels divide it into ~4 registers. It IS crowned: a narrow recessed neck, then a large fluted/gadrooned amalaka-like ring of roughly 18–22 fat lobes that overhangs the neck like a mushroom cap, then a small squat cap above. This crown is the single most recognisable feature of the building — and it sits on the SIDE CHAPEL, not the sanctum. Growse independently confirms its identity: the chapel has "its single door, which is at the east end" with a raised Sanskrit inscription over it, and the panelled tower in the 2004 and 2006 photographs is precisely the one with a single doorway and an arched inscription-niche above it at its base.

3. GATEWAY — SURVIVES. Growse p.251: "The court-yard is entered, after the ascent of a flight of steps, through a massive square gateway with a pyramidal tower, which groups very effectively with the two towers of the temple." Photographed 2026 (Commons "Radha Madan Mohan Temple (70353)"): plain flat ashlar wall with a small barred window, a heavy cornice of carved pendant brackets, then a stepped pyramidal (phamsana) superstructure of ~7–8 receding courses ending in a short ridge.

WHAT IS LOST:
- The NAVE ROOF. Growse p.251: "Its total height would seem to have been only about 22 feet, but its vaulted roof has entirely disappeared." Model the nave as a roofless walled shell.
- The CHOIR TOWER. Growse p.251: "the upper part of the choir tower has also been destroyed." Note Growse's own phrase two sentences later — "the two towers of the temple" — i.e. by 1880 only two towers stood. Do not add a third.
- The original deity, removed to Jaipur and thence to Karauli (Growse p.252).

THE ROOFLINE, PLAINLY: a long low roofless rectangle (nave) running east–west; a low stump where the choir tower was; then the plain blunt bare sanctum tower; then, offset to its south, the taller panelled tower with the big fluted crown; and detached to the south across the courtyard, the pyramid-capped gateway. Nothing on the skyline is gold, bulbous, onion-shaped, spiky or flagged.

STATE IN 2026: both towers are wrapped in bamboo/steel scaffolding for ASI conservation (Commons photo 19 April 2026). ASI's Science Branch completed a ₹22 lakh chemical cleaning of the exterior "from the shikhara down to the base" in 2025–26, removing soot and biofilm — so the stone is markedly redder now than in photographs before ~2024.

## The elevation

Growse's plate "Temple of Madan Mohan at Brinda-ban" (facing p.252, scale 50 ft = 1 inch) gives the plan; the text gives the vertical. The plate has NO north arrow — the text fixes orientation: sanctum at the WEST, door at the EAST, chapel on the SOUTH flank. The plate is therefore drawn with south at top and west at right.

SEQUENCE ALONG THE AXIS (east → west): east end-wall with square door → NAVE (57 ft internal) → CHOIR (20 ft square) → SANCTUM (20 ft square) → and, branching south from the sanctum through a short ~3.5 m corridor, the CHAPEL.

NAVE, side elevation (SOURCED + DERIVED from the plate):
- "The nave has three openings on either side and a square door at the east end" (p.251).
- Measured off the plate at the scale fixed by Growse's own 57 ft: per side, east solid stub ≈3.6 m, opening ≈1.2 m, pier ≈1.7 m, opening ≈1.8 m, pier ≈1.9 m, opening ≈1.4 m, then a long solid return of ≈5.7 m up to the choir.
- OPENING:SOLID on each nave flank ≈ 25% void : 75% solid, i.e. about 1:3. The three openings are clustered in the eastern ~40% of the flank; the western ~60% is blank wall. This asymmetry is on the plate and is easy to miss.
- Walls ≈1.95–2.10 m thick, so the openings are deep tunnel-like recesses, not windows.
- Nave height only ~22 ft (6.7 m) — a LOW, LONG, THICK-WALLED box, not a lofty hall.

ARCH GRAMMAR (this is the Mathura eclectic idiom Growse describes at Gobind Deva, p.242: "the arches are decorative only, not constructural"):
- Real openings are square-headed / flat-lintelled on straight jambs.
- Above them sit DECORATIVE pointed or cusped (trefoil/ogee) arched niches cut as relief panels. Photographs of the chapel base (2004, 2006) show exactly this: a flat-lintelled door with a small pointed-arch niche directly above it — that niche is the setting of the Sanskrit inscription.
- Deep arched recesses with projecting bracketed eaves appear on the adjoining hall walls (2006 photo).
- There are no true radiating arches on this building (Gobind Deva has them; Madan Mohan does not, per Growse's comparative account).

CHAPEL TOWER, storey by storey (from photographs — Commons "Radha Madan Mohan Temple (65984)" 2026 is the clearest):
1. Base storey: octagonal, panelled but coarser, with the single east door and the arched inscription-niche.
2. Four panelled registers, each separated by a heavy triple moulding: a bead/pearl course, a row of large pendant bud-corbels (drop-shaped bosses, ~20–24 per circuit), and a fillet.
3. Within each register, each of the 8 faces carries 3 vertical columns × 4–5 rows of sunk square panels. Centre column = a deep-cut lotus rosette medallion; flanking columns = diamond/lozenge motifs. Ornament is geometric and floral — NOT figural, NOT narrative.
4. Each of the 8 angles carries an engaged colonnette carved as a continuous string of beads, running the full height.
5. Neck (griva): a short recessed octagonal drum with 1–2 rows of small panels.
6. Crown: a big fluted/gadrooned ring of ~18–22 fat lobes, overhanging the neck by a clear margin (a mushroom, not a sphere), then a small squat cap. Opening:solid on the tower = effectively 0% — the only aperture in the whole tower above the base is one small square vent under the crown.

SANCTUM TOWER: same octagonal parabolic profile, same plan diameter, but ZERO panelling. Plain coursed ashlar, one flat angle-rib per corner, one small high opening, blunt bare top. Its whole design argument is contrast with its neighbour.

NO URUSHRINGAS. Victor Jacquemont, quoted verbatim by Growse at p.188 and identified by Growse as describing Madan Mohan and Jugal Kishor: "Two of these temples have the pyramidal form peculiar to the early Hindu style, but without the little turrets which in the similar buildings at Benares seem to spring out of the main tower that determines the shape of the edifice. They have a better effect, from being more simple, but are half in ruins." Single clean mass; no clustered miniature spires.

Growse elsewhere (p.256, "Origin of the Hindu Sikhara") confirms the plan form: "The lower story of the modern temple which, though most commonly square, is occasionally, as in the Madan Mohan and Radha Ballabh examples, an octagon, and therefore a near approach to a circle."

## Material and colour

MATERIAL (SOURCED):
- Red sandstone throughout the original fabric. Growse quotes Jacquemont, p.188: "All the buildings are constructed of red sandstone, of a closer grain and of a lighter and less disagreeable colour than that used at Agra: it comes from the neighbourhood of Jaypur, a distance of 200 miles." (Modern attribution is usually the Bharatpur/Rupbas–Bansi Paharpur belt; Jacquemont's "neighbourhood of Jaypur" is the 1830s attribution and I found no source reconciling the two. Practical consequence is the same: a lighter, pinker, finer-grained stone than Agra Fort red.)
- MIXED FABRIC IN THE NAVE — this is the "brick" line you were after. Growse, p.252: "The nave, ruinous as it is, was evidently to a great extent rebuilt in comparatively recent times, the old materials being utilized as far as possible, but when they ran short, the place of stone being supplied by brick." So the nave walls must read as salvaged ashlar patched and topped with small red brick, not as clean masonry. Brick coursing is visible in the 2004 and 2026 photographs on the ruinous masses beside the towers.
- Later/secondary: lime plaster (yellow-cream) on the 19th–20th-century porch and boundary walls; modern cement render on the compound walls; since ~2024, red sandstone SLABS paving the terrace/courtyard where grass formerly grew (Vrindavan Today; Amar Ujala 2026).

- IMPORTANT TEXTURE NOTE: the stone is strongly BANDED course by course. Individual ashlar courses on the plain tower and the retaining walls run from deep red-brown to buff yellow-ochre, in horizontal stripes 0.25–0.5 m deep. A single flat red albedo will look wrong immediately. This banding is clearly visible in the 2026 close view.

## The setting

POSITION (SOURCED / MEASURED, OpenStreetMap, ODbL):
- OSM way 334671983, "मदन मोहन मंदिर" / "Madan Mohan Temple", centroid 27.580253 N, 77.687610 E. The mapped precinct polygon is ~62 m N–S × 57 m E–W.
- Growse, p.249: the temple "stands at the upper end of the town on a high cliff near the Kali-mardan, or as it is more commonly called, the Kali-dah Ghat" — i.e. on Dwadashaditya Tila, the mound above the old Yamuna bank.

THE MOUND IS THE BUILDING, FROM BELOW. From the Parikrama Marg approach what you actually see is a colossal battered revetment: a sloping retaining wall of red sandstone and brick, 12–18 m high (ESTIMATED from photographs; no sourced figure found), with a straight parapet pierced by small square vents and with two engaged ROUNDED bastions at its angles. The temple towers only appear above and behind it. A single long straight masonry stair with a plain parapet climbs the grassed slope from the north-west, in two flights with a landing. Growse built the eastern approach himself in 1875 (p.252): "a new approach to the court-yard opened out from the east with a flight of masonry steps up the ascent," costing Rs. 200 from the municipality.

THE 9–10 FOOT DROP. Growse, p.251, on the nave's east door: "immediately outside of which the ground has a precipitous drop of some 9 or 10 feet; thus the only entrance is from the side." Build that cliff edge; it is a real navigation hazard and a real visual fact.

HOW CLOSE THE TOWN COMES (MEASURED, OSM, distance and bearing from the temple centroid):
- Sanatana Goswami's bhajan kutir — 51 m, bearing 218° (SW), inside/abutting the precinct
- Advaita Vat — 101 m, 60°; Surya Ghat — 104 m, 24°; Ashta-sakhi temple — 123 m, 90°
- Parikram Marg (tertiary road) — 178 m, 308° (the approach side, with the VIP parking Vrindavan Today names)
- Kaliya Mardan temple 272 m / Old Kaliya temple 354 m / Kaliya Ghat 362 m / Prabodhananda Sarasvati samadhi 370 m, all bearing 225–235° (SW)
- Banke Bihari temple — 287 m, 96° (E)
- Jaipur Mandir (Radha Madhav) — 933 m, 163° (SSE)
Modern 2–4 storey painted brick and rendered houses press directly against the compound on the south and east; the 2004 photograph is taken up a lane barely 3 m wide with the tower filling the slot at the end.

VISIBLE FROM A DISTANCE? Yes, but selectively. The crowned chapel tower is among the tallest old things in Vrindavan and clears the surrounding roofs; it reads from the Parikrama Marg, from the open ground of the old riverbed to the north-west, and from the ghats. From inside the lane network you get only framed slot views. Growse's own testimony that he wanted to copy this tower for the dome of his church at Mathura (p.162: "I originally intended it to be a copy of a Hindu sikhara, such as that of the temple of Madan Mohan at Brindaban") is evidence the silhouette was a landmark.

THE RIVER — TIME-DEPENDENT. Growse's 1883 plate draws the Jamuna lapping the compound wall immediately below, with a flight of steps down to it (Praskandan Ghat, which his ghat list at p.~300 records as "re-built by Gosains of temple of Madan Mohan"). TODAY the Yamuna is roughly 0.8–1.0 km away to the NE/E; the ground at the foot of the mound is dry, occupied by the Parikrama Marg, parking and buildings. Choose your epoch and be consistent.

OTHER SETTING ITEMS FROM GROWSE (all c.1880, mostly gone):
- An unfinished chhatri of Raja Ratan Singh "near the Madan Mohan temple at Brinda-ban, where it is still to be seen in its unfinished state, as left at the time of his sudden death" — it never rose beyond the first storey, standing "in one of the large gardens outside the town beyond the Madan Mohan temple."
- "The large gardens beyond the temple of Madan Mohan, on the old Delhi road," which Jacquemont saw from a distance as "an island of verdure in the sandy plain" (Growse p.188–189). Built over today.
- A Bengali temple of Sringar Bat "near the Madan Mohan" (p.257).
- Mahalla "Ghera Madan Mohan" — the quarter around the temple, in Growse's list of Vrindavan mahallas.

RECENT CHANGE TO THE GROUND. Until ~2024 the terrace/courtyard inside the precinct was natural grass; ASI replaced it with red sandstone tiling, which Vrindavan Today reports and criticises. If you are building "today", pave it.

## Dimensions

SOURCED — F. S. Growse, Mathura: A District Memoir, 2nd ed. 1883, p.251, verbatim:
"The temple, as we now see it, consists of a nave 57 feet long, with a choir of 20 feet square at the west end, and a sanctuary of the same dimensions beyond. The nave has three openings on either side and a square door at the east end, immediately outside of which the ground has a precipitous drop of some 9 or 10 feet; thus the only entrance is from the side. Its total height would seem to have been only about 22 feet, but its vaulted roof has entirely disappeared."

So, SOURCED (internal, feet → metres):
- Nave internal length 57 ft = 17.37 m
- Choir 20 ft square = 6.10 × 6.10 m
- Sanctuary 20 ft square = 6.10 × 6.10 m
- Nave total height ~22 ft = 6.71 m (roof gone above this)
- Ground drop outside the east door 9–10 ft = 2.75–3.05 m

DERIVED — measured off Growse's plate (facing p.252, "Scale 50 feet = 1 inch"), scaled by anchoring his own 57 ft figure; ±8%:
- Nave external length ≈ 62 ft = 18.9 m
- Nave external width ≈ 30.6 ft = 9.33 m; internal width ≈ 17.3 ft = 5.27 m; wall thickness ≈ 6.4–6.9 ft = 1.95–2.10 m
- Nave flank piers ≈ 5.5–6.4 ft = 1.67–1.95 m square; the three openings ≈ 1.2 m, 1.8 m, 1.4 m clear
- Choir external ≈ 25.5 ft = 7.8 m square
- Sanctum external, max diameter across the stellate/faceted plan ≈ 30 ft = 9.16 m
- Chapel external, max diameter ≈ 31 ft = 9.4 m; chapel internal chamber ≈ 15.5 ft = 4.7 m square (much thicker walls than the sanctum — a near-solid, slender tower)
- Sanctum centre → chapel centre ≈ 33.7 ft = 10.3 m, linked by a corridor ≈ 11 ft (3.4 m) wide
- Gateway block ≈ 39 × 42 ft = 11.9 × 12.8 m external, two internal cells labelled "Tower" and "Gate"
- Whole temple body, east wall to west face of sanctum ≈ 117 ft = 35.8 m
- Walled precinct on the 1883 plate ≈ 156 × 159 ft = 47.5 × 48.6 m

MEASURED (OpenStreetMap, ODbL, way 334671983): today's mapped precinct polygon 62 m N–S × 57 m E–W — larger than Growse's, consistent with later accretions.

HEIGHTS — ESTIMATED, no reliable sourced figure exists. Photogrammetry from three photographs (2006 wide view, 2023, 2026), scaling by the 9.2 m plan diameter of the towers:
- Chapel tower, courtyard level to top of the lobed crown: 20–24 m (best single value ~22 m)
- Sanctum tower, courtyard to blunt top: 13–16 m (best single value ~14.5 m)
- Gateway, ground to pyramid ridge: 11–14 m
- Mound revetment, lane level to courtyard level: 12–18 m
- Therefore the crown of the chapel tower stands roughly 34–40 m above the Parikrama Marg.
PUBLISHED FIGURES, ALL IN CONFLICT AND NONE PRIMARY: "a height of 50 feet" (15.2 m) — Mathura district administration and UP Tourism; "20 m high" — Wikipedia / brajrasik.org; "60-ft high shikhar" (18.3 m) — travel aggregators. My photogrammetry sits closest to the 20 m figure for the crowned tower and closest to the 50 ft figure for the plain sanctum tower, which may be how the two numbers arose.

ASI STATUS (SOURCED): Monument of National Importance, "Temple of Madan Mohan, Brindaban", Mathura district, Agra Circle, monument number N-UP-A197.

## What NOT to build

- DO NOT crown the sanctum tower. Instinct says amalaka + kalasha + gold finial + flag. The plain sanctum tower ends in a blunt, bare, slightly flattened rounded cap with nothing at all on top — no amalaka, no pot, no metal, no dhvaja. Confirmed in the Daniell aquatint of 1789 and in every photograph 2004–2026. This is the inverse of the Govind Dev lesson: there the dome exists and is invisible; here the crown you expect simply is not there.
- DO NOT put the fluted lobed crown on the main sanctum. It belongs to the SIDE CHAPEL. Growse, p.251: the sanctum tower is 'a plain octagon'; the chapel is 'much more highly enriched, the whole of its exterior surface being covered with sculptured panels'. Independently confirmed: the panelled tower is the one with a single doorway and an arched inscription-niche at its base, which Growse states is the chapel's east door. Getting this backwards inverts the whole composition.
- DO NOT make the sanctum tower the tallest. The subsidiary chapel's tower is ~50% taller and is the landmark. That is architecturally odd and it is exactly why a modeller 'corrects' it by instinct.
- DO NOT give the two towers the same treatment. The entire design argument is the contrast: one tower is blank coursed ashlar with eight flat angle-ribs, the other is 100% covered in sunk panels, bead-string angle colonnettes and pendant bud bands. Matching them destroys the building.
- DO NOT add urushringas — clustered miniature spires climbing the flanks of the shikhara, Khajuraho/Bhubaneswar style. Jacquemont, quoted by Growse at p.188 and explicitly identified as describing this temple: 'without the little turrets which in the similar buildings at Benares seem to spring out of the main tower'. Single clean mass.
- DO NOT model an onion dome or a bulbous Mughal profile. The towers are parabolic/bullet-shaped octagonal cones, widest at the plinth, tapering continuously. 'Curvilinear' here means a rekha-type curve on an octagonal plan, not a bulge.
- DO NOT build a third tower over the choir. Growse, p.251: 'the upper part of the choir tower has also been destroyed', and two sentences later he refers to 'the two towers of the temple'. It has been a stump since at least 1880. If you see a third stepped-pyramidal mass in photographs, that is far more likely the gateway.
- DO NOT roof the nave. 'Its vaulted roof has entirely disappeared' (p.251). It is a roofless walled shell ~6.7 m high. Do not substitute a modern flat slab, a barrel vault, a dome or a small shikhara.
- DO NOT build the nave in clean uniform ashlar. Growse, p.252: it 'was evidently to a great extent rebuilt in comparatively recent times, the old materials being utilized as far as possible, but when they ran short, the place of stone being supplied by brick.' Salvaged red sandstone blocks of mismatched size, patched and capped with small red brick. Visible in photographs.
- DO NOT put the usable entrance on the east end. The square east door opens straight onto a 9–10 ft (2.75–3.05 m) drop; 'thus the only entrance is from the side' (p.251). In a walkable 1:1 build this is a fall hazard and a blocked door a player will walk into. Model the drop and route entry through the side openings and the gateway.
- DO NOT set it on flat ground. It stands on Dwadashaditya Tila behind a 12–18 m battered revetment with rounded engaged bastions and a vented parapet. From below, that retaining wall is visually more of the mass than the temple. A long straight masonry stair climbs the grass slope from the Parikrama Marg side.
- DO NOT put the Yamuna at its foot in a present-day build. Growse's 1883 plate shows the river lapping the compound; today it is roughly 0.8–1.0 km away to the NE/E and the foot of the mound is dry ground, road and parking. Pick one epoch.
- DO NOT merge it with the NEW Madan Mohan temple. There is a separate, later, still-active temple outside the compound. Growse, p.251: worship 'is ordinarily performed in an elegant and substantial edifice erected on the other side of the street under the shadow of the older fane', and his plate marks it 'NEW TEMPLE' as a small rectangle across the street. He dates it c.1821, built by the Bengali Kayastha Nand Kumar Ghos, who also built the new Gopinath temple (p.254). Other sources say 1819, Nanda Kumar Basu / Nandalal Vasu of Baharu. It is a plain 19th-century Bengali-patron building at or near the foot of the mound, NOT a tower-temple — and it is where the replica deities are actually worshipped. Place it as a second, separate, much humbler building.
- DO NOT confuse this with the 'Jaipur Temple' / Radha Madhav. srimandir.com wrongly calls that 'also known as the Radha Madan Mohan Temple'. It is a different building, built 1917 by Sawai Madho Singh II, OSM-measured 933 m away at bearing 163° (SSE) — carved white/cream Jaipur stone, pillared halls, a completely different idiom. Nothing about it belongs on Dwadashaditya Tila.
- DO NOT pull reference from the other Madan Mohan temples. There are at least six traps: Bishnupur (BRICK AND TERRACOTTA, Bengali char-chala — and the Wikimedia Commons description of the 1789 Daniell aquatint of THIS temple wrongly describes it as 'built in the 17th century of brick and terracotta and covered in terracotta plaques in the Bengali manner', which is Bishnupur's description attached to Vrindavan's picture); Karauli (where the original deity went); Cooch Behar; Ramkeli; Gokul (Growse dates it 1511); and two 19th-century Madan Mohan temples in Mathura city — one by the Sami Ghat built 1859 by Seth Anant Ram, one in Asikunda Mahalla built by Dhanraj Bohra (Growse's building lists).
- DO NOT carve figures on the chapel tower. Growse's phrase 'sculptured panels' means ornament, not narrative. The photographs show sunk square panels containing lotus rosettes in the centre column and lozenge/diamond motifs in the flanking columns, plus bead-string colonnettes and rows of pendant bud-corbels. No deities, no couples, no friezes of figures.
- DO NOT use true radiating arches. At Gobind Deva Growse notes true arches 'as in our Gothic cathedrals', and he explicitly contrasts the buildings. Here the real openings are square-headed on straight jambs; the pointed and cusped arches are relief panels applied over them, decorative only.
- DO NOT model the courtyard as grass for a 2025+ build. ASI replaced the natural grass with red sandstone tiling around 2024 (Vrindavan Today). Conversely, do not pave it for a pre-2024 build.
- DO NOT render the stone as clean bright red. Before the 2025–26 ASI chemical cleaning it was soot-black and moss-green over much of the surface (measured V 41–45% in 2004). After cleaning it is far redder. Also, the ashlar is strongly BANDED course by course from deep red-brown to buff ochre — a single flat red albedo reads as plastic.
- DO NOT model the 2026 state without scaffolding. Both towers are currently encased in bamboo/steel scaffolding for ASI works. If you want a clean silhouette, target 2006–2023.
- WATCH THE शिखर TRAP. Hindi and travel sources say things like 'the shikhar is 60 ft' and 'Aurangzeb dismantled the shikhar'. Here शिखर means the crown/top of the sanctum tower, not a separate spire, and Growse — the only eyewitness surveyor — attributes the losses to ruin and neglect, not to a named iconoclast. He never mentions Aurangzeb in connection with this building.

## Uncertain — flag, do not assert

- WHICH TOWER IS WHICH is an inference, not a direct statement. Growse says the sanctum tower is plain and the chapel tower is panelled, and he says the chapel has a single east door with a raised Sanskrit inscription over it; the panelled tower in the 2004 and 2006 photographs is the one with a single door and an arched niche above. That chain is strong but it is a chain. If a future source shows the panelled tower over the sanctum, the entire silhouette section inverts. This is the single highest-stakes item here and I want it flagged.
- GROWSE'S PLATE HAS NO NORTH ARROW. My orientation (south at top, west at right, Jamuna on the north/north-west, street and gateway on the south, new temple to the south-east) is deduced from his text — 'choir of 20 feet square at the west end', 'a square door at the east end', 'attached to its south side is a tower-crowned chapel'. If Growse used those compass words loosely, the whole plan flips.
- NO HEIGHT IS SOURCED. Published figures are 50 ft, 60 ft and 20 m and they contradict each other; none cites a survey. My 20–24 m (chapel tower) and 13–16 m (sanctum tower) are photogrammetry scaled off the plan diameter, from oblique upward-tilted phone photographs. Treat as ±20%.
- THE MOUND HEIGHT AND REVETMENT HEIGHT are pure estimates from photographs. I found no source giving the height of Dwadashaditya Tila or the number of steps in the approach stair.
- IS THE NAVE RE-ROOFED TODAY? Growse is unambiguous that it was roofless in 1883. But Vrindavan Today and press reports describe grass that 'had grown atop the roof' being replaced with stone slabs, and separately describe courtyard grass being tiled. I could not resolve whether that is a terrace over the nave or the open courtyard. Assume a roofless nave, but verify before committing interior geometry.
- I COULD NOT POSITIVELY IDENTIFY THE CHOIR STUMP in any photograph. A third stepped-pyramidal mass appears in the 2006 and 2023 wide views and in a 2026 close-up; I read it as the gateway because Growse describes the gateway as having a pyramidal tower and because its position relative to the other two does not match the choir's place on the plan. Moderate confidence only.
- GROWSE'S INSCRIPTION DATE IS INTERNALLY INCONSISTENT as OCR'd: 'an inscription with the date Samhat 1684 (A.D. 1827)'. Samvat 1684 is AD 1627; Samvat 1884 is AD 1827. The printed text almost certainly reads 1884. Either way it records a pilgrimage, not the building.
- THE FOUNDATION DATE '1580' AND THE PATRON 'RAM DAS KAPUR OF MULTAN' ARE NOT SOURCED BY GROWSE AS FACT. He gives the story from the Bhakt Sindhu of Lachman Das, 'a modernized version of the Bhakt Mala' — a devotional text — and gives NO date. The chapel's inscription names Sri Gunanand as its founder and Growse says it 'would certainly be later than that of the main building'. Classify 1580 as LEGEND-adjacent tradition, widely repeated but not attested by the eyewitness survey.
- THE STONE'S PROVENANCE is disputed: Jacquemont (via Growse, p.188) says 'the neighbourhood of Jaypur, a distance of 200 miles'; modern practice attributes Braj temple stone to the nearer Bharatpur/Rupbas belt. I found no source that resolves it.
- THE LOBE COUNT ON THE CROWN (~18–22) is eyeballed from photographs at oblique angles. I would not bet on the exact number.
- THE NEW TEMPLE'S DATE AND PATRON conflict across sources: Growse says c.1821, Nand Kumar Ghos, a Bengali Kayastha; Wikipedia says 1819, Nanda Kumar Basu, zamindar of Baharu; brajrasik says Nandalal Vasu. Its position also conflicts — Wikipedia says 'at the top of the hill', brajrasik says 'at the foot of the hill', Growse's plate puts it OUTSIDE the compound across the street. I would follow Growse and his plate.
- THE 1.5 KM FIGURE FOR THE JAIPUR TEMPLE in the brief does not match OSM: I measure 933 m at bearing 163°. Either way it is a wholly separate building, but if you are placing both in one world, use the measured figure.
- THE COMMONS CATEGORY IS CONTAMINATED. Several files titled 'Madan Mohan' in search results are Bishnupur, Cooch Behar or Sarisha, and the Vrindavan Daniell aquatint carries a description text that belongs to Bishnupur. Do not harvest that category blindly for reference imagery.

## Independent check

Confidence: HIGH on the architecture and on source verification — I did not take the survey's word for anything load-bearing. I downloaded the three Commons exteriors at full resolution and examined the roofline, gallery and cornice myself; pulled Growse, Entwistle and Case as full text and grepped the actual sentences; extracted the ASI PDF and searched it; exported the Wikipedia wikitext; and re-queried Overpass and recomputed the footprint geometry, bearings, road clearances and neighbour distances locally. The centroid error, the Yamuna bearing, the Madan Mohan/Karauli error, the Paharpur mis-citation, the interior-stairs error and the carved west-wall cornice are all things I derived independently and would stand behind. MEDIUM on three: the srimandir misquote (a single WebFetch through a small model, and the page could have changed since the survey was written — worth one manual look before acting on it); the Gokulananda POI (OSM naming in this quarter is poor enough that both nodes may be mislabelled upstream); and the 9-foil arch count, which is a cusp-counting judgement on a compressed JPEG. OVERALL this is a strong survey — the crowning element, the thing that has gone wrong three times, is correct this time and I verified it from photographs rather than prose, and the Growse index trap is a genuinely valuable catch that I confirmed word for word. My corrections are mostly in the geo-data and the citation chain, not in the building.

### Corrections

- CENTROID IS ~7.4 m OFF — the single most consequential error for a 1:1 build. The survey gives the OSM footprint centroid as 27.58538 N, 77.69865 E. I re-queried way 335527344 via Overpass and computed the polygon area centroid: that stated point is NOT the centroid, it is essentially the midpoint of the WEST wall (it sits 0.01 m from the west edge). True area centroid is 27.585379 N, 77.698725 E — 7.35 m further east. Placing the building on the published coordinate shifts the whole temple ~7.4 m west, i.e. onto the Parikrama Marg. This also explains the survey's own unresolved '~12 m' disagreement with the Wikipedia coordinate (27°35'7.3"N 77°41'55.6"E): from the stated point the gap is 12.8 m, from the true centroid it is 5.6 m. Use 77.69872 E.
- THE YAMUNA IS NORTH-WEST, NOT NORTH-EAST. The survey's setting section states 'Yamuna channel 261 m NE'. Overpass gives the nearest point of the mapped Yamuna way at 260.8 m, bearing 316.9° — the distance is right to within a metre, the direction is ~90° wrong. Corroborated by the survey's own other bearings, which I re-derived and which are correct: Chir Ghat due WEST (273.8°), Keshi Ghat due NORTH (2.5°). The river wraps the town round the N and W of this site; it is never NE of it. This matters for sun/water direction and any camera work.
- MADAN MOHAN WENT TO KARAULI, NOT JAIPUR. The silhouette section says the deity never left Vrindavan 'unlike Govind Dev, Gopinath, Madan Mohan, Radha Damodar, which went to Jaipur'. Entwistle's own text (which the survey cites elsewhere) says otherwise: 'Madanmohan was taken to Karauli in 1728 by Raja Gopal Singh' and, of the Madanmohan Goswamis, 'they went with their deity to Jaipur and Karauli'. Govind Dev, Gopinath and Radha Damodar → Jaipur is correct; Madan Mohan → Karauli. This is exactly the wrong-place class of error the project has already been burned by, and Madan Mohan is itself a Vrindavan temple this project will model.
- THE SRIMANDIR QUOTATION DOES NOT REPRODUCE. doNotBuild #6 is built on a quoted Hindi sentence, 'यह विशाल मंदिर नदी किनारे स्थित है' (this vast temple stands on the riverbank), attributed to srimandir.com. Fetching that page, the 1826 sentence quoted elsewhere in the survey IS there verbatim ('मंदिर का वर्तमान ढांचा 1826 में लखनऊ निवासी शाह बिहारी लालजी द्वारा निर्मित करवाया गया था'), but the 'vast/riverbank' sentence is not. What the page says is 'यह मंदिर एक प्राकृतिक सौंदर्ययुक्त क्षेत्र, नदी के समीप स्थित है' — 'near a river', with no विशाल (vast) and no नदी किनारे (on the bank). The ADVICE is independently correct (222 m², river 261 m away, dense town between) so keep the rule — but the quoted evidence for it is not accurate and should be removed or re-sourced.
- THE PAHARPUR QUARRY CITATION IS OVER-GENERALISED. The materials section says 'Growse records that Braj builders of this period took their shafts from the Paharpur quarry in Bharatpur territory (p. 263)'. In Growse's actual text that sentence belongs to ONE specific building — the temple of Radha Indra Kishor, built by Rani Indrajit Kunvar and completed at the end of 1871: 'The piers are composed of four conjoined pillars, each shaft being a single piece of stone, brought from the Paharpur quarry in Bharatpur territory.' Growse makes no general claim about Braj builders and says nothing whatever about Radha Raman's stone. Bansi Paharpur is a plausible guess for a Braj building of this date, but it is inference, not a record, and should be relabelled.
- THE REMOVED STAIRS WERE INTERIOR — THEY DID NOT LAND ON THE FACADE. The elevation section describes the exterior plinth as 'flanked by low carved dado blocks with rosette medallions (the stubs left where the removed stairs once landed)', and doNotBuild #13 repeats it. Case p.82 puts those stairs inside: 'For about a century, two sets of stairs led from the courtyard up to the platform' — that is the inner 35-ft courtyard rising to the 4.5-ft antechamber platform that only Goswamis now use. Nothing in Case places any stair on the outer south face. doNotBuild #13 even contradicts itself, asserting the blocks flanking the outer door are where they landed and then adding 'This matters if the interior is ever modelled.' The dados flanking the outer door are ordinary carved dados; do not model them as stair stubs.
- CASE'S 'FOUR STEPS' ARE INSIDE THE VESTIBULE, NOT THE PLINTH STEPS. The sources entry credits Case p.81 with 'four steps' in the same breath as the plinth, and the elevation gives 'Plinth: 0.55–0.65 m above the courtyard, 3–4 marble steps'. Case actually writes: 'Just inside is a small vestibule, where shoes and socks are to be left—for none may be worn in the temple—and to the right a small flight of four steps leads to the interior of the temple.' Those four steps are internal and turn right. Exterior plinth steps do exist and are visible in both photographs, so the geometry survives, but Case is not a source for them and the citation should be dropped.
- THE WEST WALL'S CORNICE IS NOT PLAIN. The elevation and doNotBuild #11 describe the blank western ~5.7 m as finished with 'a plain stepped cornice and a row of small pointed merlons (kanguras)'. At full resolution the 2023 frontal photograph (Radha Raman Temple 2.jpg, 4080x2296) shows a carved TWO-BAND cornice running the entire width of that wall: an upper row of small pointed dentil-like motifs above a continuous frieze of larger multi-cusped blind arcading, over a projecting string course. 'Blank, zero openings' and 'heavily soot-streaked' are both strongly confirmed — but shading that cornice as plain would drop a real and conspicuous carved band. The rule 'do not carve the full 14.65 m' is still right; the top ~1 m of the west wall is the exception.
- THE 114 m NNW POI IS NOT THE GOKULANANDA TEMPLE. The setting section lists, as MEASURED, 'The Gokulananda/Radhavinod temple with Gadadhar Pandit's tooth samadhi ~114 m NNW (bearing 341°)'. Overpass returns at exactly 114 m / 341.0° a node named 'Храм Вамши Гопала и Гададхарв Дант самадхи' — Vamshi Gopala temple plus Gadadhar tooth samadhi. The node actually named Gokulananda, 'श्री राधा गोकुलानंद मंदिर', is at 92 m, bearing 8.2° (essentially due north). The survey then leans on the 341° figure in its `uncertain` section to argue for a north-western outer gateway; that argument rests on the wrong POI and should be redone against the 92 m / 8° node.
- UPPER-GALLERY ARCHES ARE ABOUT 9-FOIL, NOT '5- OR 7-FOIL'. The elevation states 'Ground-floor flanking niches and all five upper arches = 5- or 7-foil with pendant cusp drops.' Counting cusps on the full-resolution frontal photograph, each gallery arch reads as roughly 9 lobes (possibly 11). doNotBuild #18 gives the correct range (5-, 7-, 9- or 11-foil), so only the elevation text needs fixing — but a 7-foil arch modelled where a 9-foil belongs is visible at close range across five openings.
- 'THE DOMES NEVER BREAK THE SKYLINE' IS NOT SUPPORTED. The silhouette says the cream saucer domes 'sit LOWER than the gable apex' and 'never break the skyline'. In the frontal 2023 photograph the left cream dome reads level with, or marginally above, the gable apex — and because it stands further back, a low camera would render an equal-height object LOWER, so level-in-image implies at-least-equal-in-fact. The four gilt finials are unambiguously above the gable apex, which the survey's own numbers concede (finials 10.0–10.5 m vs gable 9.3 m). Keep the domes low and wide, but drop the claim that nothing behind the gable reaches the skyline.
- FALSE PRECISION ON HEIGHTS. Every vertical is quoted to 0.1 m (4.1 m, 6.9 m, 8.4 m, 9.3 m) under a declared ±10–15% band, which is ±0.9 m at gable height. My own independent scale check on the 2023 photograph — standing adult (~1.70 m) against the doorway — gives a door of ~2.0 m against the stated 1.9 m, and a gable apex 8.8–10.5 m above the courtyard paving against the stated 9.9 m. The figures are defensible as central estimates and I am not disputing them, but they should be written as ranges, because at present they read as measurements and a downstream builder will treat them as such. (Same for the footprint: I get 15.27 m N–S and 223.4 m² against the stated 15.18 m and 222.3 m².)

### Independently confirmed

- WHAT CROWNS THE BUILDING — VERIFIED DIRECTLY FROM THE PHOTOGRAPHS, NOT FROM DESCRIPTION. I downloaded the three Commons exteriors at full resolution and examined the roofline. There is a single large smooth curved gable over the central bay; its ends turn DOWN past the springing (the bangaldar signature, clearly visible on the right-hand return); it carries a moulded bead-and-dentil archivolt and a tympanum frieze of small blind cusped niches following the curve, of which I counted ~12 with both ends cropped, consistent with the stated ~14. There is NO shikhara, no spire, no amalaka, no urushringa, no chhatri anywhere on the building. This is the claim the project has got wrong three times and this time it is right. Note that a travel-site claim of 'a dome-shaped roof and a gold-plated spire' did surface in my search — doNotBuild #2 correctly anticipates exactly that.
- THE REST OF THE SKYLINE IS ALSO REAL. Visible in the 2023 photographs at full resolution: low, wide, pale-cream domes with a distinctly scalloped / lotus-petal (gadrooned) skirt, wider than tall, sitting behind the gable; four small gilt kalash finials on thin stalks, yellow-metallic against the sky; and a thin flagstaff at the west end carrying a dark pennant. All four elements exist as described. The survey's caution that it cannot prove the domes and finials belong to the temple rather than to the old temple or a Goswami house behind is appropriate — though their symmetrical placement flanking the gable argues they are the temple's own.
- THE FIVE-ARCH GALLERY, 1 + 3 + 1, IS CORRECT. I counted independently in both the 2014 photograph and the more frontal 2023 one: one recessed outer arch, a broad pier, three arches on the projecting central bay carried on slender turned colonnettes, a return pier, one recessed outer arch. Also confirmed: jali filling the lower portion of each opening, a pierced diagonal-lattice balustrade between short urn-headed posts, heavily carved spandrels with rosettes, pendant cusp drops, and the deep chhajja on large S-scrolled consoles. The small deity figurine in a niche above the door lintel is there too.
- GROWSE'S INDEX TRAP IS REAL AND EXACTLY AS DESCRIBED — the most valuable item in the survey. I pulled Growse's full text and grepped it. 'Radha-Raman, temple of, 262' is the ONLY index entry for the name. Page 262 reads: 'the temple of Radhá Raman, completed within the last few years. The founder is Sah Kundan Lal, of Lakhnau, who has built on a design suggested by the modern secular buildings of that city... fronted with a colonnade of spiral marble pillars, each shaft being of a single piece', continuing over the page 'resembles nothing so much as a disreputable London casino... Ten lakhs of rupees are said to have been wasted on its construction.' That is the Shahji temple. Growse's only genuine Radha Raman references are pp. 198–99 ('Gopal Bhatt, founder of the temple of Radha Raman'; 'Gopal Bhatt, who beautified the temple of Radha Raman with all that he possessed'), the festival entry 'Sravan Badi 5. — Radha Raman Ji ka dhio dhio', and mahalla no. 9 at p.271. Every one of these quotations is verbatim correct, and Growse gives the 16th-c temple no architectural notice at all. Given that research/shahji.md already exists in this repo, the double-build risk is live and this warning must stay attached.
- THE ASI NEGATIVE EVIDENCE CHECKS OUT. I downloaded the CPM list PDF and extracted it. Mathura district entries 116–119 are, exactly and in order, Temple of Govind Deo, Temple of Jugal Kishore, Temple of Madan Mohan, Temple of Radha Ballabh — all Brindaban. A full-text search of the document returns no 'Radha Raman' or 'Radharaman' anywhere (the other Radha-named entries are Bengal monuments and a J&K site). So doNotBuild #16 — no ASI signage, no blue plaque, no protection railings — is correctly grounded.
- THE WIKIPEDIA TRAPS ARE BOTH PRESENT IN THE RAW WIKITEXT. I exported the article source: `elevation_m = 169.77` and `architecture = [[Rajasthani architecture` are both there, and the body does say 'The temple houses the original Shaligram deity of Krishna alongside goddess Radha.' Entwistle p.412 flatly contradicts the last: 'There is no accompanying image of Radha; instead her presence is indicated by a mukut.' The infobox also gives `creator = Gopala Bhatta Goswami`, which is the wrong-founder hazard for the 1826 fabric. All three doNotBuild items built on these (#7, #8, and the 1826 dating) are well founded.
- ENTWISTLE IS QUOTED VERBATIM AND ACCURATELY. I pulled the full text and read both passages. p.219: 'In 1826 a Seth named Biharilal, who was given the title of Shah by the Nawab of Lucknow, built a new temple in Vrindaban for the deity of Radharaman. His two grandsons, Kundanlal and Phundanlal, settled in Vrindaban in 1856... In 1868 Lalitkishori built a temple in the italianate style.' p.412: the tall gateway, Radharaman Ghera, the samadhi of Gopal Bhatt near the entrance, 'There is no accompanying image of Radha; instead her presence is indicated by a mukut', and 'The present temple was built in 1826 by Shah Biharilal of Lucknow, grandfather of Kundanlal and Phundanlal, the founders of Shahji temple.' All correct, including the §32 note that the Radhavinod/Gokulanand street 'begins in front of the gateway to Radharaman Ghera'.
- CASE IS QUOTED ACCURATELY THROUGHOUT — I checked every architectural claim against the PDF. Confirmed verbatim: 'built of the same light pink sandstone as most of Vrindaban's nineteenth-century buildings'; 'The simple doorway on the south side of the temple... is flanked by two standing male figures holding lotus buds'; 'an inner courtyard about thirty-five feet square, partially open to the sky but at night and in bad weather covered by a sliding tin roof'; the haveli style as 'one, two, or three stories built around an inner courtyard'; 'a checkerboard of black and white marble'; 'a marble platform about four and a half feet high'; 'surrounded on the other three sides by porticos, each with three arches that were recently painted a slightly surprising bright pink and green'; the antechamber's 'three cusped arches, these of unpainted sandstone'; 'an embossed silver double-leaved door'; 'There the deity stands facing east'; the Temple Entry Act and the removal of two sets of stairs; 'a ten-foot-long fire pit in the old temple behind the present one'; 'And it is built to look like a home'; and the two late-16th-century 'very plain, small buildings' behind, 'now used as the kitchen, dining room, and bedroom for the deity'. Also confirmed is Case's own Shah Kundan Lal account (p.80) which the survey correctly reports as conflicting with Entwistle.
- THE CHRONOLOGICAL ARGUMENT AGAINST CASE'S ATTRIBUTION IS SOUND. The survey rejects Case's Kundan Lal founding story on the grounds that Wajid Ali Shah was deposed in 1856, Kundanlal settled in Vrindavan that year, Shahji was built in 1868, so a Kundanlal-built sandstone temple would have to postdate 1868 — which Growse, writing in 1883 and describing the marble temple as newly finished, does not support. I verified each link (Entwistle's 1856 and 1868 dates; Growse's 'completed within the last few years'). The reasoning holds, and the survey is right to go with 1826/Biharilal while flagging it unresolved rather than asserting it.
- THE FOOTPRINT AND MOST SETTING DISTANCES RE-DERIVE, SEVERAL EXACTLY. Re-querying Overpass and computing locally: wall bearings 179.8° and 89.8° (exact match); south edge splitting 5.71 + 8.92 m (exact match); Parikram Marg at 9.39 m from the footprint edge at bearing 270.0°, i.e. due west, matching the stated '9.3 m WEST of the blank west wall'; ten buildings mapped within 150 m (exact); Keshi Ghat 210 m at 2.5°; Jugal Kishor 178 m at 1.3°; Gopinath 121 m at 52.2°; Chir Ghat 199 m at 273.8°; nearest standing water 180 m (stated 177). The OSM way carries wikidata Q7280191 and the name 'Sri Radha Raman Mandir'.
- THE SIGNBOARD READING IS CORRECT, AND I FOUND AN INDEPENDENT CROSS-CHECK THE SURVEY MISSED. At full resolution the 2023 board reads 'प्राकट्य : वैशाख शुक्ल पूर्णिमा सं. १५९९' exactly as quoted — Samvat 1599 = 1542 CE. (I initially misread the much blurrier 2014 board as १५६६ and was wrong.) Independently corroborating it: the red banner in the same 2023 photograph advertises the '481 वां प्राकट्योत्सव' dated 05 मई 2023 — and 2023 − 481 = 1542. So doNotBuild #5, that 1542 is the appearance of the deity and not the date of the fabric, is solidly anchored in the temple's own signage.
- THE COLOUR WORK IS HONEST AND REPRODUCIBLE — including, importantly, where it declines to measure. Re-sampling the same two photographs myself: soot on the west wall comes back near-neutral at S 4% (survey: S 4% — exact); sandstone in self-shadow at H 26° / S 39% against the survey's recommended H 28° / S 37%; the east neighbour measurably paler and less saturated than the temple (S 11% vs S 20–39%), which supports doNotBuild #12. Critically, I verified the survey's own caveat: sampling the cream domes and the gilt finials returns 51–70% of pixels clipped at or above 250, so they genuinely cannot be measured from these images — and the survey labels them INFERRED and low-confidence rather than passing them off as measurements. That is the right call, correctly disclosed.
- ALL CITED SOURCES EXIST AND ARE CORRECTLY ATTRIBUTED. I resolved every Commons file through the API: Entrance Radha Raman Temple, Vrindavan.jpg (Ekabhishek, 2014-10-25, 2448x3264), Radha Raman Temple.jpg and Radha Raman Temple 2.jpg (Kridha20, 2023-06-17, 4080x2296), Radha Raman Temple, Vrindavan.jpg (2009, 1600x1200), and Radharaman Temple at Vrindavan.jpg (Imakanksha, 2025) — all present with the stated authors, dates and dimensions. Growse, Entwistle, Case and the ASI list all resolve at the given URLs. No fabricated sources.
- THE ARITHMETIC IS INTERNALLY CONSISTENT. The 4%-open ground storey checks: 8.9 x 5.8 = 51.6 m², door 1.9 x 1.1 = 2.09 m², ratio 4.05% ≈ 1:24, as stated. The courtyard-fits-envelope tension the survey raises is real and correctly computed (a 10.67 m courtyard inside 14.65 x 15.18 m leaves 1.99 m E–W and 2.26 m N–S per side), and I agree with its resolution — trust the OSM envelope, shrink the courtyard — though even at 10.0 m the depth left for a sanctum is uncomfortably tight and this should stay open.

### Contamination risks

- Growse's index is a permanent trap: 'Radha-Raman, temple of, 262' points at the Shahji temple, and the p.262 sentence itself names the building 'the temple of Radha Raman'. Any researcher, LLM or citation-follower that reaches Growse via that index will describe Shahji's spiral marble pillars and Italianate facade as Radha Raman's. This project already has research/shahji.md queued, so the failure mode is building the same temple twice, in the wrong place, in the wrong material. Keep this warning bound to the asset permanently, not just in this survey.
- Wikimedia Commons is contaminated the same way. My Commons search for 'Radha Raman Temple Vrindavan' returned about 25 Shahji temple files against only 5 genuine ones, including File:Chhote Radha Raman Deities Shahji Temple Vrindavan.jpg — a file whose title contains 'Radha Raman' but which was photographed at Shahji. Any future image-harvesting step for this temple must filter on the Shahji name, not just on 'Radha Raman'.
- Wikipedia's infobox field `elevation_m = 169.77` is rendered by summarisers and knowledge panels as 'Height: 169.77 m (557 feet)'. The survey flags it, and the value really is in the raw wikitext — but this will keep resurfacing every time anyone re-reads the article. It is the ground elevation of Vrindavan; the building is ~10 m.
- Wikipedia's body text asserts the temple 'houses the original Shaligram deity of Krishna alongside goddess Radha', which Entwistle contradicts (Radha is present as a mukut, not a murti). Any AI-generated summary of that article will reintroduce a Radha figure, and the instinct to carve a Radha–Krishna relief over the door follows directly from it.
- Travel aggregators assert both 'a dome-shaped roof and a gold-plated spire' and 'built of red sandstone and white marble'. I hit the first of these in my own search. Both are wrong — the photographs show a curved gable with low cream domes and gilt kalash finials, and Case specifies light pink sandstone. These claims are high-ranking in search and will keep coming back.
- OSM in this quarter is sparse, hand-traced and partly Russian-language pilgrim mapping, with at least four similarly-named religious POIs inside 120 m: 'Radha-Ramana' (37 m, distinct from the temple way), 'Sri Gopal Bhatta Samadhi Mandir' (36 m), 'श्री राधा गोकुलानंद मंदिर' (92 m) and 'Храм Вамши Гопала и Гададхарв Дант самадхи' (114 m). The survey already tripped on this once — it read the Vamshi Gopala node as Gokulananda. Any POI lookup here must be checked by name in the original script, not by distance alone.
- Specifically: the separate 'Radha-Ramana' POI 37 m east of the temple way is a snapping hazard. If an importer matches the landmark by name it may bind the temple to that node rather than to way 335527344, compounding the centroid error already noted.
- srimandir.com — the project has already been warned that this domain conflates Radha Madan Mohan with the Jaipur temple. Its Radha Raman page is reliable on the 1826/Shah Bihari Lal point (I reproduced that sentence verbatim) but the survey's other quotation from it did not reproduce. Treat the domain as citation-unsafe: usable for corroboration, not as a quotable source.
- The survey's own stated centroid, if it has already been copied into tools/osm-import/config.mjs or any generated content module, will place this temple ~7.4 m west of its footprint and partly into the Parikrama Marg. Worth grepping the import config for 77.69865 before anything else is built on it.
