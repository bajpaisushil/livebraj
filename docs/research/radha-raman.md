# radha-raman — exterior survey

*Researched 2026-09-28 by one agent and independently checked by a second whose brief was to refute it. Labels are the researcher's own: MEASURED / SOURCED / DERIVED / ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN.*

*Survey and checker are paired by the workflow journal's launch key, not by completion order — see BACKLOG 2026-09-30 for why that matters.*

## What it is

A modest two-storey Braj haveli-temple rebuilt in 1826 by a Lucknow banker, hidden at the back of a walled Goswami compound (Radharaman Ghera), whose entire architecture is one ~8.9 m carved sandstone frontispiece on its SOUTH side crowned by a curved bangaldar gable — no shikhara, no visible dome, and essentially nothing visible from the public lane.

## The silhouette

WHAT SURVIVES (SOURCED/MEASURED): The present temple block, 14.65 x 15.18 m, is a courtyard building of 1826. It is intact and in daily use. Behind it (north, reached by a narrow passage) stand the two small late-16th-c. buildings — the original shrine built for Radharaman and a slightly larger one added later — now used as the deity's kitchen, dining room and bedroom (Case, Seeing Krishna, p. 80). These are "very plain, small buildings" and are NOT visible from the courtyard or the lane.

WHAT IS LOST: nothing architectural is recorded as lost. The deity never left Vrindavan (unlike Govind Dev, Gopinath, Madan Mohan, Radha Damodar, which went to Jaipur), so there is no ruined 17th-c. shell here. Two flights of stairs from the courtyard up to the sanctum platform were REMOVED after the Temple Entry Act (Case, p. 82) — that is the only recorded subtraction.

THE ROOFLINE, PLAINLY (ESTIMATED from Commons photographs, see doNotBuild #1):
What crowns this building is a large, flattened, CURVED SANDSTONE GABLE (a bangaldar / chala-derived arched hood) over the central bay of the upper gallery. Its apex sits ~9.3 m above the temple plinth (~9.9 m above the courtyard paving). The curve spans ~5.0 m, rises ~1.3 m above the flanking cornices (rise/span ~0.26, +/-30%), and its ends turn DOWN past the springing rather than dying into a straight line. Its face carries a moulded bead-and-dentil archivolt and a tympanum frieze of ~14 small blind cusped-arch niches following the curve.

Either side of the gable the parapet is FLAT — plain moulded cornices over the two outer bays, top at ~8.4 m.

Behind and below the gable, peeping over the parapet, are at least two (probably three) LOW, WIDE, PALE-CREAM SAUCER DOMES with a scalloped / lotus-petal (gadrooned) skirt. They are wider than tall, they sit LOWER than the gable apex, and they are only partly visible. They are not onion domes and they do not read as domes at all from most angles.

Also on the skyline: four small GILT KALASH FINIALS on thin stalks, tips at ~10.0–10.5 m, standing on the roof behind the gable; and one thin FLAGSTAFF at the west end carrying a dark pennant, top ~10.5 m.

Nothing on this building rises above ~10.5 m. There is no tower, no spire, no amalaka, no urushringa, no gopura, no chhatri-on-columns. The flagstaff is the only element that would clear the neighbouring rooflines.

## The elevation

Only ONE elevation exists architecturally: the SOUTH face, seen from the compound courtyard. The deity inside faces EAST; the doorway is on the SOUTH (SOURCED: Case p. 81 — "The simple doorway on the south side of the temple"). The OSM footprint runs 179.8°/89.8°, i.e. within 0.2° of true cardinal (MEASURED), consistent with deliberate cardinal alignment.

The south wall is in TWO UNEQUAL PARTS (DERIVED from the OSM node split on the south edge, 5.71 m + 8.93 m, corroborated by the photographs):
• West ~5.7 m — BLANK. A tall, windowless, heavily soot-streaked sandstone/plastered wall, ~8.3 m to its cornice, finished with a plain stepped cornice and a row of small pointed merlons (kanguras). Banners are hung on it. Zero openings.
• East ~8.9 m — THE CARVED FRONTISPIECE. Everything worth modelling is here.

FRONTISPIECE, STOREY BY STOREY (heights ESTIMATED by photogrammetry, +/-10–15%):

Plinth: 0.55–0.65 m above the courtyard, 3–4 marble steps, flanked by low carved dado blocks with rosette medallions (the stubs left where the removed stairs once landed).

GROUND STOREY (plinth to chhajja soffit ~5.8 m). Tripartite, symmetrical:
 – Centre bay (~3.3 m wide): one very large MULTI-CUSPED ARCH (9- or 11-foil), apex ~4.1 m, its tympanum now filled by the painted temple signboard. Inside/below it a plain RECTANGULAR doorway ~1.9 m high x ~1.1 m wide with carved jambs, a small deity figurine in a niche above the lintel, and a heavy diamond-studded wooden door. Two small standing male figures holding lotus buds flank it (SOURCED, Case p. 81; the right-hand one is visible in the 2014 photo).
 – Two outer bays (~1.95 m each): each a tall, deeply recessed, BLIND cusped-arch niche (~2.6 m) on a carved dado, with a smaller cusped blind panel above. These are alcoves, not windows.
 OPENING:SOLID — the ground storey is ~8.9 x 5.8 m = 51.6 m² with ~2.1 m² of actual void. That is roughly 1:24, i.e. ~4% open. This solidity is the single most important fact about the elevation.

CHHAJJA / BALCONY SLAB at ~5.8–6.0 m: a deep projecting eave carried on 8–10 large S-scrolled consoles spaced ~0.9–1.1 m, ~0.5 m deep, with carved panels between them.

UPPER STOREY — an open arcaded JHAROKHA GALLERY, floor ~6.0 m, cornice ~8.4 m:
 – Jali balustrade, top ~6.9 m (~0.87 m high): pierced lattice panels between short urn-headed posts.
 – FIVE cusped arches, arranged 1 + 3 + 1. The central THREE sit under the curved gable on a projecting bay ~5.0 m wide, carried on slender turned colonnettes with heavily carved spandrels and pendant cusps. The outer TWO sit back under the flat cornice returns, separated from the centre by broad piers.
 – Each arch ~1.1–1.2 m clear; the lower ~0.9 m of each is filled with jali.
 OPENING:SOLID — the gallery band (~8.9 x 2.4 m = 21.4 m²) is ~27% fully open and ~50% open counting jali.

ARCH GRAMMAR: every arch on this building is a MULTI-CUSPED (multifoil) Mughal-derived arch. Ground-floor centre = large 9/11-foil. Ground-floor flanking niches and all five upper arches = 5- or 7-foil with pendant cusp drops. Nothing here is a plain semicircle, a pointed Gothic arch, or a corbelled Nagara opening. The ONLY curve that is not cusped is the big smooth gable above.

The net read: a SOLID, almost fortified base with one small door, carrying a LIGHT, PIERCED, OPEN gallery, capped by a curved gable. That inversion is what makes it read as a haveli rather than a temple — which is exactly the point (Case p. 79: "it is built to look like a home").

## Material and colour

MATERIAL (SOURCED): "the same light pink sandstone as most of Vrindaban's nineteenth-century buildings" (Case p. 81). Growse records that Braj builders of this period took their shafts from "the Paharpur quarry in Bharatpur territory" (p. 263) — i.e. Bansi Paharpur buff/pink sandstone. The antechamber arches inside are explicitly UNPAINTED sandstone (Case p. 82). Courtyard paving and the inner floor are BLACK AND WHITE MARBLE in a chequer with a diagonal zigzag border. The cream domes read as lime-plastered masonry, not sandstone. Finials are gilt/brass. The courtyard has a SLIDING TIN (corrugated metal) roof (Case p. 81).

COLOURS. All sampled from Wikimedia Commons photos (Ekabhishek 2014 Samsung, Photoshop-processed and warm; Kridha20 2023 vivo, cool and hazy). The two disagree by ~10° of hue and ~20 points of saturation, so I give a MEASURED range and a recommended midpoint. Treat these as good to about +/-8% on V and +/-12 points on S.

WEATHERING IS LOAD-BEARING HERE. The single biggest visual difference between a correct and an incorrect model is vertical soot/water streaking. The blank west wall is almost half-blackened from the parapet down. The carved frontispiece has grime pooled in every recess and bright, near-clean projecting mouldings. A uniform clean sandstone shader will look wrong at any distance.

## The setting

WHAT SURROUNDS IT (MEASURED, OSM way 335527344 and neighbours, ODbL):
• Parikrama Marg runs N–S 9.3 m WEST of the temple's blank west wall. A short residential spur runs EAST off it and dead-ends ~5 m short of the temple's SW corner. These are the only mapped roads within 60 m.
• Keshi Ghat 208 m due NORTH (bearing 2.5°). Jugal Kishor temple 177 m N. Gopinath 121 m NE. The Gokulananda/Radhavinod temple with Gadadhar Pandit's tooth samadhi ~114 m NNW (bearing 341°). Nidhivan 109 m. Chir Ghat 200 m W. Brahma Kund 334 m.
• Yamuna channel 261 m NE; nearest standing water 177 m. The temple is NOT on the riverbank.
• Ten buildings mapped within 150 m; the fabric is continuous. Ground is flat alluvium at ~170 m ASL; no slope, no terracing.

HOW CLOSE THE TOWN COMES: immediately. The temple is a party-wall building in a dense Goswami quarter. Growse lists "Radha Raman" as mahalla no. 9 of Brinda-ban (p. 271) — it is a NEIGHBOURHOOD, not a monument in a precinct. Entwistle: the Goswamis "live nearby, most of them in Radharaman Ghera, a compound around the temple that is entered through a tall gateway" (Braj, p. 412).

THE APPROACH (SOURCED, Case pp. 78–81): you enter Radharaman Ghera from the street through a massive doorway with heavy wooden doors that are closed at night. Inside is a courtyard lined with Goswami HOUSES. A small passage to one side leads to a red sandstone archway and the shrine marking where the deity manifested, with the tree in which the basket of shaligrams hung; next door is the memorial shrine of Gopala Bhatta and the later Goswamis. Crossing the courtyard and passing through a SECOND massive wooden-doored gateway brings you into a second courtyard, also lined with houses, with a communal hand-pump. Only then do you face the temple — "a simple sandstone structure at first glance hardly distinguishable from the houses around it."

So: gate → courtyard of houses → gate → courtyard of houses → facade. Two thresholds, two compressions, then release.

IS IT VISIBLE FROM ANY DISTANCE? No. Not from the lane, not from the Parikrama Marg, not from Keshi Ghat, not from the river. Neighbouring Goswami houses are 2–4 storeys and several have been rebuilt TALLER than the temple. The only element that might clear the surrounding rooflines is the thin flagstaff. Plan the camera work accordingly: there is no hero shot of this building from outside, and any long view you invent is fiction.

PRESENT-DAY CLUTTER IN THE COURTYARD (visible in the 2023 photographs, and load-bearing for 1:1 realism): a painted arch-shaped signboard over the door reading "स्वयंभू श्री राधारमण मन्दिर / Swayambhoo Shri Radharaman Mandir"; a yellow "वर्जित क्षेत्र" prohibition notice; a large red vinyl banner with UPI/Paytm/PhonePe/BHIM QR logos and the temple phone number; a second banner warning about monkeys and pickpockets; a small CCTV camera and a horn loudspeaker bracketed onto the carved stone; overhead electrical cables slung diagonally across the courtyard at ~4–5 m; steel pipe handrails on the entrance steps; shoes left in rows on the marble. In the 2014 photograph the east neighbour was a bamboo-scaffolded building site with corrugated iron sheeting; by 2023 it is a finished pale-stone Goswami house. Model the 2023 state.

## What NOT to build

- NO SHIKHARA. There is no tower of any kind, and never was one on this building. What crowns it is a curved sandstone gable at ~9.3 m. WHY YOU'D GET THIS WRONG: it is one of the Seven Temples, its infobox says 'Rajasthani architecture', and Hindi sources say शिखर meaning simply 'the top'. Note also that the four Vrindavan temples ASI actually protects for their spires — Govind Deo, Jugal Kishor, Madan Mohan, Radha Ballabh — do NOT include Radha Raman (ASI CPM list, Mathura district entries 116–119). Its absence from that list is the negative evidence.
- NO ONION DOME, NO GOLD SPIRE. There ARE small low cream saucer domes with a gadrooned skirt and four gilt kalash finials — but they sit BEHIND the curved gable, are wider than tall, and never break the skyline. WHY YOU'D GET THIS WRONG: exactly the Govind Dev mistake in reverse. Here the dome-ish things exist but are nearly invisible; last time the dome existed and was cased in a flat block. Always resolve this from a photograph, never from a description.
- DO NOT PUT THE FAMOUS FACADE ON THE LANE. The 5.7 m road-facing edge in the OSM data is the BLANK western part of the south wall, not the entrance elevation. The compound (Radharaman Ghera) is entered from the street through a tall gateway; the temple stands at the back behind ONE OR TWO courtyards lined with Goswami houses (Case pp. 78–80; Entwistle p. 412). From the public lane you see a gateway and blank walls.
- DO NOT BUILD GROWSE'S DESCRIPTION OF 'THE TEMPLE OF RADHA RAMAN'. Growse's index says 'Radha-Raman, temple of, 262' — but pp. 262–63 describe the SHAHJI temple, Sah Kundan Lal's Italianate marble building with 'a colonnade of spiral marble pillars' and a facade 'resembling nothing so much as a disreputable London casino'. That is a different building on the other side of Jaisingh Ghera, and it is already queued on this project. Building it here means building it twice, in the wrong place, in the wrong material. Growse gives the 16th-century Radha Raman NO architectural notice at all — he mentions it only as Gopal Bhatt's foundation (pp. 198–99).
- DO NOT DATE THE FABRIC TO 1542. 1542 is the APPEARANCE OF THE DEITY, and that is what the sign over the door records (प्राकट्य: वैशाख शुक्ल पूर्णिमा सं. १५९९). The present building is 1826, by Shah Biharilal of Lucknow (Entwistle, Braj, pp. 219 and 412). The only 16th-century fabric on site is two plain small sheds behind the temple, now the deity's kitchen and bedroom, invisible from the courtyard. A 1542 aesthetic — Govind Dev's red sandstone Akbari-period masonry — is three centuries wrong here.
- DO NOT MAKE IT VAST, AND DO NOT PUT IT ON THE RIVERBANK. Hindi aggregators say 'यह विशाल मंदिर नदी किनारे स्थित है' (this vast temple stands on the riverbank). It is 222 m² and ~10 m tall, and the Yamuna is 261 m away with a dense town in between. Travel-site prose is not a source.
- DO NOT BUILD ANYTHING 169.77 m TALL. That number is in the Wikipedia infobox as `elevation_m` — it is the GROUND ELEVATION of Vrindavan above sea level. Rendered summaries turn it into 'Height: 169.77 m (557 feet)'. Use it as terrain datum, never as building height.
- NO RADHA MURTI, AND NO RADHA–KRISHNA PAIR ON THE FACADE. Entwistle, p. 412: 'There is no accompanying image of Radha; instead her presence is indicated by a mukut [crown].' English Wikipedia's claim that the temple 'houses the original Shaligram deity of Krishna alongside goddess Radha' is wrong. This kills the instinct to carve a Radha-Krishna relief over the door.
- DO NOT LEAVE THE INNER COURTYARD OPEN TO THE SKY IN A ROOF-LEVEL OR AERIAL LOD. It has a SLIDING TIN ROOF, closed at night and in bad weather (Case p. 81). From above, the centre of this building is corrugated metal over a ~10.7 m square, not a dark open well.
- DO NOT GIVE THE GROUND STOREY WINDOWS. It is ~4% open — one small door ~1.9 x 1.1 m, and two BLIND cusped niches that look like openings in a low-res photo but are solid recessed alcoves. Opening:solid is about 1:24. Adding windows destroys the whole point of the elevation, which is a fortified base carrying a pierced gallery.
- DO NOT CARVE THE WHOLE SOUTH WALL. Only the eastern ~8.9 m is the carved frontispiece. The western ~5.7 m is a blank, heavily soot-blackened wall with a plain stepped cornice and small merlons, used to hang banners on. Carving the full 14.65 m makes it read as a palace.
- DO NOT GIVE THE TEMPLE AND ITS EAST NEIGHBOUR THE SAME MATERIAL. The Goswami house immediately east was rebuilt between 2014 and 2023 in pale, clean, crisply machine-cut stone (measured ~#C3B4A2, S 17%) against the temple's darker weathered sandstone (~#A98A6B, S 37%). If they share a shader they will merge into one building and the temple will disappear.
- DO NOT RESTORE THE SANCTUM STAIRS. Two flights ran from the courtyard up to the platform for about a century and were REMOVED when the Temple Entry Act required temples to open to all Hindus (Case p. 82). Only Goswamis and temple servants go up now. The carved dado blocks flanking the door are where they landed. This matters if the interior is ever modelled.
- DO NOT EXPRESS THE UNBROKEN FIRE ON THE EXTERIOR. No chimney, no smoke stack, no visible hearth, no glow on the facade. The fire is in a 3.05 m pit inside the OLD temple behind the present block, reached by a narrow passage, and no outsider is admitted (Case p. 95; Vrindavan Today). It is fuelled with cow-dung cakes and banked overnight. At most you get faint smoke from a roof vent, and even that is my inference, not a source.
- DO NOT PAINT THE EXTERIOR PORTICOS PINK AND GREEN. Case (p. 81) records that the three porticos 'were recently painted a slightly surprising bright pink and green' — that is the INNER courtyard. The exterior arches are explicitly unpainted sandstone (p. 82).
- DO NOT ADD ASI SIGNAGE, BLUE PLAQUES OR PROTECTION RAILINGS. Radha Raman is NOT a centrally protected monument; it does not appear anywhere in the ASI CPM list. Brindaban's protected four are Govind Deo, Jugal Kishor, Madan Mohan and Radha Ballabh. It is a living temple run by a Goswami panchayat.
- DO NOT MAKE THE APPROACH GRAND. No axis, no plaza, no vista, no processional steps. The lane is residential and roughly 3 m wide; Entwistle notes the Gokulananda/Radhavinod temple sits 'along the narrow street that begins in front of the gateway to Radharaman Ghera'. The drama is compression — gate, courtyard, gate, courtyard — not approach.
- DO NOT USE PLAIN OR POINTED ARCHES. Every arch on this building is MULTI-CUSPED (5-, 7-, 9- or 11-foil) with pendant cusp drops and carved spandrel rosettes. The only non-cusped curve on the whole elevation is the smooth gable itself. A pointed or round-headed arch anywhere reads instantly as the wrong century and the wrong region.
- DO NOT PUT THE DOOR ON THE WEST SIDE FACING THE PARIKRAMA MARG. The west wall is blank. The door is on the SOUTH; the deity faces EAST (Case p. 81–82).
- DO NOT SHADE IT AS CLEAN SANDSTONE. Vertical soot and water streaking is the dominant visual signal — the blank west wall is close to half-black from the parapet down, and grime pools in every recess of the carving while the projecting mouldings stay bright. A uniform stone shader will look wrong even at 30 m.

## Uncertain — flag, do not assert

- WHO BUILT THE PRESENT TEMPLE, AND WHEN. Two accounts conflict. (a) Entwistle (Braj, pp. 219, 412, citing A. L. Kapur, Vraj ke bhakti, vol. 3 p. 4): built 1826 by Shah Biharilal of Lucknow, grandfather of Kundanlal and Phundanlal who built Shahji Mandir in 1868. Hindi sources repeat this ('वर्तमान ढांचा 1826 में लखनऊ निवासी शाह बिहारी लालजी द्वारा'). (b) Case (Seeing Krishna, pp. 80–81) reports the Goswamis' own founding story: Shah Kundan Lal, finance minister to Wajid Ali Shah, built the marble Shahji temple first, the Goswamis refused to move the deity into it, and he then 'resigned himself to building the modest sandstone temple'. Case flags in her own endnote 8 that Entwistle differs. (b) is chronologically difficult — Wajid Ali Shah was deposed in 1856 and Kundanlal settled in Vrindavan that year, so a Kundanlal-built sandstone temple would postdate 1868, which Growse (writing in 1883 and describing the marble temple as newly finished) does not support. I go with 1826/Biharilal but both are secondary sources; I found no inscription and no primary document.
- ALL HEIGHTS. Every vertical figure is photogrammetry from two uncalibrated phone photographs, scaled against standing adults assumed at 1.68 m. Both cameras were low and close, which foreshortens upper features. Confidence +/-10–15%, worse above the chhajja. The gable's rise/span in particular ranged from 0.26 to 0.38 between the two photos; I took the flatter reading because the wider shot has less convergence.
- THE 8.93 m FRONTISPIECE WIDTH. This is DERIVED from an OSM node splitting the south edge at 5.71/8.93 m, matched to the visible break between blank wall and carved wall in the photographs, and cross-checked against my bay arithmetic (which closes at ~8.9 m). But the node may simply be a shared vertex from an adjacent trace. Error could be +/-1.5 m.
- THE CREAM DOMES. I can see at least two, probably three, low gadrooned domes above the parapet in the 2023 photographs. I cannot prove they belong to the temple rather than to the Goswami house or the old temple behind it. Two of them sit on what looks like the temple's own roofline. Their colour is INFERRED, not measured — every sample clipped against a blown white sky.
- THE GILT FINIALS. Clearly metallic and yellow in the crops, four of them on thin stalks. Every pixel sample clipped to white, so the hex value I give is a conventional brass, not a measurement. I also cannot tell what they stand on — the sanctum roof, small kiosks, or the old temple behind.
- WHERE THE OUTER GATEWAY IS. Entwistle says 'a tall gateway'; Case says 'a massive doorway with heavy wooden doors'. Neither gives a bearing. OSM shows the Parikrama Marg 9.3 m west and an 11 m spur lane running east off it, dead-ending 5 m short of the temple's SW corner — which argues for a western or south-western approach. Entwistle's note that the Gokulananda/Radhavinod street 'begins in front of the gateway' and the OSM POI for that temple at 114 m NNW argues for a northern or north-western one. I could not resolve it.
- WHERE THE SAMADHI AND THE APPEARANCE-PLACE SHRINE ARE. Case and Entwistle both say they are near the entrance to the Ghera, off the first courtyard, reached by a small passage, with a red sandstone archway and the tree that held the basket. Bharatdiscovery says they are to the SOUTH (दक्षिण) of the temple. OSM puts a 'Sri Gopal Bhatta Samadhi Mandir' polygon, 10.25 x 13.56 m, 22–43 m NORTH. These cannot all be right. OSM in this quarter is low quality and I would not trust its POI placement.
- WHETHER CASE'S 35 ft COURTYARD FITS. A 10.67 m square inside a 14.65 x 15.18 m envelope leaves ~2.0–2.3 m per side for portico plus sanctum. Buildable, but tight. Either the courtyard is nearer 10.0 m or the footprint is slightly under-traced.
- NUMBER OF STOREYS. The south facade reads unambiguously as two — solid ground storey plus open gallery. Case describes the haveli type as 'one, two, or three stories built around an inner courtyard' without saying which this is. There may be a partial third level or roof terrace at the rear that no photograph I found shows.
- THE APPEARANCE DATE ITSELF. The temple's own signboard reads Samvat 1599 (1542 CE), which is what Entwistle and Wikipedia give. Brajrasik.org says the deity appeared in Samvat 1591 (1534 CE) and the temple was built in Samvat 1645 (1588 CE). Case's endnote 6 adds that the temple received a land grant from Akbar in 1598 (Mukherji & Habib, PIHC 48). I use 1542 but the sectarian sources are not unanimous.
- I FOUND NO MEASURED DRAWING, NO ASI CONSERVATION REPORT AND NO PUBLISHED PLAN OF THIS BUILDING. Growse published plans of Govind Dev, Jugal Kishor, Madan Mohan, Radha Ballabh, Radha Gopal and Radha Indra Kishor and did not draw this one. That silence is itself informative — in 1883 it was an unremarkable 57-year-old haveli — but it means there is no authoritative geometry to check my estimates against.

## Independent check

Confidence: HIGH on the architecture and on source verification — I did not take the survey's word for anything load-bearing. I downloaded the three Commons exteriors at full resolution and examined the roofline, gallery and cornice myself; pulled Growse, Entwistle and Case as full text and grepped the actual sentences; extracted the ASI PDF and searched it; exported the Wikipedia wikitext; and re-queried Overpass and recomputed the footprint geometry, bearings, road clearances and neighbour distances locally. The centroid error, the Yamuna bearing, the Madan Mohan/Karauli error, the Paharpur mis-citation, the interior-stairs error and the carved west-wall cornice are all things I derived independently and would stand behind. MEDIUM on three: the srimandir misquote (a single WebFetch through a small model, and the page could have changed since the survey was written — worth one manual look before acting on it); the Gokulananda POI (OSM naming in this quarter is poor enough that both nodes may be mislabelled upstream); and the 9-foil arch count, which is a cusp-counting judgement on a compressed JPEG. OVERALL this is a strong survey — the crowning element, the thing that has gone wrong three times, is correct this time and I verified it from photographs rather than prose, and the Growse index trap is a genuinely valuable catch that I confirmed word for word. My corrections are mostly in the geo-data and the citation chain, not in the building.

### Corrections to the survey above

- CENTROID IS ~7.4 m OFF — the single most consequential error for a 1:1 build. The survey gives the OSM footprint centroid as 27.58538 N, 77.69865 E. I re-queried way 335527344 via Overpass and computed the polygon area centroid: that stated point is NOT the centroid, it is essentially the midpoint of the WEST wall (it sits 0.01 m from the west edge). True area centroid is 27.585379 N, 77.698725 E — 7.35 m further east. Placing the building on the published coordinate shifts the whole temple ~7.4 m west, i.e. onto the Parikrama Marg. This also explains the survey's own unresolved '~12 m' disagreement with the Wikipedia coordinate (27°35'7.3"N 77°41'55.6"E): from the stated point the gap is 12.8 m, from the true centroid it is 5.6 m. Use 77.69872 E.
- THE YAMUNA IS NORTH-WEST, NOT NORTH-EAST. The survey's setting section states 'Yamuna channel 261 m NE'. Overpass gives the nearest point of the mapped Yamuna way at 260.8 m, bearing 316.9° — the distance is right to within a metre, the direction is ~90° wrong. Corroborated by the survey's own other bearings, which I re-derived and which are correct: Chir Ghat due WEST (273.8°), Keshi Ghat due NORTH (2.5°). The river wraps the town round the N and W of this site; it is never NE of it. This matters for sun/water direction and any camera work.
- MADAN MOHAN WENT TO KARAULI, NOT JAIPUR. The silhouette section says the deity never left Vrindavan 'unlike Govind Dev, Gopinath, Madan Mohan, Radha Damodar, which went to Jaipur'. Entwistle's own text (which the survey cites elsewhere) says otherwise: 'Madanmohan was taken to Karauli in 1728 by Raja Gopal Singh' and, of the Madanmohan Goswamis, 'they went with their deity to Jaipur and Karauli'. Govind Dev, Gopinath and Radha Damodar → Jaipur is correct; Madan Mohan → Karauli. This is exactly the wrong-place class of error the project has already been burned by, and Madan Mohan is itself a Vrindavan temple this project will model.
- THE SRIMANDIR QUOTATION DOES NOT REPRODUCE. doNotBuild #6 is built on a quoted Hindi sentence, 'यह विशाल मंदिर नदी किनारे स्थित है' (this vast temple stands on the riverbank), attributed to srimandir.com. Fetching that page, the 1826 sentence quoted elsewhere in the survey IS there verbatim ('मंदिर का वर्तमान ढांचा 1826 में लखनऊ निवासी शाह बिहारी लालजी द्वारा निर्मित करवाया गया था'), but the 'vast/riverbank' sentence is not. What the page says is 'यह मंदिर एक प्राकृतिक सौंदर्ययुक्त क्षेत्र, नदी के समीप स्थित है' — 'near a river', with no विशाल (vast) and no नदी किनारे (on the bank). The ADVICE is independently correct (222 m², river 261 m away, dense town between) so keep the rule — but the quoted evidence for it is not accurate and should be removed or re-sourced.
- THE PAHARPUR QUARRY CITATION IS OVER-GENERALISED. The materials section says 'Growse records that Braj builders of this period took their shafts from the Paharpur quarry in Bharatpur territory (p. 263)'. In Growse's actual text that sentence belongs to ONE specific building — the temple of Radha Indra Kishor, built by Rani Indrajit Kunvar and completed at the end of 1871: 'The piers are composed of four conjoined pillars, each shaft being a single piece of stone, brought from the Paharpur quarry in Bharatpur territory.' Growse makes no general claim about Braj builders and says nothing whatever about Radha Raman's stone. Bansi Paharpur is a plausible guess for a Braj building of this date, but it is inference, not a record, and should be relabelled.
- THE REMOVED STAIRS WERE INTERIOR — THEY DID NOT LAND ON THE FACADE. The elevation section describes the exterior plinth as 'flanked by low carved dado blocks with rosette medallions (the stubs left where the removed stairs once landed)', and doNotBuild #13 repeats it. Case p.82 puts those stairs inside: 'For about a century, two sets of stairs led from the courtyard up to the platform' — that is the inner 35-ft courtyard rising to the 4.5-ft antechamber platform that only Goswamis now use. Nothing in Case places any stair on the outer south face. doNotBuild #13 even contradicts itself, asserting the blocks flanking the outer door are where they landed and then adding 'This matters if the interior is ever modelled.' The dados flanking the outer door are ordinary carved dados; do not model them as stair stubs.
- CASE'S 'FOUR STEPS' ARE INSIDE THE VESTIBULE, NOT THE PLINTH STEPS. The sources entry credits Case p.81 with 'four steps' in the same breath as the plinth, and the elevation gives 'Plinth: 0.55–0.65 m above the courtyard, 3–4 marble steps'. Case actually writes: 'Just inside is a small vestibule, where shoes and socks are to be left—for none may be worn in the temple—and to the right a small flight of four steps leads to the interior of the temple.' Those four steps are internal and turn right. Exterior plinth steps do exist and are visible in both photographs, so the geometry survives, but Case is not a source for them and the citation should be dropped.
- THE WEST WALL'S CORNICE IS NOT PLAIN. The elevation and doNotBuild #11 describe the blank western ~5.7 m as finished with 'a plain stepped cornice and a row of small pointed merlons (kanguras)'. At full resolution the 2023 frontal photograph (Radha Raman Temple 2.jpg, 4080x2296) shows a carved TWO-BAND cornice running the entire width of that wall: an upper row of small pointed dentil-like motifs above a continuous frieze of larger multi-cusped blind arcading, over a projecting string course. 'Blank, zero openings' and 'heavily soot-streaked' are both strongly confirmed — but shading that cornice as plain would drop a real and conspicuous carved band. The rule 'do not carve the full 14.65 m' is still right; the top ~1 m of the west wall is the exception.
- THE 114 m NNW POI IS NOT THE GOKULANANDA TEMPLE. The setting section lists, as MEASURED, 'The Gokulananda/Radhavinod temple with Gadadhar Pandit's tooth samadhi ~114 m NNW (bearing 341°)'. Overpass returns at exactly 114 m / 341.0° a node named 'Храм Вамши Гопала и Гададхарв Дант самадхи' — Vamshi Gopala temple plus Gadadhar tooth samadhi. The node actually named Gokulananda, 'श्री राधा गोकुलानंद मंदिर', is at 92 m, bearing 8.2° (essentially due north). The survey then leans on the 341° figure in its `uncertain` section to argue for a north-western outer gateway; that argument rests on the wrong POI and should be redone against the 92 m / 8° node.
- UPPER-GALLERY ARCHES ARE ABOUT 9-FOIL, NOT '5- OR 7-FOIL'. The elevation states 'Ground-floor flanking niches and all five upper arches = 5- or 7-foil with pendant cusp drops.' Counting cusps on the full-resolution frontal photograph, each gallery arch reads as roughly 9 lobes (possibly 11). doNotBuild #18 gives the correct range (5-, 7-, 9- or 11-foil), so only the elevation text needs fixing — but a 7-foil arch modelled where a 9-foil belongs is visible at close range across five openings.
- 'THE DOMES NEVER BREAK THE SKYLINE' IS NOT SUPPORTED. The silhouette says the cream saucer domes 'sit LOWER than the gable apex' and 'never break the skyline'. In the frontal 2023 photograph the left cream dome reads level with, or marginally above, the gable apex — and because it stands further back, a low camera would render an equal-height object LOWER, so level-in-image implies at-least-equal-in-fact. The four gilt finials are unambiguously above the gable apex, which the survey's own numbers concede (finials 10.0–10.5 m vs gable 9.3 m). Keep the domes low and wide, but drop the claim that nothing behind the gable reaches the skyline.
- FALSE PRECISION ON HEIGHTS. Every vertical is quoted to 0.1 m (4.1 m, 6.9 m, 8.4 m, 9.3 m) under a declared ±10–15% band, which is ±0.9 m at gable height. My own independent scale check on the 2023 photograph — standing adult (~1.70 m) against the doorway — gives a door of ~2.0 m against the stated 1.9 m, and a gable apex 8.8–10.5 m above the courtyard paving against the stated 9.9 m. The figures are defensible as central estimates and I am not disputing them, but they should be written as ranges, because at present they read as measurements and a downstream builder will treat them as such. (Same for the footprint: I get 15.27 m N–S and 223.4 m² against the stated 15.18 m and 222.3 m².)

### Independently confirmed

- WHAT CROWNS THE BUILDING — VERIFIED DIRECTLY FROM THE PHOTOGRAPHS, NOT FROM DESCRIPTION. I downloaded the three Commons exteriors at full resolution and examined the roofline. There is a single large smooth curved gable over the central bay; its ends turn DOWN past the springing (the bangaldar signature, clearly visible on the right-hand return); it carries a moulded bead-and-dentil archivolt and a tympanum frieze of small blind cusped niches following the curve, of which I counted ~12 with both ends cropped, consistent with the stated ~14. There is NO shikhara, no spire, no amalaka, no urushringa, no chhatri anywhere on the building. This is the claim the project has got wrong three times and this time it is right. Note that a travel-site claim of 'a dome-shaped roof and a gold-plated spire' did surface in my search — doNotBuild #2 correctly anticipates exactly that.
- THE REST OF THE SKYLINE IS ALSO REAL. Visible in the 2023 photographs at full resolution: low, wide, pale-cream domes with a distinctly scalloped / lotus-petal (gadrooned) skirt, wider than tall, sitting behind the gable; four small gilt kalash finials on thin stalks, yellow-metallic against the sky; and a thin flagstaff at the west end carrying a dark pennant. All four elements exist as described. The survey's caution that it cannot prove the domes and finials belong to the temple rather than to the old temple or a Goswami house behind is appropriate — though their symmetrical placement flanking the gable argues they are the temple's own.
- THE FIVE-ARCH GALLERY, 1 + 3 + 1, IS CORRECT. I counted independently in both the 2014 photograph and the more frontal 2023 one: one recessed outer arch, a broad pier, three arches on the projecting central bay carried on slender turned colonnettes, a return pier, one recessed outer arch. Also confirmed: jali filling the lower portion of each opening, a pierced diagonal-lattice balustrade between short urn-headed posts, heavily carved spandrels with rosettes, pendant cusp drops, and the deep chhajja on large S-scrolled consoles. The small deity figurine in a niche above the door lintel is there too.
- GROWSE'S INDEX TRAP IS REAL AND EXACTLY AS DESCRIBED — the most valuable item in the survey. I pulled Growse's full text and grepped it. 'Radha-Raman, temple of, 262' is the ONLY index entry for the name. Page 262 reads: 'the temple of Radhá Raman, completed within the last few years. The founder is Sah Kundan Lal, of Lakhnau, who has built on a design suggested by the modern secular buildings of that city... fronted with a colonnade of spiral marble pillars, each shaft being of a single piece', continuing over the page 'resembles nothing so much as a disreputable London casino... Ten lakhs of rupees are said to have been wasted on its construction.' That is the Shahji temple. Growse's only genuine Radha Raman references are pp. 198–99 ('Gopal Bhatt, founder of the temple of Radha Raman'; 'Gopal Bhatt, who beautified the temple of Radha Raman with all that he possessed'), the festival entry 'Sravan Badi 5. — Radha Raman Ji ka dhio dhio', and mahalla no. 9 at p.271. Every one of these quotations is verbatim correct, and Growse gives the 16th-c temple no architectural notice at all. Given that research/shahji.md already exists in this repo, the double-build risk is live and this warning must stay attached.
- THE ASI NEGATIVE EVIDENCE CHECKS OUT. I downloaded the CPM list PDF and extracted it. Mathura district entries 116–119 are, exactly and in order, Temple of Govind Deo, Temple of Jugal Kishore, Temple of Madan Mohan, Temple of Radha Ballabh — all Brindaban. A full-text search of the document returns no 'Radha Raman' or 'Radharaman' anywhere (the other Radha-named entries are Bengal monuments and a J&K site). So doNotBuild #16 — no ASI signage, no blue plaque, no protection railings — is correctly grounded.
- THE WIKIPEDIA TRAPS ARE BOTH PRESENT IN THE RAW WIKITEXT. I exported the article source: `elevation_m = 169.77` and `architecture = [[Rajasthani architecture` are both there, and the body does say 'The temple houses the original Shaligram deity of Krishna alongside goddess Radha.' Entwistle p.412 flatly contradicts the last: 'There is no accompanying image of Radha; instead her presence is indicated by a mukut.' The infobox also gives `creator = Gopala Bhatta Goswami`, which is the wrong-founder hazard for the 1826 fabric. All three doNotBuild items built on these (#7, #8, and the 1826 dating) are well founded.
- ENTWISTLE IS QUOTED VERBATIM AND ACCURATELY. I pulled the full text and read both passages. p.219: 'In 1826 a Seth named Biharilal, who was given the title of Shah by the Nawab of Lucknow, built a new temple in Vrindaban for the deity of Radharaman. His two grandsons, Kundanlal and Phundanlal, settled in Vrindaban in 1856... In 1868 Lalitkishori built a temple in the italianate style.' p.412: the tall gateway, Radharaman Ghera, the samadhi of Gopal Bhatt near the entrance, 'There is no accompanying image of Radha; instead her presence is indicated by a mukut', and 'The present temple was built in 1826 by Shah Biharilal of Lucknow, grandfather of Kundanlal and Phundanlal, the founders of Shahji temple.' All correct, including the §32 note that the Radhavinod/Gokulanand street 'begins in front of the gateway to Radharaman Ghera'.
- CASE IS QUOTED ACCURATELY THROUGHOUT — I checked every architectural claim against the PDF. Confirmed verbatim: 'built of the same light pink sandstone as most of Vrindaban's nineteenth-century buildings'; 'The simple doorway on the south side of the temple... is flanked by two standing male figures holding lotus buds'; 'an inner courtyard about thirty-five feet square, partially open to the sky but at night and in bad weather covered by a sliding tin roof'; the haveli style as 'one, two, or three stories built around an inner courtyard'; 'a checkerboard of black and white marble'; 'a marble platform about four and a half feet high'; 'surrounded on the other three sides by porticos, each with three arches that were recently painted a slightly surprising bright pink and green'; the antechamber's 'three cusped arches, these of unpainted sandstone'; 'an embossed silver double-leaved door'; 'There the deity stands facing east'; the Temple Entry Act and the removal of two sets of stairs; 'a ten-foot-long fire pit in the old temple behind the present one'; 'And it is built to look like a home'; and the two late-16th-century 'very plain, small buildings' behind, 'now used as the kitchen, dining room, and bedroom for the deity'. Also confirmed is Case's own Shah Kundan Lal account (p.80) which the survey correctly reports as conflicting with Entwistle.
- THE CHRONOLOGICAL ARGUMENT AGAINST CASE'S ATTRIBUTION IS SOUND. The survey rejects Case's Kundan Lal founding story on the grounds that Wajid Ali Shah was deposed in 1856, Kundanlal settled in Vrindavan that year, Shahji was built in 1868, so a Kundanlal-built sandstone temple would have to postdate 1868 — which Growse, writing in 1883 and describing the marble temple as newly finished, does not support. I verified each link (Entwistle's 1856 and 1868 dates; Growse's 'completed within the last few years'). The reasoning holds, and the survey is right to go with 1826/Biharilal while flagging it unresolved rather than asserting it.
- THE FOOTPRINT AND MOST SETTING DISTANCES RE-DERIVE, SEVERAL EXACTLY. Re-querying Overpass and computing locally: wall bearings 179.8° and 89.8° (exact match); south edge splitting 5.71 + 8.92 m (exact match); Parikram Marg at 9.39 m from the footprint edge at bearing 270.0°, i.e. due west, matching the stated '9.3 m WEST of the blank west wall'; ten buildings mapped within 150 m (exact); Keshi Ghat 210 m at 2.5°; Jugal Kishor 178 m at 1.3°; Gopinath 121 m at 52.2°; Chir Ghat 199 m at 273.8°; nearest standing water 180 m (stated 177). The OSM way carries wikidata Q7280191 and the name 'Sri Radha Raman Mandir'.
- THE SIGNBOARD READING IS CORRECT, AND I FOUND AN INDEPENDENT CROSS-CHECK THE SURVEY MISSED. At full resolution the 2023 board reads 'प्राकट्य : वैशाख शुक्ल पूर्णिमा सं. १५९९' exactly as quoted — Samvat 1599 = 1542 CE. (I initially misread the much blurrier 2014 board as १५६६ and was wrong.) Independently corroborating it: the red banner in the same 2023 photograph advertises the '481 वां प्राकट्योत्सव' dated 05 मई 2023 — and 2023 − 481 = 1542. So doNotBuild #5, that 1542 is the appearance of the deity and not the date of the fabric, is solidly anchored in the temple's own signage.
- THE COLOUR WORK IS HONEST AND REPRODUCIBLE — including, importantly, where it declines to measure. Re-sampling the same two photographs myself: soot on the west wall comes back near-neutral at S 4% (survey: S 4% — exact); sandstone in self-shadow at H 26° / S 39% against the survey's recommended H 28° / S 37%; the east neighbour measurably paler and less saturated than the temple (S 11% vs S 20–39%), which supports doNotBuild #12. Critically, I verified the survey's own caveat: sampling the cream domes and the gilt finials returns 51–70% of pixels clipped at or above 250, so they genuinely cannot be measured from these images — and the survey labels them INFERRED and low-confidence rather than passing them off as measurements. That is the right call, correctly disclosed.
- ALL CITED SOURCES EXIST AND ARE CORRECTLY ATTRIBUTED. I resolved every Commons file through the API: Entrance Radha Raman Temple, Vrindavan.jpg (Ekabhishek, 2014-10-25, 2448x3264), Radha Raman Temple.jpg and Radha Raman Temple 2.jpg (Kridha20, 2023-06-17, 4080x2296), Radha Raman Temple, Vrindavan.jpg (2009, 1600x1200), and Radharaman Temple at Vrindavan.jpg (Imakanksha, 2025) — all present with the stated authors, dates and dimensions. Growse, Entwistle, Case and the ASI list all resolve at the given URLs. No fabricated sources.
- THE ARITHMETIC IS INTERNALLY CONSISTENT. The 4%-open ground storey checks: 8.9 x 5.8 = 51.6 m², door 1.9 x 1.1 = 2.09 m², ratio 4.05% ≈ 1:24, as stated. The courtyard-fits-envelope tension the survey raises is real and correctly computed (a 10.67 m courtyard inside 14.65 x 15.18 m leaves 1.99 m E–W and 2.26 m N–S per side), and I agree with its resolution — trust the OSM envelope, shrink the courtyard — though even at 10.0 m the depth left for a sanctum is uncomfortably tight and this should stay open.

### Contamination risks

- Growse's index is a permanent trap: 'Radha-Raman, temple of, 262' points at the Shahji temple, and the p.262 sentence itself names the building 'the temple of Radha Raman'. Any researcher, LLM or citation-follower that reaches Growse via that index will describe Shahji's spiral marble pillars and Italianate facade as Radha Raman's. This project already has research/shahji.md queued, so the failure mode is building the same temple twice, in the wrong place, in the wrong material. Keep this warning bound to the asset permanently, not just in this survey.
- Wikimedia Commons is contaminated the same way. My Commons search for 'Radha Raman Temple Vrindavan' returned about 25 Shahji temple files against only 5 genuine ones, including File:Chhote Radha Raman Deities Shahji Temple Vrindavan.jpg — a file whose title contains 'Radha Raman' but which was photographed at Shahji. Any future image-harvesting step for this temple must filter on the Shahji name, not just on 'Radha Raman'.
- Wikipedia's infobox field `elevation_m = 169.77` is rendered by summarisers and knowledge panels as 'Height: 169.77 m (557 feet)'. The survey flags it, and the value really is in the raw wikitext — but this will keep resurfacing every time anyone re-reads the article. It is the ground elevation of Vrindavan; the building is ~10 m.
- Wikipedia's body text asserts the temple 'houses the original Shaligram deity of Krishna alongside goddess Radha', which Entwistle contradicts (Radha is present as a mukut, not a murti). Any AI-generated summary of that article will reintroduce a Radha figure, and the instinct to carve a Radha–Krishna relief over the door follows directly from it.
- Travel aggregators assert both 'a dome-shaped roof and a gold-plated spire' and 'built of red sandstone and white marble'. I hit the first of these in my own search. Both are wrong — the photographs show a curved gable with low cream domes and gilt kalash finials, and Case specifies light pink sandstone. These claims are high-ranking in search and will keep coming back.
- OSM in this quarter is sparse, hand-traced and partly Russian-language pilgrim mapping, with at least four similarly-named religious POIs inside 120 m: 'Radha-Ramana' (37 m, distinct from the temple way), 'Sri Gopal Bhatta Samadhi Mandir' (36 m), 'श्री राधा गोकुलानंद मंदिर' (92 m) and 'Храм Вамши Гопала и Гададхарв Дант самадхи' (114 m). The survey already tripped on this once — it read the Vamshi Gopala node as Gokulananda. Any POI lookup here must be checked by name in the original script, not by distance alone.
- Specifically: the separate 'Radha-Ramana' POI 37 m east of the temple way is a snapping hazard. If an importer matches the landmark by name it may bind the temple to that node rather than to way 335527344, compounding the centroid error already noted.
- srimandir.com — the project has already been warned that this domain conflates Radha Madan Mohan with the Jaipur temple. Its Radha Raman page is reliable on the 1826/Shah Bihari Lal point (I reproduced that sentence verbatim) but the survey's other quotation from it did not reproduce. Treat the domain as citation-unsafe: usable for corroboration, not as a quotable source.
- The survey's own stated centroid, if it has already been copied into tools/osm-import/config.mjs or any generated content module, will place this temple ~7.4 m west of its footprint and partly into the Parikrama Marg. Worth grepping the import config for 77.69865 before anything else is built on it.

## Built — 2026-10-02 (RadhaRamanMandir.js)

What was here before: the shared haveli (Radha Damodar and Radha Shyamsundar
come through it too) with a walled ring drawn round it on a guess, turned 90
degrees.

**Where and which way.** On the checker's true area centroid of OSM way
335527344, in a frame with +lx EAST and +lz SOUTH (rot 0): the door is on the
south, the deity faces east. The block is the way's own 14.7 x 15.3 m, its
south edge split at the node into the blank west 5.71 m and the carved east
8.93 m.

**The facade**, as the survey and checker give it: the blank west part to
8.3 m with the checker's carved two-band cornice (string course, a frieze of
blind cusped arcading, small pointed dentils, merlons), soot streaks from the
cornice down, the 2023 red vinyl banner and a yellow notice; the frontispiece
— a solid ground storey (the great 9-foil arch to 4.1 m with the painted
board in its tympanum, the 1.1 x 2.0 m door with carved jambs, the niche over

at 5.8-6.0 m on nine consoles, the gallery of five ~9-foil arches 1 + 3 + 1
with jali in their lower 0.87 m and colonnettes, the centre three forward on
the chhajja under the curved gable (5 m span, apex 9.3 m, ends turning down
past the springing, a two-band archivolt, twelve blind niches following the
curve); two low cream gadrooned saucer domes behind, their crowns level with
the gable's apex; four gilt finials to 10.0-10.2 m; the flagstaff to 10.5 m
with its dark pennant. The CCTV camera and the horn are bolted on (2023).

**Measured against the photographs:** sampled off the render, the frontispiece
is H31 S36 against the survey's midpoint H30 S37; the neighbour S13 against
