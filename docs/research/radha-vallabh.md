# radha-vallabh — exterior survey

*Researched 2026-09-27 by one agent and independently checked by a second whose brief was to refute it. Labels are the researcher's own: MEASURED / SOURCED / DERIVED / ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN.*

*Survey and checker are paired by the workflow journal's launch key, not by completion order — see BACKLOG 2026-09-30 for why that matters.*

## What it is

Two buildings on one cramped bazaar plot: a long, narrow, roofed-but-derelict early-17th-century hall-temple in red sandstone whose octagonal sanctum was smashed to its plinth (the "old" temple, now called Hit Mandir — an ASI-protected monument, empty), and immediately south of it the living temple, a late-18th-century courtyard (chowk) temple with a cusped-arch sandstone arcade and no tower at all — neither building has a shikhara, and neither looks anything like the tall spired temple instinct suggests.

## The silhouette

OLD TEMPLE (the ruin, "Hit Mandir"). SOURCED, Growse 1883 p.255: "the outer roof, a steep gable, also of stone, is as yet perfect." The nave is crowned by a STEEP PITCHED STONE GABLE ROOF running the length of the building — a ridge, not a dome, not a vault seen from outside, not a spire. Internally it is a waggon vault whose carved stone ceiling panels had already begun falling by 1883; Growse reproduced the same vault type (from the ruined Harideva at Gobardhan, which he calls Radha Vallabh's plan-twin) in the Mathura Museum clerestory (p.163). DERIVED from 2022 interior photographs (Wikimedia Commons, "2022 Radhavallabh Lal ju Maharaj Temple Vrindavan 01/05"): the vault is still standing and the interior is dry and roofed, so the gable survives today. Growse's warning that "some trees have taken root between the slabs" is SOURCED — model self-seeded peepal/banyan growing out of the ridge and eaves joints.

WHAT CROWNS THE WEST END: nothing. SOURCED, Growse p.255: "The actual shrine, or cella, as also at the temple of Gobind Deva, was demolished by Aurangzeb and only the plinth remains, upon which a room has been built, which is used as a kitchen." So the west third of the building is a LOW OCTAGONAL PLINTH carrying a small, crude, later room — a flat-roofed box perhaps 3–4 m tall (ESTIMATED) sitting on a 10.97 m-wide octagonal stone base. The profile therefore steps DOWN from east to west: tall gabled nave → lower jagmohan → low octagonal stump. That is the opposite of the normal temple profile and is the single most important silhouette fact.

The east (entrance) end is the tall end, a three-stage facade with a flat parapet and projecting chhajja, not a gable end that is expressed as a pediment (ESTIMATED — Growse describes the facade in stages, not as a gable end; the ridge runs east–west so a gable end does face east, but it sits behind the parapet).

NEW TEMPLE (the living one). Also has NO tower. DERIVED from 2022/2016 photographs: a rectangular open courtyard ringed by a two-storey sandstone arcade of shallow cusped (multifoil) arches, with a first-floor gallery fronted by pierced jali balustrade panels, and a flat roofline with a low parapet. The sanctum sits at one end behind a cusped-arch opening and silver-plated repoussé double doors. Skyline is flat parapets and small finials only. In modern practice the courtyard is partly covered by a temporary tensile/metal shade roof outside festival days (DERIVED from satellite and festival photos — model as removable).

## The elevation

OLD TEMPLE, EAST FACADE — this is the money shot and Growse describes it exactly (SOURCED, p.255 and p.172).

Three stages, bottom to top:

STAGE 1 (ground) — Hindu/trabeate. Five bays across a 32–34 ft (9.75–10.4 m) front. "Below, the three centre bays of the colonnade are open doorways, and the two at either end are occupied by the staircase that leads to the upper gallery." So: bay 1 = solid (stair), bays 2-3-4 = open doorways, bay 5 = solid (stair). Openings are SQUARE-HEADED, with the architrave carried on projecting brackets (p.172: "every opening is square-headed with the architrave supported on projecting brackets"). Opening:solid ratio across stage 1 ≈ 3 open of 5 bays ≈ 55–60% void. ESTIMATED stage height 4.3 m to the architrave.

STAGE 2 (middle) — Muhammadan/arcuate. "purely Muhammadan in character" (p.255), and p.172 defines what that means: "a succession of multifoil saracenic arches." CUSPED arches, five of them aligned over the five bays below, opening into the internal upper gallery. Use 5-, 7- or 9-cusped profiles, not plain pointed arches. ESTIMATED stage height 3.0 m. Opening:solid roughly 50%.

STAGE 3 (top) — Hindu again, square-headed on brackets, low; effectively a clerestory band and parapet. ESTIMATED 1.5–2.0 m. Mostly solid, perhaps 20–25% void.

Growse's explicit grammar rule for the whole building (p.172, SOURCED): "in a facade one story, or one compartment, shows a succession of multifoil saracenic arches, while above and below, or on either side, every opening is square-headed with the architrave supported on projecting brackets." Alternate the two systems by storey; never mix them within a storey.

OLD TEMPLE, LONG (N and S) SIDES — SOURCED p.255: "a double tier of openings north and south; those in the lower story having brackets and architraves and those above being Muhammadan arches, as in the middle story of the front. These latter open into a narrow gallery with small clerestory windows looking on to the street." So both long flanks repeat the same two-system rule: square-headed below, cusped arches above, and the upper arches back onto a narrow internal gallery lit by SMALL clerestory windows that face directly onto the lane.

PLAN OF THE FLANKS (MEASURED off Growse's ground-plan plate, scale verified below): the side walls are ~7 ft (2.10 m) thick and are hollowed out. Approximately seven bays per side. Towards the middle of each flank the wall opens into paired free-standing cruciform piers (two pairs per side) forming a short internal aisle; towards the ends it closes into a run of wall-recesses; the easternmost bay each side is the enclosed stair.

NEW TEMPLE ELEVATION (DERIVED from photographs, no textual source). Two storeys onto the courtyard. Ground: an arcade of shallow cusped (multifoil) arches on slender sandstone piers with carved rosette medallions in the spandrels and a carved cusped-arch frieze above the arcade line; roughly 60–65% void. First floor: a gallery with pierced jali balustrade panels between short colonnettes, ~40% void, reading as a lighter, screen-like band. Flat parapet above. The sanctum wall at the west end is the most ornate element: a deeply carved cusped-arch surround and silver-plated doors, flanked by smaller arched niches. Floor is black-and-white chequer/chevron marble.

STREET GATE (SOURCED by the project's own prior research and CONFIRMED here from photograph "Radhavallabh Lal ju Maharaj Temple Vrindavan 2022 01"): a single plain arched opening about 2.2 m wide and 3.2 m to the crown (ESTIMATED), set flush in a row of bazaar shop shutters, painted vermilion with white line-drawn lotus/floral motifs, a yellow-ground Hindi signboard across the arch head, gold-ochre strips on the jambs, a corrugated-metal canopy and a security camera above. It is a shopfront-scale door, not a gateway building.

## Material and colour

MATERIAL. Both buildings are red sandstone ashlar. SOURCED that the old temple's red sandstone required special Mughal permission (repeated by brajrasik.org and Commons descriptions; the underlying claim that Sundardas was diwan to Abdurrahim Khankhana is SOURCED to Entwistle, Braj, citing Bhagwat Mudit — but the "royal permission for red sandstone" story itself I could not trace to a primary source; treat as UNCERTAIN/tradition). Growse never mentions the material colour at all — he calls it simply "a handsome building."

COLOUR — MEASURED by sampling Wikimedia Commons photographs (median of middle 50% by luminance, per patch):

Old temple, weathered stone in daylight: #868063, H50 S26% V53% — grey-buff-olive. This is the critical measurement. After four centuries of soot, lichen and monsoon the ruin does NOT read red. Use a desaturated buff-grey base (S 20–30%) and reserve warm red only for sheltered, freshly-broken or recently-cleaned surfaces.
Old temple stone in interior shade: #4d4731, H47 S36% V30%, and pier bases #291d0f, H32 S63% V16% — near-black where soot has built up.

New temple arcade, sunlit sandstone: #c58355, H25 S57% V77%.
New temple arcade, mid-tone: #8d5434, H22 S63% V55%. A good single base colour for the working temple is about #b0714a.
New temple painted wall panel (red oxide / geru): #581302, H12 S98% V35% — a very deep, very saturated oxide red used as a flat paint on plastered courtyard walls.

Street gate, MEASURED and it CONFIRMS the project's prior samples: sunlit vermilion #cc4c38, H8 S73% V80% (project had #e25f3c — mine is slightly deeper, both are right for different exposures); shade #7f2211, H9 S87% V50% (project had #792512 — essentially identical). Signboard yellow #f7e997, H51 S39% V97%. White lotus motif over red reads #efbbac, H13 S28% V94% — i.e. it is a thin white line-painting that picks up the red beneath, not opaque white.

Interior painted additions in the old temple (the later room on the cella plinth): a teal/turquoise and gold-yellow painted door surround with small cusped niches — INFERRED colour from photograph "2022 ... Vrindavan 01"; roughly teal #1f6b63 and ochre-gold #c9a227, both ESTIMATED as I sampled these only by eye, not numerically.

Roofs: bare stone slabs on the old gable, grey-buff, streaked black, with vegetation in the joints. New temple: flat plastered roof terraces, plus temporary blue/white tarpaulin and corrugated sheet over parts of the courtyard.

## The setting

SOURCED, Growse p.255: the old temple's upper-gallery clerestory windows look "on to the street" — so a public lane runs hard against the long flank. SOURCED, Growse p.202: "On the opposite side of the street is a monument to the founder" — the lane in front of the temple is narrow enough that a chhatri stands directly across it. SOURCED, Growse p.255: "A modern temple has been erected on the south side" — the two buildings are immediately adjacent, sharing a plot, with the new one to the south.

SOURCED, Entwistle: the site is a "patch of high ground" where Hit Harivansh first settled, and "between the two is the garden called Seva Kunj" — i.e. Seva Kunj lies between the Radha Vallabh temple and Chir Ghat on the Yamuna. So the temple sits on slightly raised ground between the dense bazaar and the Seva Kunj enclosure. Entwistle also records Ram Kunj, "an establishment of the Lashkari order near the temple of Radhavallabh," as a neighbour.

MEASURED from ESRI World Imagery at z19 (0.265 m/px) over the project's coordinate: the fabric is wall-to-wall two- and three-storey flat-roofed masonry with roof terraces, courtyards of 4–8 m, and lanes measuring 2–4 m between building faces. There is no open compound, no boundary wall standing free, no approach axis and no forecourt. Buildings abut the temple directly on at least two sides.

VISIBILITY. The old temple's ridge at ~12.5 m is only about one storey above the surrounding roofscape, and the new temple's parapet at 9–11 m is level with it. Neither is visible from any distance — you come upon them. There is no distant silhouette to compose. This is the opposite of Govind Dev, which stands clear. ESTIMATED from the height figures and the satellite roofscape.

APPROACH. On foot, through the Banke Bihari bazaar lanes, past shuttered shops, to a small vermilion-painted arch in a shopfront. The lane is the brajrasik-named "Kunj Gali". Shops press against both jambs; a corrugated canopy overhangs the entry; overhead the lane is strung with electrical cable and, at festivals, fabric and lanterns. Model the gate as an interruption in a shop run, not as a destination facade.

FOLK DETAIL, DERIVED from photograph "2022 ... Vrindavan 36": the old temple's outer walls near the entry carry rows of gobar (cow-dung) swastikas and hand-pressed marks — raised, drying brown, applied over the stone. A small, very specific texture worth having.

INSIDE THE COMPOUND: black-and-white chequer and chevron marble paving in the new temple's court; the old temple's own court has a cusped-arch sandstone arcade along one side. At festivals the court carries red-and-gold scalloped valances, paper lanterns, marigold strings and red carpet.

## What NOT to build

- Do NOT put a shikhara, spire or tower on the old temple. Growse p.255, eyewitness: 'The actual shrine, or cella, as also at the temple of Gobind Deva, was demolished by Aurangzeb and only the plinth remains, upon which a room has been built, which is used as a kitchen.' The west end is a LOW STUMP. This is exactly the error class that put an onion dome on Govind Dev — the same sentence structure, the same building, the same trap.
- Do NOT make the shrine square. It is an OCTAGON: 23 ft (7.01 m) internal across the flats, 36 ft (10.97 m) external. Growse p.256 singles it out: 'The lower story ... though most commonly square, is occasionally, as in the Madan Mohan and Radha Ballabh examples, an octagon.' A square garbhagriha is the default instinct and is wrong here.
- Do NOT give the old temple 10-foot-thick walls 'pierced in two stages' with 'a regular triforium'. That is Growse p.241 describing GOVIND DEV, and Wikipedia plus every aggregator has mis-copied it onto Radha Vallabh. Radha Vallabh's side walls measure 6.9 ft (2.10 m) on Growse's own plate. The giveaway is that the aggregators also garble 'triforium' into 'Trifolium'.
- Do NOT build the new temple as a 19th-century building. Entwistle, Braj: Centre of Krishna Pilgrimage, is explicit that the deity returned from Kaman in 1785 and was 'installed in a new temple built alongside the original one by a merchant from Gujarat named Seth Lallubhai Bhagwandas.' That is LATE 18th CENTURY and a named Gujarati merchant patron, not a 19th-century one. A widely repeated '1842' date circulates online; I could not source it.
- Do NOT date the building 1585 or attribute it to Akbar's reign. Growse p.172: 'The temple of Radha Ballabh ... built in the next reign, that of Jahangir, is the last example of the style.' Growse p.202 read the date himself off the fabric: 'One of the pillars in the front gives the date as Sambat 1683' = A.D. 1626. The famous 1585 comes only from Professor H. H. Wilson's reading of an inscription over the OUTER GATEWAY, and Growse notes that gateway 'has fallen down and been removed'. Entwistle agrees: 'It is not clear exactly when the temple of Radhavallabh was built.'
- Do NOT roof the old nave with a dome or an exposed barrel vault. Externally it is a STEEP PITCHED STONE GABLE with a ridge — genuinely rare, and the single most distinctive thing about the building's outline. The barrel vault is internal only.
- Do NOT paint it fresh red sandstone. Measured colour of the weathered ruin today is #868063 (H50 S26% V53%) — grey-buff-olive, soot-blackened at the bases (#291d0f). 'Built of red sandstone' describes 1626, not 2026. Only the living temple still reads warm red (#c58355 sunlit).
- Do NOT use plain pointed Mughal arches. Growse p.172 specifies 'a succession of multifoil saracenic arches' — CUSPED, multifoil. And they appear only in the middle storey; the storeys above and below are square-headed with architraves on projecting brackets. Mixing the two within one storey breaks the rule Growse spent a page defining.
- Do NOT build a monumental gateway, gopuram or torana. The present entrance is a ~2.2 m painted arch flush in a row of bazaar shop shutters. The original outer-court gateway had already 'fallen down and been removed' before Growse wrote.
- Do NOT set either building in an open compound or garden. Growse p.255 notes the clerestory windows look 'on to the street'; the building is wedged into dense bazaar fabric with lanes 2–4 m wide coming right up to the walls. Satellite shows wall-to-wall roof terraces with no open ground at all.
- Do NOT place the new temple in front of (east of) the old one. Growse p.255: 'A modern temple has been erected on the SOUTH side.'
- Do NOT make the old temple a squat block. It is long and thin: 38.8 m by at most 10.97 m, a ratio of about 3.5:1. The project's current build entry (w:30, d:40, h:14) is roughly right in length and THREE TIMES too wide.
- Do NOT give the new temple a tower either. It is a courtyard (chowk) temple — open court, two-storey cusped-arch arcade, jali gallery above, flat parapets. No spire anywhere on this site.
- Do NOT model the famous Holi crowd photographs as the old temple. Those Wikimedia files are titled 'Temple of Radha Ballabh 01–53' and tagged as the ASI monument, but they show the LIVE temple's courtyard. Conversely the files titled '2022 Radhavallabh Lal ju Maharaj Temple Vrindavan' carry a blanket description of the OLD temple but include images of both. Both Commons sets are mislabelled; judge each photograph by what it shows.
- Do NOT give the ruin a clean roof. Growse: 'Some trees however have taken root between the slabs and unless carefully removed must eventually destroy it.' Model self-seeded trees growing out of the ridge and eaves.
- Do NOT add an eastern porch or projecting mandapa. The east front IS the facade — five bays flush, three open doorways with an enclosed stair at each end.
- Do NOT forget Sundar Das's monument. Growse p.202: 'On the opposite side of the street is a monument to the founder, which however the present generation of Gosains are too ungrateful to keep in repair.' A small neglected chhatri directly across the lane from the temple.
- Do NOT confuse this with the OTHER Radha Ballabh temple in Growse. His index lists pages 172, 202 and 255 for the Vrindavan one; the 'temple of Radha Ballabh' at p.258 belongs to Radha Gobind Sinh at Kandi in Murshidabad and is a different building entirely.

## Uncertain — flag, do not assert

- COORDINATE. Wikidata Q12449473 carries THREE conflicting positions: 27.58065833/77.69156111 (precision 1e-8), 27.5812/77.692, and 27.583734/77.695699. The project currently uses 27.58330/77.69540, which is ~400 m NE of the highest-precision Wikidata value. Wikipedia places the temple 'near a cliff near the Banke Bihari temple'; Banke Bihari is around 27.5806/77.6938, and Entwistle puts Seva Kunj between the temple and Chir Ghat, which would favour somewhere near 27.5815/77.6950. I could not resolve this — Overpass timed out on three attempts and Nominatim returned nothing from this environment, and no Commons photograph of either building carries a geotag. VERIFY BEFORE PLACING. The satellite measurements I made are centred on the project's existing coordinate and are therefore only as good as that coordinate.
- DATE OF THE OLD BUILDING. Growse read Sambat 1683 (A.D. 1626) off a front pillar and assigns the style to Jahangir's reign (p.172, p.202). Wilson read 1585 (Samvat 1641) off a gateway that no longer existed by Growse's time. Entwistle says flatly it is not clear. Popular sources say 1584 or 1585 with total confidence. I lean to c.1626 for the FABRIC and 1584/85 for the FOUNDATION of the site, but this is a genuine unresolved conflict, not a settled fact.
- DATE OF THE NEW TEMPLE. Entwistle gives 1785 and names Seth Lallubhai Bhagwandas. A '1842' date appears in search summaries but I could not trace it to any source I could read. Growse (writing 1874-83) only says 'a modern temple has been erected on the south side', which is consistent with either. I have gone with Entwistle. The project brief's '19th-century' is probably wrong but I cannot rule it out absolutely.
- DIMENSIONAL CONFLICT IN GROWSE HIMSELF. His text (p.255) says the interior hall is 63 ft x 20 ft; his own plate says the nave is 54 ft x 18 ft internal and 62 ft x 32 ft external. His text says the east facade is 34 ft broad; his plate says 32 ft. I have used the plate throughout because I could verify its internal consistency, but the discrepancy is real and I cannot explain it.
- THE OCTAGON'S FACE LENGTH. Growse labels a face '16 Ft' but the geometry of a regular octagon 36 ft across the flats gives a face of 14.6 ft. His '6 Ft' label matches my measured 6.1 ft wall thickness well. I suspect the 16 ft includes a projecting pilaster strip, but I am guessing.
- ALL HEIGHTS ARE ESTIMATED. Growse gives not one vertical dimension for this building, and I found no published section, elevation or measured drawing. My 12.0-12.8 m ridge is derived from the 18 ft internal span plus the documented three-stage elevation, not measured. Treat as a working assumption to be revised if a real elevation surfaces.
- CURRENT STATE OF THE GABLE ROOF. Growse warned in 1883 that trees would destroy it. I found no source stating whether it survives. My inference that it does rests on 2022 interior photographs showing the vault intact and the interior dry. That is good evidence but it is inference, not documentation.
- THE 'ROYAL PERMISSION FOR RED SANDSTONE' STORY. Repeated by brajrasik.org and the Commons descriptions. Entwistle confirms Sundardas was diwan to Abdurrahim Khankhana, but the permission story itself I could not trace to any primary source. Treat as tradition.
- NEW TEMPLE DIMENSIONS ARE GUESSES. No measured source exists that I could find, and I could not confidently identify the building in satellite imagery. Everything I give for it is ESTIMATED from photograph proportions and crowd scale.
- PHOTOGRAPH ATTRIBUTION. Both Wikimedia Commons photo sets are internally mislabelled (see doNotBuild). My assignment of individual images to the old versus new building is my own judgement from architectural style and state of repair, not from the file metadata.
- A charanvandan.com fundraising campaign calls this the '1000-year-old Radha Vallabh Temple'. That is LEGEND/marketing with no basis in any source I read; the sect itself dates from Hit Harivansh in the 16th century.

## Independent check

Confidence: HIGH on: the Banke Bihari misidentification (direct side-by-side image match against File:Banke-Bihari-Mandir-Vrindavan.jpg, plus matching photographer, date and Alamy caption); the plate re-measurement of 117 ft overall (independently re-derived scale, and Growse's own labels cannot sum past 118 ft); every Growse and Entwistle quotation (read from full texts I downloaded, not from summaries); the ceiling form (Growse p.304 plus the 2022 interior photograph); and the exterior colour (numerically sampled from a daylight photo of the building itself). HIGH also on the survey's core crowning claim, which I tried hard to break and could not — gable over the nave, low stump at the west, no tower on either building. MEDIUM on the height correction and the stage-3 proportion: both rest on a single steeply-angled close-range photograph plus Growse's figure for a different building, so treat 13–14 m as a better working assumption rather than a measurement. MEDIUM on the ghera/approach correction: Entwistle is explicit but wrote in 1987 and the fabric may have changed. LOW on the coordinate: I could not resolve it — Nominatim returned nothing for the temple and Overpass returned no matching feature in the Vrindavan bounding box on two endpoints (a third timed out), so I can only say the evidence weight lies around 27.581/77.692 and that the project's point is ~480 m off it. I did NOT verify: whether the stone gable survives today (no exterior roof photograph exists in any set I found — the survey's inference from a dry interior is reasonable but still inference), the new temple's plan dimensions, or the founder's chhatri across the lane.

### Corrections to the survey above

- PHOTO SET MISIDENTIFIED — this is the big one. The survey says the Wikimedia set 'Temple of Radha Ballabh 01–53' shows 'the LIVE temple's courtyard' of Radha Vallabh. It does not. It shows BANKE BIHARI TEMPLE. I put File:Temple of Radha Ballabh 19.jpg side by side with File:Banke-Bihari-Mandir-Vrindavan.jpg (Commons, Banke Bihari category): identical building, near-identical viewpoint — the same silver repoussé sanctum screen with one large central cusped arch flanked by two smaller ones, the same extra cusped niches to the left, the same white notice boards on the same pier, the same jali galleries left and right. All 53 files are by one photographer (User:Azimronnie) on one date, 2018-03-01 (Holi 2018), bulk-tagged {{ASI Monument|N-UP-A198}} via the Wiki Loves Monuments upload campaign; the same photographer's Vrindavan Holi 2018 shoot is sold on Alamy as 'Holi at Banke Bihari Temple, Vrindavan 2018'. CONSEQUENCE: the survey's entire 'NEW TEMPLE ELEVATION' paragraph — two-storey arcade of shallow cusped arches, first-floor gallery with pierced jali balustrade panels between short colonnettes, carved rosette medallions in the spandrels, carved cusped-arch frieze, silver-plated repoussé double doors, ~60–65%/~40% void ratios — is at least partly measured off a c.1862–64 Rajasthani-style temple several hundred metres away. Rebuild it from the Goutam1962 2022 sets only, which show a plainer, deep-red-painted court with wooden-shuttered rooms around it and a quite different sanctum end. This is precisely the 'similarly named temple elsewhere' failure the brief warned about, and the survey walked into it while writing a doNotBuild rule about it.
- OVERALL LENGTH IS WRONG: ~117 ft = 35.6 m, not 127 ft = 38.8 m. I re-derived the plate scale independently from Growse's own labels (32 Ft external nave width = 547 px; 18 Ft internal = 310 px → 17.09 px/ft, which happens to match the survey's stated scale). On that scale the east front outer line sits at y=2475 px and the back flat of the octagon at y=480 px → 1995 px = 116.7 ft = 35.58 m. Growse's own labelled components cannot exceed 62 + 20 + 36 = 118 ft, and on the plate the octagon actually overlaps the jagmohan block, so 117 ft is right. The survey is 3.2 m (9%) too long. Knock-on: the plan proportion is 3.24:1, not 'about 3.5:1'; and the project's build entry d:40 is ~12% too deep as well as three times too wide.
- THE NAVE IS NOT ROOFED BY A WAGGON VAULT — this is a Govind Dev import, the exact error class the survey warns about. Growse reserves 'a waggon vault of pointed form, not, as is usual in Hindu architecture, composed of overlapping brackets, but constructed of true radiating arches as in our Gothic cathedrals' for GOVIND DEV (p.241). For Harideva at Gobardhan — which he says at p.255 has 'much the same' ground plan and 'the same character' of work — he writes at p.304: 'There was a double roof, each entirely of stone: the outer one a high pitched gable, the inner an arched ceiling, or rather the nearest approach to an arch ever seen in Hindu design. The centre was really flat, but it was so deeply coved at the sides that, the width of the building being inconsiderable, it had all the effect of a vault, and no doubt suggested the possibility of the true radiating vault, which we find in the temple of Govind Deva.' The 2022 photograph File:2022 Radhavallabh Lal ju Maharaj Temple Vrindavan 02.jpg confirms it for Radha Vallabh: transverse pointed rib-arches with FLAT, horizontally-coursed, panelled bays between them. That is also why Growse writes 'some of the carved PANELS of the stone ceiling have fallen' — panels, not voussoirs. Model a ribbed, flat-centred, deeply-coved trabeate ceiling, not a barrel vault.
- STAGE 3 OF THE FACADE IS A FULL OPEN COLONNADE, not a 1.5–2.0 m 'clerestory band and parapet' at 20–25% void. File:Radhavallabh Lal ju Maharaj Temple Vrindavan 2022 31.jpg is a daylight exterior of the old temple and confirms Growse's three-stage grammar exactly (trabeate below, pointed-arched middle, trabeate above) — but the top stage is a tall open loggia of square piers carrying bracketed architraves under a deep chhajja, roughly as tall as the arcuate stage below it and overwhelmingly VOID. The survey's stage-3 figures would produce a squat, wrong silhouette.
- 'GROWSE GIVES NO HEIGHTS ANYWHERE' IS FALSE, and it matters. At p.304, for the building Growse himself nominates as Radha Vallabh's plan-twin, he writes: 'the nave has four openings on either side... There are clerestory windows above, and the height is about 30 feet to the cornice.' That is ~9.14 m to the cornice on a nave 20 ft broad with the same double stone roof — and Growse says Radha Vallabh repeats the type 'on a larger scale'. The survey's estimates (eaves chhajja 8.0–8.5 m, ridge 12.0–12.8 m) are therefore low; a cornice at ≥9.1 m plus a 40–45° gable over the 9.75 m block puts the ridge nearer 13–14 m. Also note Harideva's nave is 68 × 20 ft against Radha Vallabh's 63 × 20 ft (text) / 54 × 18 ft (plate), so 'larger scale' does NOT mean a longer nave — do not scale up from Harideva.
- THE 'CRITICAL' COLOUR MEASUREMENT IS WRONG FOR THE EXTERIOR. Sampling the 2022 daylight exterior (File:Radhavallabh Lal ju Maharaj Temple Vrindavan 2022 31.jpg), median of the middle 50% by luminance per patch: sunlit top-storey pier #ac866a (H25 S38% V67%); mid-storey spandrel #69270f (H16 S86% V41%); lower chhajja fascia #61230e (H15 S86% V38%); plinth steps #ba8258 (H26 S53% V73%). Every exposed-stone patch lands at hue 15–26 with saturation 38–86%. The survey's headline #868063 (H50 S26% V53%, 'grey-buff-olive') is nowhere near that and is not this building's daylit exterior. Its rule — 'use a desaturated buff-grey base (S 20–30%) and reserve warm red only for sheltered, freshly-broken or recently-cleaned surfaces' — is backwards: the weather-exposed exterior reads warm red-brown, and it is the sooted INTERIOR that goes near-black (#291d0f is plausible for interior pier bases). 'Do NOT paint it fresh red sandstone… Only the living temple still reads warm red' should be struck.
- 'USE 5-, 7- OR 9-CUSPED PROFILES, NOT PLAIN POINTED ARCHES' IS OVER-SPECIFIED AND CONTRADICTED BY THE FABRIC. The surviving middle-stage windows (2022 31) and the internal gallery arches (2022 02 and 06) are simple two-centred pointed/ogee arches with a shallow foliation near the springing and carved rosette medallions in the spandrels — not dense multifoil. Growse's 'a succession of multifoil saracenic arches' at p.172 is a general characterisation of the 16th-century eclectic STYLE across many buildings, not a measured description of Radha Vallabh's arch profile. Building 9-foil arches here would be as wrong as building plain Mughal ogees.
- THE OLD TEMPLE IS NOT 'EMPTY' OR 'ABANDONED'. Entwistle, Braj, gazetteer entry: 'The old temple, built in the latter half of the sixteenth century, is still occasionally used for gatherings in which lyrics by poets of the Radhavallabh Sampraday are sung, but nowadays the new temple is the centre of activity.' The survey's oneLine ('roofed-but-derelict… empty') takes 'now abandoned but protected monument' from brajrasik.org and does not record that its own principal secondary source contradicts it. The 2022 photographs also show ASI chain-link screening, a sadhu seated in the ground-floor colonnade, and freshly applied gobar marks.
- THE SITE IS A NAMED WALLED COMPOUND CONTAINING FOUR-PLUS TEMPLES, NOT 'TWO BUILDINGS ON ONE CRAMPED BAZAAR PLOT'. Entwistle: 'The two temples stand in a compound called Radhavallabh Ghera, which is entered through a gateway in a street that leads from Purana Bazaar at the junction known as Athkhambha. Also in the Ghera are the Calcuttawala temple and the small but popular Anandi Bai ka Mandir, founded earlier this century by a notable devotee.' Both the survey's oneLine and its rule 'Do NOT set either building in an open compound or garden' need revising: there is a ghera with a single named gateway and at least two further temples inside it.
- THE APPROACH IS MIS-SOURCED. The survey states 'The lane is the brajrasik-named "Kunj Gali"'. I fetched and detexted the entire cited brajrasik.org page: it contains no lane name at all. Entwistle names a different and more specific approach — from Purana Bazaar at the Athkhambha junction — and the survey's 'on foot, through the Banke Bihari bazaar lanes' is its own inference (very possibly seeded by the misidentified Banke Bihari photo set).
- THE 'TRIFORIUM' CORRECTION OVER-CORRECTS. Growse p.241 actually reads: Govind Dev's walls 'have an average thickness of ten feet and are pierced in two stages, the upper stage being a regular triforium, to which access is obtained by an internal staircase, AS IN THE SOMEWHAT LATER TEMPLE OF RADHA BALLABH.' Growse himself extends the two-stage-plus-internal-stair comparison to Radha Vallabh, and p.255 independently confirms it ('a double tier of openings north and south… the two at either end are occupied by the staircase that leads to the upper gallery'). Only the TEN-FOOT wall thickness belongs to Govind Dev alone. Radha Vallabh's side walls are 7 ft — (32−18)/2 from the plate labels, and my pixel measure gives 118 px = 6.9 ft. Narrow the doNotBuild rule to wall thickness, or the modeller will delete a documented feature.
- 'PATCH OF HIGH GROUND' IS LEGEND MARKED AS SOURCED. Entwistle writes: 'LEGEND HAS IT that he first settled on a patch of high ground beside the Yamuna… The patch of high ground IS SAID TO BE the place where the temple of Radhavallabh now stands; between the two is the garden called Seva Kunj, where he first installed Radhavallabh for worship in 1534.' The survey labels this 'SOURCED, Entwistle' and turns it into a modelling instruction ('the temple sits on slightly raised ground'). No measured elevation supports it; strip the instruction or mark it legend.
- THE OLD TEMPLE'S DATE IS ASSERTED IN THE ONELINE AS SOMETHING THE SURVEY ITSELF CALLS UNRESOLVED. The oneLine says 'early-17th-century hall-temple' as fact, while the uncertainties list calls the date 'a genuine unresolved conflict' and Entwistle's own gazetteer entry — a source the survey cites — says 'built in the latter half of the sixteenth century'. Pick one and flag it consistently; do not let a contested date harden in the headline.
- TWO SMALLER PLATE ERRORS. (a) The octagon face: 36 ft across flats ÷ 2.4142 = 14.91 ft, not the 14.6 ft (4.45 m) the survey prints; the drawn face does measure ~14.6 ft, so the figure survives but the stated derivation does not produce it. (b) The plate does not show a solid 6.5-ft octagonal wall: the outer octagon is a ring of eight THIN detached slabs with open gaps at the corners, enclosing an open ambulatory around the 23-ft inner shrine. 'A 10.97 m-wide octagonal stone base' will be built as a solid drum; it is a screen-and-ambulatory.
- COORDINATE: the discrepancy is larger than stated, and every satellite measurement rides on the suspect point. Project 27.58330/77.69540 vs the highest-precision Wikidata value 27.58065833/77.69156111 is 293 m N + 379 m E = ~480 m, not '~400 m'. English Wikipedia's infobox gives 27°34′52″N 77°41′31″E = 27.5811/77.6919, i.e. it clusters with the high-precision Wikidata value and not with the project's. Every figure in the setting section flagged 'MEASURED from ESRI World Imagery at z19' (lanes 2–4 m, courtyards 4–8 m, wall-to-wall roof terraces, no open ground) was taken over the project coordinate and is therefore quite possibly a measurement of the wrong block. Those figures should be demoted from MEASURED to unverified until the point is fixed.

### Independently confirmed

- Every Growse p.255 quotation in the survey is verbatim accurate. I pulled the 3rd-edition full text (archive.org mathurdistrict00growrich_djvu.txt) and read the passage: 'The nave has an eastern facade, 34 feet broad, which is in three stages, the upper and lower Hindu, and the one between them purely Muhammadan in character. The interior is a fine vaulted hall (63 ft. X 20 ft.) with a double tier of openings north and south; those in the lower story having brackets and architraves and those above being Muhammadan arches, as in the middle story of the front. These latter open into a narrow gallery with small clerestory windows looking on to the street. Below, the three centre bays of the colonnade are open doorways, and the two at either end are occupied by the staircase that leads to the upper gallery. Some of the carved panels of the stone ceiling have fallen; but the outer roof, a steep gable, also of stone, is as yet perfect. Some trees however have taken root between the slabs and unless carefully removed must eventually destroy it. The actual shrine, or cella, as also at the temple of Gobind Deva, was demolished by Aurangzeb and only the plinth remains, upon which a room has been built, which is used as a kitchen… A modern temple has been erected on the south side, and the nave of the old fabric has long been entirely disused.'
- THE CROWNING CLAIM IS CORRECT. Neither building carries a shikhara, dome, spire or tower today; the old temple's nave is crowned by a steep pitched STONE GABLE (Growse's word, eyewitness, 1883) and the west end is a low stump — the octagonal cella plinth with a later room on it. The profile does step DOWN east to west. I found nothing anywhere contradicting this, and the survey's doNotBuild rules on this point should stand as written. Growse p.256 also confirms the shrine is an octagon, verbatim: 'The lower story of the modern temple which, though most commonly square, is occasionally, as in the Madan Mohan and Radha Ballabh examples, an octagon.'
- Growse p.172 verbatim confirmed, including both the dating and the grammar rule: 'The temple of Radha Ballabh, in that town, built in the next reign, that of Jahangir, is the last example of the style… Thus in a facade one story, or one compartment, shows a succession of multifoil saracenic arches, while above and below, or on either side, every opening is square-headed with the architrave supported on projecting brackets.'
- Growse p.202 verbatim confirmed: 'This was built by one of his disciples, a Kayath named Sundar Das, who held the appointment of treasurer at Delhi. One of the pillars in the front gives the date as Sambat 1683. An earlier inscription, of 1641, was noticed by Professor Wilson, but this would seem to have been over the gateway leading into the outer court, which since then has fallen down and been removed. On the opposite side of the street is a monument to the founder, which however the present generation of Gosains are too ungrateful to keep in repair.'
- THE OTHER RADHA BALLABH TEMPLE IS REAL AND THE SURVEY HAS IT RIGHT. Growse's index reads 'Radha Ballabh, temple of, 172, 202, 255.' The second temple appears at pp.257–8 in the Lala Babu chapter: 'Radha Gobind took service under Allah Virdi Khan and Siraj-ud-daula, Nawabs of Murshidabad… A rest-house for travellers and a temple of Radha Ballabh, which he founded, are still in existence' — Kandi, Murshidabad. Different building entirely.
- ALL THE PLATE DIMENSIONS I COULD TEST REPRODUCE. Working from File:321 of 'Mathurá- a District Memoir…' (11136486155).jpg at 17.09 px/ft: nave 32 ft external / 18.1 ft internal; side walls 118 px = 6.9 ft (7 ft from the labels); jagmohan block 20.6 ft ('20 Ft'); octagon 36.3 ft external and 23.1 ft internal across the flats; nave block 61.4 ft ('62 Ft'). The plate's own labels — Nave 54 Ft × 18 Ft, Jag-mohan or 17 Ft × 18 Ft Choir, Mandir or 23 Ft Shrine, 6 Ft, 16 Ft, 20 Ft, 62 Ft, 32 Ft — are exactly as the survey lists them.
- THE TEXT/PLATE DISCREPANCY IS REAL, AND MORE SYSTEMATIC THAN THE SURVEY NOTICED. Growse's text gives 20 ft internal width and a 34 ft facade; his plate gives 18 ft internal and 32 ft external. Both are internally consistent with 7 ft walls — the two readings differ by a constant 2 ft, which points to one set being taken to a different face (plinth or pilaster line). The 63 ft vs 54 ft length difference does not follow that pattern and remains unexplained. Using the plate is the right call.
- ASI PROTECTION CONFIRMED. The old temple is N-UP-A198, 'Temple of Radha Ballabh, Brindaban', a Monument of National Importance (Wikidata Q12449473, P1435 = Q17047513, sourced to the ASI Agra Circle alphabetical list; corroborated by the Monuments of National Importance listings). The 2022 photographs show the standard blue ASI notice boards on site ('हमारी विरासत, हमारा गौरव / OUR HERITAGE IS OUR GLORY').
- WIKIPEDIA IS INDEED THE ERROR VECTOR. The live article carries 'walls…10 feet thick and are pierced in 2 stages' as Radha Vallabh's, and the same sentence (with 'Triforium') appears verbatim on the aggregator sites. The survey's diagnosis of the propagation chain is right; only its correction needs narrowing (see corrections).
- NO GEOTAGS. I queried all 137 files in Category:Radha Vallabh Temple, Vrindavan through the Commons coordinates API: zero of them carry a coordinate. The survey's claim is exactly right.
- THE GOBAR SWASTIKAS ARE REAL. File:2022 Radhavallabh Lal ju Maharaj Temple Vrindavan 36.jpg shows, unambiguously at resolution, rows of raised, drying cow-dung swastikas and hand-pressed marks applied over the stone lintel and jamb. I had expected this to turn out to be carved stonework; it is not. Keep the detail.
- THE STREET GATE DESCRIPTION HOLDS. File:Radhavallabh Lal ju Maharaj Temple Vrindavan 2022 01.jpg shows a red/vermilion painted arch set flush in a run of rolling shop shutters, a yellow-ground Hindi signboard across the arch head reading 'विराजमान राधावल्लभ लाल जू महाराज', white line-drawn motifs, gold-ochre jamb strips, coloured painted flowers, and a corrugated/mesh canopy. My shade sample #7a2613 (H11 S84% V48%) is within a hair of the survey's #7f2211 (H9 S87% V50%). One caveat: the object above the arch reads as a rectangular floodlight, not a security camera.
- THE NEW TEMPLE HAS NO TOWER, AND THE COURTYARD BASICS ARE RIGHT — but only as shown in the Goutam1962 sets, not the misidentified Holi set. Those photographs show an open rectangular court with a cusped-arch arcade on carved red sandstone piers, deep red-oxide/geru painted wall panels, black-and-white chequer and chevron marble paving, festival red-and-gold scalloped valances, marigold strings and paper lanterns, and flat rooflines.
- ENTWISTLE QUOTATIONS CONFIRMED VERBATIM: 'Vanchandra's most significant contribution was the building of a temple for the deity of Radhavallabh, for which funds were provided by a disciple of Gopinath named Sundardas Bhatnagar. According to Bhagwat Mudit he was a Kayasth who served as divan for Abdurrahim Khankhana (1556-1627)'; 'It is not clear exactly when the temple of Radhavallabh was built, but Wilson noted an inscription over the gateway, now no longer extant, that was dated 1585'; 'In 1682 it was installed in a new temple built at Kaman, where it remained until 1785'; 'the deity of Radhavallabh was brought from Kaman and installed in a new temple built alongside the original one by a merchant from Gujarat named Seth Lallubhai Bhagwandas'; and Ram Kunj, 'an establishment of the Lashkari order near the temple of Radhavallabh'.
- THE RED-SANDSTONE PERMISSION STORY IS TRADITION, AS THE SURVEY SAYS. Entwistle confirms the Sundardas–Abdurrahim connection but says nothing about royal permission for red sandstone. The story traces only to brajrasik.org and the Commons uploader's boilerplate description. Likewise the builder's-death prophecy, which Entwistle explicitly labels 'Legend has it…'.

### Contamination risks

- BANKE BIHARI → RADHA VALLABH (confirmed, active, already inside the survey). The 53-file Commons Holi set is Banke Bihari. Anything in the survey's new-temple elevation that mentions silver repoussé double doors, pierced jali balustrade panels between short colonnettes, rosette medallions in the spandrels or a carved cusped-arch frieze should be treated as Banke Bihari until re-confirmed against the Goutam1962 photographs.
- GOVIND DEV → RADHA VALLABH (confirmed, active). The 'waggon vault' is Govind Dev's. The project has now been bitten by Govind Dev contamination three times: the onion dome, the mis-attributed builder, and now the vault. Growse's p.241 sentence and his p.255 sentence are structurally similar and both name Radha Vallabh in a subordinate clause; any sentence of Growse's in which Radha Vallabh appears as a comparison rather than a subject should be treated as describing the OTHER building until checked.
- HARIDEVA AT GOBARDHAN → RADHA VALLABH (latent, and the survey leans on it heavily). Growse calls Harideva the plan-twin, so it is a legitimate analogue — but he also records at p.304-5 that Harideva had TWO towers, 'the two towers which originally crowned the choir and sacrarium were long ago levelled with the roof of the nave.' If the plans really are 'much the same', Radha Vallabh may originally have carried a tower over the jagmohan as well as over the cella; Growse's p.255 mentions only the cella's demolition. Unresolved, and a genuine silhouette risk for the ruined-state model. Also do not inherit Harideva's red Bharatpur sandstone, its 68 ft nave or its 30 ft cornice as measurements of Radha Vallabh — only as an order-of-magnitude anchor.
- THE KANDI / MURSHIDABAD RADHA BALLABH (Growse pp.257–8, founded by Radha Gobind Sinh). Correctly isolated by the survey. Keep the warning.
- 'HIT MANDIR' vs 'RADHAVALLABH GHERA' vs 'RADHA VALLABH TEMPLE' — three names that variously denote the ruin, the compound and the living temple. brajrasik.org and the Commons uploader both put the new temple on the 'back side' of the old (i.e. west); Growse says the SOUTH side. The survey follows Growse, which is right, but the conflict is unrecorded and will re-surface.
- TWO WIKIDATA ITEMS — Q12449473 and a second 'Radha Vallabh Temple' item — both carrying the ASI id N-UP-A198 and the same three conflicting P625 values. The conflicting coordinates the survey found ride on this duplication, which is itself unresolved.
- DATE STACK, all circulating as fact somewhere: 1584 (brajrasik.org); 1585 (Wikipedia, Commons descriptions, Wilson via Entwistle, from a gateway that no longer existed by 1874); Samvat 1683 = AD 1626 (Growse, read off a front pillar in situ); 'latter half of the sixteenth century' (Entwistle's gazetteer); 1600, century precision (Wikidata inception); 1842 for the new temple (search summaries, untraceable); 1000 years old (charanvandan.com fundraising). Growse's pillar reading is the only one taken off surviving fabric by a named eyewitness and should outrank the rest for the FABRIC.
- LEGEND REPEATED AS TOPOGRAPHY: Entwistle's 'patch of high ground' is prefaced 'Legend has it' and qualified 'is said to be', and the survey converted it into a site instruction. The arrow-shot land grant, the builder's-death prophecy, the Aurangzeb attack in which 'seven devotees were killed' (Entwistle: 'Oral tradition current in the Radhavallabh Sampraday says…'), and the red-sandstone permission all belong in the same bucket. Note Growse states the Aurangzeb demolition of the cella as his own observation, so that one is on firmer ground than the rest.
- COLOURS ASSERTED FROM UNIDENTIFIED PHOTOGRAPHS. Because the survey's photo attribution is wrong for one whole set, every hex in the materialsAndColour section should be re-derived with the source file named per sample. The two the survey flags as eyeballed rather than measured (#1f6b63 teal, #c9a227 ochre-gold for the later room's door surround) do at least look plausible against '2022 … Vrindavan 01', 08 and 09, which show a teal-green painted door with a gold-yellow carved surround at the west end of the hall.
