# iskcon-krishna-balaram — exterior survey

*Researched 2026-09-27 by one agent and independently checked by a second whose brief was to refute it. Confidence labels are the researcher's own: MEASURED / SOURCED / DERIVED / ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN.*

## What it is

It is not one building but a walled 1.47-hectare compound whose architectural star is Prabhupada's 1983 Samadhi Mandir — a two-storey Rajasthani white-marble monument with a 70-ft curvilinear shikhara and a monumental cusped marble arch-bridge — while the 1975 temple it commemorates is a low, flat-roofed, cream-painted courtyard mandir with a black-and-white chequerboard marble floor and no spire at all.

## The silhouette

WHAT SURVIVES (all of it — nothing here is ruined or lost; this is a living 1975–present complex).

THE THING THAT MAKES THE SKYLINE IS THE SAMADHI, NOT THE TEMPLE. [DERIVED, high confidence — see `uncertain` for the residual doubt.] Standing on Bhaktivedanta Swami Marg you see exactly one tall white marble spire, immediately to the LEFT of the main gate. The official campus signboard (photographed on Commons) places "B. SRILA PRABHUPADA'S SAMADHI" at that corner and "A. TEMPLE" set back ~25–30 m north of it. The geotagged 2019 Commons photo titled "Srila Prabhupada samadhi in Vrindavan" (27.571994, 77.677528) shows that same spire-plus-domes-plus-arch composition. No second spire appears anywhere behind it in the 2013 street photographs. Conclusion: the marble spire is the Samadhi's, and the main temple is low and flat-roofed.

THE SAMADHI MANDIR SILHOUETTE, bottom to top [MEASURED from photographs for form; heights ESTIMATED except the sourced 70 ft]:
1. Moulded marble plinth ~1.2–1.5 m, carrying a carved frieze of elephants in file and a lotus-petal course.
2. Two storeys of marble wall (~4.5 m each), each divided into bays by pilasters.
3. A CORNICE THAT UNDULATES. This is the single most distinctive line in the whole complex and it is easy to miss: the eave is NOT straight. It is a chain of shallow downward-curving ogee/cyma sweeps, one per bay — the Bengali "bangaldar" curve, borrowed into Rajput marble. Run it as a repeating S-curve along every roof edge.
4. A roof terrace with a parapet alternating pierced jali panels of small cusped arches with solid vase-shaped balusters (~0.9–1.1 m).
5. On the terrace: TWO large open pavilions (chhatris) flanking the centre, plus a smaller third chhatri sitting on the pier of the great arch. Each chhatri = an octagonal ring of ~8 clustered piers (slender paired colonnettes) with cusped arches and jali tympana, carrying a DOWNTURNED WAVY CANOPY EAVE, and above that a broad, low, deeply GADROONED dome — 20–28 fat lobes, each ending in a leaf tip at the base, wider than it is tall, no pinched neck. Finial: inverted-lotus + small GILDED kalash.
6. Rising behind and between them: THE SHIKHARA. A fat, bulbous, curvilinear rekha-type spire — roughly as tall as it is wide at the base, a parabolic bell rather than a cone. Smooth white marble faces, articulated only by ~12–16 shallow raised VERTICAL BANDS and a few horizontal mouldings near the top. Four small urushringa spirelets cluster at the shoulders on stepped offsets, each with its own tiny ribbed amalaka + kalash, reaching ~55–60 % of the spire's height.
7. Summit stack (~20–22 % of spire height): a broad fluted AMALAKA (flattened ribbed disc) → necking → 3–4 diminishing ribbed discs → small kalash pot → short metal mast carrying a gilt ball and a SAFFRON FLAG.

THE GREAT ARCH. Between the Samadhi (west) and Prabhupada's Museum (east) springs a monumental cusped marble arch that is in fact a BRIDGE linking the two buildings' upper galleries; the processional path from the gate to the temple passes underneath it. Deep cusped intrados with a fringe of pendant cusps, a carved floral archivolt, a balustraded deck above, and a small domed chhatri riding the crown. This is the most photographed object on the site and it is absent from your current model.

THE TEMPLE'S OWN SILHOUETTE. Low, flat, quiet. A single-storey (locally two-storey) arcaded range around an open courtyard, flat roof, pierced cusped-arch parapet screen, no dome, no spire. In the 23 Feb 2024 ESRI imagery a green shade net is stretched over the open courtyard (a teal rectangle ~15.5 × 7.2 m) and the same net appears in ground photographs. Its skyline contribution is essentially a parapet line at ~6–6.5 m.

## The elevation

A. SAMADHI MANDIR / MUSEUM (white marble) [form MEASURED from photos; ratios ESTIMATED ±15 %]

Storey 0 — plinth, 1.2–1.5 m. Solid. Carved elephant frieze, lotus-petal course. Opening ratio 0 %.
Storey 1 — ground, ~4.5–5.0 m. Bay rhythm ~3.5–4.0 m. Each bay: a tall CUSPED-ARCH recess (pointed-ogee head with 5 or 7 cusps) framed by a moulded band, the upper two-thirds filled with a FISH-SCALE JALI (overlapping scalloped arcs, not star-geometry, not floral). Opening:solid reads about 15–20 % void — the jalis are visually solid at distance. Doorways occupy 1–2 bays.
Cornice 1 — the wavy bangaldar sweep, with a row of PENDANT LOTUS-BUD DROPS hanging beneath it and, on some runs, a band of carved swans.
Storey 2 — first floor, ~4.0–4.5 m. Same bay rhythm but opened up: projecting jharokha balconies on curved brackets, cusped-arch windows with jali, vase-baluster railings. Opening:solid about 30–35 % void.
Cornice 2 + parapet — ~0.9–1.1 m, pierced cusped-arch screen alternating with vase finials.
Terrace level — chhatris and shikhara as described in `silhouette`.

Approach: a DOUBLE CURVING STAIRCASE climbs the front, its balustrade formed of carved SWAN/GOOSE figures — a row of birds, not turned balusters — with vase newels. Carved lions flank the inner shrine. ("White marble elephants, lions, swans, and peacocks will decorate the facade" — Back to Godhead, Sept 1980.)

Arch grammar throughout: the CUSPED MULTIFOIL arch (Mughal-Rajput), 5 or 7 cusps, with an ogee apex. There is not a single plain semicircular or plain pointed arch on the marble work.

B. TEMPLE COURTYARD ARCADE (cream-painted plaster, NOT marble) [MEASURED from photographs with human-scale check; ±15 %]

Floor — black-and-white chequerboard marble laid at 45°, squares roughly 0.30–0.45 m, with a broad plain black border band at the edges. The centre of the courtyard is SUNKEN, reached by 3–4 low steps (total drop ~0.45–0.60 m); risers black, treads white; one semicircular projecting step on the main axis.
Piers — square, ~0.55–0.65 m, ~3.0–3.5 m to the capital, bay spacing ~3.2–3.8 m. Cream shaft with SALMON-PINK base ring and capital ring.
Arches — cusped multifoil, springing ~3.2 m, crown ~4.8 m. Rise ~1.7 m. The tympanum/spandrel field is PALE CELADON GREEN, with salmon-pink foliate ornament applied over the cream. Opening:solid across the arcade ≈ 60 % void / 40 % pier.
Spandrel wall above — cream field ~1.6–2.0 m with pink floral medallions and a pink cornice band.
Parapet — pierced screen of small cusped arches, ~0.8–1.0 m.
Total to parapet top ≈ 6.0–6.5 m.
Back wall between piers — tall cusped-arch frames (~1.6 m wide × 2.6 m high) holding Krishna-lila oil paintings. Signage in the arcade: GIFT SHOP, BOOK STALL, MAHAPRASAD, Govinda's Restaurant.
Altars — three, on one side, raised a few steps, behind large cusped openings with carved dark-wood/silver-clad doors. Left: Gaura-Nitai with Prabhupada and Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati. Centre: Krishna and Balarama. Right: Radha-Shyamasundar with the gopis Lalita and Vishakha. [SOURCED — Wikipedia and vrajvrindavan.com agree.]
Courtyard furniture: a TAMAL TREE growing from a circular marble planter, a large carved marble basin/fountain, and a memorial pillar to Sripad Aindra Dasa (1953–2010), who ran the 24-hour kirtan from 1986.

C. OUTER ARCADED APPROACH (cream, between the gate and the temple)
Same cream piers and cusped arches, but freestanding and lined with large mural paintings in pink-outlined cusped frames set into the boundary wall. Distinctive detail: large CHALICE/GOBLET-SHAPED PLANTERS sit on top of short piers along the walk. Paving here is chequerboard laid orthogonally, not diagonally.

D. THE GATE ON BHAKTIVEDANTA SWAMI MARG
A cream/buff painted gatehouse with pink-outlined arches, two cream domed chhatris with pink-tipped finials, a tall cusped-arch portal with heavy brown studded wooden doors, a framed deity painting on each side, and black wrought-iron double gates with ornamental scrollwork. NOT marble.

## Material and colour

MATERIALS — and the mistake to avoid is assuming the whole place is marble. It is not. Marble is confined to (i) the Samadhi Mandir, (ii) Prabhupada's Museum, (iii) the great arch/bridge, and (iv) all the paving. Everything else — the temple courtyard arcade, the outer walk, the gate, the halls, the guesthouse, the gurukula — is RENDERED MASONRY, PAINTED.

1. Rajasthani white marble (Makrana-type) — Samadhi, Museum, arch, staircases, balustrades, paving. [SOURCED: "magnificent carved Rajasthani white marble Mandir"; Wikipedia: "built of white marble".]
2. Cement/lime plaster over brick, painted — everything else. [DERIVED from photographs: visible render texture, paint edges and repainting seams.]
3. Gilded metal kalash finials on the chhatri domes; painted metal mast and cloth saffron flag on the shikhara.
4. Black wrought iron — perimeter railings, gates, window grilles.
5. Black/dark-grey marble or slate — the dark squares of the chequerboard and all step risers.
6. Green HDPE shade net over the temple courtyard [MEASURED in ESRI imagery 2024-02-23 and in ground photos].

COLOUR — all values below are DERIVED from photographs (sampled in Python, then judgement-corrected for warm evening / green-net-tinted light). They are NOT instrument measurements. Treat as ±5 in V and ±8° in H.

White marble, full sun: #F2EDE1 — H 40°, S 6 %, V 95 %. (Raw sample from the sunlit shikhara face was #F3EEDD, H 46°, S 9 %, V 95 % — a warm off-white.)
White marble, shade: #D6CFC1 — H 37°, S 9 %, V 84 %. (Raw sample #CEC0A4.)
Marble grey veining streaks: #BDB7AC — H 40°, S 9 %, V 74 %. Never use #FFFFFF; this stone is cream-grey and it is what makes it read as marble rather than plastic.
Gilded kalash: base #C9A227 (H 45°, S 81 %, V 79 %), highlight #E8C55A.
Saffron flag: #EE7B14 — H 28°, S 92 %, V 93 %.

Cream/buff plaster, sun: #EADCB4 — H 42°, S 23 %, V 92 %.
Cream/buff plaster, shade: #C6B38C — H 40°, S 29 %, V 78 %. (Raw samples ran #C2AC7D to #958362 under the green net; correct upward for daylight.)
Salmon-pink mouldings and ornament: #E3A783 — H 20°, S 42 %, V 89 %.
Pale celadon arch tympana: #A8C0B4 — H 154°, S 12 %, V 75 %. (Raw sample #B5BFAD, H 93°, S 9 %, V 75 %; it reads as a soft eau-de-nil grey-green.)
Deeper pink outline on the gate and boundary murals: #C97B5C — H 17°, S 55 %, V 79 %.

Chequerboard, white square: #E3E1DA — H 45°, S 4 %, V 89 %, with grey veins #B9B7B0.
Chequerboard, black square: #26282A — H 210°, S 9 %, V 16 %. Reads near-black but is a very dark grey-green marble, not pure black.
Wrought iron: #1C1C1C.
Shade net: #1E8A6E at ~40 % opacity.

A note on the palette as a whole: the complex is a two-tone composition — a cool white marble monument sitting inside a warm cream-and-pink painted enclosure, with a hard black-and-white graphic floor tying them together. If you paint everything marble-white you destroy the thing that makes the courtyard photographs look the way they do.

## What NOT to build

- DO NOT put onion domes on the temple. There are no onion domes anywhere on this site. The domes are broad, LOW, deeply GADROONED (20-28 fat lobes, each ending in a leaf tip at the base), wider than they are tall, with no pinched neck and no bulge above the springing. An onion dome is a Mughal-tomb form; this is a Rajput chhatri form. They will read as completely the wrong building if you keep the onion.
- DO NOT put the domes on square drums. They sit on OPEN PILLARED CHHATRIS - an octagonal ring of about eight clustered piers of slender paired colonnettes, with cusped arches and jali tympana, open to the air, and a downturned wavy canopy eave between the colonnade and the dome. A square solid drum is the wrong idea entirely.
- DO NOT put three domes on the temple with the centre one on a chhatri. You have the composition inverted, and you have it on the wrong building. The correct composition (on the SAMADHI, not the temple) is: a tall SOLID smooth curvilinear shikhara in the centre, FLANKED by two domed open chhatris - side pavilions open, centre solid. Your model has open centre, solid sides.
- DO NOT give the main temple a spire or a dome at all. The 1975 Krishna Balaram Mandir is a LOW, FLAT-ROOFED, cream-painted arcaded building around an open courtyard, parapet at about 6-6.5 m, with a green shade net stretched over the courtyard. Every tall marble thing in the photographs belongs to the 1983 Samadhi complex. This is the single biggest correction in this brief.
- DO NOT treat Prabhupada's samadhi as 'a white marble samadhi beside it'. It is the dominant monument of the whole site and probably 70 percent of your polygon budget: a two-storey Rajasthani white-marble complex of TWO buildings - the Samadhi (west) and the Museum (east) - joined overhead by a monumental cusped marble ARCH-BRIDGE that you walk underneath to reach the temple. It has a 70-ft shikhara, two big domed chhatris, a double curving staircase, and carved elephants, lions, swans and peacocks.
- DO NOT omit the great arch. It is the most photographed object on the site and the thing that makes the place recognisable. Clear span roughly 8-11 m, deep cusped intrados with a fringe of pendant cusps, carved floral archivolt, balustraded deck on top, a small domed chhatri riding the crown.
- DO NOT make the courtyard and colonnade white marble. Only the Samadhi, the Museum, the arch and the PAVING are marble. The temple courtyard arcade, the outer walk, the gatehouse and every other building are rendered masonry PAINTED CREAM/BUFF, with salmon-pink mouldings and PALE CELADON-GREEN cusped-arch tympana. Painting everything marble-white destroys the two-tone composition that the site actually is.
- DO NOT lay a plain stone or single-colour floor. The paving throughout is BLACK-AND-WHITE CHEQUERBOARD MARBLE, squares roughly 0.30-0.45 m, laid at 45 degrees in the temple courtyard (orthogonal on the outer walk) with a broad plain black border band. It is the strongest graphic element on the site and a flat grey floor will kill the whole read.
- DO NOT make the courtyard floor flat. The centre is SUNKEN 0.45-0.60 m below the ambulatory, reached by 3-4 low steps with black risers and white treads, with one semicircular projecting step on the main axis. Devotees sit on those steps; that is what the space is for.
- DO NOT use a plain semicircular, plain pointed or plain ogee arch anywhere. The governing arch form is the CUSPED MULTIFOIL (5 or 7 cusps with an ogee apex), on the marble work and on the painted arcades alike. Get this one profile right and most of the building follows.
- DO NOT make the roof eaves straight. The marble cornices UNDULATE - a chain of shallow downward-curving ogee sweeps, one per bay, the Bengali bangaldar curve. It runs along every roof edge and every canopy. A straight eave will make the model look like a generic Indian temple.
- DO NOT use geometric star jali or floral jali in the windows. The lattice is a FISH-SCALE pattern - rows of overlapping scallops - set in tall cusped-arch frames, filling the upper two-thirds of the recess. It is used everywhere and it is very specific.
- DO NOT use pure white #FFFFFF for the marble. It is a warm cream-grey off-white, about #F2EDE1 in sun and #D6CFC1 in shade, with grey veining. Pure white reads as plastic and is the commonest way 3D temples look fake.
- DO NOT carve figures on the shikhara. It is SMOOTH white marble with about 12-16 shallow raised vertical bands and a few horizontal mouldings near the top - nothing else. The figural carving (elephants, lions, swans) is on the plinths, balustrades and staircases below.
- DO NOT crown the chhatri domes with amalakas. The amalaka + kalash stack belongs only to the shikhara summit (fluted amalaka, necking, 3-4 diminishing ribbed discs, kalash, then a metal mast with a SAFFRON FLAG). The chhatri domes get a simple inverted-lotus and a GILDED kalash.
- DO NOT put the goshala inside the compound. The campus signboard carries an arrow pointing OUT of the gate: the Bhaktivedanta Swami Goshala is a separate roughly 8-acre property on the Vrindavan Parikrama Marg, with 350+ cows. There are no cow sheds in the temple compound.
- DO NOT model one generic 'guest house'. There are at least two distinct ones and they are in different places: the Krishna Balarama Guesthouse INSIDE the compound to the north (950 m2, 27.57278/77.67725, with Govinda's Restaurant next to it), and the MVT Guesthouse & Restaurant OUTSIDE the fence immediately behind/north, gated, with large lawns and gardens.
- DO NOT build it on cardinal axes. Every structure on the site shares one grid rotated 12.3 degrees west of north (long axes bearing 347.7 deg, cross axes 77.8 deg). This is measured off OSM and confirmed against imagery.
- DO NOT make it a landmark visible across the town. The spire tops out around 21-23 m and is boxed in by three- and four-storey hotels and ashrams. From any distance the skyline here belongs to Prem Mandir (527 m west) and the Chandrodaya Mandir site (3.3 km WSW). Budget the LOD accordingly.
- DO NOT model the eastern third of the compound as a finished building. In the 23 Feb 2024 satellite imagery the Gurukula block and the ground east of it are an excavated construction/demolition site, roughly 100 x 60 m of bare orange earth with cranes and spoil heaps.
- DO NOT put the deities or external figural sculpture on the temple's outside. The three altars are INSIDE, on one side of the courtyard, raised a few steps, behind large cusped openings with carved dark-wood and silver-clad doors. What the courtyard walls carry are framed Krishna-lila OIL PAINTINGS in cusped-arch frames.
- DO NOT forget the tamal tree. There is a mature tamal growing out of a circular marble planter in the temple courtyard, and the courtyard is built around it. Also in the courtyard: a large carved marble basin and a memorial pillar to Sripad Aindra Dasa (1953-2010).
- DO NOT reach for Growse for this building. 'Mathura: A District Memoir' was published in 1883, ninety-two years before this temple existed. Growse has nothing whatever to say about it and any 'Growse on ISKCON' claim would be fabricated.

## Uncertain — flag, do not assert

- THE BIGGEST ONE: whether the main temple building carries any shikharas or domes over its three altars. My evidence that it does not: (a) the official campus signboard puts the Samadhi on the road corner left of the gate and the Temple set back 25-30 m north; (b) the 2013 street photographs show exactly ONE marble spire, immediately left of the gate, with no second spire behind it; (c) the geotagged 2019 Commons photo 'Srila Prabhupada samadhi in Vrindavan' (27.571994, 77.677528) shows that identical spire-plus-domes-plus-arch composition; (d) the 2024 satellite shows the temple block as a flat pale roof around a netted courtyard with no dome shadow. Against it: dozens of travel sites say the temple is 'constructed with white marble... with intricately carved walls and domes', but every one of those is aggregator copy with no source and they almost certainly describe the samadhi. If you can get one clear drone or rooftop photograph looking NORTH-WEST over the compound, it settles this in one second. Until then, build the temple low.
- Heights. Only ONE height is sourced anywhere: the samadhi's 70-ft central spire (Back to Godhead, Sept 1980, and that was a projection before completion, not an as-built measurement). Every other height in this brief is photographic proportion against that anchor, on a slightly upward-looking photograph, so the upper figures are probably conservative. Treat as plus or minus 20 percent.
- Samadhi opening date. Back to Godhead's January 1984 issue reports the dedication on 6, 7 and 8 November - so 1983, with S. B. Chavan, India's Minister of Planning, as guest of honour. The September 1980 article said it was 'slated for completion by the fall of 1981'. Several web sources say 1991 instead; I could not find a primary source for 1991 and I think it is wrong, but marble and museum fit-out may well have continued for years after 1983, which could be where 1991 comes from.
- Goshala founding year. ISKCON's own pages give both 1975 ('the goshala started by Srila Prabhupada in 1975') and 1976 ('personally begun by Srila Prabhupada in 1976'). I could not resolve it. Its polygon is not in OpenStreetMap and I could not fix its coordinates or extent beyond 'about eight acres on the Parikrama Marg'.
- Colours. All hex values are derived from amateur photographs taken in warm evening light or under a green shade net, sampled and then judgement-corrected. They are not instrument measurements and there is no published paint specification. The cream and the pink are the most reliable (they appear consistently across several photographers); the celadon green is the least reliable because every photograph of it was shot under the green net.
- Chequerboard square size. I could not measure this cleanly - the floor is always seen in steep perspective. 0.30-0.45 m is a human-scale estimate from three photographs and could be out by a third.
- Architect. Back to Godhead (Sept 1980) names His Holiness Surabhir-abhipalayantam dasa Goswami (Surabhi Swami), a Dutch-trained disciple of Prabhupada, as the designer of the SAMADHI. I found no source naming the architect of the 1975 TEMPLE. It is widely assumed to be the same man, and the shared architectural vocabulary supports that, but no source I found says so - do not state it as fact.
- The number of chhatri dome lobes (20-28) and chhatri piers (about 8) are counted off photographs at an oblique angle and could each be out by a few.
- The 'tamal tree, one of five in Vrindavan, around which the temple is built' is a Back to Godhead photo caption - a devotional claim, not a botanical or documentary one. The tree is definitely there; the 'one of five' is not something I can source independently.
- OSM's small building polygons inside the compound (Welcome Centre, Matchless Gifts, Internet Access, Bhisma Office, Vrindavan.tv, BBT Book Display) are identical 5-node rectangles in a neat row and were almost certainly transcribed from the campus signboard rather than traced from imagery. Trust them for what and roughly where, not for shape or size.
- I could not establish which way the deities face, or which side of the courtyard the altars are on. The signboard drawing is too schematic and no photograph I found resolves it.

## Independent check

Confidence: HIGH on everything re-derived from primary files, MEDIUM on the colour and satellite material. I did not take the survey's readings on trust: I downloaded Growse's 1883 full text from the Internet Archive and read pp.14, 132, 254, 260-261 and 266 directly (every quotation checks out verbatim); I pulled way/99418417 and way/99427181 from the OSM API and recomputed sides, bearings, areas and centroids myself (which is how I found the 12 m centroid error); I queried Overpass for named roads; I fetched the trust's, UP Tourism's, TourMyIndia's and Vrindavan Today's pages; and I downloaded the eleven cited Commons photographs, cropped them and counted the kalashas (7 on the rajagopuram, 5 on the Vaikunth Dwar), the talas (7), the gatehouse arches (3+5+3=11, and 5 in the crown), and re-sampled the colours. The "what crowns it" question - the flagged risk - I consider settled in the survey's favour by my own crop of the crest, with one exception: the survey's "shallow ribbed dome" on the west gatehouse is a misreading of a single bangaldar vault. Where I am LESS confident: I could not verify any ESRI satellite measurement (the five prakara rings, the 31 m westward offset of the nest, the tank/garden quadrant split, the west gatehouse footprint, the tower-top count), so I have defaulted those to corrections or contamination notes as instructed rather than endorsing them; and the colour work is limited by the fact that these are uncalibrated phone JPEGs in two different lighting conditions, so my re-samples establish the hue family but cannot adjudicate exact hexes. The Pushkar contamination finding I regard as high-confidence and the single most actionable item here.

### Contamination risks

- RANGJI TEMPLE, PUSHKAR (Ajmer district, Rajasthan) IS THE MOST DANGEROUS CONFUSION AND THE SURVEY NEVER MENTIONS IT. It is also called Rangji / Rangnath, also dedicated to Ranganatha, also the FIRST temple of the Ramanuja Sampradaya in its region, also 19th century, also founded by a SETH (Seth Puran Mal Ganeriwal, 1823 or 1844 depending on source), also a Dravidian-plus-Rajput/Mughal hybrid, and also has a SEVEN-TIERED GOPURAM. Crucially, Pushkar's gopuram carries a widely published height of 125 FEET (38.1 m) - and Vrindavan's has none at all. That number sits squarely inside the 38-49 m shadow-derived range the survey computed and then rightly distrusted, which makes it exactly the figure most likely to leak in and 'solve' the survey's self-declared weakest number. There is also a separate 'Old Rangji Temple' in Pushkar, so travel content about 'Rangji Temple' is routinely ambiguous about which town it means. Rule: any height that ever surfaces for 'the Rangji gopuram' must have its town verified before use.
- TRUST BOARD 1873 vs GROWSE'S 1868 COMMITTEE - A CONFLICT THE SURVEY REPEATS WITHOUT FLAGGING. The trust says the Trust Board was formed in 1873. Growse p.261 says 'In 1868, the whole estate was transferred by the Swami - the deed of transfer bearing a stamp of Rs. 2,000 - to a committee of management, who on his death were bound to appoint a successor.' Growse was writing within a few years of the event and names the stamp duty; the trust's 1873 may be a later re-constitution. Do not present either as settled.
- THE GURU'S NAME DIVERGES BETWEEN SOURCES AND RISKS BECOMING TWO PEOPLE. Growse: 'Swami Rangacharya' (with a footnote listing his Sanskrit works and giving his death as 26 March 1874). The trust and several travel sites: 'Sri Rangadeshik Swamiji' / 'Sri Rangadesik Swamiji' - and some of those recast him as the INITIATOR of the building rather than the supplier of plans ('construction was initiated in 1845 by Sri Rangadesik Swamiji'). Do not let the variant spawn a second figure, and do not let it shift founder credit away from the Seths.
- 'OVER 15 ACRES' IS IN CIRCULATION FOR THIS COMPOUND AND IS ROUGHLY DOUBLE. 773 x 440 ft is 7.8 acres / 3.16 ha. Several aggregators state 'over 15 acres' alongside a corrupted 'outer wall measuring 770 feet by 440 feet'. The corruption of Growse's own number in the same sentence as the area error is a warning that the whole aggregator chain around this temple is unreliable, including for the 'eight gopurams' and 'six storied' claims.
- 'EIGHT GOPURAMS' AND 'SIX STORIED' BOTH CIRCULATE WIDELY. Vrindavan Today: 'eight stately yet delicate south-Indian style gopurams'. Multiple aggregators: 'eight gopuras built in Dravidian style... one of which is six storied and totally covered with beautiful carvings'. My photographic count settles SEVEN talas on the rajagopuram, so 'six storied' is refuted; the count of eight remains unresolved and must not be built as eight. The trust and UP Tourism describe only two principal gopurams.
- GROWSE'S WORD 'GOPURA' AND THE 1949 CAPTION'S WORD 'DOME' ARE BOTH LOOSE - AND THIS PROJECT HAS BEEN BURNED BY EXACTLY THIS. The Government of India Photo Division titles a photograph of an unambiguously sala-crowned Dravidian tower 'The dome of the temple of Ranga at Brindaban, Mathura', and adds 'Build recently' about an 1851 building. A period caption saying 'dome' is not evidence of a dome - the same trap as Growse's 'the central dome is perfect' at Govind Dev, which put an onion dome on the outside of a building that has an internal vault.
- File:Gate of Shri Rangji Mandir Vrindavan.jpg IS PROBABLY NOT THE 93-ft WEST GATEHOUSE. The survey uses it as its evidence for the west forecourt ('a wide, almost empty sandy-paved lane in front of it with plain modern sandstone-block buildings alongside'). But its arch is a tall pointed ogee, not the broad multifoil cusped arch shown in both files explicitly captioned 'Western Gate', and the structure reads as about two storeys against the west gatehouse's three-plus-crown. It is most likely a secondary gate. Do not derive the character of the west forecourt from it - and note it conflicts with the survey's satellite-derived forecourt of 'trees, parked cars and buses, stalls, and a curving access road'.
- THE CHAR-BAGH IS A SATELLITE READING DRESSED IN MUGHAL VOCABULARY. 'Formal four-square char-bagh, four beds each ~18 x 18 m, cross axial paths ~3 m wide, small square platform/pavilion at the crossing' has no textual source whatever - Growse says only 'a fine tank and garden'. Given that the same 20 m-grid technique produced the 12 m centroid error, treat the garden's internal geometry as invented until a photograph or plan turns up. The same applies to the four inner prakara rings (136x104, 100x72, 68x44, 52x26 m), which the survey itself concedes are read off a grid to +/-3 m through roofs and awnings.
- THE SHEESH MAHAL IS ABSENT FROM THE SURVEY. File:Sri Rangji Mandir Courtyard with Dhwaja Stambha carries a sign beside the flagstaff enclosure reading 'शीश महल / SHEESH MAHAL - मात्र 5/- रुपये / ONLY 5/-RUPEES - DO WATCH IT', alongside 'NO PHOTOGRAPHY'. A ticketed mirror-hall inside the inner court is a real interior the brief does not account for at all.
- 'THE ONLY LARGE ESTABLISHMENT IN ALL UPPER INDIA THAT IS OWNED BY THE FOLLOWERS OF RAMANUJA' IS AN 1883 STATEMENT, NOT A PRESENT-DAY FACT. The survey quotes it from Growse p.14 in its source list without a date qualifier. Pushkar's Rangji temple alone (also Ramanuja Sampradaya) shows how the landscape differs once you leave Growse's 'Upper India' framing.
