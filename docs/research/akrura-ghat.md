# akrura-ghat

*Researched and independently verified 2026-09-27. Confidence: partial.*

## oneLine

Shri Akrur Dham (locally Shri Akrur Mandir, Shri Radha Puram, Vatsalya Gram) — a walled temple garden on the old Akrur Ghat, standing alone on the Yamuna floodplain roughly midway between Vrindavan and Mathura, about 940 m back from where the river now runs, where Akrura saw Krishna and Balarama in the water and where Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is said to have lodged during his Vrindavan visit of 1515.

## location

Between Vrindavan and Mathura, on the west (right) bank floodplain of the Yamuna, in open farmland — not inside Vrindavan town. Postal identity, per a business listing: Shri Akrur Mandir, Shri Radha Puram, Vatsalya Gram, Temple Road, Vrindavan 281121.

Bearings and distances recomputed from the node against OSM/Nominatim coordinates — all of the account's figures verified:

- Pagal Baba temple: 0.96 km NW (323°), at 27.55863, 77.67916
- Bhatrond / "Bathran Bihari" temple (OSM node 7287733294): 0.61 km NNE, at 27.55664, 77.68800 — Growse's Bhat-rond
- Vrindavan Chandrodaya Mandir: 4.67 km WNW (300°)
- Vishram Ghat, Mathura: 5.12 km S (180°)
- Mathura Junction railway station: 8.05 km S (188°) — note this, because press copy claiming "16 km from Mathura railway station" cannot be describing this site
- Vrindavan 132 kV substation: about 0.7 km NW; District Combined Hospital Mathura 0.88 km NNW; Shri Laxmi Life Line Hospital 1.03 km NNW
- Mathura Road: 0.86 km WNW
- Yamuna: 0.94 km E
- The Ramanreti Krishna-Balaram tree, near ISKCON Vrindavan: roughly 3.5 km away — a different place, listed here only so it is not built here

ROUTE (WVA-VVRS, and corroborated by Stephen Knapp's "across the road nearby is a path leading to Akrura Ghat" from the Pagal Baba complex): travelling from Vrindavan toward Mathura, Vatsalya Gram appears on the right; turn LEFT opposite it onto a narrow asphalted lane; the lane drops off the bluff and, after a few hundred metres past a small village, the temple is on the RIGHT (south) side with nothing around it but fields.

Older bearings for cross-checking: Gaudiya sources and Brajdiscovery place Akrur Ghat "four miles north of Mathura and one mile south of Vrindavan", with Akrur-gaon adjacent. Growse (1883) calls Akrur "a hamlet between Mathura and Brinda-ban" and also describes buildings in it. The site's alternative scriptural name is Brahma-hrada, "Brahma's pool", after the vision granted there to Nanda and the cowherds.

## architecture

VERDICT ON THE ACCOUNT UNDER REVIEW: the geography is excellent — I re-measured every figure against OpenStreetMap and they hold. The architecture section is a different matter, and I have to be blunt about it: NONE of it is independently sourceable. Wikimedia Commons has no photograph of this site (a 2 km geosearch returns only Neem Karoli Baba's ashram); there is no Wikidata item; OSM maps no building or wall footprint anywhere within 800 m; the Vrindavan Research Institute's own temple list does not include the site. The entire description of stone, arches, brackets, spire finish, plaques and boundary comes from one researcher reading photographs. I could neither confirm nor refute it. It is internally consistent and plausible, and the two elements that a third party does confirm (the Panchmukhi Hanuman shrine and the Shiva linga, both attested in the WVA-VVRS text by 2013) match. Treat the whole of it as one-source observation, not as established fact.

What I can carry forward, corrected:

A single walled compound of about five separate built things in a garden, all modern, not one designed monument.

1. MAIN MANDIR — single storey with a flat walkable roof terrace and a spire. Pale pink-buff Rajasthani sandstone (Bansi Paharpur / Dholpur family), machine-cut. Deep open pillared verandah across the full front wrapping into open colonnaded side wings; round columns rendered white with maroon bands at base and neck, carrying white carved elephant-bracket capitals; deep flat slab roof with a carved dentil eaves course. Back wall of the verandah: three cusped multifoil arched bays, the two outer ones filled with pierced jali, the centre one the sanctum approach. In that centre bay the sanctum doorway is visibly older than the shell: moulded painted stucco in white, yellow, green, teal and red, with vertical registers of small crowned figures in cusped niches up both jambs, floral scroll borders, swastika-and-kalash motifs on the reveal, and above the lintel a gilt crest of one long ribbed barrel-dome flanked by two small ribbed onion-domed kiosks. Carved timber double doors, brass ring handles, applied white floral bosses. Sanctum a small chamber with a white marble throne canopy on four slender turned colonnettes, cusped arch, black-and-gold floral inlay in the spandrels, magenta cloth backdrop, two-stepped simhasana draped in orange-gold brocade. Roof terrace edged by a parapet of square pierced jali panels alternating with plain posts, small chhatris at the corners.
   SPIRE: rising from the centre-rear of the terrace, a smooth bulbous curvilinear shikhara, CEMENT-RENDERED AND WHITEWASHED, with two or three flat RED painted bands around its lower mouldings and another at the neck, small amalaka and kalash. Plastered, not stone; no carved latas, no urushringas.
   Side wings: open galleries with jali balustrades between piers, holding black-framed glazed cases of painted panels — an internal parikrama of pictures. The WVA-VVRS text names the scenes shown: PUTANA, BAKASURA, KALIYA, GOVARDHAN. (The account's "and Chaitanya's life" is its own reading; the sourced list is Krishna-lila.) Faded red Devanagari on the verandah wall: "श्री कृष्ण शरणम् मम".
   Forecourt: grey-and-white stone slabs in a chequer/diagonal pattern, slightly below the plinth, reached by two or three broad shallow steps in grey-white marble.

2. THE TREE THROUGH THE VERANDAH — a large old tree rising through the front of the mandir platform at its right-hand end, trunk passing through the roof slab, low cream-washed curved masonry seat round its base, brass bell hung above. Build the tree; DO NOT call it the Krishna-Balaram tree (see doNotBuild).

3. THE BANYAN — a very large old Ficus benghalensis, fused multi-stem trunk, hanging aerial roots, base daubed with sindoor and wrapped in cloth: the tree pointed out as Chaitanya's resting place. NOTE A CONFLICT: the WVA-VVRS text places this banyan "to the right of the altar", i.e. tight against the shrine, while the account places it free in the garden. Either there are two trees or one siting is wrong. Unresolved — keep the photographic siting but flag it.

4. PANCHMUKHI HANUMAN PAVILION beside the banyan — small free-standing square open pavilion with a PITCHED ROOF OF RED CLAY PAN TILES over a white fascia, the only pitched tiled roof on site. Square piers clad in dark maroon marble with white bands, walls faced in white-grey veined marble with black skirting and lintel band, stainless-steel tube railings on a grey plastered plinth about 0.6 m high, grey stone floor with white inlaid grid lines, "राम राम" in orange either side of red-painted timber doors, brass bell, ceiling fan. Dedication plaque dated 2 June 2009 (one-source).

5. SHIVA MANDAPA — open pavilion in the same pink-buff sandstone: slender fluted columns on lotus bases, cusped arches, low pierced balustrades in a fish-scale jali pattern, grey stone floor, steps with a black marble inlay strip. Centre: a raised BLACK octagonal jalahari holding a white marble linga with a metal naga hood. Black marble donor plaque, gold lettering, dated 28 March 2010 (one-source).

6. ANCILLARY BLOCK behind the Shiva mandapa — plain two-storey whitewashed rendered building, barred windows, panelled double doors, more black donor plaques. Wholly utilitarian.

7. GARDEN AND ENCLOSURE — clipped hedges, lawn, banana plants, young trees. Low wall faced in beige tile/stone with piers and ball finials carrying black iron railings with gold spearhead finials and circular scroll panels; elsewhere plain plastered wall or mesh. Small red-and-yellow tin signboard "श्री अक्रूर धाम" at the gate. A large white marble information board in a sandstone frame, lettered in green, inside.

8. THE GHAT — there is no stone ghat at the temple. Correct. The river is 938 m away.

TERRAIN — THE ACCOUNT GETS THIS WRONG AND IT MATTERS FOR A 3D MODEL. The setting is not simply "flat irrigated fields". SRTM 30 m sampled along an east-west transect through the site: the Mathura Road ridge stands at 176–178 m, the Bhatrond/"Bathran Bihari" temple node 612 m NNE stands at 179 m, Akrur Dham itself is at 169 m, and the river is at 162–166 m. So there is a khadar bluff of roughly 10 m between the high ground carrying the Mathura Road and this site, and the temple stands on the low floodplain terrace below it; the approach lane drops off that bluff on its way east. Growse independently describes the same escarpment in the 1880s: the Bhat-rond temple stands "on the verge of a cliff overlooking a wide expanse of alluvial land". Model the bluff — it is why the temple is invisible from the main road.

SURROUNDINGS — flat irrigated fields on the terrace, a small village on the approach lane, scrub and low woodland. Two 132 kV six-cable transmission lines cross the area (OSM ways 116046189 and 519130763, both tagged cables=6, voltage=132000): the nearer line's conductors pass 89 m WEST of the site, with pylons at 130 m NW and 173 m SSW; the second line is 783 m NNW. The Vrindavan 132 kV substation is about 0.7 km NW, the District Combined Hospital Mathura 0.88 km NNW and Shri Laxmi Life Line Hospital 1.03 km NNW. A cluster of small water bodies (probable old channel remnants) lies 0.86–1.03 km N. The skyline here is pylons and trees, not spires. All of this is confirmed in OSM.

THE NEIGHBOUR THE ACCOUNT MISSED. The OSM point it lists only as a bearing — "Bathran Bihari" temple, 612 m NNE at 27.55664, 77.68800 — is Growse's BHAT-ROND. Growse describes it in the hamlet of Akrur as "a solitary tower containing an image of Bihari Ji. In front of it is a forlorn little court-yard with walls and entrance gateway all crumbling into ruin", with the Bhat-mela held on Kartik Purnima on the road between it and a Seth's garden opposite. This, not Akrur Dham, is the genuinely old monument in this landscape, and it is the Bhojan-sthal / "Bhatrol gaon" of the Braj tradition. If the model covers this square kilometre it should be there, on the high ground, as a tower with a ruinous forecourt — and it must not be merged into the Akrur Dham compound.

## distinguishing

1. THE DEVOTEE IN THE CENTRE, THE LORD FLANKING HIM. Akrura stands in the middle with Krishna and Balarama one on each side. Four independent sources say exactly this and it is the single recognisable fact about the shrine. (The account's stronger claim — that this is "the one altar in Braj where a bearded human devotee occupies the centre" — is overstated and should be dropped. Braj is full of saint shrines: Neem Karoli Baba's samadhi mandir is about 1.9 km from this very node, the Goswami samadhis stand at Radha Damodar, Haridas at Nidhivan, Pagal Baba 0.96 km NW. What is unusual here is not a devotee on an altar but a devotee centred and the Lord placed to either side of him in a Krishna temple.)

2. A GHAT WITH NO RIVER AND NO STEPS. The place is called a ghat; there is no flight of steps and no water. Measured: the Yamuna centreline is 938 m due east (bearing 84°). The WVA-VVRS text says independently that "the Yamuna has now moved away by about 1 km from Akrura Ghat"; Stephen Knapp says it "used to be along the Yamuna River until the river changed its course". A pilgrim walks out across fields.

3. THE SURABHI COW AND CHAITANYA'S FOOTPRINTS FACING THE ALTAR. Three sources: the cow is opposite the altar and the lotus footprints of Chaitanya are beside her. Together with the flanking arrangement, this is the shrine interior. The footprints were missing from the account and are the more distinctive of the two.

4. A LIVING TREE GROWING UP THROUGH THE VERANDAH SLAB at the right-hand end of the mandir front, boxed by a cream masonry seat, brass bell above. Photographic, not sourced, but very specific.

5. THE WHITE PLASTERED, RED-BANDED SPIRE ON A PINK-BUFF STONE BODY. Cement render over warm sandstone — that clash is the silhouette from the lane. Photographic.

6. THE COMPOUND READS AS DONATED PIECES, NOT A DESIGN. Run by the Shri Akrurdham Gopinath Foundation (Regd.), Mathura-Vrindavan; the Varshney (Barahseni) community claim descent from Akrura and treat this as their kul-tirtha, and the dated plaques on site carry Varshney/Gupta donors. Hence five unrelated pavilions scattered in a garden rather than one composition. The community's descent claim is well attested online; the specific text the account reports from the marble board (Vrishni-kula, the Shurasena republic, Vrishni coins in the British Museum, the eleventh mural in Parliament) is a reading of one photograph and I could not corroborate it.

7. A ~10 M BLUFF BEHIND IT AND PYLONS BESIDE IT. The temple sits on the floodplain terrace, 10 m below the Mathura Road ridge, with a 132 kV line 89 m to the west. Agricultural-industrial, not picturesque.

## doNotBuild

- DO NOT build a riverfront ghat. No flight of stone steps to water exists here. Do not put the Yamuna at its foot, do not give it a Keshi-Ghat embankment, boats or a river-facing facade.
- DO NOT put it in Vrindavan town or on the parikrama marg. It is 4.17 km SSW of Keshi Ghat, out in fields, on one narrow lane.
- DO NOT build the 2026 government scheme as though it stands. ₹6.35 crore sanctioned (₹3.17 crore released) in May 2026 by the UP government, developed by the Braj Teerth Vikas Parishad with the Irrigation Department, and first proposed as far back as May 2024 in a slightly different form (35 × 14 × 5 m then). Nothing I found says work has finished, and one later line of Hindi copy that says a ₹7.6 crore "new Akrur Ghat" is "under construction" is unattributed and inconsistent with the ₹6.35 crore figure. If you build the red-stone steps, the 5 m bathing platform, the sheet piling or the 220 m feeder channel, label them PROPOSED.
  Correct one thing the account asserts: no source states where those works go relative to the temple. The news says only "on the right bank of the Yamuna" at Akrur Ghat; the 2024 report says the ghat is by Akrur village with the temple adjacent. Placing them down at the water is a sound INFERENCE — a 220 m channel cannot reach a temple 938 m from the river, and the approach lane's east end is 131 m from the mapped river centreline and about 1.1 km ESE of the temple — but it is inference, so mark it as such.
- DO NOT trust "16 km from Mathura railway station / 15 km from the new bus stand". That is press boilerplate from the ghat story and does not fit this site: Mathura Junction is 8.05 km due south in a straight line (about 9–10 km by road), and Vatsalya Gram is reported as 5 km from Mathura. Drop the figure.
- DO NOT call the tree through the verandah "the Krishna-Balaram tree". THE KRISHNA-BALARAM TREE IS SOMEWHERE ELSE: it is the yamalarjuna at Ramanreti on the Vrindavan parikrama marg, about 440 m from the ISKCON Krishna-Balaram Mandir and roughly 3.5 km from here, a pale trunk growing from a dark one, whose white side has been dying since 2004, also known as Chaitanya Bait. No source connects a two-coloured tree to Akrur Ghat. Build the Akrur Dham tree as an observed physical feature with its dark and pale streaks if the photographs show them, but give it no name and no dead white limb.
- DO NOT import the Varshney Samaj's "AKRUR DEV DHAM" MEGA-PROJECT. That scheme — colossal hilltop statues of Krishna and Akrura, cave temples, waterfalls, a Child University, a medical centre, "several acres" — is at Panethi on the Delhi–Kolkata GT Road near ALIGARH, about 80 km away, and as far as the record shows it is a plan on paper. It is not this place. Its renders are the exact Chandrodaya trap.
- DO NOT import SHRI VARSHNEY MANDIR, ALIGARH (shrivarshneymandir.org), which has a statue of Shri Akrur Ji Maharaj over a huge grand gateway. Different temple, different city. Akrur Dham has no gateway of that kind.
- DO NOT give this compound a gopuram or grand gateway at all. Low tiled wall, black iron railings with gold spear finials, a small painted tin signboard.
- NOT a carved stone shikhara. Cement render, whitewashed, smooth, bulbous, painted red bands. No rekha latas, no urushringas, no stone jointing.
- NOT red sandstone. Pale pink-buff. Do not reach for Agra red.
- DO NOT put Ahalya Bai's baoli here. Growse records "a large and handsome bauli of red sand-stone, with a flight of 57 steps leading down to the level of the water", the gift of the Maratha queen of Indore, standing on the old riverside road in this same corridor between Vrindavan and Akrur. It is a stepwell, not a ghat, it is not at Akrur Dham, and it is the likeliest reason someone will try to give this site red stone steps. Leave it out unless you are modelling the whole corridor.
- DO NOT merge the Bhatrond / "Bathran Bihari" tower into this compound. It is a separate temple 612 m NNE, on the high ground, with its own ruinous forecourt.
- NOT ancient fabric. Several sources, including Amar Ujala, call it an ancient temple. Everything visible is 20th–21st century: machine-cut stone, cement, stainless steel, Mangalore-pattern tiles, plaques of 2009 and 2010. Only the painted-stucco sanctum doorway looks older than its shell. Do not weather it like a medieval monument, do not carve it like Govind Dev, do not ruin it.
- NOT multi-storey. One storey plus a walkable roof terrace; only the spire and the corner chhatris rise above that.
- DO NOT make Krishna dark and Akrura fair — the photographs show the reverse — but equally, do not state as fact that Akrura is larger than the Lord. The sources say only that he stands in the centre.
- DO NOT clear the site of infrastructure. The pylons, the substation, the hospitals and the plain whitewashed office block are part of what stands there.
- DO NOT INVENT A FOUNDER OR A FOUNDATION DATE for the main mandir. The record gives none.
- CORRECT THE ACCOUNT'S CLAIM ABOUT GROWSE. It says Growse "names Akrur only as a hamlet on the Ban-jatra route and describes no building there". That is false. Growse's index carries three entries for Akrur, and in the chapter on the Mathura–Brinda-ban road he describes buildings in the hamlet: the Bhat-rond temple with its crumbling courtyard and gateway, the Seth's garden opposite, the Bhat-mela at Kartik Purnima, and nearby Ahalya Bai's 57-step baoli and Seth Kushal's 40-bigha walled garden with a stone temple in it. His festival calendar also records "Janaki Navami. Held at Akrur. Sita's birthday." What remains true, and is the point worth keeping, is that GROWSE DESCRIBES NO AKRURA / KRISHNA-BALARAMA TEMPLE AT THE GHAT AND GIVES IT NO FOUNDER AND NO DATE.

## deities

MAIN SANCTUM — attested by four independent sources (WVA-VVRS/akruraghat blog, Brajrasik, Gaudiya Treasures of Bengal, Stephen Knapp), all of which say the same thing in almost the same words:

- SHRI AKRUR JI, standing in the CENTRE.
- SHRI KRISHNA and SHRI BALARAM, standing one on either side of him.

That is the whole of what the record supports. The devotee is central and the Lord flanks him. His beard, his dark complexion, his being set forward of the other two, and his being larger than Them are all readings of photographs and are not in any citable source — model them if the photographs show them, but do not state them as documented.

FACING THE ALTAR, inside the same chamber:
- SURABHI / KAMADHENU, the divine cow. Three sources place her specifically "opposite the altar". This belongs in the build list, not only in the notes.
- SHRI CHAITANYA MAHAPRABHU'S LOTUS FOOTPRINTS (charan-chinha), immediately next to the Surabhi cow. Three sources. This object is MISSING from the account under review and must be added — it is a physical thing on the floor of the shrine, normally a slab bearing carved paduka-marks.

SEPARATE PAVILIONS IN THE GARDEN (both independently attested as existing by 2013, via the WVA-VVRS site page):
- PANCHMUKHI HANUMAN (five-faced).
- A SHIVA LINGA.

The site's registered body is the "Shri Akrurdham Gopinath Foundation (Regd.), Mathura-Vrindavan", and several pilgrim sources speak of an "ancient Gopi Nath temple" at Akrur Ghat in addition to the Akrura–Krishna–Balarama shrine. This is unresolved: either the main mandir IS locally the Gopinath temple, or there is a second shrine the account missed. Do not invent a second shrine, and do not confuse it with the Goswami Radha-Gopinath temple of Vrindavan town (Madhu Pandit's, deity gone to Jaipur), which is a different and far older building 4 km away.
