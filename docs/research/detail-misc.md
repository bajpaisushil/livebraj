# Braj architectural detail: misc

*Researched and verified 2026-09-27. Impact: transforms-it.*

## whatItIs

THREE ELEMENTS, and Growse records the Braj workmen's own names for all three in his glossary (pp. 427-430, 3rd ed.), which is worth using for the helper names because it keeps the vocabulary honest:

- **dasa** — "in architecture, a string-course" (Growse, Glossary, p. 428). A thin horizontal band, same stone as the wall, projecting a hand's breadth. It is a SHADOW LINE, not a shelf. It does two jobs: it marks a floor or stage line, and it caps the register below it.
- **chhajja** — "stone eaves of a house or other building, supported on projecting brackets" (Growse, p. 427). This is the big one. Braj's chhajja is not a Mughal chhajja quoted politely; Growse repeatedly singles out its DEPTH as the local signature: Rani Lachhmi's kunj at Kesi Ghat has "unusually broad eaves which have a wavy pattern on their under-surface and are supported on bold brackets" (p. 264); Banke Bihari's gateway is praised for "the extremely bold projection of its eaves" (p. 141); the whole modern Braj street style is "protected from the weather by broad eaves, the under-surface of which is brightly painted" (p. 155). THE SOFFIT IS PAINTED AND THE BRACKETS ARE VISIBLE. A plain slab is not a chhajja.
- **alin** — "a stone jamb of a doorway; a pilaster, or attached pillar, as distinguished from khambh, a detached pillar" (p. 426); also **jharap**, "a prop, an attached shaft or pilaster" (p. 430). Supporting terms: **chhari** = the shaft, **bharna** = the capital, **chhina** = "the capital of a pillar, WHEN IT HAS BRACKETS ATTACHED TO IT" (p. 427) — i.e. Braj distinguishes a plain capital from a bracket capital lexically, which tells you the bracket capital is the normal one. **dila** = a panel; **dehri** = "a threshold; also a strip of pavement between two piers of an arcade"; **gokh** = "a window on an upper story with a projecting balcony".

THE GRAMMAR, as Growse actually describes these buildings:

HORIZONTAL. A Braj facade is explicitly read in STAGES, and he counts them every time. Radha Vallabh: an east facade 34 ft broad "which is in three stages, the upper and lower Hindu, and the one between them purely Muhammadan in character" (p. 255). Govind Dev: the walls "are pierced in two stages, the upper stage being a regular triforium" (p. 241). Sati Burj: "a total height of 55 feet and is in four stories: the lowest forms a solid basement" (p. 118). Radha Vallabh's interior repeats it: "a double tier of openings north and south; those in the lower story having brackets and architraves and those above being Muhammadan arches".

That is the single most important fact for this vocabulary and it is not decorative: **the register change is an ORDER change.** Lower register = trabeate, square-headed, lintel on brackets (Hindu). Middle register = cusped arches (Muhammadan). Upper register = trabeate again. Growse names this alternation as the defining note of the whole Akbari Braj style: "in a facade one story, or one compartment, shows a succession of multifoil saracenic arches, while above and below, or on either side, every opening is square-headed with the architrave supported on projecting brackets" (p. 172). A temple built as one uniform register of cusped arches is wrong for every building in the 1590-1627 group.

For towers the rule is stated outright: at Govind Dev's choir tower, "the stages of the tower diminish from the bottom upwards in regular proportion and all bear the same ornamentation" (p. 247). Equal ornament, linear diminution — that is the recipe for every tiered element you have.

VERTICAL. The wall is divided into BAYS by pilasters or piers, and Growse's bay counts are astonishingly consistent: FIVE, or THREE. Radha Indra Kishor: "a square of seventy feet divided into three aisles of five bays each". Jai Singh's Ghera pavilion: "two aisles divided into five bays by piers of coupled columns of red sandstone". Rani Lachhmi's kunj: "a colonnade of five arches standing on a high plinth". The Bharatpur cenotaphs at Gobardhan: "a colonnade of five open arches on each side", and the cella has "each wall divided into three bays, of which one is left for the doorway, and the remainder are filled in with reticulated tracery". Gopinath: "a curious arcade of three bracket arches, serving apparently no constructural purpose, but merely added as an ornamental screen to the south wall". Hari Dev: "four openings on either side" — the one even number, and it is a long flank, not a facade.

So: ODD bay counts on a face, centre bay takes the door or the main arch, and a BLIND arcade added purely as wall-dressing is documented Braj practice (Gopinath), which is exactly the cheap trick this vocabulary needs.

The pier itself: not a single shaft. "The piers are composed of four conjoined pillars, each shaft being a single piece of stone" (Radha Indra Kishor); "piers of coupled columns"; "massive clustered pier" (Ganga Mohan Kunj). And the shaft profile is documented: "square at bottom, then an octagon, and then polygonal", with bells carved in relief on the shafts at Govind Dev, a second cushion capital added on top when more height was wanted, two columns stacked for loftier buildings, "and afterwards it became the fashion to make even short columns with a notch in the middle, so as to give them the appearance of being in two pieces" (pp. 171-172). That notch is a free detail: one thin box.

WALL FIELD BETWEEN THE BANDS. Two documented fillings, and they are period markers. Akbar-period: square panels with a vase of flowers in low relief (Chaube Ji ka Burj, Mathura — "the walls are divided into square panels, in each of which, boldly carved in low relief, is a vase filled with flowers", p. 154). Late Jat / 19th-century: Growse is contemptuous but precise — "the wall-ornamentation consists of nothing but an endless succession of niches and vases repeated with wearisome uniformity" (p. 173). Niche-and-vase is the correct late filling, and "wearisome uniformity" is exactly what a cheap instanced loop produces, so the budget constraint and the history agree for once.

=== THE REGISTER LADDER — use FRACTIONS OF H, never absolutes ===
H = height from finished floor to the top of the chhajja slab. Default H = 4.6 m for a single-storey Braj temple [P].

  0.00 H   ground / court level
  0.12 H   top of moulded plinth          (0.55 m at H=4.6)   [P]
  0.24 H   dado string course, OPTIONAL — only on a face over 5 m long with no opening in it   [C]
  0.52 H   arch springing / lintel line   (2.40 m)            [P]
  0.80 H   MAIN STRING COURSE, over the arch crowns (3.68 m)  [C, keyed to 0.52H springing + arch rise]
  0.80-0.96 H  the frieze register: 0.75 m of niche-and-vase panelling or blind arcading  [G for the motif, C for the height]
  0.96 H   chhajja soffit / bracket heads (4.42 m)
  1.00 H   top of chhajja slab            (4.60 m)            [P]
  1.20 H   top of parapet                 (5.50 m)            [P]

Two-storey front: repeat the ladder, floor line at 3.0-3.4 m [C] for a house or kunj, 4.2 m for a tower [D from Sati Burj]. A string course MUST sit on every floor line — that is what a floor line is, from outside.
Monumental Akbari temple: cornice height = 1.5 x internal hall width [D, Hari Dev]. Govind Dev's two stages divide roughly 7.4 m trabeate + 4.6 m triforium, which is what the existing builder already uses (LandmarkGenerator.js:2941-2942) and it is right.

=== STRING COURSE (dasa) — NUMBERS ===
  total height        0.18-0.28 m   (≈ 0.05 H). Two slabs: 0.18 lower + 0.10 upper cap.
  projection          0.09-0.11 m per side for the lower slab; 0.05-0.06 m for the cap.
  ratio that matters  projection ≈ 0.4-0.5 x band height. Deeper than that = shelf.
  count               a wall of H ≤ 4 m gets ONE. 4-6 m gets TWO. Over 6 m or multi-storey, one per stage, never more than four.
  colour              on SANDSTONE: same hex, shade only — tint(color, 0.90) for the recessed slab, tint(color, 1.05) for the lit cap. On LIME PLASTER: a genuinely different tone (ochre band on cream wall), because a plastered Braj temple picks its registers out in colour.
  All [C] for the millimetres; the record genuinely does not publish a measured moulding profile for any Vrindavan temple. Growse draws elevations at 20 ft = 1 inch, which cannot resolve a 0.2 m band.

=== CHHAJJA (cornice) — NUMBERS ===
  projection      0.75-0.95 m on a temple front; 0.55-0.70 m on a house or compound wall.
                  Comparator: Rajasthani haveli chhajjas are published at "up to 0.6 m"; Growse calls Braj eaves "unusually broad" and "extremely bold" by comparison, so Braj sits ABOVE the Rajasthani figure. Take 0.85 m as the Braj default. [C, anchored on a published haveli figure + Growse's explicit comparative]
  slab thickness  0.14-0.16 m, plus a 0.08-0.10 m fascia lip standing on its outer edge.
  slope           2-4 degrees, or none. Flat is acceptable at phone resolution; the lip does the work.
  brackets        0.16 m wide x 0.34 m deep drop x (0.80 x projection) long.
  bracket spacing 1.0-1.2 m on a wall run — 1.1 m default [P/C]. DOUBLE THEM AT EVERY CORNER (one on each face, both within 0.25 m of the quoin).
  door-hood spacing 0.25-0.30 m — Jugal Kishor's side doorways have EIGHT brackets under one small hood [G]. So the vocabulary needs TWO spacings: 1.1 m for a wall, 0.28 m for a doorway hood or a jharokha canopy. This contrast is a real, documented Braj thing and it is free.
  soffit          painted, a different colour from the slab [G, twice]. Red-ochre or green on a cream building.
  Akbari group only: instead of dense brackets, "large projecting heads of elephants and sea-monsters" AT INTERVALS along the cornice [G, Hari Dev]. Interval ≈ 2.5-3.5 m, i.e. one per bay, not one per metre. [C for the interval]

=== PILASTER (alin) — NUMBERS ===
  width           0.30-0.35 m [P, "piers 0.30-0.35 m square"]
  projection      0.08-0.12 m from the wall face (≈ 1/3 of its width). A pilaster proud by more than half its width has become a buttress.
  base block      0.30 m tall, 0.04 m wider each side than the shaft
  capital         0.42 m wide x 0.16 m tall, plus a 0.20 m bracket-impost above it (the chhina, the bracket capital)
  notch           a 0.10-0.12 m tall band at 0.5 of shaft height, 0.02 m proud — Growse's documented fashion of notching short columns "so as to give them the appearance of being in two pieces"
  clustered pier  0.55-0.70 m square for a real structural pier: four conjoined shafts [G]
  bay width, blind pilaster rhythm on a wall:  1.8-2.8 m, 2.4 m default
  bay width, real arcade:                      2.4-4.3 m  (4.27 m at Radha Indra Kishor [D], 2.07 m if Radha Vallabh's 10.36 m facade is 5 bays [D])
  BAY COUNT:  face ≤ 7 m → 3 bays.  8-14 m → 5 bays.  over 14 m → 7 bays. ALWAYS ODD, centre bay takes the opening. [G — five and three are the only counts Growse ever records on a facade]
  pilaster count = bays + 1, and the two at the corners are DOUBLED (coupled), per "piers of coupled columns" [G].
  A pilaster STOPS at the string course above it. A second, shorter one starts above that course. It never runs plinth-to-cornice on a two-stage wall.

=== A MODULUS THAT MAKES THE NUMBERS LAND (offer, not a claim) ===
Akbar's Ilahi gaz ≈ 0.813 m. On that module: band height 1/4 gaz = 0.20 m, chhajja projection 1 gaz = 0.81 m, pilaster width 3/8 gaz = 0.30 m, blind bay 3 gaz = 2.44 m, storey 4 gaz = 3.25 m, plinth 2/3 gaz = 0.54 m. Every one of those falls inside the ranges above. I have NOT found a published statement that these specific Braj temples were set out in gaz, so treat it as a convenient generator that produces defensible numbers, not as a documented fact.

=== FIRST, THE THING THAT IS ACTUALLY WRONG ===

`grep -rn "registers(\|chhajja(\|mouldedPlinth(\|parapet(\|jharokha(" client/src` returns ONLY the five definition lines. Zero call sites in 6,569 lines. All sixteen temple builders draw their walls with raw `b.box(...)` and then go straight to `cuspedArch` and `dome`. That is, precisely and mechanically, why "no single temple looks exactly as it really is" and why they photograph as boxes: the correction was written, documented with a survey comment at lines 405-421, and never wired in. Before writing any new geometry, wire these five into all sixteen builders. That alone is most of the fix.

=== THE MeshBuilder FACTS A BUILDER NEEDS ===
- `b.box(cx, cy, cz, w, h, d, color, rot, faces, topColor)` — **cy is the BOTTOM of the box**, not its centre (MeshBuilder.js:68, y0=0/y1=h). Every band stacks by adding its own height.
- `b.panel(cx, cy, cz, w, h, color, rot, lift)` — cy IS the centre here. 2 triangles. This is how you get a painted chhajja soffit and a niche back for nothing.
- `faces` bitmask: 0b000001 top, 0b000010 bottom, 0b000100 -Z, 0b001000 +Z, 0b010000 -X, 0b100000 +X. A bracket projecting +Z from a wall never shows its top (slab above) or its -Z (against the wall): pass 0b111010 (= 58) and it costs 8 triangles instead of 12.
- `tint(color, k)` at line 970 is the shading helper; use it, never a second hard-coded hex.

=== RECIPE 1: stringCourse() — one box wrap, 12 triangles for the whole building ===
A band that wraps all four faces is ONE box slightly larger than the wall, not four boxes:

  function stringCourse(b, cx, y, cz, w, d, rot, color, h = 0.20) {
    b.box(cx, y,     cz, w + 0.20, h * 0.75, d + 0.20, tint(color, 0.90), rot, 0b111100); // no top/bottom
    b.box(cx, y + h * 0.75, cz, w + 0.11, h * 0.25, d + 0.11, tint(color, 1.06), rot);     // the lit cap
  }

Two boxes, 22 triangles, for a band that runs right round the temple. Call it twice per building. This is the single cheapest realism-per-triangle item in the whole vocabulary — cheaper than one cuspedArch.

BUT: where a face has a large central arch, the band must not run through it. Add an optional `breakAt` — draw the band as two segment boxes per face, stopping 0.15 m short of the arch frame on each side. Costs 4 boxes per broken face instead of 1. Only do it on the entrance face.

=== RECIPE 2: pilaster() — 2 boxes, 16 triangles, seen from one side ===
  function pilaster(b, cx, y, cz, rot, color, h, w = 0.32, out = 0.10) {
    b.box(cx, y,         cz, w,        h,    out, tint(color, 1.04), rot, 0b111010); // shaft
    b.box(cx, y + h * 0.5 - 0.05, cz, w + 0.04, 0.10, out + 0.02, tint(color, 0.92), rot, 0b111010); // the notch
    b.box(cx, y + h,     cz, w + 0.10, 0.16, out + 0.04, tint(color, 0.90), rot, 0b111011); // capital
    b.box(cx, y + h + 0.16, cz, w + 0.16, 0.14, out + 0.10, tint(color, 1.02), rot, 0b111011); // bracket impost
  }

4 boxes, ~32 triangles each with masks. For a 12 m face at 5 bays that is 6 pilasters = ~190 tris per face. If that is too much, drop the notch box first (it reads only within ~8 m) and then the impost.

Place them with a bay loop, corners doubled:
  const n = w <= 7 ? 3 : (w <= 14 ? 5 : 7);
  for (let i = 0; i <= n; i++) { const t = i / n - 0.5; ... }
  plus one extra pilaster inset 0.34 m from each corner (the coupled corner pier).

=== RECIPE 3: chhajja() — mostly already right at line 458, three fixes ===
The existing helper is sound: brackets at 1.1 m, slab plus lip. Three corrections:
 (a) its bracket boxes are full 12-triangle boxes; pass faces 0b111010 → 8. On a 12x12 m temple that is 44 brackets, so 176 triangles saved per temple, 2,800 across sixteen.
 (b) it has NO SOFFIT COLOUR. Growse documents a painted underside twice. Add one `b.panel()` per face, lying flat under the slab, in an ochre or red accent — 2 triangles per face, 8 per temple. This is a nine-triangle change that adds the most visible documented Braj detail in the whole set.
 (c) add a `step` parameter so a doorway hood can pass 0.28 and get Jugal Kishor's eight close brackets.

=== RECIPE 4: the frieze register — niche-and-vase, instanced ===
Between the main string course (0.80 H) and the chhajja (0.96 H) sits a 0.75 m band. Fill it per bay with one blind niche: a `b.panel()` in tint(color, 0.72) for the dark recess (2 tris) + a 0.10 m box frame around it (8 tris with masks) + a two-box vase, 0.12 m wide, in the niche (16 tris). 26 tris per bay, 6 bays per face. If the budget bites, keep the dark panel and the frame and drop the vase — the shadow rectangle is 90% of the read at street distance.

Blind arcading is the documented alternative and is cheaper still: Gopinath has "a curious arcade of three bracket arches, serving apparently no constructural purpose, but merely added as an ornamental screen". So a row of `cuspedArch(..., shade = 0x2a1e16)` with NO opening behind it is not a cheat, it is what the building does.

## whereItAppears

=== HAS IT, AND IT IS THE SCULPTED AKBARI KIND (1580-1627, red Bharatpur/Rupbas sandstone) ===
GOVIND DEV, MADAN MOHAN, GOPINATH, JUGAL KISHOR, RADHA VALLABH. Growse treats these as one series with Hari Dev at Gobardhan, and he is explicit that they share detail. For all five:
- Registers are STRUCTURAL STAGES, not applied bands: trabeate below, arcuate in the middle, trabeate above. Radha Vallabh's 34-ft facade in three stages is the cleanest documented case; Govind Dev is two stages with a triforium.
- The band is the SAME RED STONE as the wall. Never a colour change. Shade only.
- The cornice is deep and sculpted, with "large projecting heads of elephants and sea-monsters" at intervals (Hari Dev, and the closest surviving parallel for the group).
- Gopinath carries a purely decorative three-arch blind screen on its south wall, on a "fine boldly moulded plinth", on a terrace with a carved stone front that Growse personally dug out.
- Jugal Kishor: the eight-bracket door hood, north and south.
- CRITICAL NEGATIVE, documented: Govind Dev "has none of the coarse figure sculpture which detract so largely from the artistic appearance of most Hindu religious buildings" (p. 251). So Govind Dev's bands, brackets and pilasters must stay GEOMETRIC. No elephant brackets, no figures. Its shafts carry bells in relief and that is all. Getting this wrong is worse than leaving the detail off.
- Also documented for Govind Dev: the moulded plinth was buried under 2.44 m of debris until 1873, and clearing it was the making of the elevation. The existing builder already lifts it (FL = ground + 1.1, line 2941). Good. The bands are missing.

=== HAS IT, IN THE 19TH-CENTURY PLASTERED / STREET-FRONT KIND ===
RADHA RAMAN (1826), BANKE BIHARI (1864), SHAHJI (1876), RADHA DAMODAR, RADHA SHYAMSUNDAR, RADHA GOKULANANDA, SEVA KUNJ walls, the KESI GHAT kunjes, KATYAYANI (1923). Growse's description of the style then in vogue is a literal spec for these: "the front is of carved stone with a grand central archway and arcades on both sides let out as shops on the ground floor. Storey upon storey above are projecting balconies supported on quaint corbels, the arches being filled in with the most minute reticulated tracery of an infinite variety of pattern, and protected from the weather by broad eaves, the under-surface of which is brightly painted."
- Here the register IS picked out in colour, because the surface is lime plaster.
- Here the jharokha helper (line 522, also uncalled) belongs, at every upper-storey bay.
- Banke Bihari specifically: Growse saw it new — "a large square red sandstone block of plain, but exceedingly substantial, character, with a very effective central gateway of white stone... the delicacy of its surface carving, and the extremely bold projection of its eaves". PLAIN BLOCK, ONE RICH GATEWAY, VERY DEEP EAVES. The current builder gives it a string course at line 2515 and that is the only band in the whole file — it is on the right building.
- Radha Raman: the project's own survey already warns it is nearly indistinguishable from the houses around it. Give it the street-front grammar, not a temple grammar.

=== POINTEDLY DOES NOT HAVE IT ===
RANGJI (1845-1851). Dravidian, built by south Indian Sri Vaishnavas under Swami Rangacharya — Growse notes it as "the only large establishment in all Upper India" of that sampradaya. Its horizontal grammar is a KAPOTA cornice with kudu dormers and a hara of miniature shrines, and its pilasters are Dravidian pilaster-and-niche pairs on each gopuram tier. NO bracketed Braj chhajja, NO cusped-arch register, NO merlon parapet. The current builder already reaches for "pilastered niches under a barrel-vaulted shala roof" (line 3445) which is correct — do not overwrite it with the Braj kit. The one Braj-vocabulary item it shares is the tiered-diminution rule: equal ornament, regular diminution, per Growse on Govind Dev's tower.
PREM MANDIR (2012), ISKCON KRISHNA BALARAM (1975), CHANDRODAYA, CHAAR DHAM (2025). Modern white marble and concrete. They have cornices, but carved marble bands, not sandstone slabs on brackets, and no painted soffit. Applying the chhajja here is the second-most-likely way to make these sixteen look wrong.
GOPISHWAR MAHADEV, IMLI TALA, VAMSI VAT, NIDHIVAN, BRAHMA KUND. Shrine-and-tree sites. The horizontal grammar here belongs to the ENCLOSURE — the compound wall and its gate — not to a temple mass. Give the wall one band and a coping; give the gate the full kit.
KESI GHAT, YUGAL GHAT, CHIR GHAT, KALIYA GHAT. Ghat architecture: the registers are the step courses themselves, plus the cornices of the stone kiosks. Growse: "a succession of ghats reaching for a distance of about a mile and a half", "graceful stone kiosques", and at Kesi Ghat the two Bharatpur ranis' kunjes with the unusually broad wavy-soffit eaves on bold brackets. Kesi Ghat is the best place in the whole town to spend triangles on a chhajja, because it is the one elevation everybody photographs.

## mistakes

1. **Leaving the helpers uncalled.** Already the live bug. Five finished helpers, a 17-line survey comment, zero call sites. Fix this before adding anything.

2. **Absolute band heights.** A band hard-coded at y = 2.0 m lands at mid-wall on a 4.6 m temple and at ankle height on Govind Dev. Always pass a fraction of H. `registers()` at line 553 currently defaults to `at = [2.0]` — an absolute — and derives the pilaster top as `max(at) * 1.7`, which is arbitrary and produces a pilaster stopping at 3.4 m on every building regardless of its cornice.

3. **Bands that do not line up between adjacent masses.** The single strongest realism cue in this whole vocabulary: the SAME course must wrap the sanctum, the porch, the flanking chapels and the compound gate at ONE height. Braj builders did this because the courses are real stone beds. Two masses with bands 0.3 m apart look worse than two masses with no bands at all.

4. **Band too fat, too proud.** 0.40 m tall and 0.30 m out is a shelf, and in flat shading it grows a bright top face that reads as a ledge of snow. Keep it 0.18-0.28 m tall, 0.09-0.11 m proud, and mask the top face away (0b111100) on the lower slab.

5. **Running the string course straight through the arch.** It must die into the arch frame or break over the opening. This is the difference between masonry and gift-wrap.

6. **One pilaster from plinth to cornice on a two-stage wall.** The Braj wall changes ORDER at the string course — trabeate below, arched above. The pilaster stops at the course and a shorter one starts above. A single giant order is European and reads instantly wrong.

7. **Even bay counts on a facade.** Growse records five and three, never four, on a front. An even count puts a pilaster where the door should be.

8. **Uniform bracket spacing round the corner.** Brackets must double at the quoin, one on each face. A corner with a 1.1 m gap either side of it collapses visually and the chhajja looks unsupported exactly where it cantilevers furthest.

9. **Forgetting the painted soffit.** Growse mentions it twice and it is 2 triangles per face. A grey underside makes the deepest, most characteristic element in Braj architecture read as a concrete awning.

10. **Figural brackets on Govind Dev.** Explicitly contradicted: Growse praises it for having "none of the coarse figure sculpture". Elephant and makara brackets belong on Hari Dev's cornice, Jugal Kishor's door hoods, the Mathura balcony near Sati Burj, and the Mathura quadrangle with "bold brackets carved into the form of elephants and swans" — not on Govind Dev.

11. **Colour-shifting a band on sandstone.** On the Akbari red-sandstone group the band is the same stone: shade it, do not tint it a different hue. On lime plaster the opposite is true. Getting this backwards makes a 16th-century temple look like a 20th-century one.

12. **Cross-contaminating Rangji and the marble temples.** Braj chhajja + merlons on Rangji, or on Prem Mandir, undoes the one thing those buildings are for: being visibly not-Braj in the middle of Braj.

13. **Overclaiming the numbers.** The record on mouldings is genuinely THIN. No published measured drawing gives the height or projection of a named string course on any Vrindavan temple. Growse's elevations are at 20 ft to the inch. The band millimetres above are a synthesis of his documented overall dimensions, the project's own photographic surveys, and craft-plausible ranges — defensible, buildable, and not to be written into a comment as "measured". The bay counts, the stage counts, the bracket counts, the building dimensions and the painted soffit ARE documented, and those are the ones worth citing in code comments.
