# ter-kadamba

*Researched and independently verified 2026-09-27. Confidence: partial.*

## oneLine

Ter Kadamb is the walled kadamb grove and bhajan-sthali of Srila Rupa Goswami about 1.7 km north-east of Nandgaon village — the place where Krishna climbed a kadamb to "ter" (call) His cows by name — today a painted, low-rise Gaudiya Math-kept compound of vine-covered bowers, a rasa-mandala platform, two named kunds and one old bent tree, materially rebuilt and extended between 2023 and 2025.

## location

27.71983 N, 77.39941 E — area centroid of OpenStreetMap way 335855976, "Ter Kadamb" (amenity=place_of_worship, religion=hindu, denomination=vaishnavism). Nominatim's own centre for the same way is 27.71970, 77.39943. The bhajan kutir, OSM way 335855975, is at 27.71958, 77.39963.

Administratively: Nandgaon, Chhata tehsil, Mathura district, Uttar Pradesh. Nominatim's reverse geocode returns "Ter Kadamb, MDR143W, Nandgaon, Chhata, Mathura, Uttar Pradesh, India" — but note that the "MDR143W" element is Nominatim borrowing the nearest classified road name. The OSM way itself carries no address tag of any kind.

Relative to landmarks, all recomputed: 1.69 km at bearing 054° (NORTH-EAST, not ENE) from the OSM Nandgaon village node at 27.71097, 77.38568; about 2 km from Nand Bhavan; Asheshwar Mahadev temple 281 m due SOUTH at bearing 175°, across the dirt road, and that is the landmark drivers and pilgrims are told to ask for. The metalled Nandgaon–Javat road, OSM way 218266622, highway=secondary, ref MDR143W, asphalt, passes 700 m to the north-west at bearing 316°. Access is by OSM way 335855014, a 2.06 km unpaved unclassified track that passes 33 m from the kutir; the 2012 directions say it is "the first dirt road on the left" if you come from Pavan Sarovar and turn toward Barsana, and the site is about 1.5 km down it on the left. Barsana is 8.3 km SSW.

On "midway between Nandgaon and Javat": every source repeats it, but it is a quotation from Sridhar Maharaja's Sanskrit verse inscribed on the site ("nandagrāma ta eva yāvaṭa-pathe madhya-sthale"), meaning it stands on the Nandgaon–Yavat road. It is 1.5–1.7 km from Nandgaon. I could not find Javat/Yavat in OpenStreetMap under any spelling, so I cannot give a hard figure for the other leg — but the site is plainly not the geometric midpoint.

It is NOT in or near Vrindavan town: 33.4 km straight-line ESE to Banke Bihari, about 50 km by road via Chhata; 50–54 km from Mathura, about an hour's drive.

## architecture

THE HEADLINE PROBLEM: THE ACCOUNT IS THREE TO FIVE YEARS STALE.

Their newest verified photographs are from 2021 and they say so honestly. But the site was substantially rebuilt afterwards. Rupanuga Bhajan Ashram's dated first-hand report of 11 January 2024 states: "There has been a lot of renovation here and a new temple kuṇḍa called 'Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma Kuṇḍa' has been made." An Instagram post of 31 January 2025 announces "Ter Kadamba, New Temple manifested." And the one photograph in that January 2024 report that looks into the compound shows built fabric that the 2021 account rules out. Building their 2021 description is the Chandrodaya error inverted: not a render that was never built, but a state that no longer stands.

WHAT I CONFIRMED FROM THE JANUARY 2024 PHOTOGRAPHS (I downloaded and read them directly).

The Rupa Toran is REAL and almost exactly as described. A freestanding gateway of two rendered piers, a flat lintel band painted BRIGHT RED, over it a cream gable with a cusped, cloud-lobed silhouette carrying raised letters "श्री RUPA TORAN" with a SWASTIKA at the end of the line, and on the apex a stacked RED-AND-WHITE KALASH FINIAL. Pale inscription plaques on both piers. A dark iron grille in the opening, a pendant lamp hanging in it, a red fire extinguisher on the right pier. Scaled against people it is of the order of 4.5–5.5 m overall. Their single most distinctive architectural claim is verified.

The merlon-and-trident boundary crest is REAL. Along the wall right of the entrance runs a repeating crest of pointed lotus-bud merlons with slender three-pronged TRIDENT forms standing between them — exactly the unusual combination they describe. But in January 2024 the whole crest reads WHITE, not red-orange, and at the entrance it sits on top of a solid rendered wall well above head height, not on a 1.0–1.1 m parapet you look over.

THE OUTER APPROACH IS NOT WHAT THEY DESCRIBE. They have "a plain gateway of two white square piers and a flat lintel" over "a bare, pale dust forecourt used for parking." As of January 2024 it is: an ARCHED gateway in a SALMON-PINK rendered boundary wall; the flanking piers clad in speckled grey GRANITE slab divided by two thin white banding courses; a pair of tall BLACK steel gates, roughly 2.2–2.5 m, with vertical bars, a large circular motif and white Hindi lettering on the leaf; PINK vinyl signboards screwed to the granite; and a forecourt and entrance apron of INTERLOCKING CONCRETE PAVER BLOCKS, grey with red-brown accent courses. Against the wall, green steel mesh TREE GUARDS with BOUGAINVILLEA climbing the pink render, and a green works plaque reading "वन विभाग … मथुरा 2020-21" — a UP FOREST DEPARTMENT plantation plaque of 2020–21. Build the pavers, the granite, the tree guards and the bougainvillea.

THERE IS A PITCHED ROOF TALLER THAN FIVE METRES. Behind the Rupa Toran stands a building with a GABLED roof in pale green sheeting and a salmon-red rendered gable end, carrying at least two RED-AND-WHITE KALASH FINIALS on the ridge, standing clearly higher than the toran's gable. It is not a shikhara and not a tower, but it is not flat-roofed and it is not under five metres. This is very likely part of the 2023–25 building work. Their "two small single-storey flat-roofed blocks" and "nothing over about five metres" cannot both be true of the site today.

THE PERGOLA IS A VINE BOWER, AND THEY LEFT THE VINES OUT. This is the correction most likely to change the look of the model. historypandit.com describes "टेर कदम्ब का लताओं से आच्छादित प्रवेश द्वार" (an entrance clothed in creepers) and "यहां आज भी सुंदर लताओं से आच्छादित कुंज हैं। कुंज इतने सघन हैं कि धूप मुश्किल से जमीन तक पहुंच पाती है" — beautiful vine-covered kunj, so DENSE THAT SUNLIGHT BARELY REACHES THE GROUND. Five of the twenty-four brajrasik album captions are about creepers: "Beautiful way under creepers hanging", "Long view of hanging creepers", "Inside view of hanging creepers", "Close view of beautiful creepers". Their concrete post-and-beam grid with timber poles is plausible as the armature, and the mahamantra lettering on the beams is the kind of detail a photograph gives you. But the structure is a LATA-KUNJ, a planted arbour under heavy vine cover, and the light under it is deep shade, not the "lattice of branch-shadow" they describe. Clothe the frame.

THE WATER. Two names are in play and there may be two bodies. Older sources call the tank TER KADAMBA-KUNDA (vrindavanforest.com; historypandit's "टेर कदम्ब कुंड"). The January 2024 report names a NEW one, KRSNA-BALARAMA KUNDA. OpenStreetMap maps two adjoining waters. Their account treats it as one restored tank and never names it. In the January 2024 photograph of Krsna-Balarama Kunda I can see: a rectangular tank of dark green-brown water; a broad RED-BROWN (salmon) stone coping and step in the foreground; side banks of ROUGH GREY STONE PITCHING sloping into the water with grass berms above — NOT the "straight plastered cement revetments" they specify; a broad red-brown stepped platform with small cheek posts and a low rail across the far side; rough grass, scrub and trees beyond; and several small low BROWN-RED domed shelters or benches scattered on the far bank, which matches their description. Two long bamboo poles project into frame from above, consistent with an arbour at the near edge.

THE MARBLE PLAQUES ARE PHOTOGRAPHED AND I READ THEM. White marble slabs, landscape format, recessed into a wall clad in BEIGE-CREAM VEINED marble (not the "polished grey-white" they give), each surrounded by a thick TEAL-GREEN painted plaster border with traces of an earlier RED coat beneath. Incised, black-filled Devanagari in two ruled panels with small flourishes. One carries Sridhar Maharaja's verse "nandagrāma ta eva yāvaṭa-pathe madhya-sthale sundare / ṭer-ītyarākhya-kadambake-bhajana-pīṭho'taiva rūpa-prabhoḥ / pūrvair atra ca nirmitaṁ bahu-kṛtāt kakṣatrayaṁ bhikṣubhir / asmābhir hy adhunaiva marmara-śilā-dayair-bhūṣitaṁ saṁskṛtam" — recording that the THREE CHAMBERS (kakṣa-trayam) built here earlier were renovated and decorated with MARBLE by his party. A third plaque sits in a PINK semicircular tympanum over the kutir door and is headed "श्री श्री रूप-गोस्वामि-पादानां भजन-कुटीरम्" — the door plaque names the building. Their reading of that doorway is right in structure and wrong in two colours: the tympanum is PINK, not red, and the mahamantra above it is in RED Devanagari on a PALE BLUE band, not white Devanagari.

THE HISTORY THEY OMITTED ENTIRELY. Their account reads as anonymous folk building. It is not. Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura singled the place out; B. R. Sridhar Dev-Goswami Maharaja called it his favourite place in all Vraja-mandala and carried out repairs there, recounted in his own words in 1987 ("he told me that this is my place… there is something to be done there, some renovation needs to be done"); his Sanskrit verses are engraved on marble on site; the three chambers were marble-faced by his party. The present mahant is Sripada Hrsikesa Maharaja, in the line of Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaja, a disciple of Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati. THIS EXPLAINS THEIR OWN PUZZLE: the "HRISEKESH MANCH" plaque with Odia script beneath is named for the mahant, Hrsikesa Maharaja. That their photo-reading threw up a name that turns out to be independently documented is the strongest evidence that they were genuinely looking at photographs of this site.

THE GEOMETRY, CHECKED AGAINST OSM DIRECTLY. Every polygon figure they give is correct. Way 335855976 is a seven-sided polygon, sides 195/58/127/66/24/51/127 m, bounding box 221 m N–S by 205 m E–W, area 2.57 ha, tagged amenity=place_of_worship, religion=hindu, denomination=vaishnavism, name="Ter Kadamb". Way 335855975 "Rupa Gosvami Bhadjan Kutir" is 5 x 4 m; way 335855978 building=temple is 10 x 6 m and sits 13 m WEST of the kutir (they said 12 m west). Ways 335855971 (22 x 40 m) and 335855973 (sides 25/44/7/24/32/68 m) are both natural=water, water=pond. All four lie inside the compound polygon. Way 335855014 is a 2.06 km highway=unclassified, surface=unpaved that passes 33 m from the kutir — a through track, not a spur.

But their pond axes are SWAPPED. Together the two waters span about 60 m NORTH–SOUTH by 70 m EAST–WEST; they say "roughly 70 m north–south and 45 m east–west". Way 335855973 alone is 34 m N–S by 70 m E–W. The long axis of the water is EAST–WEST. And "a big restored tank filling the north and east" is wrong: both waters sit in the north-centre and north-WEST (longitudes 77.3990–77.3997) while the polygon runs east to 77.40054. The eastern lobe is open park with no mapped water. The "narrow earthen neck you can walk along" is an inference from the 2–9 m gap between the two polygons, not something a source states.

THE TREE. Their refusal to build a textbook Neolamarckia cadamba is right, and right for a reason they did not cite: Braj Chetna records that the tree became BENT and LOW under the weight of the cowherd boys' lunch bundles and "is still like this today". Build it low, bent and spreading. On species, be more careful than they are. The identification of the Braj kadamb as Mitragyna parvifolia (kaim) is a real and well-argued position — N. cadamba is a moist-forest tree of north-east India that would not survive unaided in dry Braj, whereas M. parvifolia is native and locally dominant — but it is a contested scholarly argument, not settled fact, and popular sources still gloss kadamba as "burflower tree" (= Neolamarckia). State it as a judgement.

The grove itself is mostly gone and mostly young. Several sources agree: "many kadamba trees here have all disappeared on account of stagnant rainwater"; "only a few kadamba trees remain"; new kadambs planted by devotees. Add the 2020–21 Forest Department plaque and the steel mesh tree guards and the picture is clear: a handful of old trees among recent planting in guards.

STILL UNVERIFIED — neither confirmed nor refuted by any independent source I could reach. There is NO open photographic corpus for this site: Wikimedia Commons geosearch within 2 km returns only Holi photographs from Nandgaon village 1.47 km away, and a Commons text search for "Ter Kadamb" returns nothing at all. So the following rest entirely on their own photo-reading — plausible, internally consistent, and unsupported: the eight named painted cows and their names; the blue elephant, the two horses; the three see/hear/speak-no-evil monkeys on the lilac arch; the white marble peacock-backed throne "श्री व्रज बिहारी मंच" and the black umbrella; the marble rasa dais at 20–25 m; the pergola at 8 x 5 bays; the mint-green statue plinths; the chequerboard kutir floor; the stone-flag-and-sand split with its painted red kerb; the five steps behind the toran; and the whole 2013 colour scheme. Treat all of it as one researcher's testimony.

WHAT IS INDEPENDENTLY SOLID. The rasa platform is confirmed three separate ways — "a rasa-mandala platform established here that commemorate the rasa-lila pastimes" (vrindavanforest), "View of Maharaas Sthali" (brajrasik album), "रासलीला का मंच" (historypandit) — and brijwale adds that it is in live use: "the platform where the mischievous pastimes of Radha Rani and Lord Krishna are staged by artists disguised as Radha-Krishna." Build it, and build it as a working stage. Live PEACOCKS are on site (two sources). Daily kheer prasad is served, commemorating the story of Radharani coming as a little girl with kheer for Rupa Goswami. A copy of the manuscript of Upadesamrita is kept there. Gopashtami is the one busy day: brajwasis bring small boys dressed as Krishna and Balarama from Nandgaon, hold a samaj of devotional singing, and worship the cows and feed them grass and jaggery.

Timings are confirmed: 06:00–17:30 in winter, 06:00–18:30 in summer.

## distinguishing

Their two chosen distinguishers survive, but one of them needs rewriting and I would rank a third above both.

FIRST, AND VERIFIED IN A 2024 PHOTOGRAPH: THE RUPA TORAN AND THE TRIDENT-AND-LOTUS-BUD CREST. A small freestanding gateway whose cream gable is cut into a cusped, cloud-lobed silhouette, carrying raised letters "श्री RUPA TORAN" with a swastika beside them, over a band of lintel painted bright red, topped by a stacked red-and-white kalash finial. Running away from it, the boundary is crowned with pointed lotus-bud merlons with slender three-pronged TRIDENTS standing in every gap. I have seen both of these in an independent photograph dated January 2024. Nothing else in Braj is crested like that. Note that the crest reads WHITE in 2024, not the red-orange they specify.

SECOND: THE VINE BOWER GROWN INTO THE TREE. Their instinct is right and their description is half-dressed. The courtyard has no roof. A grid of pale-yellow concrete posts and beams, the posts hand-painted with naive folk flower-vines, the beams lettered in red Devanagari with the mahamantra and "श्रीरूप गोस्वामी जी की भजन स्थली", has been built through and around one enormous old low-spreading tree with rough poles thrown over it. But the whole thing is a LATA-KUNJ: it is planted with creepers and the Hindi sources say the bowers are so dense that sunlight barely reaches the ground. So it is not a lattice of dappled branch-shadow, it is a deep green tunnel of vine with a concrete skeleton and a living tree leaning through it. That combination — concrete frame, ancient bent tree, and heavy creeper all reading as one structure — is the thing.

THIRD, AND THEY MISSED IT: THIS IS A GAUDIYA MATH PLACE, AND IT SAYS SO IN MARBLE. Recessed in the marble-faced walls, inside teal-green painted borders, are incised white marble slabs carrying Sanskrit verses composed by Sridhar Maharaja, one of which records that the three chambers here were "decorated and renovated with marble by us mendicants". The kutir door plaque, in a pink semicircular tympanum under the mahamantra painted in red on pale blue, reads "श्री श्री रूप-गोस्वामि-पादानां भजन-कुटीरम्". The altar platform is signed HRISEKESH MANCH with Odia script beneath — for Sripada Hrsikesa Maharaja, the present mahant, in Bhakti Saranga Goswami's line. The place is not anonymous folk building. It is a documented twentieth-century Gaudiya Math restoration of a sixteenth-century bhajan-sthali, and the inscriptions are how it tells you.

FOURTH, still on their word alone but worth building if true: THE COWS HAVE NAMES PAINTED ON THEM. Cement cows on pale mint-green plinths, each in a painted jhul carrying her name in red Devanagari — Gulabi, Lali, Kumkumi, Dhumar, Khamra, Pili, Kari, Dheori. "Ter" means to call, and this is the place where Krishna called His cows by name, so the conceit is exactly right for the site. I could not verify the statues or the names anywhere. In their favour, the names are not copied from the standard textual lists (Syamali, Dhauli, Pitambari, Kalindi; or Shyama, Bhuri, Kapila) but read as Braj vernacular cognates of them, which is what you would expect of real painted labels and not of a fabrication.

## doNotBuild

THEIR WARNINGS THAT STAND, UNCHANGED AND VERIFIED:

1. NOT IN VRINDAVAN. 33.4 km ESE straight-line to Banke Bihari, roughly 50 km by road. If the model puts it near Vrindavan it is in the wrong part of Braj. Verified against OSM.

2. DO NOT CONFUSE IT WITH RUPA GOSWAMI'S KUTIR AND SAMADHI AT RADHA-DAMODAR in Vrindavan. Those are inside the town, in the Radha-Damodar courtyard. Correct warning — but DROP their "red-sandstone" gloss on Radha-Damodar. Wikipedia gives Jiva Goswami and 1542 and the samadhis of Rupa, Jiva and Krishnadasa Kaviraja in the premises, and says nothing about red sandstone. Do not carry an unsupported material claim into a second model.

3. NOT STONE, NOT CARVED, NOT RED SANDSTONE HERE EITHER. Rendered brick and concrete in flat commercial paint, with marble only as cladding and small pieces. Confirmed in every photograph I saw, with the addition of polished granite slab on the outer gate piers.

4. DO NOT BUILD A TEXTBOOK KADAMBA. No tall straight bole, no upward whorls, no orange pompom flowers. Braj Chetna independently records the tree as BENT and LOW "still like this today". Build it low, heavy-limbed and leaning.

5. DO NOT ROOF THE COURTYARD. The bower is open to the sky above the vines.

6. DO NOT PAVE EVERYTHING. Rough grass, scrub, dust and brick paths across the outer park.

7. THE RASLILA PLATFORM HAS NO STAGE BUILDING. No roof, no proscenium, no backdrop, no seating.

8. DO NOT ADD A MURTI OF RUPA GOSWAMI. A framed picture in a marble niche. Independently confirmed by the brajrasik caption "Portrait of Shri Roop Goswami".

9. DO NOT BUILD THE 7 M TOWER FROM THE GUIDEBOOK LINE. I found and read the original: terkadamba.blogspot.com, December 2012 — "The place that marks where the bhajana kutir of Sanatana Gosvami is located is a 7m (21 ft) high brick building." It names SANATANA, not Rupa, and it does sit at the end of driving directions. Their catch is correct and it was worth making. But see correction (E) below — do not turn it into a positive claim that nothing on site exceeds five metres.

CORRECTIONS — THINGS IN THEIR ACCOUNT THAT WOULD THEMSELVES BE BUILT WRONG:

A. DO NOT BUILD THE 2021 SITE. It is not the current one. "A lot of renovation" and a new Krsna-Balarama Kunda by January 2024; a "New Temple manifested" announced 31 January 2025. Their own instruction "USE THE LATER COLOUR SCHEME… build the 2021 scheme" is now the stale instruction. Build 2024 or later, or state the vintage on the model.

B. DO NOT PAINT THE MERLONS RED-ORANGE. In January 2024 the merlon-and-trident crest reads WHITE.

C. DO NOT BUILD A PLAIN WHITE OUTER GATEWAY ON A DUST FORECOURT. As of 2024: an arched salmon-pink rendered gateway, piers clad in speckled grey granite with two white banding courses, tall black steel gates with a circular motif and white Hindi lettering, pink vinyl signboards, an apron of interlocking concrete pavers, green steel tree guards, bougainvillea on the wall, and a UP Forest Department 2020–21 plaque.

D. DO NOT MAKE THE WHOLE BOUNDARY A ONE-METRE SEE-OVER WALL. At the entrance the merlon-and-trident crest tops a solid rendered wall well above head height — of the order of two metres. Their 1.0–1.1 m version may describe an inner enclosure; it is not the outer boundary at the gate.

E. DO NOT ASSERT "NOTHING OVER FIVE METRES, NO SPIRE OF ANY KIND." A building with a pitched gable roof in pale green sheeting, a salmon-red gable end and at least two red-and-white kalash ridge finials stands behind the Rupa Toran, taller than it. Not a shikhara, not a tower — but not flat and not under five metres.

F. DO NOT BUILD SMOOTH PLASTERED CEMENT REVETMENTS. The 2024 photograph of Krsna-Balarama Kunda shows rough grey stone pitching on the side banks with grass berms above, a red-brown coping, and a broad red-brown stepped platform with cheek posts and a low rail. Their warning against a wild weed-choked pond is right; their warning against ghats on all four sides is probably right; the material is wrong.

G. DO NOT LEAVE THE PERGOLA BARE. It is a creeper-clad kunj so dense that sunlight barely reaches the ground. Plant it.

H. DO NOT SWAP THE POND AXES. The two waters together run about 60 m north–south by 70 m EAST–WEST, not 70 north–south by 45 east–west. And the water is in the north-centre and north-WEST of the compound; the EAST lobe is open park.

I. DO NOT LEAVE THE KUNDS UNNAMED, AND DO NOT ASSUME THERE IS ONLY ONE. Ter Kadamba-kunda is the older name; Krsna-Balarama Kunda is the new one made by January 2024; OSM maps two adjoining waters. Braj Chetna also mentions a nearby lake called Shyam Talaiya, which may be the unnamed OSM water 313 m south by the Mahadev temple. Unresolved.

J. DO NOT PUT AN OSM ADDRESS ON IT. Way 335855976 carries only amenity, religion, denomination and name. "MDR143W" appears only in Nominatim's SYNTHESISED reverse-geocode, borrowed from the nearest classified road 700 m away. Their line "OSM gives the address road as MDR143W" would send the next checker looking for a tag that does not exist.

K. DO NOT USE THEIR CENTRE POINT AS A CENTROID. 27.71980, 77.39966 is the arithmetic mean of the seven vertices. The true area centroid is 27.71983, 77.39941, about 25 m west; Nominatim returns 27.71970, 77.39943.

L. IT IS NORTH-EAST OF NANDGAON, NOT ENE. Bearing from the OSM village node is 054°; the distance, 1.69 km, is right.

M. DO NOT REPEAT "MIDWAY BETWEEN NANDGAON AND JAVAT" AS A MEASUREMENT. It is a quotation. It comes from Sridhar Maharaja's Sanskrit verse inscribed on site — "nandagrāma ta eva yāvaṭa-pathe madhya-sthale" — and every guidebook has copied it. The site is 1.5–1.7 km from Nandgaon and Javat is considerably further; I could not locate Javat in OpenStreetMap at all.

N. ADD A CONFUSION THEY MISSED: KADAM KHANDI. Sridhar Maharaja himself called this place "Kadam-khandi", and sources describe a former "kadamba-khandi" grove here. But Kadam Khandi is ALSO a distinct site on the Yamuna bank at Vrindavan — a small temple marking where Nityananda recovered the Bankima Raya Deity. Do not merge them.

O. QUALIFY "DO NOT BUILD A CROWD." Quiet, yes — but it is a staffed temple with a mahant, loudspeaker horns on a post by the toran, daily kheer prasad, and a Gopashtami samaj when boys dressed as Krishna and Balarama are brought from Nandgaon and live cows are worshipped and fed grass and jaggery. Not a deserted ruin.

P. DO NOT TREAT THE PAINTED MENAGERIE AS ESTABLISHED. Build it if you accept their testimony — I think it is probably sound — but no independent source mentions the named cows, the elephant, the horses, the monkey arch or the peacock throne, and there is no open photographic corpus for this site at all.

## deities

Krishna and Balarama (Krsna-Balarama), standing Deities — corroborated by the brajrasik.org photo captions ("Darshan of Shri Krishna", "Distant Darshan of Shri Krishna Balram"), by historypandit.com ("कृष्ण और बलराम की मूर्तियाँ" in the bhajan kuti), and by the pastime itself (Krishna and Balarama tending the cows). The new tank is named Krsna-Balarama Kunda for the same reason. The other researcher left the Deities unnamed — name them.

Separately, and importantly: Srila Rupa Goswami is present as a FRAMED PICTURE in a cusped marble niche in the kutir, not as a murti. The brajrasik album caption "Portrait of Shri Roop Goswami" confirms this independently. The same album has "Portrait of Charan of Shri Radharani" — there is apparently also a Radharani charan (footprint) image on site that the other account omits.
