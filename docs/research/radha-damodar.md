# radha-damodar — exterior survey

*Researched 2026-09-28 by one agent and independently checked by a second whose brief was to refute it. Labels are the researcher's own: MEASURED / SOURCED / DERIVED / ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN.*

## What it is

Architecturally this is not a "temple" at all in the shikhara sense — it is a walled Braj haveli-compound roughly 1,493 m² and 65 m long, entered through a carved buff-sandstone portal set flush into the wall of a narrow lane, opening onto a two-storey arcaded courtyard of cusped arches and jali-balustraded galleries, with a walled samadhi garden in the northern third holding free-standing Bengali chala-roofed tomb shrines; nothing in it rises above the two- and three-storey neighbours, and Growse, who saw it in the 1870s, dismissed its fabric outright.

## The silhouette

**What crowns it: nothing.** There is no shikhara, no dome, no gold finial, no tower of any kind, and no evidence one ever existed. Every photograph I could measure — courtyard views c.2000, c.2005 and 2019, and the 1970s Prabhupada photographs — shows flat roofs with plain horizontal parapets and a thin coping band. The one thing that rises is a samadhi roof at ~5.5 m, and the neighbours overtop it. [MEASURED from photographs / SOURCED for the absence]

**This is a Govind Dev trap in reverse.** Govind Dev's dome survives but is invisible from outside. Here there is nothing to be invisible. Growse's own words (3rd ed., p. 257, of the smaller Brindaban temples including this one): *"Though of ancient date, they have been often renewed and possess no special architectural merit."* He devotes his Radha Damodar paragraph entirely to the tombs and to a priestly quarrel of 1875 — not one word about the building. That silence, from the man who catalogued every cornice at Govind Dev, is evidence. [SOURCED]

**What does break the roofline — the one real silhouette event.** Rupa Goswami's samadhi mandir is a free-standing square cell carrying a **Bengali chau-chala/bangla roof**: a continuous doubly-curved plastered masonry shell whose eave **sags in a deep concave curve at the middle of each side and sweeps up into a sharp, knife-edged point at each corner**, projecting roughly 1.2–1.5 m clear of the wall below. The crest is not a dome — it is a short rounded ridge carrying a **fluted/gadrooned collar with radiating rib-lobes**, and **three squat turned finials in a row** along it, not one. Overall ~5.0–6.0 m to the finial tips, over a cell of ~4.0–4.5 m. [MEASURED from photographs, ±20%]

**Second silhouette element.** At least one more shrine of the same chala-roofed type stands in the yard (visible beside Rupa's in a 2019 photograph), and a tall blank unplastered lakhori-brick mass of three-or-more storeys rises immediately behind the samadhi in a c.2000 photograph — with small square window holes and no render at all. I could not establish whether that brick mass belongs to the temple or to the neighbour.

**What is lost.** Nothing tall. Do not invent a destroyed tower. What has genuinely gone: the eroded exposed lakhori-brick courtyard walls visible in the 1970s photographs have been re-rendered; the Rupa samadhi was stripped back to bare render and re-plastered around 2019; and the window in Prabhupada's kitchen that once looked onto Rupa Goswami's samadhi has been partitioned off. [SOURCED — vrajvrindavan.com]

**What survives.** The site and the institution are continuous since VS 1599 / 1542–43 CE, but the *fabric* is not. The deities were removed to Jaipur c.1670 and returned 1739; the visible building is 18th–19th century and later, repeatedly renewed. [SOURCED]

**From any distance: invisible.** Surrounding blocks of two and three storeys (~7–10 m) press on the compound on all sides in every photograph. This building contributes nothing to the Vrindavan skyline. You cannot see it until you are in the lane, facing the gate.

## The elevation

Four distinct elevations matter, and they use **two different arch grammars** — this is the single most useful thing in this section.

---
**1. THE STREET PORTAL (the only public face)** [MEASURED from the Mathura district administration's own photograph]

A carved pale buff-sandstone frontispiece, flat against the lane, ~5.8 m wide × ~5.0 m to the top of its chhajja. It is *applied to a plain plastered building* — above the chhajja the cream render simply carries on. Composition, bottom up:

- Plain stepped sandstone plinth with low masonry **benches flanking the opening** (people sit on them), and a **relief panel of a cow** on the right-hand plinth block.
- **Two flanking niches**, ~0.55 m wide × 1.3 m tall, each with a **9-foil cusped arch** head and a plain sunk back panel.
- **Central arch**, span ~2.0–2.2 m, rise ~0.9 m. Its archivolt is a **scalloped/serrated band of ~21–25 small semicircular cusps** (counted ~23), set over a broad **~5-foil inner arch**. This is NOT the 9-foil cusped arch used everywhere else here.
- Inside the arch head, above the door, a **small square niche holding a deity image with orange cloth**.
- The actual door opening is **rectangular**, ~1.6–1.8 m × 2.0–2.2 m, and is kept open — you look straight through into a sunlit courtyard of white columns.
- **Foliate arabesque frieze** across the full width above the arch; plain oval medallions in sunk panels left and right.
- Jambs carried in **three narrow vertical ornament bands** — beaded, chain-of-rosettes, and fine dentil — plus a lozenge-relief panel on each pilaster face.
- Crowned by a **chhajja on ~10 heavy double-volute (S-scroll) brackets** in a visibly **redder stone** than the buff portal below, then a plain parapet band of small square panels.

**Opening : solid ≈ 3.6 m² void in ~29 m² of face — about 12% void (17% if the niches count as half-void).** [DERIVED from photo proportions]

---
**2. THE MAIN COURTYARD FACADE — two storeys**

*Ground floor:* a **three-bay arcade of ogee-headed cusped arches of ~9–11 foils**, on slender round columns with ring mouldings and bracket capitals, raised on 5–6 stone steps with a turned-baluster railing across the centre bay. Bay clear width ~2.6 m, height ~3.2 m. Arch soffits picked out in a contrasting colour. **Opening : solid ≈ 53% void** — a genuinely open arcade, the most transparent elevation in the compound. [DERIVED]

*Frieze:* painted rosette medallions and green foliate scroll with large red flowers in the spandrels (c.2000 scheme), then a **deep chhajja on a continuous run of 20+ closely spaced small brackets**.

*First floor:* mostly solid — a plain plastered wall with a green metal grille window and a **gallery with a turned-baluster railing**. ~15–20% void.

*The carved variant of this same facade* (Holi photographs) shows the richer version: a **projecting jharokha gallery on ~8 massive carved S-scroll brackets**, above which runs a **balustrade of 7–8 pierced jali panels each with a DIFFERENT lattice pattern** (interlaced circles, diagonal grids, star nets), separated by short turned colonnettes. Ground floor below: tall flat pilasters with sunk relief panels, large cusped blind-arch recesses, and horizontal bands of scrolling foliate relief.

---
**3. THE DARSHAN HALL INTERIOR ELEVATION — two storeys inside**

A long wall of **deeply sunk rectangular panels, each with a raised relief composition under a cusped-arch canopy**, ~7–8 across, separated by flat pilasters. A moulded string course and projecting ledge form a first-floor **gallery**, above which runs a row of **7–9 cusped blind niches in relief**. The sanctum is at one end: a deep arched recess framed by carved cream stone with clustered colonnettes and a cusped arch, over a stepped white marble altar. **Almost entirely blind — under 10% void.**

---
**4. THE SAMADHI-YARD WALLS AND SHRINE FRONTS**

*Rupa Goswami's samadhi, front face* (~4.3 m wide × ~2.6 m to the eave springing):
- **Three blind ogee-cusped arches of ~7 foils**, drawn in raised relief and outlined in a contrasting colour, springing from short pilaster stubs.
- **Disc roundels** in the spandrels, small star/rosette motifs at the apexes.
- A **small square jali ventilator (~0.35 m)** in each arch tympanum.
- Below: **two large jali screens, ~0.85 × 1.15 m, of flowing floral-arabesque openwork** (not geometric), in plain sunk panels, flanking a central door ~1.0 × 2.0 m.
- Flanking piers carry **sunk panels with raised diamond/lozenge motifs and stepped corners**.
- A **twisted-rope torus moulding** runs as a plinth band at ~0.35 m.
- A large carved **rosette medallion ~0.35–0.45 m diameter** sits above each arch apex.
- The side faces are treated identically — it is free-standing and finished on all faces.
- **Opening : solid ≈ 28–36% void.** [DERIVED]

*Krishnadas Kaviraja Goswami's samadhi — a DIFFERENT grammar.* This one is **a doorway in a courtyard wall, not a free-standing shrine**. Round/segmental arched head with a broad moulded archivolt, **two large gilded scrolled consoles with bulbous drop pendants** flanking the springing, a white marble bilingual plaque in the tympanum, and below it a rectangular opening in a plain moulded architrave with **turquoise-painted diamond-mesh steel double doors**. No cusps at all. This reads 19th-century and colonial-inflected — a later layer sitting alongside the Mughal-Rajput vocabulary.

*Plain courtyard walls:* cream limewash, flat top with a thin projecting coping, rectangular openings, a modern louvred window, a glazed tile dado, and large painted Hindi notice-boards straight onto the render. **~14% void.**

---
**THE DETAIL THAT WILL SAVE YOU MOST TIME:** in the 1970s photographs of the courtyard wall beside Rupa's samadhi, the **cusped arches are moulded in raised plaster on a FLAT wall, over openings that are RECTANGULAR** — a tall dark timber door and a rectangular star-lattice jali, each with a blind ~9–11-foil cusped arch floating above it and flat plaster pilaster strips with three-band capitals beside it. Extrude those outlines into real arches and the whole building turns into a film set.

## Material and colour

**MATERIALS** [MEASURED where stated]

- **Street portal:** dressed, finely carved **buff/pale-ochre sandstone**, weathered and grimy at the base. Its chhajja and bracket row read as a **redder stone** (or are so soot-darkened they read that way — I cannot separate the two).
- **Everything else structural:** **thin lakhori brick in thick lime mortar, plastered and limewashed.** MEASURED from the 1970s Prabhupada photographs where the brick is exposed: **course pitch ~5–6 cm** (brick ~3.5–4.5 cm thick plus a ~1.5 cm lime joint), brick length ~3.5–4× its height, i.e. ~17–20 cm. Faces spalled, rounded and badly eroded; courses visibly irregular. This is genuinely old fabric.
- **Ornament:** cusped arches, rosettes, lozenge panels and rope mouldings are **modelled in lime plaster on flat walls**, not carved stone — except on the main hall facade and the gate, which are carved.
- **Jalis:** two distinct types. **Geometric star/triangle lattice** in plaster or soft stone on the courtyard walls; **flowing floral-arabesque openwork** on Rupa's samadhi.
- **Roofs:** flat RCC/brick terraces with plain parapets; the samadhi shrines are **plastered masonry shells**.
- **Samadhi yard:** **white/cream marble** aedicules and chest-cenotaphs, plus at least one **red sandstone** aedicule; **black wrought-iron grille gates** with diamond patterns, brass bosses and padlocks.
- **Floors — three distinct systems, do not use one:** diagonal **black-and-white chequerboard marble** in the darshan hall; **grey-white stone slabs with a patterned mosaic border strip** in the main courtyard; **terracotta-red stone flags banded with white marble** in the samadhi yard. Prabhupada's room: **rough-dressed stone flags**, large and irregular.
- **Dado:** glazed **turquoise-and-white patterned tile**, ~1.0–1.2 m, along the courtyard wall bases.
- **Modern accretions in the fabric, not optional:** green steel monkey mesh on angle-iron over the approach passage and yard edges, tin/asbestos sheeting, surface-run electrical conduit and distribution boxes, festoon lights, ceiling fans, a wall air-conditioner, brass stanchions with red rope.

**THE COLOUR FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST: there is no canonical colour scheme.**

Rupa Goswami's samadhi is photographed in **three completely different liveries inside twenty years** — bone-cream with **dark red** arch outlines and roundels (c.2000); the whole roof shell repainted **terracotta-red/salmon** with the trim switched to **turquoise and lilac** with ochre roundels (c.2005); and stripped to bare render and re-plastered **fresh white** (c.2019). The main hall likewise changes from ochre-with-olive-green (c.2000) to dusty terracotta-pink (2019). **Pick a date and commit to it. Do not average them, and do not treat any one as "the" colour.**

The stable reading across all dates: **warm cream and pale ochre limewash as the ground, white picking out arch frames, columns, brackets and balusters, with one saturated accent colour for arch outlines, roundels and reveals that is repainted every repaint cycle.** Plus dark unpainted timber doors, weathered buff sandstone at the gate, and white marble in the samadhi yard.

## The setting

**Location.** OSM way 334674939, `amenity=place_of_worship`, `name="Sri Radha Damodara Mandir"`, `religion=hindu`. Centroid **27.583733 N, 77.695654 E**. Wikipedia's 27.5836 / 77.695743 sits 17 m away — independent agreement. Cross-checked again against the project's own Radha Gokulananda survey, which put Radha Damodar 410 m at bearing 229° from that compound; back-projecting gives 27.5838 / 77.6956. Three independent routes to the same spot. [MEASURED]

**The town comes right up to the wall, on every side.** Every photograph shows two- and three-storey flat-roofed blocks in cream and pink render pressing directly against the compound, with water tanks, parapets, green-shuttered windows, cylindrical wire mesh cages on the roofs, and stretches of bare unrendered lakhori brick. There is no forecourt, no plaza, no setback, no garden edge, no view corridor. The compound is a hole cut into a solid urban block.

**Visible from any distance: no.** Nothing in the compound exceeds ~5.5–6 m; the neighbours run 7–10 m. It is comprehensively overtopped and contributes nothing to the skyline. This is a building you discover by turning a corner.

**Named streets are surprisingly far off** [MEASURED, Overpass]:
- **Loi Bazar — 201 m, bearing 153° (SSE).** Every travel source says "at Loi Bazar". It is not; it is 200 m into the lanes off it.
- **Parikram Marg — 399 m, bearing 53° (NE).** The parikrama route does **not** pass this door, unlike Radha Gokulananda.
- Bankhandi Mahadev Bazar — 407 m, 212°.
- Radha Gokulananda Mandir — 410 m, 49° (NE).

Sources also place it "at Nikunj Lane, very near Sewa Kunj" and next to Radha Shyamsundar Mandir. [SOURCED]

**The approach — the surveyed entrance is on the EAST.** OSM node **3417359583 is shared** between the compound polygon and residential lane way **334674940** — a deliberately mapped gate, at local-frame **E 38.0 m, N 29.3 m** (about 29 m north of the compound's south tip, on the east boundary). That lane runs ENE then E for **26.1 m** to a junction. A second lane (way 973380955, 57 m) runs north–south about **12 m west** of the compound. [MEASURED]

**What the approach actually feels like** [MEASURED from photographs]: a narrow passage roughly **2.0–2.5 m wide**, hemmed by a rendered wall with a low white parapet and pipe railing on one side and a **full-height green-painted steel monkey-mesh cage (~3 m)** on the other, **partly built over** so that you pass under a concrete slab and into shadow, with tin sheeting and overhead wire mesh above. Behind the railing, small white samadhi markers with ribbed conical caps stand on a raised terrace. The lane at the gate is overhung by a dark timber beam and the upper storeys of the flanking buildings; motorcycles are parked against the portal; the flanking plaster is plastered over with painted notice boards.

**Then the gate.** You arrive frontally on the carved sandstone portal and look **straight through the open doorway into a sunlit courtyard of pale columns**. That contrast — dark, narrow, caged lane to bright open courtyard — is the whole architectural experience of arriving here, and it is the thing to get right.

**Inside, the compound is a long north–south strip.** The OSM polygon (below) breaks naturally into a broad southern block, N 0–37 m, 21–39 m wide, and a narrower northern arm, N 36–65 m, ~20 m wide. Bharatdiscovery independently states that the samadhis of Jiva and Krishnadas are **behind the main temple** and that **Rupa Goswami's bhajan kutir and samadhi mandir are in the northern section**, with Bhugarbha's nearby — which matches the polygon's shape exactly. **Main temple and courtyard south; samadhi garden north.** [SOURCED + DERIVED]

**The courtyards have trees in them** — including a large gnarled old tree standing in a square raised stone kerb with a white marble plaque, its trunk wrapped in red thread, cloth strips and garlands, and saplings staked to bamboo. Not an ornamental planting; a venerated one. There is also a hall of cows (a pink-pillared gaushala) within or immediately adjoining the compound.

## What NOT to build

- **Do not build a shikhara, a dome or a finial on the main temple.** There is no tower here and no evidence one ever existed. Growse walked past it in the 1870s and wrote that the smaller Brindaban temples "possess no special architectural merit" (3rd ed., p. 257) — and then spent his whole Radha Damodar paragraph on the tombs and a priests' quarrel. The tallest thing in the compound is a ~5.5 m samadhi roof. Flat roofs and plain horizontal parapets throughout. This is the opposite of the Govind Dev lesson: there is no hidden dome, there is simply nothing.
- **Do not give Rupa Goswami's samadhi a hemispherical dome or a North Indian chhatri.** It is a Bengali chau-chala/bangla roof: a continuous doubly-curved plastered shell whose eaves SAG at the middle of each side and sweep UP into knife-edge points at the corners, overhanging 1.2–1.5 m clear of a 4.3 m cell. The crest is a short rounded ridge with a fluted, fan-ribbed collar and THREE squat finials in a row — not one finial, not a kalasha on a dome. Get this wrong and you have lost the only distinctive silhouette in the compound.
- **Do not build the Bengali namesakes — this is the single biggest image-search trap on this subject.** Wikimedia Commons holds roughly FORTY photographs of "Radha Damodar temple at Ghutgoria/Ghutgeria, Bankura district" and "Radha Damodar Temple at Dayalbazar, Kshirpai, Paschim Medinipur" — West Bengal brick temples with ratna pinnacles and terracotta panels — against TWO of the Vrindavan building, both of which are interiors. Worse, the ASI's own Centrally Protected Monuments list has a "Radha Damodar Temple" at Birsingha, Bankura, West Bengal, under the Kolkata Circle. There is also Radha Damodar Temple, Junagadh (Gujarat). Any bulk image scrape will build you a Bengali ratna temple.
- **Do not make it ASI-protected or conserved.** I grepped the ASI CPM list (asi.nic.in/pdf/CPM_List.pdf): the Brindaban entries under Agra Circle are Temple of Govind Deo, Temple of Jugal Kishore, Temple of Madan Mohan and Temple of Radha Ballabh. Radha Damodar is NOT among them. It is a living, privately run goswami temple — which is exactly why its fabric is repainted every few years, surface-cabled, signboarded, monkey-meshed, tiled and partly roofed in modern materials. Do not render it as a tidy conserved monument.
- **Do not extrude the cusped arches into real arches.** The commonest detail here is a cusped arch MOULDED IN RAISED PLASTER ON A FLAT WALL, sitting above a RECTANGULAR opening. The 1970s photographs show it unambiguously: a dark timber door and a rectangular star-lattice jali, each with a blind 9–11-foil cusped outline floating above it and flat plaster pilaster strips with three-band capitals beside it. Rupa's samadhi does the same — three blind 7-foil arches in relief over rectangular jalis and a rectangular door. Extrude them all and the building becomes a film set.
- **Do not use one arch grammar everywhere.** The street gate uses a SCALLOPED/SERRATED archivolt of ~21–25 small semicircular cusps over a broad ~5-foil inner arch. The courtyard arcade uses ogee-headed cusped arches of ~9–11 foils. The samadhi uses blind 7-foil arches in relief. And Krishnadas Kaviraja's samadhi doorway uses NO cusps at all — a round/segmental arch with a moulded archivolt and two large gilded scrolled consoles with drop pendants, a 19th-century colonial-inflected form. Four grammars, four dates, one compound.
- **Do not build the street gate as a freestanding gateway you drive or walk under.** It is a flat carved sandstone FRONTISPIECE applied to the face of a plain plastered building, set flush into the lane wall. Above the chhajja the cream plaster simply continues up into an ordinary upper storey. No gopuram, no detached darwaza, no gatehouse.
- **Do not tidy the samadhi yard into three neat shrines.** It is a dense thicket packed shoulder to shoulder: two or more large chala-roofed shrines, white marble aedicules on turned colonnettes with padlocked black iron grille gates, at least one RED SANDSTONE aedicule, low white marble chest-cenotaphs with Devanagari cut into their faces, and small conical-capped markers. They span four centuries — one plaque reads "ESTABLISHED: JULY 21, 2005". Sources describe them arranged in ROWS ("the first one in the second row").
- **Do not give the Govardhan shila a building, a shrine or a rock outcrop.** It is a portable black stone, roughly oval and maybe 0.4 m across, laid flat on a tray on the altar with its markings picked out in yellow paint and garlanded with marigolds. It is an object, not architecture.
- **Do not build Prabhupada's bhajan kutir as a hut.** "Kutir" is misleading in exactly the way शिखर is. It is a plastered ROOM inside the courtyard block: thick masonry walls, cream/buff render over a dado, a deep arched wall niche, a roshandan ventilator grille set high above door height, a six-panel timber double door with iron ring handles and a sliding bolt, and a floor of large irregular rough-dressed stone flags. Today it also holds a wall air-conditioner, a ceiling fan, framed pictures, brass stanchions with red rope, and a seated murti of Prabhupada. A small kitchen adjoins it.
- **Do not model a single Radha-Krishna pair in a small garbha-griha.** The altar is a WIDE, SHALLOW, MULTI-SET altar roughly 4–5 m across carrying six or more deity groups — Radha-Damodar plus Vrindavanchandra, Chhail Chikani, Radha-Vinoda, Radha-Madhava, Gaura-Nitai and Jagannath — under a canopy, flanked by carved pale sandstone columns with fluted shafts and foliate bell capitals.
- **Do not leave out the cages, cables and signboards.** Green steel monkey mesh on rusted angle-iron over the approach passage and courtyard edges; tin and asbestos sheeting; surface-run conduit and distribution boxes; festoon lights; ceiling fans; and LARGE painted Hindi notice boards applied directly to the plaster — including one reading that photography is strictly prohibited. A clean render is a wrong render, and the photography ban is also why exterior evidence is so thin.
- **Do not use one floor material.** Three distinct systems are photographed: DIAGONAL black-and-white chequerboard marble in the darshan hall; grey-white stone slabs with a patterned mosaic border strip in the main courtyard; terracotta-red stone flags banded with white marble in the samadhi yard. Plus a glazed turquoise-and-white tile dado about 1.0–1.2 m up the courtyard walls.
- **Do not make it visible from a distance, and do not give it a forecourt.** Two- and three-storey blocks press directly against the compound on every side and overtop everything in it. There is no plaza, no setback, no view corridor. The approach is a 2.0–2.5 m lane, partly built over, caged in green mesh on one side — dark and enclosed until you arrive frontally on the gate and look straight through into a sunlit courtyard. That dark-to-bright contrast IS the architecture of arriving here.
- **Do not put the entrance on Loi Bazar or on the parikrama route.** Loi Bazar is 201 m away at bearing 153°; Parikram Marg is 399 m at 53°. Every travel site says "at Loi Bazar" and it is 200 m into the lanes off it. The surveyed entrance is on the EAST boundary at local frame E 38.0, N 29.3, reached by a 26 m unnamed residential spur — OSM node 3417359583 is genuinely shared between the compound polygon and that lane.
- **Do not pick a colour scheme and treat it as canonical.** Rupa's samadhi is photographed in three completely different liveries within twenty years: bone-cream with dark-red arch outlines (c.2000), the whole roof repainted terracotta-red with the trim switched to turquoise and lilac (c.2005), and stripped back and re-plastered fresh white (c.2019). The main hall goes from ochre-with-olive-green to dusty terracotta-pink over the same period. Choose a date and commit. Do not average them.
- **Do not build a picturesque ruin without knowing which wall.** There IS exposed, heavily eroded thin lakhori brick here — measured course pitch 5–6 cm, bricks 3.5–4.5 cm thick — in the 1970s courtyard photographs and in a tall blank three-storey-plus brick mass behind Rupa's samadhi c.2000. But most of that has since been re-rendered, and I could not establish whether the tall brick mass belongs to the temple or to its neighbour. Do not scatter romantic ruin across the compound.

## Uncertain — flag, do not assert

- **Is the samadhi yard now under a modern roof?** A 2019-era photograph in the Braj Ras gallery shows TWO chala-roofed samadhi shrines standing beneath a modern RCC slab carried on tall blue-painted square concrete columns, on a bare earth floor under fluorescent tubes. Earlier photographs (c.2000–2005) show the same shrine type under open sky. Either a shed was built over part of the yard, or that photograph is from a different compound in a gallery that is not perfectly curated. This changes a large part of the model and I could not resolve it.
- **The tall bare-brick mass.** A blank, unplastered, three-storey-plus lakhori-brick wall with small square window holes rises immediately behind Rupa Goswami's samadhi in the c.2000 photograph. I cannot tell whether it is part of the temple, a ruined older structure, or a neighbouring building. One travel source says "much of the facade of the archway has crumbled, leaving the bare, original bricks exposed" — but that is an aggregator sentence I could not trace to a primary source, so I have not built on it.
- **Which deity is the original.** Bharatkosh (bharatdiscovery.org) states plainly that the original Radha Damodar resides in JAIPUR and that a प्रतिभू विग्रह (pratibhu vigraha) is worshipped in Vrindavan. Most English sources say the reverse — the originals returned in 1739 and the pratibhu stayed in Jaipur. Direct contradiction. Not geometry-relevant, but flagged so nobody writes it into a label.
- **Sanatana Goswami's samadhi.** Growse (3rd ed., p. 257) says the temple contains the ashes of Jiva "as also of his two uncles, the Gosains Rupa and Sanatan" and refers to "the three shrines". Modern Gaudiya accounts place Sanatana's samadhi at Madan Mohan, not here. Growse also attributes the founding of Govind Dev to Rupa AND Sanatana jointly, which conflicts with the standard attribution (Rupa→Govind Dev, Sanatana→Madan Mohan) — so his detail here may be second-hand. Do not silently resolve this; note it in the model's data.
- **Every height in this brief is photogrammetric, none are surveyed.** I found no plan, no section, no measured drawing and no INTACH or conservation report for this building. Heights come from human figures and assumed door widths in small JPEGs at oblique angles, and my two independent estimates of the samadhi's overall height (5.9 m from one photo, 5.75 m from another with a different scale reference) agreed only because I corrected for camera distance by eye. Treat everything as ±15–20%.
- **Colour confidence is low except for one photograph.** Only the 2019 restoration shot has large, flat, evenly lit areas; the rest are small, old and colour-cast. The Mathura district portal's gate photograph runs visibly warm (saturations of 48–77% on stone). And my visual reading of the Krishnadas Kaviraja doorway wall as "dusty rose" measured out as a near-neutral grey-lilac at #B4B3BA — a warning that eyeballing colour from these sources is unreliable.
- **Internal zoning is inferred, not surveyed.** I split the compound into "main temple and courtyard south / samadhi garden north" by matching the OSM polygon's shape (broad block N 0–37, narrow arm N 36–65) against Bharatkosh's statement that the samadhis of Jiva and Krishnadas are behind the main temple and that Rupa's bhajan kutir and samadhi are in the NORTHERN section. The two agree, which is encouraging, but I have no plan confirming where the darshan hall, the gaushala, Prabhupada's rooms or the kitchen sit within the polygon.
- **The OSM polygon itself is traced from Bing/Maxar imagery, not surveyed on the ground.** 1,493 m² is large for a Vrindavan lane temple (Radha Gokulananda, surveyed earlier on this project, is 1,106 m²) and the polygon may include an adjoining plot. The entrance node is genuinely shared between the compound way and the lane way, which I verified against the OSM API, so that at least is deliberate mapping.
- **Rupa's samadhi versus Rupa's bhajan kutir.** Sources say the samadhi is "on the right side of the temple across from his bhajana kutir" — the two stand facing each other. The photograph I have relied on most heavily is filed as "rupa-goswami-samadhi1" and I have taken that at face value; it could conceivably be the kutir. The two may well share the chala-roofed form in any case.
- **Cusp counts are approximate.** The gate arch's ~21–25 small cusps and the flanking niches' ~9 foils were counted from a 400-pixel-wide photograph upscaled. The courtyard arcade's 9–11 foils likewise. Build them to the count you can see clearly at the camera distances your player will actually stand at.
- **No historic photograph.** I found nothing from the 19th century — no British Library, ASI or Bourne image of this building. The earliest usable images are the 1970s Prabhupada photographs, and the earliest exterior of the samadhi is c.2000. Everything before that is text only.
- **Growse's page reference.** I verified p. 257 two ways: the index entry reads "Rádha Damodar, temple of, 257", and the passage is the last paragraph before "Of the modern temples, five claim special notice", which is followed by the page footer 258. The OCR is poor throughout (the text renders the name variously as "Iladlia Diimodar", "Ihidha D&modar" and "R&dha Damodar"), so I have quoted conservatively and normalised spelling.

## Independent check

Confidence: High on the two things that matter most, medium on the rest. HIGH that nothing crowns this building — Esri satellite imagery overlaid on the OSM polygon shows only flat terraces with no tower or tower-shadow anywhere inside the boundary, which is independent of the survey's photographic and argument-from-silence reasoning; the survey is right and for better reasons than it gave. HIGH on the five missed internal OSM polygons, the Parikram Marg and Loi Bazar distance errors, the centroid offset, the ASI double entry and the ~97-file Bengali trap — all re-derived from the raw OSM API and the ASI PDF and reproducible exactly. HIGH that the samadhi is roughly half the stated size: three estimates from two independent methods (OSM footprint 3.3 x 2.7 m; human-figure scaling in two separate photographs giving 3.0-3.7 m span and ~3.5 m height) all converge well outside the survey's own error bars. MEDIUM on the colour corrections — the c.2000 photograph has a severe cyan/green cast that makes any hue call unsafe, which is itself the point, and my 'several shrines, not several repaints' reading is a better fit to the evidence than the survey's but is not proven. MEDIUM-LOW on the portal being oversized; it sits inside the survey's stated tolerance and only matters because it leans the same way as the samadhi error. NOT ESTABLISHED either way: whether the tall bare-brick mass belongs to the temple or a neighbour, and whether the photographed shrine is Rupa's samadhi or his bhajan kutir — the survey was right to leave both open, and I could not close them. Overall the survey is unusually good: its geometry recomputes to 0.1 m, its Growse and ASI citations are exact, its colour sampling reproduces to within a couple of hex points, and its hardest architectural observations (the three finials, the gadrooned collar, the cusped arches moulded flat over rectangular openings) are all verifiable and correct. The errors are concentrated in one place — it under-used OpenStreetMap after mining it well for the outline, and it over-scaled its photogrammetry.

- **The samadhi-yard furniture is confirmed**, including the detail most likely to have been invented: white marble aedicules on turned colonnettes with padlocked black iron diamond-pattern grille gates, low marble chest-cenotaphs with Devanagari cut into their faces, small white conical-capped markers, and the plinth legibly reading 'ESTABLISHED: JULY 21, 2005' in Braj Ras images 11 and 12.

- **The wide multi-set altar is confirmed** (File:Radha Damodar temple in Vrindavan.jpg, 2023): six or more deity groups across one shallow altar, flanked by pale fluted columns with foliate bell capitals, under a canopy. Not a single pair in a small garbha-griha. The founding attribution is also consistent across four independent sources — Wikipedia, mathura.nic.in, bharatdiscovery (Samvat 1599, Magh Shukla Dashami) and the temple's own English plaque photographed in 2026 — all Jiva Goswami, 1542. Bharatdiscovery does say plainly that the original deity is in Jaipur with a pratibhu vigraha in Vrindavan, so the survey's flagged contradiction is real and correctly left unresolved. Cross-check to the project's own radha-gokulananda.md (1,106 m2) also verified.

### Contamination risks

- **The 'official' temple website is now a hijacked domain.** radhadamodarmandir.com — which search results and Wikipedia-adjacent sources still recommend — returns HTTP 301 to psychologische-beratung-acar.de, a German psychological-counselling site. Do not fetch, cite, or let any agent follow it. The survey did not use it, but anything re-running this research will be offered it as the top authority.
- **The most official-looking source in the set contains the single most likely source of a phantom tower.** Image 2023010939-1 on the Mathura district administration page (mathura.nic.in, a Government of Uttar Pradesh site) is a painted decorative backdrop reading 'Sri Sri Radha-Damodar Mandir, Vrindavan India, Since 1542' which depicts a romanticised landscape with TALL TEMPLE SHIKHARAS in the background. It sits on the same page, from the same CDN path, as the one genuine photograph of the street portal. Any scrape of that page will hand a modeller a government-hosted picture of towers labelled with this temple's name. Given that crowning elements have been wrong three times on this project, this specific file should be named in the backlog as a known poison.
- **Wikimedia Commons File:Radha Damodar Temple.JPG is categorised 'Banke Bihari Temple'.** A file carrying the plainest, most correct-looking name for this building is a different Vrindavan temple. The survey did not catch this one. The blandest filename is the most dangerous.
- **The Bengal namesakes are ~97 Commons files across four West Bengal sites and TWO ASI-protected monuments**, not the ~40 files and one ASI entry the survey states: Ghutgoria/Ghutgeria (Bankura, ~46 files, and ASI CPM entry 26 'Temple of Radha Damodar Jew'), Kshirpai Dayalbazar (Paschim Medinipur, ~44), Ajodhya (Bankura, 6), Suri (Birbhum, 1), plus ASI CPM entry 3 at Birsingha (Bankura). These are ratna-pinnacled terracotta brick temples. The ASI list ALSO contains 'Brindaban Chandra Temple, Birsingha, Bankura', so filtering on the word Brindaban does not protect you.
- **Jaipur.** Because the deities genuinely went to Jaipur c.1670, searches for 'Radha Damodar temple Jaipur' return a real, different, currently-functioning temple. This is the same failure mode the project was already warned about with srimandir.com conflating Radha Madan Mohan with the Jaipur Temple. Bharatdiscovery's statement that the ORIGINAL deity is still in Jaipur, against the English sources' claim that the originals returned in 1739, is an unresolved direct contradiction — the survey flagged it correctly and it must not be silently resolved in any label.
- **The survey's 'Rupa Goswami samadhi' is a composite of at least two different shrines.** Its identification rests on one third-party filename (radha.name's 'rupa-goswami-samadhi1'), which it honestly flags. But the front-face description merges details that do not co-occur: the c.2000 photograph shows THREE blind cusped arches with roundels between them, while the 2019 restoration photograph shows ONE cusped arch flanked by two plain lozenge panels. Braj Ras image 03 proves there are at least two more chala shrines of the same type in the yard. So the elaborate front-elevation spec in the brief may describe no single building. This is the same structural error as the crowning-element failures — an imperative doNotBuild built on a single unverified image identification.
- **Legends repeated as history without labels.** Four devotional/traditional claims appear in the sources block and the silhouette section with no flag: 'four parikramas of the temple equal one of Govardhan'; the Govardhan shila bearing Krishna's footprints 'given to Sanatana Goswami by Krishna himself'; the deities 'shifted to Jaipur in 1670 when Aurangzeb invaded' and 'brought back in 1739'; and the survey's own assertion that 'the site and the institution are continuous since VS 1599 / 1542-43 CE', which it marks [SOURCED]. All of these trace to devotional and government-tourism pages repeating tradition. None has documentary or archaeological support. Growse, the only near-contemporary observer the survey cites, dates nothing and says only 'of ancient date'. The 1542 founding is reasonable to carry as tradition, but it should not be written into model data as established fact, and the 1670/1739 chain in particular should be labelled as the temple's own account.
- **A methodological risk worth recording, since it caused four of the errors above:** the survey measured distances to OpenStreetMap way CENTROIDS rather than nearest approach. That inflated Loi Bazar, quadrupled the Parikram Marg figure, and caused it to miss a second Parikram Marg segment 101 m to the NW and the Sewa Kunj way 109 m to the south. Any future survey on this project should measure nearest-point distances, and should always query for ways CONTAINED BY the compound polygon, not only the polygon itself — that second habit would have found all five internal buildings.
