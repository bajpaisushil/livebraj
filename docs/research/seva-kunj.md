# seva-kunj

*Researched and independently verified 2026-09-27. Confidence: partial.*

## oneLine

A walled sacred grove of low sprawling scrub trees in old Vrindavan, just south-south-west of Radha Damodar, which you walk around inside a caged tunnel-like parikrama corridor; the buildings — a marble pavilion, a raised paved terrace and a small cream twin-domed temple called the Rang Mahal — stand together along ONE SIDE of the enclosure, not in the middle, and the Radhavallabhi Goswamis lay a bed there each evening before locking the gates at dusk.

## location

Old Vrindavan, Mathura district, Uttar Pradesh. OpenStreetMap way 99418412. My recomputed centroid is 27.58240 N, 77.69498 E (the other account's 27.58239 / 77.69494 agrees). Polygon extents: latitude 27.58203 to 27.58277, longitude 77.69428 to 77.69589.

Bearings, all recomputed by me from OSM and Wikipedia coordinates:
- Shri Radha Damodar Mandir (OSM way 334674939, 27.58376 / 77.69561): 163 m NNE, bearing 22 deg. Seva Kunj lies SSW of Radha Damodar. Guide sites say variously south-west and south-east; the coordinates say SSW and the other researcher was right to trust them.
- Shri Radha Shyamsundar Mandir (OSM nodes 4648837990 / 4569639794): 97-118 m ENE. This is the NEAREST major temple and the other account omitted it. It is also the most likely candidate for the "white multi-turreted spire of a larger temple" seen over the rooftops — unconfirmed, do not assert it in-game.
- Nidhivan: two defensible figures. OSM way 99418393 centroid is 392 m ENE; Wikipedia's Nidhivan point is 280 m ENE. Edge to edge is well under 200 m. The popular "200 m apart" is NOT as loose as the other account claimed — give a range, 200-400 m depending on what you measure.
- Shri Radha Raman Mandir (OSM way 335527344): 495 m NE. The other account's "about 500 m NE" is right.
- Banke Bihari Mandir — CORRECTED. Direction right, distance understated. OSM node 7172091726 puts it 517 m WSW; Wikipedia's coordinates put it 529 m WSW. Not "roughly 350-450 m". "Banki Bihari Bazar" (OSM way 99418413) is 599 m SW, which the other account also had.
- Shri Radha Vallabh Mandir — MISSING from the other account and worth having, since the Radhavallabhi Goswamis keep Seva Kunj and the postal address cites their road. Wikipedia gives 27.5812 / 77.6920, which is about 320 m WSW.
- Imli Tala (OSM node 7292358445): 148 m NW. The other account's "a short walk north" is roughly right; NW is better.
- Govind Dev Temple (OSM way 99427138): 450 m ESE.
- Loi Bazar (OSM way 99427129, highway=residential) runs off the south-east; the unnamed residential lane OSM way 99427174 wraps the south and west.

Postal address as published by temple-guide sites: Kunj Gali, near Radha Vallabh Temple Road, Gotam Nagar, Vrindavan 281121. Cars do not reach it; the last stretch is on foot or cycle-rickshaw.

HOURS. Two independent sources agree. Public darshan roughly 08:00-11:00 and 17:30-19:30, gates shut after the evening aarti and nobody remains overnight. The aarti schedule (Brajrasik) is summer Mangala 05:30, Shringar 08:30, Rajbhog 12:30, Sandhya 17:30, Shayan 19:15; winter 07:00, 09:00, 12:00, 16:30, 18:30 — Brajrasik also gives the overall span as 05:30-19:15 summer and 07:00-18:30 winter, which is the aarti span, not public darshan. The other account had both right.

THE DATE — the other account's reasoning is WRONG and this matters. It said tourism sites' "revealed by Hit Harivansh in 1590" must be false because his accepted dates are 1502-1552, so he was "dead nearly forty years by 1590". In fact 1590 is a VIKRAM SAMVAT year. Brajrasik's biography prints the traditional Samvat years as bare numbers — born 1559, reached Vrindavan 1590, disappeared 1609 — which convert (subtract 57) to 1502, 1533 and 1552 CE, exactly the accepted CE dates. Wikipedia's Radha Vallabha Sampradaya article independently dates the sampradaya's founding at Vrindavan to 1535 CE, which corroborates 1533. So V.S. 1590 = 1533 CE falls squarely within his life and there is no contradiction. Keep the advice not to put a date in-game, but if you want one, "Vikram Samvat 1590, that is 1533 CE, within the Radhavallabhi tradition" is defensible. Separately, do not merge this with the Radha Vallabh Temple itself, which Wikipedia dates to 1585 CE, built by Sundardas Bhatnagar, a disciple of Vanachandra, Hit Harivansh's son — a different building and a different date. Note also that Wikipedia calls the Radha Sudha Nidhi "traditionally attributed" to Hit Harivansh; the plaques on the corridor wall are signed with his name, so build them as signed, but the attribution is not undisputed scholarship.

## scale

ENCLOSURE — verified independently. I recomputed the shoelace area on OSM way 99418412's own geometry and got 9,250 m2 with extents 158 m east-west by 82 m north-south and a perimeter of 403 m, against the other researcher's 9,313 m2 and the same extents. The 0.7% gap is an earth-radius constant, not an error. An honest working footprint is about 150 x 65 m, roughly 0.9 ha. CAVEAT stands: the way is tagged name:ru "Сева-кунджа и Кишори-кунджа", so it may cover Kishori Kunj as well. The three natural=wood polygons inside it (OSM ways 673975826 / 673975829 / 673975827) recompute to 2,484 + 1,389 + 1,572 = 5,445 m2, leaving 3,806 m2 for buildings, courtyard and paths. The other researcher's 5,480 and 3,800 are right.

PARIKRAMA CORRIDOR. Clear width about 2.4-2.8 m, crown about 3.0-3.2 m, vault springing about 2.0-2.2 m. Scaled off the standing adult and the street dog visible in the brajrasik photographs — I checked the same photographs and agree. The polygon perimeter is 403 m, so if it is a full circuit it is 400-430 m. It demonstrably turns at least one corner. Full circuit unconfirmed.

TEMPLE — revised DOWN. Single storey, parapet at about 5 m, two domes roughly 3-4 m in diameter. Apex with finial: the other account said 10-11 m on the reasoning that the dome group reads as tall as the two-and-a-half-storey shells behind it. In the one exterior view I found, the apexes sit clearly BELOW the roofline of the three-storey blocks behind them, and those blocks are further from the camera and so should read smaller. I put the apex at 7.5-10 m and would treat +/-2.5 m as the honest error bar. Ground footprint not documented; the interior room reads about 4.5-6 m wide by rows of seated people.

RAISED TERRACE. Floor about 2-4 m above grove level — inferred from sightlines over the boundary wall onto town rooftops and down onto an adjoining roof, not measured. Parapet about 0.9-1.0 m.

PAVILION. Columns about 2.6-2.9 m to the capital, flat roof about 3.2-3.5 m plus balustrade. It seats well over a hundred at samaj gayan and Holi, so the covered hall is on the order of 12 x 10 m, at least three bays each way. Inferred from crowd size, not measured.

MARBLE SEAT. About 2.2-2.6 m wide including the armrest blocks, 1.5-1.8 m deep, about 1.7-2.0 m to the gable apex, on a plinth about 0.25 m; three treads about 0.3 m rise each. Agrees with the other account.

CONCRETE PORTAL — proportion corrected. Piers roughly 0.55-0.65 m square; total height about 4.3-4.8 m; lintel soffit about 3.5-3.8 m; cross-beam about 2.7-3.0 m. Clear opening about 1.5-2.2 m, i.e. roughly HALF the soffit height — not the 2.4-2.8 m claimed, which would make it nearly square. The proportion (opening ~= half the soffit height) is the reliable figure; the absolute metres are a photo read.

GOLD CHHATRI — revised UP. About 1.3-1.6 m square in plan, about 2.0-2.3 m to the top of the canopy.

LALITA KUND — revised NARROWER. Sunken slot about 2.0-2.6 m wide between plaster walls about 2 m high; broad red-sandstone treads descending roughly 1.5-1.8 m (four or five visible below the camera; "about seven" in total is plausible but unconfirmed); well mouth about 1.1-1.4 m across; water about 0.3-0.5 m below the platform.

## architecture

VERDICT FIRST: I checked this sceptically and most of it holds up. I found 24 captioned photographs of Seva Kunj on brajrasik.org and three CC-licensed photographs on Wikimedia Commons that the other researcher apparently did not reach, and they independently confirm the caged corridor, the marble pavilion, the gold chhatri, the painted ceiling, the Raas Sthali seat, the concrete portal, the bangaldar shrine, the painted interior, Lalita Kund, the grove and even the specific pink house outside the wall. I also recomputed the OSM geometry from scratch and got the same numbers. Nine things are wrong or misleading and are corrected below. Build from this version.

SITE LAYOUT (one correction). One walled enclosure, entered on foot. Inside: a fenced grove occupying the middle and most of the area, a covered corridor around its edge, and — this is the correction — a single BUILT GROUP along one side comprising the domed temple, the open marble pavilion, a RAISED paved terrace (Raas Sthali) and the sunken Lalita Kund. The other account's headline said the temple sits "at its heart"; its own body text said "at one side", and the photographs say one side. The grove centre has no buildings in it. Visitors never walk on the grove.

THE PARIKRAMA CORRIDOR — confirmed in full, and it is still the thing that defines the place. A caged tunnel, modern, utilitarian, unlovely. Section: a semicircular barrel vault of corrugated translucent fibreglass sheet on curved steel ribs painted dark, roughly 8-12 ribs per visible run. The sheet reads AMBER-GOLD in one run and PALE GREEN in another, in the same album — build both, it is genuine variation in age and light. Grove side: floor-to-roof green PVC-coated chain-link in green-painted steel frames, doubled in places with a finer green mesh, with gate leaves of the same construction hung mid-run with a simple latch. Outer side: a solid wall of large buff-to-grey-pink ashlar blocks with visible bed and vertical joints, and in other stretches the same wall plastered salmon-pink, carrying TWO rows of pale marble plaques inscribed in black Devanagari with verses of Shri Radha Sudha Nidhi. Tube lights hang from the vault and a grey electrical box is fixed to the wall. Floor: plain grey and red-brown stone slabs, worn and patched. From outside the corridor reads as a long green-framed cage with green shade netting draped over it, against a cream boundary wall. It runs long and straight and TURNS CORNERS — a perpendicular run is visible at one corner, which is stronger than "at least one side", though a complete circuit is still unproven.

THE GROVE (corrected: lower than stated). Not forest. Dense, sprawling, remarkably level low scrub. Multi-stemmed woody plants whose thick grey limbs lean and lie prone along the ground like driftwood, with dead gnarled limbs left where they fall. Small oval leaves on very twiggy dark grey-brown branchwork. Bare pale sandy earth beneath. Seen from above against the corridor, the canopy tops sit AT OR BELOW the corridor's springing line — so mostly 1.5-3 m, with occasional individuals to about 4 m, NOT the 2.5-4 m level canopy claimed. Colour is seasonal: dusty grey-green in the dry season, bright yellow-green in April. A large magenta bougainvillea smothers the courtyard fence — this is a courtyard feature, not just an edge detail. Monkeys throughout, including a whole row of them sitting along the top of the corridor roof and on the boundary wall.

THE PAVILION AND COURTYARD — confirmed. An open columned mandap of white-and-cream marble: slender shafts with GOLD-painted rings at base and neck, carved cream bracket capitals, cusped multifoil arches between them, flat roof, white marble balustrade above. Floor is grey-veined white marble. At least three bays; it holds well over a hundred seated people at samaj gayan and Holi. The central bay's ceiling is a shallow painted vault — deep blue ground, gold-framed panels of Radha-Krishna leelas, a green frieze beneath, an electric ceiling fan hung from the centre — confirmed exactly. The pavilion opens directly onto the green chain-link and the grove beyond. Green mats and dhurries on the floor. At Holi the marble is drenched pink with gulal.

THE GOLD CHHATRI — confirmed, and now named. A gold-painted four-post canopy standing on the marble floor over a white marble slab inscribed in red with the name Shri Harivansh, strewn with rose petals. Brajrasik captions it "Seating place of Harivansh Mahaprabhu" — it is his baithak, not an anonymous kiosk. Larger than the other account said: about 1.3-1.6 m square in plan and about 2.0-2.3 m to the top of the canopy.

THE RAAS STHALI TERRACE (corrected: it is RAISED). Pale grey-white marble laid in HERRINGBONE with a circular inlaid rosette at the centre. This is the single most important geometric correction: the terrace is NOT at grove level. From it you look over the boundary wall at town rooftops at eye level, down onto an adjoining cream roof, and at the base of the temple domes BELOW the parapet line. It stands roughly 2-4 m above the grove, over or beside the pavilion, reached by steps. A modeller reading the other account would lay it flat on the ground and the whole set of sightlines would be wrong.

Standing alone on it: a tiered seat of banded "rainbow" marble with strong diagonal pink, orange and grey-green veining — three broad treads rising to a pentagonal gabled backrest, with scrolled armrest blocks of PALER banded stone at each side, on a moulded plinth. Behind and OFFSET TO ONE SIDE of it (not centred behind it) stands the raw grey concrete portal: two square piers, a lintel, one cross-beam lower down, no ornament, weather-stained, genuinely ugly. Corrected proportion: it reads clearly taller than wide, the clear opening roughly HALF the lintel-soffit height — the other account's numbers make it nearly square, which is wrong. The terrace is edged by a cream parapet carrying rusted steel-framed diamond-mesh railing panels, with green chain-link and green shade netting beyond.

THE TEMPLE / RANG MAHAL (corrected: the domes are smoother, fewer and lower). Single storey, plastered masonry, cream to off-white lime-wash, heavily grey rain-streaked, flat roof. On the roof, TWO bulbous plaster domes — one rounder and squatter, one taller with an ogee profile and a pronounced vertical seam. Each springs from a cornice, rises to a short neck and ends in a POINTED PLASTER FINIAL. No metal kalash — confirmed. But they are NOT "deeply gadrooned with about two dozen vertical flutes": in the one exterior view they read as smooth bulbous masses with at most a broad seam or a few wide ribs. Do not cut two dozen flutes. Only two domes are evidenced; drop the possible third.

Against one flank, a small shrine with a bangaldar roof — a deep boat-hull curve in cream plaster with a rolled downturned eave over a bracketed cornice, its arched opening filled with wire mesh, rain-stained brown. Confirmed exactly.

Interior — confirmed exactly. A rectangular room entered through a white marble door-frame with carved colonnettes. Walls painted on plaster with near-life-size standing figures of Radha, Krishna and sakhis among trees, in painted frames. At the far end a gold-framed altar under a cusped gold arch with a crown, flanked by gold pilasters, with a cream pleated CURTAIN drawn across it. A Goswami sits at the altar platform. Devotees sit close-packed on the floor. Large printed Hindi notice boards in red and white are fixed to the walls beside and above the altar — build them, they are what it actually looks like. Room width reads about 4.5-6 m, slightly narrower than the other account's 5-7 m.

LALITA KUND (corrected: narrower). A small sunken chamber, not a tank. Two pink lime-washed plaster walls, heavily weathered with grey salt-leaching at the base and cracks, form a NARROW slot about 2.0-2.6 m wide — not 3 x 3 m. Broad red-sandstone treads descend between them to a square red-sandstone platform carrying four carved lotus-rosette discs at its corners and, in the centre, a cusped-edged round well mouth with a heavy moulded rim, algae-blackened, with dark green water sitting close beneath — about 0.3-0.5 m below the platform. A rusted expanded-metal screen panel stands against the back wall above a stone kerb. At ground level above, a small altar of blue-grey terrazzo pilasters on a buff base holding a gold-framed print of Radha-Krishna under a cream lace cloth, a jasmine garland and marigolds, a steel donation box, and a stained marble plaque with the Hindi notice forbidding descent. The mesh behind this altar is GALVANISED GREY in a white-painted frame, not green — do not paint every fence on the site green.

SURROUNDINGS — confirmed, and strikingly so. The town presses against the wall on every side: three- and four-storey unplastered red-brick RCC frame shells with unfilled openings, grey concrete party walls streaked black, rooftop black plastic water tanks, and — exactly as described — a salmon-pink house with a rounded corner turret of cusped arched openings and a white balustraded balcony. A painted Hindi signboard on one brick flank. Visible from everywhere inside.

WHAT IS STILL THIN. No image or description of the street entrance or gate. No plan and no measured dimension from any source — every metre figure below is a proportion read off a photograph. And no source I reached captions the domed building "Rang Mahal"; that identification still rests on text sources calling the central shrine the Rang Mahal plus interior photographs of that shrine. Keep it flagged.

## distinguishing

1. THE CAGED CORRIDOR. You walk Seva Kunj inside a green wire tunnel under a corrugated translucent barrel roof that is amber in one run and pale green in the next, reading marble verse-plaques on an ashlar wall at your shoulder while the grove stays on the other side of the mesh, untouchable, with monkeys sitting on the roof over your head. Get this right and the place is recognisable; miss it and you have built a generic garden.

2. THE RAISED HERRINGBONE TERRACE WITH THE RAINBOW-MARBLE SEAT AND THE CONCRETE GOALPOST. A pale marble terrace laid in herringbone, lifted a storey above the grove so that you look over the boundary at brick rooftops; alone on it a tiered banded-marble throne with a gabled back and paler scrolled armrests; behind and to one side a bare grey concrete portal. Nothing else in Vrindavan looks like this, and the ugliness of the portal is part of the identity.

3. TWO CREAM PLASTER DOMES WITH POINTED PLASTER FINIALS seen over a low sprawling grey-green canopy with bare brick house-backs and a salmon-pink turreted house behind them. That silhouette — dome, scrub, brick — is the Seva Kunj view.

4. THE GOLD CHHATRI over a marble slab inscribed only "Shri Harivansh", strewn with rose petals, under a deep-blue painted vault with gold leela panels and an electric fan. A name, not an image, worshipped — and it is Hit Harivansh Mahaprabhu's baithak.

5. THE CURTAIN. At the shayan aarti the gold altar is curtained, the gates are locked, and no one stays. The bed is laid behind that curtain — you never see it.

## doNotBuild

- NOT a shikhara temple. No spire, no tower, no urushringas. The tallest thing on site is a dome finial at roughly 8-10 m.
- NOT red sandstone Mughal-revival and NOT a white marble temple. Plastered brick, cream lime-wash, grey rain-streaked. Marble only for floors, pavilion columns, verse plaques, the door-frame and the seat.
- DO NOT deeply gadroon the domes. This is the other account's clearest error: it specifies "about two dozen vertical flutes". The photographs show smooth bulbous plaster masses with at most a broad vertical seam. Plaster point, no brass kalash.
- DO NOT build three domes. Two are evidenced.
- DO NOT put the temple in the middle of the grove. Temple, pavilion, terrace and Lalita Kund form one built group along ONE SIDE; the grove holds the middle and has no buildings in it.
- DO NOT lay the Raas Sthali terrace at ground level. It is raised roughly 2-4 m; from it you look down on the grove and over the boundary wall at town rooftops. Building it flat destroys every sightline on the site.
- DO NOT make the concrete portal nearly square. Clear opening is roughly half the lintel-soffit height — a tall narrow goalpost. And do not centre it behind the seat; it stands offset to one side and set back.
- DO NOT beautify that portal. Bare grey concrete, unfinished-looking, weather-stained — recognisable precisely because it is ugly.
- DO NOT build an open landscaped garden with a gravel path. The parikrama is a fenced tunnel; the grove is sealed behind floor-to-roof chain-link and the player cannot enter it.
- DO NOT build tall forest trees or a tulsi-pot garden. Sources call the trees tulsi; the photographs show low multi-stemmed woody scrub, mostly 1.5-3 m with a few to 4 m, thick grey limbs lying on bare sandy earth. Do not build them uniformly 4 m.
- DO NOT paint every fence green. Corridor and grove boundary are green PVC chain-link; the mesh at Lalita Kund is galvanised grey in a white-painted frame; the terrace parapet carries rusted steel-framed diamond mesh.
- DO NOT build Lalita Kund as a stepped bathing tank with ghats and pilgrims in the water. A narrow pink-walled sunken slot about 2.0-2.6 m wide with a round well mouth, water close beneath, and a notice expressly forbidding descent.
- DO NOT carve the interior. The leelas are PAINTED on plaster, and large printed Hindi notice boards hang on the same walls.
- DO NOT confuse this with Nidhivan, 200-400 m north-east, of the Haridasi tradition, with its own Rang Mahal. Almost every image search for "Rang Mahal Vrindavan" — including the only Wikimedia Commons photo with that title — returns NIDHIVAN'S. I looked at it: lavish embossed SILVER repoussé arches, mirrored panels, an exposed bed. None of that belongs here. Seva Kunj's altar is GOLD-framed and CURTAINED, in a room with painted plaster walls.
- DO NOT put an exposed sandalwood bed on open display. That is the Nidhivan picture.
- DO NOT place the grove in open countryside or behind a green buffer. Unplastered brick and RCC shells, blackened grey party walls and rooftop water tanks stand directly against the boundary on all sides, with a salmon-pink turreted house among them.
- DO NOT state a founding date in-game — and do NOT repeat the other account's reasoning for that. It claimed "1590" is impossible because Hit Harivansh died in 1552. Wrong: 1590 is a Vikram Samvat year = 1533 CE, within his life.
- DO NOT trust the OSM footprint blindly. The polygon is named "Seva Kunj and Kishori Kunj" in Russian and may enclose an adjoining kunj.

## deities

Radha and Krishna, as Radhavallabhi seva — the altar in the Rang Mahal is kept curtained rather than presenting a permanently visible pair, and the object of worship in the pavilion is a marble slab bearing only the name Shri Harivansh. Brajrasik also captions a darshan image "Shri Krishn serving Shri Radha at Seva Kunj", which is the theological point of the place: Krishna serves Radha here.
