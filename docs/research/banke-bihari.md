# Shri Banke Bihari Mandir, Vrindavan — exterior

*Researched 2026-09-27. Growse's eyewitness text, photogrammetry off Wikimedia
Commons photographs, ESRI satellite at z19 (0.265 m/px), OSM/Overpass, and a
parallel news/court/ASI search in English and Hindi.*

Confidence is labelled throughout: **[MEASURED]** off a photograph or tile,
**[SOURCED]** a document says it, **[DERIVED]** arithmetic from those,
**[ESTIMATE]** defensible but not established, **[UNKNOWN]** no source —
do not invent.

## Why ours read as a barracks

Six faults, in order of cost:

1. **The openings are not rectangular — they are multifoil (cusped) arches
   throughout**, and the top storey is an open arcade that is **81% void**,
   not a wall with holes in it. [MEASURED]
2. **Two colossal stacked chhajjas**, each on a *continuous* band of bulbous
   turned pendants plus a row of big carved corbels. Growse's single
   adjectival phrase for this building in 1883 is **"the extremely bold
   projection of its eaves."** It is the signature, and we had none of it.
3. **The roofline is not flat.** A jali parapet the full width, broken dead
   centre by one **three-bay kiosk with a curved bangaldar roof** and a
   flagstaff. That silhouette alone makes it recognisable.
4. **It is not cream. It is red sandstone.**
5. **It does not stand in a field.** 100% embedded in a continuous carpet of
   flat roofs, no open ground within 100 m. You cannot see it from any
   distance at all.
6. **The courtyard is roofed**, on iron girders, with ceiling fans and
   floodlights hanging from it.

## What "Rajasthani" concretely means here

Not Jaisalmer sculpture. Growse (1883, p.155) on the living Mathura/Vrindavan
street style this building belongs to: carved stone front, grand central
archway, "storey upon storey above are projecting balconies supported on
quaint corbels, the arches being filled in with the most minute reticulated
tracery… protected from the weather by broad eaves, the under-surface of which
is brightly painted." **Arcuated + bracketed + screened + deeply eaved**, in
flat pierced stone, not deep sculpture. [SOURCED]

## There are TWO elevations, not one

- **(A) The Gate No. 1 block** — the tall three-storey arcaded facade with the
  rooftop kiosk. What every famous photograph shows. It faces *into* the
  courtyard.
- **(B) The sanctum screen** — one very tall storey of large cusped arches
  across the far end of the covered hall, the deity behind it.

They look nothing alike. One generic facade is wrong twice.

### Elevation (A), bottom to top

**Storey 1 (ground).** Symmetrical and five-part: jharokha group | jharokha
group | **central arch** | jharokha group | jharokha group, reading 3+1+3.
- Centre: one tall multifoil arched recess. [MEASURED] span 816 px, rise
  576 px → **rise/span 0.71**, a tall arch. Automated silhouette extraction
  counted **8–9 lobes**, visually 9–11 — **use 9, treat as ±2**. The apex is a
  small ogee point, not a round crown.
- The arch sits in a large rectangular frame filled edge to edge with dense
  low-relief scrolling arabesque. **Ground-storey carving density is ~3x
  anything above it.**
- Flanking: paired/tripled cusped openings on slender colonnettes with foliate
  capitals over **jali balustrades** — these flanking bays *are* the jharokhas.
  Growse's Braj term is **gokh**, not jharokha.

**Chhajja 1** — [carved fascia band] + [dense continuous bulbous turned
pendants, ~0.25 m pitch] + [row of large tapering corbels]. The jharokha slabs
below have a **sawtooth/serrated drip edge**.

**Storey 2 (first floor).** A **low** band, largely in shadow: short square
piers with **rectangular, not arched** openings — a trabeated gallery, the one
place the arcuated language stops. Proportionally the shortest storey.
(~70% confidence it is a real gallery and not a deep bracketed soffit read at
a steep angle.)

**Chhajja 2** — the biggest overhang in the building, same three-part
construction, projecting further than Chhajja 1.

**Storey 3 (top).**
- **Jali parapet** at the terrace edge: pierced geometric screens between short
  square posts with small turned finials. **The jali patterns vary panel to
  panel** — at least three distinct lattices counted (fine diagonal diaper,
  star/rosette, coarse hexagon). Do not repeat one texture.
- Above it an **open arcade of exactly 9 bays** [MEASURED, automated void
  detection]: arranged **3 | 3 | 3** with two heavier square piers between
  groups; within-group pitch 239 px, between-group 304 px → the pier gap is
  **1.27x** a normal bay; opening width 193 px mean → **void:pitch = 0.81**.
  **The top storey is four-fifths air.**
- Arches spring from **slender round colonnettes** with square moulded
  capitals and bases. Profile: a broad round-to-slightly-pointed head trimmed
  with a dense band of **~20+ tiny scallops** around the intrados — a
  *micro-cusped* arch. **The two storeys use different arch grammars**: nine
  big lobes below, twenty tiny scallops above.

**Rooftop kiosk.** [MEASURED] A **three-bay** arcaded pavilion with its own
jali balustrade, centred at x=2050 px against an arcade centre of x=2048 px —
**it sits exactly over the middle group of three arches.** Use that as a hard
compositional rule. Roofed with a **bangaldar curved vault** (segmental barrel
with rolled, downturned ends and deep curved overhangs sweeping out each
side), its face panelled in a rectangular coffer grid. A flagstaff with a small
saffron/yellow flag, plus a thin lightning rod.

**Opening:solid by storey** — top arcade ~80% open, ground ~35–40%, middle
~50% but dark.

### Elevation (B), the sanctum screen

One very tall storey. **Five deeply cusped multifoil arches** on carved piers,
the central one widest, the deity behind it. Above: a rectangular carved panel
field, a strongly projecting bracketed cornice, then the hall ceiling. Pale
grey-white worn stone, **markedly paler than elevation (A)**. Fitted with
**white-painted steel lattice grilles**. (5 arches, possibly 7 — partials at
both edges.)

## Colour — and there are two valid states

**Material: red sandstone.** Growse 1883: "a large square **red sandstone**
block of plain, but exceedingly substantial, character, with a very effective
**central gateway of white stone**." Amar Ujala 2026: "मंदिर का निर्माण लाल
बलुई पत्थर से किया गया है।" Original plaster was lime-and-moorum, now
spalling. [SOURCED]

**State 1 — as photographed 2010–2012**, under a polymer coating yellowed by
UV. Measured sRGB:

| surface | hex | H / S / V |
|---|---|---|
| rooftop kiosk, sunlit | `#f3ded9` | 12 / 11 / 95 |
| kiosk pier | `#b9a09b` | 10 / 16 / 73 |
| upper arcade pier face | `#977a76` | 7 / 22 / 59 |
| arcade spandrel | `#b7937b` | 24 / 33 / 72 |
| jali parapet post | `#99796c` | 17 / 29 / 60 |
| cornice band | `#bda291` | 23 / 23 / 74 |
| ground carved field, sunlit | `#eadacb` | 29 / 13 / 92 |
| ground carved field, shade | `#b79c89` | 25 / 25 / 72 |
| chhajja fascia | `#c7ab86` | 34 / 33 / 78 |
| **chhajja soffit, deep shade** | `#1e1f23` | — / 14 / 14 |

It has been called cream *and* ochre *and* pink-red because **all three are
readings of the same wall**: hue drifts H 7–39 with light angle and saturation
stays low, 11–33%. A **dusty warm pink-buff**, never a saturated red in these
photographs. Soot crusts blacken every rain-sheltered surface.

**State 2 — today, post-June 2026: stripped, cleaned, polished natural red
sandstone.** [SOURCED] ASI Science Branch, Agra Circle, ten specialists over
three months removed the polymer coating, treated the stone with sodium
pentachlorophenate and polished it; a reversible stone-compatible coating was
applied. Ex-ASI engineer M.C. Sharma: the polymer had trapped moisture,
hollowed the stone and gone yellow-brown under UV, ruining the natural colour;
walls had "gone black through lack of maintenance."

Decoration that IS there: a **maroon/red painted signboard with white and red
Hindi lettering** under the central arch; a small white/blue enamel gate-number
plate; the sanctum screen's white-painted steel grilles. **No tilework, no
mirrorwork, no painted figural decoration outside.** And per Growse's regional
rule, the chhajja soffits are *brightly painted* — a different, brighter
colour than the wall, not a darker tint of it.

No source says the building has ever been gilded or repainted a chosen colour.
The only gold story is the toshakhana opened Oct 2025 — contents, not fabric.

## The setting — the worst of our errors

ESRI tiles at z19, gridded at 10 m and 5 m:

- **No open ground of any kind within 100 m.** A continuous unbroken carpet of
  flat roofs; the lanes appear only as thin dark cracks, many invisible because
  awnings and upper floors bridge them. Roof plots typically **6–12 m across**.
  [MEASURED]
- The Week (Aug 2022): "Temple is on narrow lanes (**6 feet**) with small shops
  on either side" — **1.8 m**, the only measured approach width in any source.
  Photographed lanes nearby run 3.5–4.5 m. Use 1.8–4.5 m, narrowing on
  approach. [SOURCED]
- **You cannot see the temple from any distance. Zero.** The only wayfinding is
  a painted "प्रवेश द्वार →" signboard bolted to an ordinary lane house. From
  the lane you see peeling pink-plaster 3-storey houses, not a temple.
- Distances [MEASURED, OSM]: nearest mapped lane 3.6 m; Banki Bihari Bazar
  88 m; Parikrama Marg 179 m; Jugal Ghat 182 m; Ashta Sakhi 165 m; Madan Mohan
  284 m. The ring road is ~180 m away and all 180 m are alley.
- Approach texture: 2–3 storey brick in cream/ochre limewash peeling to grey
  with exposed brick patches; projecting concrete balcony slabs; crude chhajjas
  of projecting timber/concrete joist ends; **black-and-white chevron pilasters**
  on shopfronts; corrugated awnings; **blue tarpaulins everywhere, visible from
  orbit**; festoon lighting; **thick bundles of overhead cable slung diagonally
  across every sightline — in the main facade photograph the cables physically
  obscure a third of the building**; loudspeaker horns bolted to the facade.
- Immediately outside: a raised platform with a turned-baluster stone railing,
  and flower sellers with cane baskets of marigold, rose and tuberose on red
  plastic sheets, packed shoulder to shoulder. Monkeys on the wires.
- Footfall, recited by the Supreme Court 15 May 2025: **30,000–40,000/day,
  1.5 lakh at weekends, 5 lakh on Janmashtami.** [SOURCED]

## Dimensions

**No source anywhere gives a single measured dimension of this building.**
Not the temple's own site, not the district government, not Growse, not any
court or ASI document.

Established [SOURCED]: cost **Rs 70,000** raised over **13 years** by
subscription; built **1862–64** by the Goswami descendants of Swami Haridas,
no architect named; **~800 devotees** can have darshan at one time (UP
minister, 2022) — the only quantification of the space that exists.

Derived / estimated, labels attached:
- Darshan floor area **~250–300 m²**, roughly **16 x 17 m** [DERIVED from the
  800 figure at 2–4 persons/m²]
- Large-span ribbed roof on satellite **~12–14 x 12–14 m** [MEASURED, low
  confidence on identification]
- Elevation (A) front width **~20–24 m** [ESTIMATE]: 9 bays at 1.9–2.2 m plus
  end piers
- Storey heights [ESTIMATE — a tilted-camera photogrammetric solve was
  attempted and is **wildly unstable at these angles, swinging 5–11 m for the
  same storey**; the researcher declined to dress that up as a measurement]:
  ground **6.0–7.0**, first floor **3.0–3.5**, top arcade **4.5–5.5**, jali
  parapet **0.9–1.1**, kiosk floor to crown **5.0–6.0**; **to main parapet
  ~15–17 m, to top of kiosk ~20–23 m**, flagstaff +2–3.
- Cross-check: it visibly overtops the surrounding 8–11 m houses yet is
  invisible from the street — consistent.
- **Discard "169.77 m / 557 ft."** English Wikipedia labels that *Elevation*,
  i.e. height above sea level. Hindi Wikipedia renders it as the temple's
  height and every blog copies it.

## What NOT to build

1. **A shikhara.** Not there. ⚠️ A trap: the June 2026 Amar Ujala ASI report
   says work will fill open joints in the walls, chhajjas and the **शिखर**,
   and fill cracks on the शिखर. In Hindi शिखर routinely means simply *the
   top/crown*. Given the photographic evidence it almost certainly means the
   rooftop kiosk. **Do not build a shikhara on the strength of that word.**
2. **Corner chhatris or corner towers.** Not visible.
3. **Kangura / merlon crenellation.** It is a jali parapet. Growse records
   kanguras only on fort-scale Braj work, never on the Vrindavan temples.
4. **A dome.** The kiosk roof is a curved bangaldar barrel with rolled ends.
5. **Rectangular openings on the ground or top storey.** Only the low middle
   storey is square-headed.
6. **Bells.** There are none anywhere in the complex, by ritual rule — the
   deity is served as a small child.
7. **An open-to-sky courtyard.** Roofed on iron girders.
8. **Open ground, a forecourt, a plaza, a free-standing boundary wall, or any
   distant view.** The corridor that *would* create a plaza is still at the
   land-registry stage — **14 registries / 1,386 m² in ten months** as of May
   2026. **The plaza does not exist today.**
9. **Mirrorwork, tilework, painted figural panels, marble.**
10. **A single flat hex colour.**
11. **Growse's Mathura-city "Temple of Bihari Ji"** — built 1850 by Chhakki Lal
    and Kanhaiya Lal, bankers of Mhow, Rs 25,000, by the Sami Ghat. **Different
    temple, different city.** A live contamination risk; both researchers
    nearly took it.
12. **Swami Haridas as the builder.** He is the 16th-century founder of the
    sampradaya. Hindi Wikipedia says he built it in 1864 and this is copied
    everywhere. It is wrong.

## Genuinely uncertain — flag, do not assert

- Exact cusp count on the central ground arch: 8–9 automated, 9–11 by eye.
- Whether the middle storey is a real trabeated gallery (~70% confidence) or a
  deep bracketed soffit seen at a steep angle.
- Sanctum screen arch count: 5, possibly 7.
- **All heights and widths above** — estimates, not measurements.
- Whether Growse's "white stone" central gateway is the one photographed. The
  ground storey does photograph paler than the storeys above, which fits.
- **Whether the gateway's upper storey — unbuilt in 1883 — was ever completed,
  and when.** [UNKNOWN] Growse: "This has yet to be completed by the addition
  of an upper story." No source records what happened next. The photographed
  facade *is* multi-storey, so something was built; the date is not recorded.
- Satellite footprint identification. OSM has **no building polygon here** —
  the temple is a bare node. Identified by roof ribbing and elimination.
- **What it looks like right now.** The best exterior photographs are 2010 and
  2012, i.e. pre-restoration. The most recent image found (April 2026) is
  interior only.

## Condition, if wear is ever modelled

The ASI/IIT survey filed in the Mathura civil court reports deep cracks in the
stone piers carrying the garbhagriha and galleries, load-bearing stones hollow
internally, water penetrating the roofs, badly rusted iron roof girders,
spalling lime-and-moorum plaster and unsafe overhead wiring. ASI's first
recommendation is to cap the number of people inside to reduce live load.
