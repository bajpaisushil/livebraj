# meera-bai

*Researched and independently verified 2026-09-27. Confidence: partial.*

## oneLine

A small 19th-century courtyard house-temple hidden in the Govind Bagh lanes behind Nidhivan — "प्राचीन मीराबाई मंदिर / OLD MEERA BAI TEMPLE" — built in 1842 on the spot where Mirabai is said to have lived, where Krishna as Radha-Manohar Girdhar Gopal stands on the altar with Radha on one side and Mirabai herself on the other.

## location

**Govind Bagh / Gobind Bagh mohalla, Vrindavan, Mathura district, UP 281121.** The locality is named for a garden said to have belonged to Govind Dev Ji; the banner over the temple door gives the address as "गोबिंदबाग, वृन्दावन".

The lane itself is named in Hindi press as **मीरा प्रेम गली (Mira Prem Gali)**; Braj Rasik calls the approach **Kunj Gali**. Both are attested. **"Holi Gali", given by one English directory, is unverified and conflicts with the banner and with every Hindi source — do not use it.**

In the block of lanes bounded by **Nidhivan** to the south-east and **Radha Damodar** to the south-west. Hindi sources place it "adjacent to the ancient Nidhivan Raj Mandir"; Braj Rasik places it "adjacent to Radha-Damodar Temple in Kunj Gali"; English guides say it faces **Shahji Temple** across a crossroad that opens at Nidhivan. Shahji is recorded as being in Gotam Nagar near Nidhivan but **has no OpenStreetMap feature, so "immediately adjacent to Shahji" cannot be checked against a surveyed coordinate** — treat it as a guidebook phrase, not a fix. It is 2–3 minutes' walk from Nidhivan's gate, on foot only.

**Coordinates: approximately 27.5852 N, 77.6971 E — and this is weaker than the other researcher makes it sound.** The figure comes from the EXIF GPS of one Wikimedia Commons photograph, "Up state vrindavan place in Mirabai house.jpg" (Suneha Gill, 6 January 2026, CC BY 4.0). I verified the file and its metadata: EXIF GPS 27.585177 N, 77.697094 E, taken on a Realme Narzo N53, uploaded under the Wiki Loves Punjab 2026 campaign. **But its own description is simply "House, old, vrindavan" — the file says nothing about Mirabai except in the filename the uploader chose.** The image does show an arcaded blue courtyard house with fan palms, a garlanded bell, a water cooler and a balcony that match this temple closely, so the identification is probably right — but it rests on one amateur upload plus a visual match, and phone GPS in a covered lane is unreliable. Treat it as a **50 m circle, not a point**.

**The temple is not mapped in OpenStreetMap.** I re-checked on 27 September 2026 via Overpass and Nominatim: no node, way or relation under Mira / Meera / मीरा anywhere in the Vrindavan bounding box, and no OSM feature for Govind Bagh, Gotam Nagar, Holi Gali or Shahji Temple either.

OSM-surveyed neighbours, which I verified individually — the other researcher's IDs and coordinates are all exactly right:
- **Nidhivan** — way 99418393, 27.58435 N, 77.69830 E → the temple sits **≈150 m north-west** of it.
- **Sri Radha Damodara Mandir** — way 334674939, 27.58376 N, 77.69561 E → **≈215 m north-east** of it.
- **Sri Radha Raman Mandir** — way 335527344, 27.58538 N, 77.69872 E → **≈160 m west** of it.

**Darshan timings — four sources, four different answers. Label whatever you pick a guess, or give the range.**
- vrajvrindavan.com: 5:00–13:00 and 16:00–20:45 (summer).
- vrindavanguide.com: summer 7:30–12:30 and 16:30–21:00; winter 8:30–12:00 and 17:00–21:00.
- vrindavandarshanguide.com: 6:00–12:00 and 16:30–19:45 (page now returns 404).
- brajrasik.org lists only aratis: Mangala arati 10:00, Shayan arati 19:00–20:00. The 10 a.m. mangala arati is odd for Vrindavan and may be a mislabel.

## scale

All figures are photogrammetric estimates scaled off standing adults at 1.70 m in ~30 photographs. No survey, plan or published dimension exists. Treat as ±25–30%. I have kept the other researcher's numbers where the photographs support them and adjusted where they do not.

**Plan**
- Open courtyard: **9–12 m × 8–11 m.** (Revised up slightly: the shrine range reads as about five bays in the photographs, which at a 2.0–2.3 m pitch gives ~10–11.5 m on that side. The original 8–10 m is at the low end.)
- Verandah in front of the shrine: **2.5–3 m deep**, with two free-standing colonnettes, running the width of one range.
- Arcade bay pitch **2.0–2.3 m**; arch opening **1.5–1.9 m** wide.
- **Supports, two kinds:** free-standing colonnettes **0.20–0.30 m** shaft; wall piers between bays **0.40–0.55 m**.
- Shrine alcove: opening **1.4–1.7 m** wide, recess **≈1.2 m** deep; throne platform **≈0.9 m** high.
- Built ranges around the court **≈3–4 m** deep, two storeys.
- **Overall envelope including all four ranges and the entrance block: roughly 16–22 m × 15–20 m.** Do not build it larger.
- Approach: covered passage **1.5–2.0 m wide × 2.4–2.6 m to a flat soffit** (revised down from 2.5 × 2.8); the gali beyond **1.5–2.5 m**; the shop lane feeding it **3–4 m**.

**Section**
- Courtyard to verandah floor: **3 marble risers, 0.40–0.55 m** total.
- Verandah floor to cusped arch apex: **2.4–2.8 m**; springing at **≈1.4 m**.
- Verandah floor to beam soffit: **3.0–3.3 m**.
- Ground floor to chhajja line: **3.2–3.5 m**; chhajja projects **0.35–0.55 m** on closely spaced flat corbels (revised down from 0.6–0.8 m).
- First storey **3.0–3.3 m**; parapet or railing **0.85–1.0 m**.
- **Total height to roof/parapet: 7.5–9 m.** Nothing on the building exceeds this.

**Street door block**
- Door opening **≈1.2 m × 2.1 m**; cusped arch head to **≈2.8–3.0 m**.
- Jharokha floor at **4.0–4.5 m**, projecting **0.7–0.9 m** on **five or six** heavy carved brackets, with a solid parapet.
- Window heads **≈6.5–7 m**; cornice **≈7.5–8 m**; parapet top **≈8.5 m**.

**Deities**
- Upper-tier murtis **0.45–0.60 m** tall, the centre Krishna clearly the tallest of the three; lower-tier utsav pair **≈0.30 m**. Shaligrams are hand-sized black stones.

## architecture

**The other researcher's account is substantially correct and unusually well observed.** I checked it against the temple's own signage read at full resolution, a 24-photograph album, a 2026 photograph, OpenStreetMap and Growse. Most of it survives. The corrections below are real but local; the shape of the building they describe is right.

**It is a haveli, not a temple.** Nothing reads as a temple from outside; no tower of any kind. Two storeys, flat roof. CONFIRMED.

**Approach.** From the Nidhivan–Radha Damodar side you leave a normal shop-lined Vrindavan lane (3–4 m wide, not narrow) and enter a **covered passage cut through a building**. **CORRECTION: the passage is not barrel-vaulted.** It has a **flat soffit** and a plain **rectangular flat-headed opening** at the far end. Walls are exposed brick — old brick with red-ochre plaster on one side, newer wire-cut brick stamped "SONU" on the other. **CORRECTION: it is paved with interlocking grey concrete paver blocks in a zigzag pattern, not brick-on-edge.** A large old **iron-sheeted double gate leaf** stands folded against one wall — the passage can be shut. Width **1.5–2.0 m**, not 2.5 m: two men walking abreast nearly fill it.

At its mouth, an **ochre-yellow washed wall** carries a hand-painted sign, "जय मीराबाई" in red above "MEERA TEMPLE" in blue with a **red arrow**. CONFIRMED exactly.

Beyond, a **kunj gali** between high walls. One wall — ochre with a blue border — carries the large hand-painted Devanagari inscription "प्राचीनतम् / भक्त शिरोमणि / श्री मीराँबाई मंदिर / मेरे तो गिरिधर गोपाल, दूसरो ना कोई…". CONFIRMED. A rusted riveted **blue enamel plate** with white lettering is wired to a wall: "दाँई तरफ मुड़िये और देखिये प्राचीनतम श्री मीराबाई मंदिर / TURN TO RIGHT AND SEE ANCIENT SHRI MIRABAI TEMPLE". CONFIRMED verbatim.

**Street door.** CONFIRMED in almost every particular. **Carved pink-buff sandstone**, the one piece of show. A wide **multi-cusped arch of large carved petal lobes** over a doorway ~1.2 m wide, closed by a plain **vertical-barred iron grille**, wire mesh in the tympanum. **Deep acanthus-like scroll foliage in the spandrels**, a running floral/beaded border band above. Directly over it a **jharokha projecting on heavy carved sandstone brackets**, its fascia set with cusped recessed panels and a dentil/bead band. **CORRECTION: the jharokha has a solid masonry parapet, not an open railing.** A **vinyl banner** tied across the carving: "प्राचीन मीराबाई मंदिर / OLD MEERA BAI TEMPLE / गोबिंदबाग, वृन्दावन / मंदिर सेवायतः- श्री प्रद्युम्न प्रताप सिंह", with a **painted Mirabai at the left end and a peacock feather at the right**. A single bare bulb hangs below it. All CONFIRMED.

**Street elevation above.** CONFIRMED exactly: shallow **recessed arch-headed panels each carrying a dark carved six-point star rosette** in the arch head, a small carved floral band over each arch, and between them **old timber windows** — weathered grey-blue, with **turned colonnettes at the jambs**, vertical wooden bars, some solid panelled shutters. A moulded, dentil-carved eaves slab and a plain parapet close the top. Warm pinkish-buff, patched, brick showing through. **Refinement: only the doorway block and the brackets read as carved stone. The panels, arches and star rosettes above are moulded lime plaster over masonry, washed pink-buff — model them as plaster, not ashlar.**

**The courtyard.** Small, open to sky, two-storey ranges all round, arcaded on opposite sides.

- **Colour CONFIRMED in daylight: pale lavender-blue / periwinkle limewash, all trim picked out in chalk white.** (A January 2026 phone photograph shows it as a saturated cyan; that is the LED lighting, not a repaint. Build the pale periwinkle.)
- Ground floor: arcade of **cusped, ogee-lobed arches**, white, with **a large white rosette medallion moulded above each arch head** and smaller white flower bosses along the frieze band. CONFIRMED.
- **CORRECTION on supports:** two kinds. The **free-standing colonnettes** in front of the shrine are slender — shaft ~0.20–0.30 m, with a foliate carved capital and a moulded base. The **wall piers** between arcade bays are wider, ~0.40–0.55 m. The account's single figure flattens a real distinction.
- **Projecting chhajja on closely spaced simple flat corbels** runs right round the court between storeys. CONFIRMED, but it projects modestly — ~0.35–0.55 m, not 0.6–0.8 m.
- **CORRECTION on the first floor: the balustrade is not uniform.** One range has a **solid plastered parapet** ~0.9 m high; another has an **iron railing**. Both are in the photographs. Behind them, blue walls, **green-painted panelled double doors**, barred timber windows, and small arched niches between.
- Verandah floors **white marble**; courtyard floor grey stone/concrete.
- **Two tall fan palms** (Livistona-type) grow straight out of the court through the balcony line, one from a round masonry planter, **both trunks wrapped in climbing money plant**. CONFIRMED.
- A dense mat of **pothos, spider plants and ferns in terracotta pots** fills the middle of the court, with a **black wire basket structure** in it — almost certainly the surviving fountain, overgrown. A row of terracotta pots also stands on a masonry shelf under one arch, and pots hang on strings from the arch soffits of the shrine range.
- Bays are used: one arch blocked by a large old timber-framed glazed panel, others by cupboards. It is a store as much as a cloister.

**The shrine.** In the middle of one arcaded range, up **three marble steps** (~0.40–0.55 m total) to the verandah. The centre bay is a deep alcove framed in **white marble with a cusped arch**, a **round convex medallion (mirror or clock) above it**, closed by a **fine wire-mesh screen** in a frame — mesh, not a bar grille.

Inside, a **white marble throne** under a **deeply pierced white marble torana** — a cusped arch with foliate scroll spandrels and a petal-fringed soffit, on two pairs of slender carved colonnettes. This is the one finely carved object in the building. The plinth carries pietra-dura-style inlaid flowers in blue, orange and green.

**CORRECTION on the back of the niche and the curtain.** The permanent back is **white marble/plaster with a painted cusped-arch outline and blue, green and red floral sprays**. The **green-and-gold pleated curtain is a dressing, not a fixture** — it is up in some photographs and absent in others, where dark maroon and navy cloths hang behind the Deities instead. Do not make green the fixed state; it is one of several.

**MISSED, and worth building:** the courtyard plaster flanking the shrine carries **painted mural figures** — tall standing sakhis in pale green saris, in soft blue-green, inside thin gilt-lined rectangular panels — plus **ochre-painted swastika marks**. The alcove's side walls and the courtyard piers are hung with **many old framed chromolithographs and photographs** of Krishna, Radha and Mirabai. These crowded pictures are most of what a visitor actually sees.

**The plaque above the shrine. CORRECTION: it is not an ancient inscription slab.** It is a **modern painted black board with gold lettering, Hindi above and English below**, hung on the blue wall. Its English reads, in part: "THIS TEMPLE IS BUILT ON THE RESIDENC[E] OF THE TOP MOST SAINT MEERA BAI BY THAKUR RAM NARAYAN SING[H] RAJ DIVAN OF BIKANER ON 1842. IN THE MIDDLE SHRI KRISHNA … FROM THE LEFT SHRI RADHA, RIGHT SHRI MEERA. MOVEABLE RADHA KRISHNA IS ON THE LOWER THORNE AND TO RIGHT THEM IS THE SHALIGRAM … SNAKE SENT BY RANA TO BITE MEERA. THE MANAGEMENT OF THIS TEMPLE DEPENDS UPON YOU, THEREFORE … DONATE YOUR DONATION". Model a painted board, not carved stone.

A **ceiling fan** and bare bulbs hang from the verandah beams; a **garlanded brass bell on a chain** hangs in front of the centre bay; **coir mats** lie on the verandah and a rug runs to the shrine.

**FOUNDER AND DATE — the account leaves this blank, and it need not be.** Four independent sources agree: **Thakur Ram Narayan Singh, a Bhati Rajput and Raj Dewan (Diwan) of the Bikaner court, built the temple in VS 1898 / 1842 CE** on the site of Mirabai's residence. The temple's own board says so. Sources differ on detail — historypandit calls him a Jaisalmer Yaduvanshi Bhati and says he built it as a memorial to his mother Lakshmi Bai, with a descendant Thakur Shiv Baksh Singh contributing. Bikaner service and Jaisalmer clan are compatible. This is as firm as anything here gets.

**Bhajan kuti.** On the right of the sanctum, per Hindi sources, painted "श्याम के रंग में" (Krishna-blue) — continuous with the court. **Refinement: what a visitor sees is not a blue cell but a small alcove-shrine swagged in gold-and-yellow satin**, holding a dark old painted portrait of Mirabai with a tanpura, a framed painting inside the drape, black Hindi verse plaques, and modern printed posters of Krishna and of Mirabai crowded round it. There is also a framed Shiva picture with the poison verse.

**Everyday clutter** — white water cooler against a pier, blue plastic stool, bucket, conduit and bare wiring stapled across the blue plaster, an LED strip on a cable, paint worn to grey and brown plaster at the pier bases and in the arch reveals. CONFIRMED, all of it, and it is most of the texture.

**Thin points, as the other researcher said and I confirm: no plan, no survey, no dimensioned drawing, no scholarly architectural description exists in open sources.** Everything is read off photographs. Shrine orientation within the court is not established — trust no compass bearing. **New negative: F. S. Growse's "Mathura: A District Memoir" (1874/1880) does not mention Mirabai or this temple at all** — full-text search of the Internet Archive scan returns no occurrence of Mira/Meera as a name and no Govind Bagh. A thirty-year-old temple in Brindaban did not reach the district memoir. Nothing in the colonial or archaeological record documents this building.

## distinguishing

**1. Mirabai stands on the altar as a third Deity.** Not a portrait, not a samadhi — a crowned, brocaded murti of her on the main throne beside Radha and Krishna, and the temple's own board names her there: "FROM THE LEFT SHRI RADHA, RIGHT SHRI MEERA". No other Vrindavan temple is known to do this. If you build one thing right, build the three-figure altar: small black-stone flute-playing Krishna in the centre in a blue-and-white striped garment, two identically dressed turquoise-and-gold crowned women flanking Him slightly lower, on a white marble inlaid throne under a pierced white marble cusped torana, with two small utsav murtis of Radha-Krishna on a cushion below and a black shaligram in a marigold tray at their feet.

**2. It is entered by a hand-painted arrow on a yellow wall.** A shop lane, a flat-roofed brick tunnel under a house with an iron gate leaf folded against its wall, an ochre wall with "जय मीराबाई / MEERA TEMPLE →" brushed on it, a rusted blue enamel plate reading "TURN TO RIGHT", a narrow gali, then a carved red sandstone door with a vinyl banner tied across the carving. That sequence is the whole experience of arriving. A modeller who builds a forecourt destroys the place.

**3. Periwinkle courtyard, chalk-white cusped arcade, white rosettes, two fan palms growing through it.** The colour pairing plus the palms with money plant spiralling up their trunks and the mat of potted pothos in the middle of the court is the visual signature. Confirmed in daylight photographs.

**4. The painted sakhis on the courtyard plaster either side of the shrine**, in soft blue-green inside thin gilt-lined panels, and the crowd of old framed chromolithographs hung between the arches. This is what the walls actually carry, and it is easy to leave out.

**5. The one surviving fountain** in the green bed in the middle of the court — a black wire basket now buried in pothos. The sevayat's line, six when Mirabai came and one left, is what pilgrims are told.

## doNotBuild

**Do not build the Chittorgarh Meera Temple.** This remains the single most likely error. Most images and 3D references labelled "Meera Bai Mandir" are the temple inside Chittorgarh Fort in Rajasthan — a stone temple with a tall curvilinear shikhara, an open pillared mandapa and a raised plinth, with the Kumbha-Shyam shrine beside it. Different building, different state. The Vrindavan one has no shikhara, no mandapa, no plinth, no free-standing form at all.

- **No tower, dome, amalaka, kalash, gopuram, gateway arch or spire.** Nothing rises above the neighbours' roofline.
- **No white marble facade.** The only marble is the shrine surround, the torana, the throne and the verandah floor and steps. Walls are limewashed plaster; the street door block alone is carved sandstone.
- **No boundary wall, compound, forecourt, plaza, parking, queue rails, metal detectors or donation counters.**
- **Do not state that no offerings are collected.** The other account says Hindi sources are explicit about this; the temple's own board is explicit the other way — "THE MANAGEMENT OF THIS TEMPLE DEPENDS UPON YOU, THEREFORE … DONATE YOUR DONATION". Drop that claim. Whether it is a registered trust is unverified either way.
- **It is not visible from any street.** No street frontage on a wide road, no visible facade from the Nidhivan junction. Its only public face is a doorway in a narrow gali.
- **No flagstaff, dhwaja, lion or elephant guardians, temple tank, or parikrama path.**
- **Not a Rajasthani palace.** "Rajasthani style" in the sources means the cusped arcades, the courtyard plan and the carved bracketed jharokha — not jharokhas everywhere, not jali screens, not Shekhawati frescoes, not a Jaisalmer yellow-sandstone haveli.
- **Do not make the interior grand.** The court is tight — arcade bays ~2.0–2.3 m, arch apex 2.4–2.8 m above the verandah. At palace scale it is wrong.
- **Do not build a samadhi of Mirabai here.** She did not die here; tradition has her leaving for Dwarka.
- **Do not barrel-vault the entrance passage.** It is a flat-soffited tunnel with a plain rectangular opening.
- **Do not lay brick-on-edge in the passage.** It is grey interlocking concrete pavers.
- **Do not carve the courtyard trim in stone.** The arches, rosettes, chhajja brackets and the star-rosette panels on the street elevation are moulded lime plaster, limewashed. Only the street doorway, its brackets and the shrine's torana and throne are real carved stone and marble.
- **Do not model the plaque above the shrine as an ancient carved shilalekh.** It is a modern painted black board with gold Hindi and English lettering.
- **Do not fix the green curtain.** It is a changeable dressing; the permanent back of the niche is white with painted floral sprays, and dark maroon and navy cloths hang there at other times.
- **Do not give the first-floor gallery one uniform balustrade.** One range has a solid plastered parapet, another an iron railing.
- **Do not paint the court a saturated Jodhpur cyan.** That is a night LED photograph. In daylight it is a pale periwinkle with chalk-white trim.
- **Do not leave the walls bare.** Painted sakhi murals flank the shrine and dozens of old framed pictures hang between the arches.
- **Do not date the building to the 16th century.** The structure is VS 1898 / 1842 CE. Only the *site* is claimed as Mirabai's.
- **Do not state Mirabai's dates as fact.** The 1524–1539 Vrindavan residence and the 1550 death are what the temple and Braj websites say; commonly cited dates elsewhere are c. 1498 – c. 1547, and Hawley ("Mirabai in Manuscript") and Nancy M. Martin ("Mirabai: The Making of a Saint", OUP 2023) treat her biography as largely hagiographic — the oldest records mentioning her are 17th-century. Narrate it as what the temple says.
- **Do not claim the sevayat family descends from Mirabai.** Sources contradict each other: descendants of Mirabai, of the family that hosted her, or of the 1841 founder. The safe statement is "the same family has held the seva for generations".
- **Do not name Pradyumn Pratap Singh as the living sevayat.** He died c. mid-2023; his son **Rudra Pratap Singh** holds the seva now. The banner over the door still carries the old name — build the banner as photographed, but do not narrate him as living.
- **Do not expect a mapped footprint.** The temple is absent from OpenStreetMap and from Growse's district memoir. There is no surveyed outline to trace.

## deities

Centre: **श्री कृष्ण राधा-मनोहर गिरधर गोपाल** (Shri Krishna Radha-Manohar Girdhar Gopal) — a small black-stone standing Krishna playing a silver flute, in a blue-and-white striped garment under a gold brocade shawl, with an ornate gold crown and peacock plume, hung with heavy bead garlands. Braj Rasik names Him "Shri Manohar Ji"; the temple's own board gives the full name.

Flanking Him, the same throne, two fair-faced crowned female figures dressed identically in turquoise-and-gold brocade with maroon veils: **श्री राधा (Shri Radha)** and **भक्त शिरोमणि श्री मीराँबाई (Bhakt Shiromani Shri Mirabai)**. The temple's bilingual board above the shrine states the order: "IN THE MIDDLE SHRI KRISHNA … FROM THE LEFT SHRI RADHA, RIGHT SHRI MEERA" (Hindi: "बीच में श्री कृष्ण राधा मनोहर गिरधर गोपाल, बाँयें श्री राधा…"). **Whose left is meant is genuinely ambiguous** — Punjab Kesari reads it as Radha on the viewer's left, Mirabai on the right. Pick one and do not claim certainty.

Lower tier: a pair of small **chal / utsav murtis of Radha-Krishna** — "MOVEABLE RADHA KRISHNA IS ON THE LOWER THORNE [throne]" — dark Krishna with flute and fair Radha, seated on a cushioned seat in front, roughly 55–60% the height of the upper Deities.

Beside Them, in a marigold-dressed metal tray at the foot of the throne, the **shaligram** — the temple says this is the snake the Rana sent in a basket to kill Mirabai, which turned to stone. (Some accounts speak of two shaligrams: hers, and the snake-shaligram.)

The three flanking figures are NOT all the same height: Krishna's crown stands clearly highest, the two women reach roughly his chin/shoulder. Close, not equal.
