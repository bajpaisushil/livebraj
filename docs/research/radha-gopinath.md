# radha-gopinath — exterior survey

*Researched 2026-09-28 by one agent and independently checked by a second whose brief was to refute it. Labels are the researcher's own: MEASURED / SOURCED / DERIVED / ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN.*

## What it is

Shri Radha Gopinath Mandir is a small, east-facing, LINEAR red-sandstone shrine of c.1589 — architecturally a near-twin of Madan Mohan (nave + choir + sanctum in a row, with a side chapel on the south), not a relative of Govind Dev — of which only the sanctum/choir block, its south chapel and an applied three-bay bracket-arch screen survive, truncated and swallowed whole by the lanes of Gopinath Ghera, so that unlike the other three great temples it has no presence on Vrindavan's skyline at all.

## The silhouette

WHAT SURVIVES (SOURCED, Growse 1883 pp.253–254, confirmed by 2021 photographs): the choir (jagamohan) and sacrarium (garbhagriha) block, the small chapel attached to the SOUTH side of the sacrarium, the applied arcade of three bracket arches screening the south wall on its carved terrace, and the great arched east front. Growse: "the nave has entirely disappeared; the three towers have been levelled with the roof; and the entrance gateway of the court-yard is tottering to its fall."

WHAT IS LOST: the entire nave/mandapa (Growse's word is "entirely"), and the superstructures. What replaced the nave's footprint is now lane, steps and 19th-century building.

THE ROOFLINE, plainly. Growse's 1883 autotype plate (facing p.254) shows the surviving block crowned by a BROKEN STEPPED STUMP: three or four receding stone courses of the tower's base, faced with grass and shrubs, with displaced slabs and a jagged, uneven top. Not a designed flat terrace — a shear. On the east front, the 2021 photographs show the walls flanking the great arch ending in a straight, level, parapet-less head at about the arch's apex, with an irregular mound of exposed grey-brown RUBBLE CORE (its ashlar facing gone) rising a little above, over the arched bay only. That is the roofline you see from the lane: flat wall-heads, one low rubble hump.

WHAT CROWNS IT TODAY — and this is the one thing I cannot settle. A 2023 photograph taken inside the ghera courtyard (Rana Safvi) shows the SAME block — identical bracket-and-rosette cornice to the Growse plate, same offset walls, same moulded plinth — carrying a COMPLETE curvilinear (Nagara latina) sikhara in red sandstone: a broad parabolic profile with a strongly projecting central lata band, flanking quarter-lobes, two small corner urushringa spirelets at the base, a spine of small carved bosses up the centre, and a modest kalash finial. Beside it on the right, an untouched grey ruined mass at cornice level. Sinha & Dhariwal (2024) independently state the temple "has a multi-spired, not an octagonal shikhara over its sanctum (only one of which survives)." Since Growse in 1883 says all three towers were down to roof level, the economical reading is a post-1883 RECONSTRUCTION of the sanctum spire; no source I found dates it. Set against that, a January 2021 photograph of the lane front shows no spire rising behind the rubble crest.

MODELLING INSTRUCTION: build the mass as Jugal Kishor with the spire sheared off (Growse's Jugal Kishor plate, facing p.254, is the best surviving analogue for the intact type), and make the sanctum spire a SEPARABLE element with two states — (a) truncated stump, matching the 1883 plate, and (b) the curvilinear latina spire with two corner urushringas, matching the 2023 photograph. Do not fuse the spire into the wall geometry.

WHAT IS NOT THERE, in any period: an onion dome, a Mughal-type dome, a ribbed or gadrooned dome, a gold finial, a flag mast on the ruin, or an octagonal spire.

## The elevation

Two faces matter. Both are read off Growse's 1883 autotype plate (south-east view of the sanctum/chapel block) and 2021 photographs of the east front.

A. THE SANCTUM/CHAPEL BLOCK, storey by storey (bottom up), from the plate:
1. TERRACE, in front of the south wall only, carrying the arcade. Front faced with carved stone: a row of panels each bearing paired rosette/medallion bosses, divided by short pilaster strips. Growse uncovered this himself — "The terrace on which this arcade stands has a carved stone front, which had been buried for years, till I uncovered it." Est. 0.5–0.9 m. Opening:solid 0:100.
2. PLINTH (vedibandha) — Growse calls it "a fine boldly moulded plinth". From the plate, about five members: a broad plain base course; a bold half-round torus; a fillet; a band of small pendant lotus-bud dentils; a top fillet. Est. 1.2–1.8 m. Opening:solid 0:100.
3. WALL, LOWER REGISTER (jangha). Large PLAIN ashlar panels within simple frames. The plan is strongly offset — a projecting central bhadra on each face, recessed karnas at the angles, pratirathas between; the angle itself is a cluster of slender vertical shafts (fluted colonnette group). Est. 2.5–3.5 m. This is where the two doorways sit. Opening:solid on the sanctum face 0:100; on the chapel's east face, one doorway ≈ 8:92.
4. MID BAND. A string course, a recessed panel band, and a dentil/sawtooth course. Solid.
5. WALL, UPPER REGISTER. Shallower recessed panels, further dentil and fillet courses. Solid.
6. CROWNING CORNICE — the building's signature. A bold projecting eave carried on DEEP PENDANT BRACKETS alternating with large rosette medallions; roughly 8–10 brackets across the visible face in the plate. This band is the fingerprint: it is identical in the 1883 plate and the 2023 photograph, which is how the two can be matched. Est. 0.6–0.9 m deep, projecting ≈0.5 m.
7. Above: the stump / spire (see silhouette).
Overall opening:solid for this block ≈ 5:95. It is a nearly blind box. Do not perforate it.

B. THE EAST (CHOIR) FRONT, as it now faces the lane:
1. ~6 rough stone steps up from the lane to a threshold; the original plinth here is largely buried and blocked with modern brick.
2. ONE great arched recess, centred. Outer archivolt: a broad, plain, boldly moulded band rising to a SHARP POINT — a straight-sided lancet/"tent" profile, not a curve. Inside it, following the intrados, a continuous fringe of small triangular cusps (saw-tooth daggers). Tympanum: a plain recessed field with a carved lotus boss at the apex and vine/makara ornament — Growse's "handsome design, elaborately decorated with arabesque sculptures."
3. Within the arch, a SQUARE-HEADED doorway with moulded jambs, a carved lintel band and small brackets; now closed with an iron gate.
4. Flanking: flat pilaster strips with moulded caps; a small cusped-arch niche to the right; the wall head horizontally moulded and LEVEL, terminating at about the arch apex.
Opening:solid on this face: the recessed arch panel occupies roughly 25–30% of the elevation; the actual VOID (the doorway) only about 10%.

C. THE ARCADE (the one feature unique to this temple). Growse: "The special feature of the building is a curious arcade of three bracket arches, serving apparently no constructural purpose, but merely added as an ornamental screen to the south wall, which already had a fine boldly moulded plinth and required no further adornment." Three bays, SOUTH wall only, standing forward of the wall on the terrace. Each arch is formed by paired carved corbel brackets meeting in a lobed outline, carried on slender shafts with moulded bases and bracket capitals, under a heavy flat lintel/cornice. In the 1883 plate the bays behind are already infilled with brick.

D. ARCH GRAMMAR — three rules, all of them things a modeller gets wrong:
(i) NO true radiating arches. Every arch here is corbelled and decorative. Growse on this very series, p.242: the arches "are decorative only, not constructural: the spandrels in the head might be — and, as a fact, for the most part had been — struck out, leaving only the lintel supported on the straight jambs, without any injury to the stability of the building." He singles out Govind Dev's true voussoir vaults as the exception in the group, not the rule.
(ii) The profile is a straight-sided POINT. Not a four-centred Mughal arch, not a Shahjahani multifoil/cusped arch, not a horseshoe. The cusping is a thin applied fringe on the intrados, not a lobed arch outline.
(iii) Doorways are trabeated: square head, moulded jambs, carved lintel, projecting bracketed chhajja hood on heavy pendant brackets.

E. ORNAMENT DISTRIBUTION. Ornament is CONCENTRATED, not spread. Plinth mouldings, the bracket-and-rosette cornice, the doorframes, the great arch, the arcade, the terrace front. The wall fields between are large, plain, coursed ashlar. Roughly 70–80% of the visible wall surface is plain stone.

## Material and colour

MATERIAL (SOURCED): red sandstone throughout the 16th-century fabric — Growse pp.241–254 and Entwistle 1987 §30, "one of the four red sandstone ones founded during the reign of Akbar". Large coursed ashlar, rubble hearting behind the facing (exposed at the broken crest). Later fabric is fired brick and lime plaster, and 19th-century work is plastered and painted.

PRACTICAL PALETTE (INFERRED from the above):
- 16th-c sandstone base albedo: H 10–18°, S 25–35%, V 50–60% — a dull brown-rose, NOT vermilion, NOT the bright Fatehpur-Sikri red.
- Weathered/eroded faces and the broken core desaturate hard: drop S to 12–15%, hold V 45–50%. Model this as a separate mask on the crest and on north/shaded faces, or the ruin will read as new.
- Painted zones sit 15–50 saturation points ABOVE the stone and about 10–15° lower in hue (redder). If you give the whole complex one red, it will be wrong twice.
- Lime/whitewash H 50°, S 5%, V 90% for the lower walls of the working temple.

TEXTURE NOTES: large ashlar joints ~0.6–1.0 m coursing; extensive brick patching and infill in openings (visible in both the 1883 plate and the 2021 photographs); grass and shrubs rooted in the broken crest; pale buff replacement blocks scattered through the wall where repaired.

## The setting

POSITION. Gopinath Ghera / Gopinath Bazar, inland Vrindavan. Entwistle §30: "The street running between the temples of Govind Dev and Gopinath is called Gopinath Bazaar." OSM (ODbL) places the temple at 27.58605 N, 77.69962 E. MEASURED distances from that node:
- Radha Gokulananda 85 m WNW; Gopal Bhatt samadhi 102 m WSW; Radha Raman 116 m SW; Jugal Kishor 138 m NW; Kesi Ghat 160 m NNW; Nidhivan 230 m SW; Gopishwar Mahadev 343 m ESE; Radha Damodar 471 m WSW; Govind Dev 514 m S; Rangji 517 m SE; Banke Bihari 1.13 km SW; Madan Mohan 1.35 km WSW.
(Node placement precision is unknown; treat as ±30 m.)

IT IS NOT A RIVERFRONT TEMPLE. Mathura district's own site and many travel pages give the address as "Parikrama Marg, Keshi Ghat", which reads as a ghat temple. It is not on the ghats; it is ~160 m inland behind two streets of houses. The Yamuna is present as a smell and a direction, not a view.

THE MOUND. Vinay Kumar Gupta (ASI Agra Circle), 2020: "Gop Tila situated at the Gopinathji ka ghera area is about 3 m in height and spread in an area of about 2 acres and is fully occupied by modern settlements." Compare, from the same paper: Goma Tila under Govind Dev, ~15 m high, ~8 acres; Dwadash Aditya Tila under Madan Mohan, ~12 m high, ~2 acres. This single comparison is the whole story of the setting — Govind Dev and Madan Mohan stand on artificial hills and are seen for kilometres; Gopinath stands on a 3 m rise that has been built over so completely that it reads as flat ground.

VISIBILITY FROM ANY DISTANCE: effectively NONE. Satellite imagery over the site (ESRI World Imagery, z18–19, ~0.27 m/px, MEASURED) shows an unbroken 160 m × 160 m field of flat-roofed two- and three-storey masonry with almost no open ground — lanes reading 2–4 m wide, a handful of light wells, one small tree. The only clear space at the temple is a court of roughly 15 × 14 m (MEASURED off imagery, ±3 m) belonging to the 19th-century temple. Neighbouring roof heights of 6–10 m are at or above the surviving ruin's wall-head. You do not see this building until you are in its lane.

THE APPROACH. From Gopinath Bazar, a narrow lane; the old temple's east front is reached up about six rough, uneven stone steps from unmade ground strewn with rubble and brick. Immediately in front of the steps: the U.P. Tourism plaque on a carved stone balustrade with lotus-bud post finials; a blue Directorate of Archaeology U.P. signboard; a leaning electricity pole; a cat's cradle of low overhead cables crossing the facade at about the springing of the arch. Modern white-plastered and brick buildings press against both flanks, one of them overhanging on a corrugated sheet.

WHAT ELSE IS IN THE GHERA. (1) The 19th-century working temple, built ABOUT 1821 by the Bengali Kayasth Nand Kumar Ghos, who also built the new Madan Mohan (Growse p.254; Entwistle dates it early 19th c.; some modern sources say 1812, 1819 or 1877). It is a haveli-type courtyard temple: two storeys, cream/salmon painted, a deep carved bracket cornice, an arcaded upper storey with jali-filled cusped arches, a central ornate darshan arch, a courtyard floored in cream marble with black diamond inserts, bird netting overhead, and a separate street front with a deep chhajja on carved brackets and painted panels. Growse: "the north side is blocked by the modern temple." (2) The samadhi of Madhu Pandit Goswami — a plain plastered chamber closed with a green painted grille, house number 191. It is NOT a monument with a tower. (3) A separate red-painted arched entrance off the street, with a trilingual Hindi/English/Bengali board.

CONTINUITY OF USE. In Growse's day the interior of the old temple "is still used as a stable." Today it is fenced, signposted, and partly used for storage — wire-mesh cages stand against one flank in the 2023 photograph.

PROTECTION STATUS — correction to the brief. It is NOT ASI-protected. Gopinath Temple is S-UP-88, a UTTAR PRADESH STATE-protected monument under the Directorate of Archaeology, U.P.; the on-site board is the blue state board, not an ASI board. (Madan Mohan and Govind Dev are the centrally protected ones.) In May 2023 the State Archaeology Department handed maintenance to a private "Smarak Mitra" under the Adopt-a-Heritage policy, which the resident Goswamis are contesting.

## Dimensions

There is NO published measured survey of this building that I could find. Every plan figure below is transferred from Growse's measurements of Madan Mohan on his own explicit statement (p.253) that Gopinath "corresponds very closely both in style and dimensions with that of Madan Mohan, already described, and has a similar chapel attached to the south side of the sacrarium."

SOURCED (Growse 1883 p.251, Madan Mohan, internal dimensions, imperial):
- Nave (mandapa): 57 ft long = 17.37 m. Three openings on either side; a square door at the east end. Total height only about 22 ft = 6.71 m. AT GOPINATH THIS IS ENTIRELY GONE.
- Choir (jagamohan): 20 ft square = 6.10 m × 6.10 m, at the west end of the nave.
- Sacrarium (garbhagriha): 20 ft square = 6.10 m × 6.10 m, beyond the choir.
- Orientation: entrance at the EAST, sanctum at the WEST, side chapel on the SOUTH of the sacrarium.
SOURCED (Growse p.254, Jugal Kishor, for calibration): its choir is "slightly larger than in the other examples, being 25 feet square" — confirming ~20 ft is the norm for this group, Gopinath included.
SOURCED (Growse p.241, Govind Dev, for contrast): nave 100 ft long, 100 ft across the transepts, walls averaging 10 ft thick.
SOURCED-ish (mathura.nic.in, U.P. Government district site): Madan Mohan stands about 50 ft = 15.2 m high. One page gives 60 ft. Take 15 m as the order of magnitude for an intact temple of this class.
SOURCED (Gupta 2020, ASI): Gop Tila ≈ 3 m high, ≈ 2 acres ≈ 8,100 m².

DERIVED (Gopinath, internal, from the above):
- Choir 6.1 × 6.1 m; sanctum 6.1 × 6.1 m; lost nave 17.4 m long × ~6.1 m wide.
- Total internal length E–W, original: 17.4 + 6.1 + 6.1 ≈ 29.6 m.
- Side chapel: Growse gives no figure. At Madan Mohan it is a small single-cell tower-crowned chapel with one east door. Take 3.0–3.7 m internal square.

ESTIMATED (wall thicknesses and external envelope — NOT sourced):
- Wall thickness 1.2–1.8 m (4–6 ft). Govind Dev's 10 ft is a colossal outlier and must not be used here.
- External envelope of the SURVIVING block (choir + sanctum + chapel): roughly 12 m (E–W) × 9–10 m (N–S), with the chapel projecting a further ~5 m south.
- External envelope of the ORIGINAL temple including the lost nave: roughly 34–36 m E–W × 9–10 m N–S.
- Terrace with the three-bay arcade, south side: ~12 m long × 2.0–2.5 m deep, 0.5–0.9 m above court level.
- Plinth height 1.2–1.8 m; wall (plinth top to cornice top) 4.5–6.0 m; cornice projection ~0.5 m.

ESTIMATED (photogrammetric, from the 2021 east-front photograph using a standing figure at 1.70 m; wide-angle lens, so ±25%):
- Doorway within the great arch: ~1.65 m wide × ~3.0 m high.
- Great arch, outer archivolt: ~2.9 m wide, apex ~5.3 m above the threshold.
- Level wall-head flanking the arch: ~5.2 m above the threshold.
- Highest surviving rubble crest: ~5.9 m above the threshold.
- Threshold ~1.2–2.0 m above the lane.
- So the ruin as seen from the lane stands roughly 7.0–8.0 m overall. It is NOT a towering fragment; it is about the height of its neighbours.

ESTIMATED (spire, if you model state (b)): from the 2023 photograph the spire measures about 0.85 of the height of the wall-plus-plinth below it. On a 6.0–7.0 m wall+plinth that gives a spire of 5.0–6.0 m and an apex about 11–13 m above the court — consistent with the ~15 m order of magnitude for Madan Mohan, and well inside the surrounding roof heights of 6–10 m plus parapets, which is why it does not read on the skyline.

MEASURED (satellite, ESRI World Imagery z18/z19, 0.27–0.53 m/px):
- Courtyard of the 19th-century temple ≈ 15 × 14 m (±3 m).
- Surrounding lanes 2–4 m wide; no open ground within 80 m.
Caution: the roofs are continuous and the OSM node precision is unknown, so I could not resolve a clean footprint polygon for the old temple from imagery. Do not treat any satellite-derived footprint for the temple itself as reliable.

## What NOT to build

- DO NOT give it skyline presence. This is the single biggest instinct-error on this building. Rana Safvi's own text says of the four Akbar-era temples 'their red sandstone shikhars and buildings tower over the holy city' — true of Govind Dev, Madan Mohan and Jugal Kishor, FALSE of Gopinath. Gupta (ASI, 2020) measures the mounds: Goma Tila under Govind Dev 15 m, Dwadash Aditya Tila under Madan Mohan 12 m, Gop Tila under Gopinath only 3 m 'and is fully occupied by modern settlements'. Satellite imagery shows neighbouring roofs at 6–10 m. Gopinath should be invisible from every distant camera position in the scene and should only resolve once the player is in its lane.
- DO NOT build the nave/mandapa. Growse p.254 is categorical: 'the nave has entirely disappeared.' A 17.4 m hall in front of the sanctum block would make your model more than twice the length of what actually stands, and would occupy ground that is now lane, steps and 19th-century building.
- DO NOT give it an octagonal spire. That is Madan Mohan's ('a plain octagon of curvilinear outline tapering towards the summit', Growse p.251). Gopinath's is a square-based multi-ratha latina type with corner urushringas — the 1883 plate's stump is a stepped square pyramid, and the 2023 photograph shows a square-based curvilinear spire. Confusing the two is the standard error because the temples are otherwise near-identical.
- DO NOT build it cruciform, domed, or 100 ft long. Greek-cross plan, central dome, waggon vaults of true radiating arches, internal triforium gallery, 10 ft walls — all of that is GOVIND DEV (Growse pp.241–242). Gopinath is a simple linear string of three cells with a side chapel. If your model has a transept, you have built the wrong temple.
- DO NOT use true voussoir arches anywhere. Growse, on this very group (p.242): the arches 'are decorative only, not constructural: the spandrels in the head might be — and, as a fact, for the most part had been — struck out, leaving only the lintel supported on the straight jambs, without any injury to the stability of the building.' Every arch is corbelled decoration over a trabeated opening. Govind Dev's true radiating vaults are the celebrated exception, not the family trait.
- DO NOT use Mughal four-centred or cusped multifoil arches. The great east arch is a straight-sided LANCET rising to a sharp point, with a thin applied fringe of small triangular cusps on the intrados. A Shahjahani engrailed arch would date the building a century late.
- DO NOT wrap the bracket arcade around the building or make it structural. Growse: 'a curious arcade of three bracket arches, serving apparently no constructural purpose, but merely added as an ornamental screen to the SOUTH wall'. Three bays, one face only, standing forward on a terrace whose front is carved with rosette panels.
- DO NOT put the side chapel on the north. It is on the SOUTH side of the sacrarium (Growse p.253, and the same arrangement at Madan Mohan). The NORTH side is blocked solid by the 1821 temple — 'the north side is blocked by the modern temple.'
- DO NOT render it as clean bright red sandstone. Measured off photographs, the surviving 16th-c fabric is a dull brown-rose at H 9–21 degrees, S 22–40%, V 48–63%, and the broken rubble crest is a warm GREY at S 13%. The saturated reds on site (#AB5957, #61110B) are modern oxide and sindoor PAINT on the working temple, and the salmon #DBA175 is plaster. Three different materials, three different palettes.
- DO NOT model it as a free-standing monument in a clearing. It is inside a walled ghera: north face built against, both flanks pressed by modern white-plastered and brick houses, an east front opening onto a 2–4 m unmade lane, six rough steps, rubble, brick infill in the openings and under the plinth, a leaning electricity pole and low overhead cables crossing the facade at arch-springing height.
- DO NOT crown the ruin with a kalash, a gold finial or a flag. In the 1883 plate the crest is broken slabs with grass and shrubs rooted in it; in 2021 it is exposed grey rubble core with its ashlar facing gone. If you model the reconstructed spire state instead, its finial is a modest stone kalash, not gilt.
- DO NOT carve the whole surface. The plate shows large PLAIN ashlar fields. Ornament is concentrated in four places only: the plinth mouldings, the bracket-and-rosette cornice, the doorframes and great arch, and the arcade/terrace front. Roughly 70–80% of visible wall is plain stone, and the block is near-blind — about 5% opening to 95% solid.
- DO NOT put an ASI board or ASI branding on it. It is S-UP-88, a UTTAR PRADESH STATE protected monument; the on-site sign is the blue Directorate of Archaeology U.P. board. The U.P. Tourism plaque beside it, on a carved balustrade with lotus-bud post finials, reads 1589 and 'Rai Sil'.
- DO NOT place it on the ghats. Mathura district's own site and most travel pages give 'Parikrama Marg, Keshi Ghat', which will push you to the riverfront. OSM puts it 160 m inland from Kesi Ghat and 514 m north of Govind Dev, on Gopinath Bazar. There is no river view from it.
- DO NOT model the original deity in the sanctum, and DO NOT write '1632' or 'Rai Singh of Bikaner' into any on-screen text. The images went to Kaman and then to Jaipur; what is worshipped in Vrindavan today is in the 19th-century temple, not the ruin. The 1632/Bikaner attribution is a travel-site error contradicted by Growse, Entwistle and the official plaque.

## Uncertain — flag, do not assert

- BIGGEST ONE — whether a spire stands over the sanctum today. Growse 1883 (p.254): 'the three towers have been levelled with the roof.' Sinha & Dhariwal 2024: the temple 'has a multi-spired, not an octagonal shikhara over its sanctum (only one of which survives)'. A 2023 courtyard photograph shows a COMPLETE curvilinear red sandstone spire with two corner urushringas on a cornice that matches the 1883 plate bracket-for-bracket. A January 2021 photograph of the lane front shows no spire behind the rubble crest — but that photo only sees the east face, so it is an argument from absence. Most likely reading: the sanctum spire was rebuilt after 1883 and no source I found dates it. I am NOT confident. Build it as a swappable element.
- Whether the great arched front now facing the lane is the CHOIR (jagamohan) east arch or the COURTYARD GATEWAY. Growse describes both in the same paragraph — 'the choir arch is of handsome design, elaborately decorated with arabesque sculptures' and 'the entrance gateway of the court-yard is tottering to its fall.' The carved cusped-fringe arch and tympanum boss favour the choir arch (it parallels Jugal Kishor exactly, where the nave fell and the choir arch became the front), but the steps up from the lane and the placement of both signboards would also suit a gateway. No source labels it.
- FOUNDATION DATE. Four figures are in circulation. Growse: 'may be slightly the earliest of the series' — i.e. at or before 1590. U.P. Tourism plaque on site: 1589. The temple's own trilingual board: 'Estb 1559 AD'. Travel sites and Sinha & Dhariwal: 1632. I would use 1589/c.1590 and treat 1559 and 1632 as unsupported.
- FOUNDER. Growse, Entwistle and the official plaque all say Raesil / Raysal Darbari (Rai Sil), a Shekhawat Kachhwaha chief of Khandela in Akbar's service. Travel sites say 'Ray Singh, son of the king of Bikaner'. Sinha & Dhariwal call Raysal the YOUNGER brother of Nunkaran; Growse (p.254) suggests the opposite, that Nonkaran of Jugal Kishor 'was the elder brother of Raesil'. Entwistle agrees with Growse. Genuine confusion in the literature.
- DATE OF THE ADJOINING HAVELI TEMPLE. Growse (eyewitness): 'about the year 1821 by a Bengali Kayath, Nand Kumar Ghos.' Entwistle: early 19th century, Nand Kumar Bos. Modern sources variously say 1812, 1819 and 1877. Growse should win.
- 'DESTROYED BY AURANGZEB' is tradition, not documentation, for this building. Entwistle is explicit: 'None of the temples in Vrindaban was demolished, but the delapidated state of the original temples of Govind Dev, Gopinath, Madanmohan, and Radhavallabh ... is ACCREDITED TO attacks made at the behest of Aurangzeb.' Growse attributes Govind Dev's razed cella to Aurangzeb but says nothing of the kind about Gopinath — he simply records it as more ruinous. Rana Safvi notes scholars who put the deity's removal in the early 18th century and link it to Jat–Kachhwaha conflict rather than to Aurangzeb.
- PURE LEGEND, clearly separated: that Vajranabha (Krishna's great-grandson) installed the deity 5,000 years ago; that it self-manifested; that Jahnava Devi's own image was set beside it. The U.P. Tourism plaque itself hedges with 'kimvadanti ke anusaar' — 'according to legend'. What is documented is only that Madhu Pandit, of Gadadhar Pandit's line, established worship of a Gopinath image said to have been found at Vamsivat.
- NO MEASURED SURVEY EXISTS that I could find — no ASI drawing, no INTACH sheet, no dimensioned plan. Every plan dimension in this brief is Growse's Madan Mohan figure transferred on his statement of close correspondence. If the correspondence is looser than he implies, the plan is wrong by that much.
- WHETHER THE SOUTH ARCADE STILL STANDS. It is the temple's most distinctive feature and I found no modern photograph of the south side at all. It is present in the 1883 plate with its bays already brick-infilled. It may have gone, or be buried behind a later wall.
- THE FOOTPRINT could not be resolved from satellite imagery. The roofs are continuous, the OSM node's precision is unknown (I would allow ±30 m), and I could not separate the old temple's roof from its neighbours' with confidence at 0.27 m/px.
- COLOUR FIGURES are median samples from consumer phone/DSLR JPEGs with HDR and unknown white balance, in harsh Braj sunlight. Hue is fairly robust; saturation and value are not. Treat V as ±10% and S as ±10 points, and expect the true stone to be slightly less saturated than the phone images suggest.
- WHETHER THE NAVE'S SITE is now open lane or is built over. Growse says the nave is gone and that he pulled down 'mean sheds' built against the choir arch; the 2021 photographs show unmade ground and steps in front of the arch, which is consistent with the nave's footprint being open — but I have no source that says so.

## Independent check

Confidence: High on the documentary and architectural findings: I read Growse's third edition (1883) and Fergusson & Burgess vol. II p.158 as page images rather than OCR, downloaded the 1880s autotype and the 2015 photograph from their originating repositories, and read the 1910 ASI plan at higher resolution than the Commons crop the surveyor used. The headline claim you were most worried about - what crowns the building - survives: it is an amalaka with a low plain cap and no kalasha, and the tall tower is over the rear shrine while the front block is genuinely flat-topped in BOTH photographs. Medium on the numbers: my sikhara scale and the survey's both hang on the same plan-derived ~9.9 m diameter at the mandap-roof ring, so the heights are only as good as that one assumption, and the axial-steps and niche-size corrections are the ones I would most want a second pair of eyes on before they go into the model. Low on anything about the building's present state - no modern photograph of the east front exists, the only modern image of any kind is eleven years old, and ASI has held the building for over a century.

### Corrections

- NICHE SIZE IS ROUGHLY HALF THE TRUE SIZE. The survey gives the blind cusped niches flanking the east door as '~0.4 x 0.9 m'. Measured off my own crop of the Growse autotype (/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-sushilbajpai-Desktop-Projects-livebraj/7fcafe1c-f37a-48dc-90d1-37908875b73c/scratchpad/g_front.png) against the doorway in the same frame, each niche recess is ~0.50x the door's width and ~0.58x its height. Using the survey's own door estimate (1.3-1.6 m x 2.8-3.3 m) that gives ~0.7-0.8 m wide x ~1.6-1.9 m tall. At 0.4 x 0.9 they will read as postage stamps on a 9 m face.
- THE SURVEY CONTRADICTS ITSELF ON THE STEPS, AND THE PLAN WINS. The elevation section says 'a flight of steps set on the FLANK ... rather than on axis'. I read Woodcut 353 from the archive.org page scan (b28711270_0002 leaf n199, ~750x570 of plan area) rather than the 728x428 Commons crop the surveyor used: east of the mandap wall the plan draws a stepped platform with a CENTRAL FLIGHT OF ~5-6 TREADS ON THE BUILDING'S AXIS, flanked by platform wings, plus a smaller flight on one flank. The flank steps in the photograph are real, but the axial flight is the primary approach and is in the very drawing the survey claims to have measured. This also softens 'There is no axial approach'. See /private/tmp/.../scratchpad/plan_stair.png and plan_hr.png.
- THE ROLL MOULDINGS SIT ABOVE THE NECK, NOT BELOW IT. The survey says the amalaka 'overhang[s] a short fluted neck that sits on a cluster of 3-4 horizontal roll mouldings'. Both photographs show, top-down: smooth cap, a small upper ribbed ring, the gadrooned amalaka, THEN a cluster of 3-4 thin roll mouldings, THEN a short plain neck, THEN a bold ring, then the cone. The mouldings are between amalaka and neck. See /private/tmp/.../scratchpad/amal.png.
- AMALAKA RIB COUNT IS TOO LOW. The survey says ~18-20 ribs in the round from '9-10 lobes across the front half'. I count ~13 lobes across the front of the 2015 photograph and ~14-15 scallops across the rim of the 1880s autotype, giving ~24-30 in the round. Model at 24+ ribs, not 18-20. (The survey's own uncertainty note concedes the plate 'suggests more' - that concession should have overridden the figure in the brief.)
- THE AMALAKA IS MUCH FLATTER THAN '~1.9 m TALL'. Using the survey's own calibration (D=9.9 m at the mandap-roof ring gives 1 px = 0.0202 m in File:Kesi Ghat - panoramio.jpg), the lobed ring spans only ~40 px vertically = ~0.8 m, plus a ~0.5 m cap above it. Diameter checks out at ~3.76 m against their 3.65 m, so the disc's proportion is roughly 4.5:1, not 1.9:1. Build it ~3.7 m across x ~0.8 m deep with a low cap, or it will read as a drum.
- THE LOWER DRUM DOES NOT TAPER. The survey's profile table has h=-4.6 m, D=9.3 m against 9.9 m at the roof ring, implying the base narrows. My right-edge silhouette scan of the 2015 photograph (axis at x=4015) gives a CONSTANT D of 474-480 px (~9.6 m) from h=-6 m up to h=+4 m, interrupted only by two projecting rings that reach ~495 px (~10.0 m). The lowest ten metres is a straight cylinder with projecting rings. Do not batter it inward at the bottom.
- 'INDEPENDENTLY CONFIRMED BY THE 1910 ASI PLAN' IS FALSE FOR THE BRACKET PITCH. The plan draws the hood slab and two end supports only; it does not draw eight brackets (see /private/tmp/.../scratchpad/plan_door2.png). The 0.28 m centres are Growse's count of eight divided into a hood width measured off that same plan - arithmetic, not a second source. Keep 0.28 m if you like, but not on this justification. I also measure the hood at ~2.38 m, slightly wider than the stated 2.1-2.3 m.
- THE 'DIRECT SUN' COLOUR REFERENCE IS PROBABLY NOT A KESHI GHAT BUILDING. File:Keshi Ghat, Vrindavan, Uttar Pradesh, India old temple 1951 - panoramio.jpg is categorised on Commons under 'Govind Dev Temple', is a consumer-camera JPEG of 18 Feb 2010 with a date stamp and scaffolding, and shows a visibly paler, pinker, better-preserved stone (S 19-34%, V 58-66%). My own samples of the actual tower give raw H 343-360 deg (drum #5C393A #5F3738 #5B3636, cone #4D3139 #553A41), not the survey's 350-10 deg band. The recommended 'H 8, S 40, V 48' is biased warm and light by the substituted building; H 0-5, S ~40, V ~40 is better supported by the one photograph that actually shows this temple.
- SINHA 2024 PAGE CITATION IS OFF BY ONE. The kunj/ghat architecture passage (burjes, tibaris, chattris, bangladar roofs, chajjas, jaalis; Rani Kishori / Rani Lakshmi / Pandavali) and Fig. 3 are on p. 46, not p. 47. I rendered the PDF and read the page furniture. (p. 49 for the Yamuna turning south, and endnote i on p. 52, are correct.)
- THE EAST DOORWAY DOES HAVE A PROJECTING HOOD. The doNotBuild says 'The east door has a rosette-studded rectangular architrave instead'. The autotype shows the east door carrying a projecting chhajja slab with a saw-tooth pendant fringe on bracket ends, above the rosette architrave and the small arcade band. Not elephant brackets - but not a flat architrave either, and it is a strong shadow line on the main face.
- 'YOU SHOULD NOT BE ABLE TO SEE SKY OR INTERIOR THROUGH IT' OVERSHOOTS THE SOURCE. Growse's own word is 'pierced tracery', and the fan reads as pierced in the plate. It is a light-admitting screen directly over the door lintel, not a solid stone panel. Model it as a perforated fan, dark from outside, with light behind.
- SMALLER COUNTS AND SURFACES ON THE EAST FRONT. The blind arcade directly above the door lintel is ~6-8 units, not 'about nine'. The mandap FLANKS are not blank either: the autotype shows at least two small framed panels/grilles on the visible flank besides the hooded doorway, so 'the same solid wall, with one small doorway on the centre line' understates them. The exposed core reads as small coursed rubble stone; I could not confirm brick in it from the plate.
- THE bsb IMAGE NUMBER FOR THE GROWSE TEXT IS WRONG. The survey cites 'image 0324' for the p. 254 Jugal Kishor entry; bsb11486712_00324 is the sikhara-from-stupa page (p. 256). The plate URL (bsb11486712_00321) is correct - it downloaded the Jugal-Kishor autotype for me. Separately, the edition worry can be closed: the archive.org third edition is dated 1883 and carries the entry on p. 254, Madan Mohan on 251, Akbar on 241, ghats on 270, mahallas on 271 - every page citation holds.
- TWO UNSOURCED SPECIFICS TO DEMOTE. (a) 'A stair rises within the masonry on the SOUTH side of the shrine' - the 1910 plan shows a stair in the shrine wall at the mandap junction on one flank, but the drawing carries no north arrow, so the side is not determinable from it, and neither Growse nor Burgess says. (b) The 'hillock': Brajdiscovery's sentence is about 'a second Jugal Kishorji' among three living deities it lists, so it may be describing a different shrine near Keshi Ghat, not the ASI ruin. Keep the caution, drop the citation's weight.
- PLAN PRECISION EXCEEDS THE SCAN. At the survey's own 9.49 px/ft on a 728x428 image, one pixel is ~32 mm. 'The east wall measures 55 px = 5 ft 10 in against Burgess's stated 5 ft 9 in' is a sub-pixel distinction (55/9.49 = 5 ft 9.5 in), so it is not the internal check it is presented as; likewise 'projecting ~0.45-0.50 m' is a +/-1.5 px claim. The gross figures are fine - my own bounding box of the plan gives ~21.8 m x ~11.2 m overall against their 21.6 x 10.7 - but the sub-decimetre figures should carry a tolerance.

### Independently confirmed

- WHAT CROWNS IT: AMALAKA, NO KALASHA - CONFIRMED, and this is the claim you asked me hardest to break. Burgess 1910 p.158 reads verbatim 'crowned by an amalasila' (I read the page image, not OCR). I downloaded File:Kesi Ghat - panoramio.jpg fresh from the Commons API and cropped the crown: a gadrooned, strongly flattened disc with a low smooth cap on top and nothing above it. The 1880s autotype, downloaded from the Bavarian State Library IIIF, shows the same ribbed ring with no finial. No kalasha, pot, trident, flag or gold in either photograph. The survey's own uncertainty about the 1880s 'open dished ring' resolves simply: that plate is a steep upward view, so you see the lobes' undersides; the 2015 view is near-level and shows the cap. Same object.
- TOWER OVER THE REAR SHRINE, FLAT-TOPPED FRONT BLOCK - CONFIRMED, and more strongly than the survey claims. It treats the flat roof as 1880s evidence only. In the 2015 river photograph the mandap block is visible left of the tower (camera GPS 27.588471/77.700119 puts it NE of the temple, so east projects to the left of frame): a red block with a bold projecting cornice, a parapet and a plainly flat roof, no stump, no chhatri. Burgess's sentence 'Above the level of the mandap roof the sikhara tapers upwards' is verbatim. The ISKCON-style inversion is not present here.
- EXACTLY THREE STRING COURSES ABOVE THE MANDAP ROOF - CONFIRMED by my own measurement, not by trusting Burgess. Scanning the tower silhouette in the 2015 photograph I find bold rings at y = 1180, 1305, 1475, 1680 and 1920; the mandap parapet sits at y ~1612, so three rings lie above roof level, one at roof level, one lower on the drum. Exactly the structure the survey's table asserts.
- THE SIKHARA PROFILE TABLE REPRODUCES. Calibrating on the survey's own D=9.9 m at the roof ring gives 1 px = 0.0202 m; their h/D pairs then come back within ~5-10% (h=+9.9/D=5.0 measures 10.1/4.85; h=+4.0/D=9.0 measures 4.1/9.49).
